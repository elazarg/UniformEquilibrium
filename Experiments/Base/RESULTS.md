# Experiment results

All results below are finite-model or interface results.  None is promoted to
the active proof tree, and none is counted as closure of a general analytic
atlas leaf.

## E01 — jointly controlled finite-group lottery

Passed.  Finite-group addition produces a uniform signal against either one
controller's unilateral change, and uniform group signals encode rational
lotteries exactly.  A raw-action transition counterexample shows that the
continuation must factor through the protected signal.

## E02 — inhomogeneous hazard scheduler

Passed.  For a power schedule `alpha_t = t^-a`, useful order `k` can recur while
harmful order `m` is summable exactly when `m > k` permits
`1/m < a <= 1/k`.  Nonvanishing irreversible leaks remain fatal.

## E03 — path-complete Livsic account

Passed.  Exact rational cycle checking recovers the potential/cycle
alternative.  A lifted `(mode,state)` potential exists in an example with no
state-only potential, and bounded `O(sqrt(T))` reset debt is `o(T)`.

## E04 — Curry–Howard atlas analyzer

Passed against the current atlas.  It found fourteen constructors, one semantic
terminal, and thirteen nonsemantic forms.  Every current nonsemantic
reconstruction type is conclusion-bearing; this is a progress-obligation map,
not thirteen concrete eliminators.

## E05 — arc orientation

Passed.  A rational analytic coordination family has pure and fully mixed
equilibrium arcs with different limiting supports.  Arc selection is therefore
a real variable.  The toy orientation determinant is not a stochastic-game
equilibrium-index theorem.

## E06 — owner monodromy

Passed.  A mixed-owner cycle has zero aggregate holonomy but typed holonomy
`(1,-1)`, producing nontrivial deck translation in finite covers.  This
formalizes custody failure but does not equate it with strategic harmfulness.

## E07 — Kelly debt boundary

Passed.  The optimal iid Bernoulli likelihood bankroll has expected log growth
`KL(1/2+p || 1/2) = 2p^2 + O(p^4)`.  It cannot pay debt linear in `p`; quadratic
debt is the critical scale.

## E08 — causal states and synchronization

Passed.  A three-hidden-state chain produces indefinitely many beliefs already
distinguished by one-step prediction.  The subset construction also recovers
the Cerny `(n-1)^2` shortest synchronization lengths through seven states.

## E09 — signed target transport in Lean

Passed with `lake env lean`.  One-sided signed target transport plus child
payoff caps gives the parent deviation cap; the lower direction is symmetric.
Lean also verifies a two-child cancellation whose signed error is zero and
whose absolute error is one.

## E10 — exact small-game census

Passed.  All 6,561 two-player `2x2` games with payoffs in `{-1,0,1}` were
classified exactly.  Removing response degeneracies leaves only the expected
unique-pure, unique-mixed, and two-pure-plus-mixed patterns.  This is a census
pipeline dry run, not an atlas census.

## E11 — continuous-time resolvent

Passed.  For `P_lambda = I + lambda A`, the reduced Abel value converges exactly
to `(I-A)^-1 g`, the rate-one exponentially killed continuous-time resolvent.
The calculation transfers occupation values, not strategic credibility.

## E12 — collateral account

Passed.  Exhaustive finite paths verify that minimum escrow is maximum prefix
drawdown.  For increments of a bounded pathwise potential it is bounded sharply
by the potential oscillation.  Expected-drift certificates need an additional
probabilistic-solvency interface.

## E13 — ledgered dissipativity in Lean

Passed with `lake env lean`.  A one-step inequality between tracking error,
storage drift, tolerance, and ledger drift telescopes to a finite-prefix bound
and an explicit average-error estimate.  Strategy and storage synthesis remain
hypotheses rather than being hidden in the theorem.

## E14 — approximate predictive compression

Passed.  A contractive two-state predictor was quantized to 33 memory states
and retained a horizon-independent error bound over 257 initial beliefs and 500
steps.  Conditioning on a rare observation amplified a tiny belief error by
nearly one thousand, proving that transition mixing alone is insufficient.

## E15 — random-phase sigma-delta lottery

Passed.  One uniform phase realized a 400-step rational rate stream with exact
one-time marginals and pathwise prefix discrepancy below one.  Once the phase
is public, however, future pulses are perfectly predictable; causal refresh is
the unresolved strategic requirement.

## E16 — Abel boundary layer and retargeting

Passed.  A one-live-state Markov reward model has Abel endpoint `23/36`, while
every fixed positive-hazard policy has Cesaro target `5/4`.  Horizon-dependent
hazards preserve the boundary layer but are not a uniform policy.

## E17 — multiscale filter bank

Passed.  A logarithmically slow scale handles every fixed finite collection of
polynomial access orders and inverse-power monitoring bills.  It does not
amplify an `exp(-1/lambda^2)` event with the same epochs, fencing the required
rate-class assumption.

## E18 — transition algebra

Passed.  Exact rational closure distinguishes a scalar identity algebra, a
four-dimensional cyclic algebra, a four-dimensional nilpotent/transient
algebra, and a generic full `3x3` matrix algebra.  Their commutant dimensions
are respectively 9, 4, 4, and 1.  Actual endpoint-operator extraction and a
general Jacobson-radical computation remain open.

## E19 — player representations

Passed.  Reynolds averaging gives the common-welfare projection, while the
stabilizer of a distinguished deviator splits player space into deviator,
opponent-average, and opponent-standard channels.  Pure transfer obstructions
can be invisible in the trivial representation without thereby being proved
harmless.

## E20 — cyclic Fourier Abel/Cesaro modes

Passed.  For a slow cyclic kernel `P_lambda=(1-c lambda)I+c lambda C`, every
nontrivial character has a nonzero Abel boundary-layer limit but vanishes in
the Cesaro limit of each fixed positive-speed policy.  This is a modewise
retargeting calculation, not a general-game diagonalization.

## E21 — equivariant averaging in Lean

Passed with `lake env lean`.  Reynolds averaging over a finite group produces
an invariant vector and commutes with equivariant rational-linear maps.  The
result applies to linear occupation, potential, and dual spaces and deliberately
does not average nonlinear Nash equilibria.

## E22 — stationary Hodge currents

Passed.  Exact rational linear algebra decomposes a stationary edge field into
an orthogonal gradient and a divergence-free cycle current.  A biased
three-cycle carries net current `1/9` and entropy production `log(2)/3`.
Owner-valued currents can cancel in aggregate while remaining nonzero in each
owner channel, so scalar circulation loses strategically relevant custody.

## E23 — adiabatic Markov tracking

Passed.  In a moving two-state chain, a target varying on the logarithmic time
scale is tracked when the spectral gap closes as `t^-0.4`: the error falls to
about `1.6e-5` by time one million.  At the critical `t^-1` gap, the error
stays near `0.127`.  Adiabatic tracking is therefore a genuine positive regime,
but it requires a quantitative separation between target drift and mixing.

## E24 — metastable Schur confluence

Passed.  Exact rational Schur-complement elimination of two fast transient
states gives the same effective generator and effective reward whether they
are eliminated jointly, first-to-second, or second-to-first.  The calculation
isolates an order-independent local coarse-graining primitive; nonlinear
strategic selection and credibility are not included.

## E25 — thermal equilibrium selection

Passed.  Scalar logit fixed-point computations in the Big Match and Sorin's
absorbing game show that the ratio of temperature to discount selects different
stationary branches.  Very cold regularization recovers the discounted targets,
whereas warmer `tau=sqrt(lambda)` scaling selects radically different limits.
Thermal smoothing is consequently an arc selector, not a universal cure for
the Abel/Cesaro target gap.

## E26 — entropy production versus linear debt

Passed.  Near detailed balance in a biased cycle, stationary current and a
current-sensitive payoff loss are first order in the bias, while entropy
production and one-step relative entropy are second order.  The tilted pressure
has the expected quadratic germ `log cosh(theta)`.  Entropy production is a
useful monitor, but by itself cannot finance a generic linear credibility debt.

## E27 — common reversible Dirichlet geometry

Passed.  Two distinct rational kernels with a common uniform invariant law
satisfy exact Dirichlet identities and common Poincare bounds, exhaustively
checked on 625 integer-valued functions.  Their shared coercive geometry gives
uniform `L2` contraction under arbitrary switching.  This is a robust positive
subclass, although general controlled kernels need not share an invariant law
or reversibility.

## E28 — finite-controller cycle verifier

Passed.  Exact cycle enumeration finds a reachable positive-mean deviation
cycle in the unsafe product.  In the safe product, the maximum simple-path
potential satisfies every edge inequality and bounds all accumulated gain.
This gives a short checker for a fixed deterministic controller while leaving
the controller producer outside the theorem.

## E29 — exact-rate memory blow-up

Passed.  All 1,259 reduced rates with denominator at most 64 have minimum
deterministic recurrent cycle length exactly equal to their denominator.  The
balanced construction attains prefix discrepancy below one.  Along rates
`1/2^k`, explicit phase memory is exponential in the binary input length even
though the target rate has a tiny description.

## E30 — contextual selector synthesis contains SAT

Passed.  For every subset of the eight signed three-variable clauses, the
assignments satisfying the formula are exactly the selectors making all clause
cycles nonpositive.  A true literal contributes `-3` and a false literal `+1`.
Thus verification is linear in the displayed cycles, while unrestricted
contextual selector synthesis contains a 3-SAT core.  This is not asserted as
a hardness classification of the full conjecture.

## E31 — commit/reveal timing

Passed.  Simultaneous XOR has zero bias against either one deviator, whereas a
public sequential last mover achieves bias `1/2`.  Ideal commitments recover
the simultaneous guarantee only with enforced or simultaneous opening;
sequential opening with selective abort again permits bias `1/2`.  Binding,
hiding, and guaranteed opening are separate resources.

## E32 — threshold secret sharing

Passed.  Exact enumeration over `GF(5)` verifies perfect one-share privacy for
a 2-of-3 Shamir scheme and reconstruction from every pair.  Once all shares are
public, each transcript determines the secret.  Private observation is the
resource producing the secret phase; the algebra alone does not provide it.

## E33 — live entropy budget

Passed.  A deterministic 24-stage stream generated by a hidden three-bit seed
has exactly three total bits of conditional entropy.  Revealing the seed makes
the total zero, while one fresh honest simultaneous XOR contribution supplies
24 bits over 24 stages.  Deterministic expansion can redistribute seed entropy
but cannot amplify it information-theoretically.

## E34 — quitting path-closure kernels

Passed (`lake env lean Experiments/QuittingPathClosureKernels.lean`).
The Lean proof establishes exact finite hazard normalization with the
surviving/no-divergence atom retained, an anchored first-divergence mixture
that preserves constants and common payoff intervals,
the finite survival-weighted Bellman telescope for arbitrary time-dependent
survival factors and residuals, its tail-closure corollary, the stationary
contraction bound `R <= eta / (1-q)`, and the fact that hazard-scaled residuals
`eta_t <= epsilon * (1-q_t)` spend at most one copy of `epsilon` over an
arbitrarily long path.  Its forward Duhamel theorem gives the matching
variable-coefficient perturbation/edit telescope.  Using the landed
opponent-action and non-solo-mass
kernels, it proves that cumulative non-solo absorption under every unilateral
deviation is the exact Duhamel sum on the opponent-only hazard and is bounded
by the loss of opponent-only live mass.  It also proves that every unilateral
quitting deviation's absolute finite-average to terminal-payoff error is
bounded by one opponent-only live-mass Cesàro envelope; if that envelope's
limit is zero, convergence is uniform over all deviations.  Finally, the
quantitative `(epsilon,epsilon^2)` calculation proves that one normalized
simplex direction erases the second hazard scale.  This closes the finite
scalar path algebra and the opponent-absorbing analytic case; deriving the
Bellman residual from local perfection, handling a surviving tail by a
stationary fallback, and retaining iterated hazard scales remain strategic
work.

## E35 — finite quitting-debt monotonicity and non-necessity

Passed (`lake env lean
Experiments/QuittingFiniteDebtMonotonicity.lean`), axiom-clean
(`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` only).  Three results about the
attained minimum aggregate surviving debt
`quittingFiniteZeroBoundaryNashBellmanMinDebt`, the scalar to which the finite
quitting Nash--Bellman chain lane has reduced uniform existence.

1. It is **antitone** in the cutoff, hence converges to its infimum
   (`minDebt_antitone`, `minDebt_tendsto_iInf`), so the landed `iInf = 0`
   criterion is equivalent to `Tendsto ... (𝓝 0)`
   (`minDebt_iInf_eq_zero_iff_tendsto`) and one exhibited chain at one cutoff
   bounds every larger cutoff (`minDebt_le_of_chain`).  The proof deletes the
   first displayed stage (admissible one cutoff lower), notes that
   reinstating a stage multiplies every playerwise debt by an
   opponent-continuation mass in `[0,1]`, and reinstates a stage in front of
   the lower minimizer using the landed compact predecessor correspondence.
2. At cutoff zero the debt is path-independent and equals the positive-solo
   mass `∑ who, max 0 (reward {who} who)` (`minDebt_zero`), which therefore
   bounds every later minimum debt; a table with all solo payoffs nonpositive
   has identically zero minimum debt (`minDebt_eq_zero_of_solo_nonpos`).  That
   is the Never branch seen through this interface and the interface's
   nonvacuity probe.
3. **The criterion is not necessary.**  The opponent-survival factor is a
   product over a player's *opponents*, so for a one-player table it is an
   empty product: a one-player quitting game with solo payoff `r > 0` has
   `minDebt = r` at every cutoff (`onePlayer_minDebt`,
   `onePlayer_iInf_ne_zero`) although it obviously has a uniform-equilibrium
   payoff (quit at stage zero — that half is hand-argued, not Lean-checked).
   So vanishing minimum debt is sufficient but never necessary as stated, and
   only its `2 ≤ card ι` restriction is open.  The defect is that the debt
   prices the deviator's late solo option absolutely, with no credit for the
   value the prescribed profile already delivers — the recurring Q84
   absolute-terminal-remainder fence.  The proposed repair (certified rather
   than zero terminal boundary, debt `survival · max 0 (solo − w)`) is
   recorded in the proof-mining source note and is not tested
   here.

Two attempted two-player promotions of the fence both collapsed: with a zero
terminal boundary, "player `j` quits surely and his continuation value equals
his quit payoff" is self-consistent, which drives the other player's opponent
survival to zero at cutoff one.  Keep both tables as generator regressions.

## E37 — positive-solo own-joining monotonicity

Passed and axiom-clean (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` only):
`Experiments/QuittingJoinMonotoneUniform.lean`.

If every player with positive singleton payoff weakly benefits from joining
every already nonempty opponent quitting coalition, all exact dynamic debts
on every positive-length zero-boundary chain vanish. The optimized min-max
debt is therefore already zero at cutoff one, and the landed exact-D compiler
produces a uniform-equilibrium payoff. The global all-player joining
monotonicity is an immediate corollary.

The same file proves the contrapositive search fence: any counterexample to
uniform existence must have a positive-solo player `i` and nonempty opponent
coalition `T` with `r_i(T) > r_i(T ∪ {i})`. This is necessary only; Q125
violates the inequality and nevertheless has a stationary equilibrium.

## E38 — certified-boundary polyhedron and prefix semigroup

Passed and axiom-clean (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` only):
`Experiments/QuittingCertifiedBoundaryPolyhedron.lean`.

For exact scalar prefix exploitability

`R(w) = max A (T + χ (w + β)) - (B + P w),`

the experiment proves the exact two-halfspace description of `R(w) ≤ ε`,
the explicit interval when `0 < P < χ`, feasibility iff the interval
endpoints are ordered, and global optimality of the balance point
`(A - T - χβ) / χ`. It also proves that max-affine best-response summaries
and affine prescribed summaries are closed under prefix concatenation:

`A' = max A₁ (T₁ + χ₁ A₂)`, `T' = T₁ + χ₁ T₂`,
`χ' = χ₁χ₂`, `B' = B₁ + P₁B₂`, and `P' = P₁P₂`.

This is a fixed-dimensional target for certified-tail intersection and
periodic/block compilers, not a producer of the tail certificate itself.
With finite real floors `A`, these summaries form an associative composition
semigroup, not literally a monoid: an identity valid on every real terminal
value would require an unavailable floor `A = -∞` (or a restricted/extended
summary domain).

## E39 — owner-solo certification dichotomy on the positive-debt branch

Passed and axiom-clean (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` only):
`Experiments/QuittingOwnerSoloCertification.lean`.  Companion
analysis in `OWNER_SOLO_CERTIFICATION_NOTE.md`.

The producer candidate is the positive-debt owner's own Q129 deviation,
promoted to prescribed play.  The exact criterion is machine-checked: for
any rate `p ∈ (0,1]`, if `0 ≤ r_i({i})` and every opponent satisfies
`(1−p)·r_j({j}) + p·r_j({i,j}) ≤ r_j({i})`, the owner-solo stationary root
is an exact terminal Nash equilibrium, and a new generic delivery lemma
(`quittingGame_isUniformEquilibriumPayoff_of_terminalNash_exact`) makes its
payoff `(r_i({i}), r_{-i}({i}))` a uniform-equilibrium payoff.  The
capstone (`uniformPayoff_or_universalJoining_of_positiveDebt`) consumes the
landed provenance layer: positive exact dynamic debt at any live date
yields either that uniform payoff or a universal joining obstruction —
at **every** rate some opponent strictly gains by quitting against the
owner.  Both obstruction witnesses are extracted in Lean: a strict
sure-quit joiner at `p = 1` and a weak preemptor
(`r_j({j}) ≥ r_j({i})`) by a finite minimum-gap argument at small rates.

Calibration: the Q125 counterexample sits in the certification branch with
feasible rate interval exactly `(0, 1/2]`, recovering its known stationary
equilibrium `(1,0)` — a value provably outside the zero-boundary chain
geometry (its forced chain values are only `(3/2, 0)` and `0`).  So the
relative-boundary producer cannot factor through chain compactification,
and the owner-solo family is the first certificate class drawn from the
debt object itself.  Together with E37's stay-out fence, a barrier table
now needs both coalition frictions simultaneously: for the forced
positive-debt owner, someone strictly prefers staying out of some
coalition, and someone strictly wants into the owner's exit at every rate.
The remaining open composition is playing the joining obstruction against
the summable-clock/terminal-packet structure of the extracted tail.

## E41 — periodic lifts and continuity of graph-directed coding

Passed and axiom-clean (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` only):
`Experiments/GraphDirectedPeriodicLift.lean`.

Two consequences of the landed compact graph-directed pullback theorem are
machine-checked. First, under a common strict contraction, every compatible
value lift inherits every period of its vertex/edge code: shift the lift by
one code period and uniqueness makes the two lifts equal. Thus a closed
support word cannot hide an aperiodic continuation-value path above it.

Second, two compatible paths with a common edge prefix of length `n` obey

`dist(value₁ 0, value₂ 0) ≤ q^n · diameterBudget`.

This is the quantitative continuity theorem for the symbolic coding map.
For a full shift, periodic codes are dense, so their exact periodic Bellman
lassos are exponentially dense in the whole graph-directed continuation
attractor. Injectivity is separate: branch separation would make the coding
a Cantor embedding, while the current overlapping boxes justify only a
continuous quotient/semiconjugacy.

## E42 — periodic closing in a contracting full shift

Passed and axiom-clean (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` only):
`Experiments/GraphDirectedFullShiftDensity.lean`.

For a one-vertex graph-directed compact system, every edge sequence is an
admissible full-shift code. Repeating its first `n > 0` edges gives a periodic
code with an exactly periodic compatible lift, and the initial values obey

`dist(periodicValue 0, value 0) ≤ q^n · diameterBudget`.

Thus exact periodic lifts are exponentially dense in every uniformly
contracting full-shift component. The theorem is about the symbolic cover;
it does not claim injective payoff coding or separated branch images.

## E45 — direction-barycenter chart: stationary values on the blow-up

## E46 — greedy buffered paths and the certified-exit decoder hook

Passed with zero diagnostics and axiom-clean (`propext`, `Classical.choice`,
`Quot.sound` only):
`Experiments/GreedyBufferedExitDecoder.lean`.  Companion to
`BufferedOneSeamReturnOrExit.lean` (same CG7 dichotomy along an arbitrary
serial orbit); this file machine-checks the **greedy** construction of
`ideas/CycleGeometryResolution.md` §8, whose exit branch is strictly
stronger.

For a relation serial on an ambient set `K`, a potential `Φ`, levels
`b ≤ a`, and a finite cell assignment, the greedy buffered path from any
start with `Φ ≥ a` satisfies, within `Fintype.card Cell` exact relation
steps (`greedyBufferedPath_return_or_certified_exit`): either two path
states with the whole displayed horizon inside the buffer `{Φ ≥ b}` share
one cell — the downstream one-seam return — or there is a **first** exit
time `N > 0` with the entire prefix buffered, `Φ(path N) < b`, potential
drop strictly above `a − b`, and — the decoder hook —
**no admissible successor of the pre-exit state stays in the buffer**
(`forall_not_mem_buffer_of_greedyStep_exit`).  So an exit certifies
failure of buffered seriality; it is never an artifact of successor
choice, which is exactly interface 4 of the CG8 anchored
repair-or-descent target.

Not claimed: decoding of relation edges into bounded game stages, anchor
or packet preservation, or any optimized-debt conclusion — those remain
CG8.

**Extension (2026-08-03, same file, axiom-clean):** the dead-end-aware
trichotomy `greedyBufferedPath_return_or_certified_exit_or_deadEnd` drops
the seriality hypothesis entirely.  From a buffered start in `K`, within
the covering number of steps the greedy path yields exactly one of: the
one-seam return with the whole displayed horizon in `K` and the buffer;
the certified first exit (buffered exact prefix, drop above `a − b`, no
buffered successor at the pre-exit state); or a **typed dead end** — an
exact buffered prefix ending at a state of `K` with no admissible
successor at all (`RelationDeadEnd`).  Since predecessor-seriality on the
positive plateau is exactly the open part of CG5, the trichotomy keeps the
compactness layer consumable while seriality is only partially available:
a dead end is a structured output for the producer, not a failed
hypothesis.
