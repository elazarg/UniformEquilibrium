# Sharp numbers of nonsingleton rewards for simultaneous quitting and nonstationarity

Center each player's nonempty-exit rewards at its own singleton. **Four is the minimum number of nonzero nonsingleton rewards for which every sufficiently accurate equilibrium can require a positive probability that all four players quit together.** This is a sparsity threshold for a requirement already known for dense games. The example below has an explicit regret bound at every reward scale $R\ge100$. [Earlier compulsory grand exits](ALL_FOUR_FIRST_QUITTERS_AND_UNBOUNDED_JOINT_DATES.md)

The entries below are the minimum numbers of nonzero centered nonsingleton rewards needed for each requirement. In the last column some pair $i,j$ has $G_{ij},G_{ji}\le0$. Every possible counterexample belongs to this singleton domain. A compulsory outcome on one date has a positive table-dependent lower bound for every sufficiently accurate profile.

| Requirement | All singleton matrices | Matrices with such a pair |
|---|---:|---:|
| At least three quitters together on one date | 0 | 1 |
| All four quit together on one date | 4 | 5 |
| Each of the fifteen coalitions has positive first-exit probability on the same date | 5 | 6 |
| Every stationary profile has regret bounded away from zero | 5 | 6 |

The last two unrestricted minima are attained by different five-entry games. Their **joint minimum is six on both domains**. In fact, every table with at most five edits admits vanishing regret on stationary profiles or on profiles with zero probability of even one simultaneous first exit. A single six-entry game attains both requirements and has an exact four-date equilibrium.

With at most three nonzero nonsingletons some three-player coalition is a pure first-date equilibrium. Before the changes all four triples are stable. Every player's comparison on a triple is with its adjacent pair or with the grand coalition. One changed entry can create a strict improvement from at most one of the four triples: in a fixed recipient row, each entry belongs to exactly one edge comparing that recipient's two actions. The other endpoint of the edge has even size. Thus some triple stays stable. Its grand-exit probability is zero and every unilateral replacement still faces a sure rival. This argument allows arbitrary singleton rewards and arbitrary signed own levels.

For the upper example take original own singleton rewards all equal to one. Put $G_{ij}=-3$ when $i=j+1$ modulo four, $G_{ij}=1$ on all other off-diagonal entries, and $G_{ii}=0$. Each column sums to $-1$ and every pair has at least one positive comparison. The only four nonzero higher rewards are the grand rewards, $Z_i(I)=R$ for every $i$. Everyone quitting at the first date is an exact original equilibrium. For every independent private profile let $e$ be the maximum original terminal regret and $p_I$ its grand first-exit probability. Then for every $R\ge100$,
$$
                         e+R p_I\ \ge\ 1/1000 .
$$

Indeed let $p_1$ be the total singleton first-exit probability and $\nu$ the probability of joint Never. Each owner can obtain at least one by quitting immediately. Summing the four prescribed payoffs therefore gives
$$
 4-4e\ \le\ 4(1-\nu)-p_1+4R p_I.
$$
Suppose $e+Rp_I<1/1000$. Then $\nu<1/1000$ and $p_1<1/250$. Until some hazard exceeds $1/2$, singletons account for at least one eighth of each date's nonblank probability: each solo probability is at least the corresponding hazard divided by eight, and the sum of the hazards bounds the nonblank probability. Therefore the first date with such a hazard exists and its reach $S$ exceeds $1-8/250$. Sort its four hazards $q_1\ge q_2\ge q_3\ge q_4$, using these subscripts for decreasing order. Since $S q_1(1-q_2)^3\le p_1$, we have $q_2>3/4$. On this date the player with the fourth hazard can quit for $1+R q_1q_2q_3$. That player's prescribed conditional payoff is at most $2+Rp_I/S$; all other original exit rewards are at most two. Deviating after joint survival costs at most $e/S$ in the conditional regret bound. Consequently $R q_1q_2q_3\le1+(e+Rp_I)/S<2$, giving $q_3,q_4<2/[100(3/4)^2]<1/25$.

Now set all grand rewards to native zero. Maximum regret on this reference table is less than $1/1000$, since every deviation payoff weakly decreases and each prescribed payoff drops by $Rp_I$. Let $i,j$ be the owners of the two largest hazards, with $G_{ij}=1$ after possibly exchanging them. At this date $i$ can wait once and quit on the next date. Its original offer is at least
$$
 1+q_j(1-q_3)(1-q_4)-3(q_3+q_4)>1+0.45 .
$$
The only negative contributions are from the small rivals' singletons, and the replacement absorbs surely. Its prescribed value is at most $1+(1-q_i)<1.25$, since a quit pays exactly one on the reference table. Pasting this deviation after survival gains more than $0.20S>1/1000$, a contradiction. Thus at each fixed $R\ge100$, every profile with $e<1/2000$ has $p_I\ge1/(2000R)$.

Three quitters can already be necessary with zero higher entries. On the same cyclic singleton matrix set all 44 native higher entries to zero. Every pure triple is an exact equilibrium. Write $d_3$ for the supremum over dates of their unconditional probability of first exit with at least three quitters. If $e+d_3<1/1000$, the same payoff sum, with $R=0$, and the first-high-hazard argument give $S>0.968$ and $q_1,q_2>3/4$. Since $S q_1q_2q_3\le d_3$, both small hazards are below $1/25$. The same wait-once comparison contradicts $e<1/1000$. Thus the respective minimum numbers of higher entries needed to compel three and four simultaneous quitters are zero and four.

## The price of grand exits on the six-entry frontier

Use four original singleton levels equal to one, and the following native singleton matrix and six nonsingleton entries, with all other native nonsingletons zero:
$$
 G=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&-1&2\\-2&0&1&2\\1&1&0&-2\\-1&-1&2&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad Z_3(013)=1,\quad Z_0(012)=-4,\quad Z(0123)=(1,1,R,-1).
$$
Write $e_R$ for a profile's maximum original terminal regret and $p_I$ for the probability of a grand first exit. There are constants $R_0,\delta>0$ such that, on every such table with $R\ge R_0$,
$$
                         e_R+R p_I\ge\delta
$$
for every profile. An exact original stationary terminal equilibrium exists there and has $p_I=\Theta(R^{-1})$. Thus the order of the lower bound on grand exits is attained. For each fixed $R\ge R_0$ it is impossible to approximate equilibrium while making grand exits arbitrarily unlikely, even with completely nonperiodic private clocks.

For existence use $y=G^{-1}e_2=(4,2,3,5/2)^\top>0$. With proper stationary hazards $q$, let $H_i$ be opponent survival, $P_i$ the passive contribution from nonempty rival exits and $Q_i$ the native quit offer. The stationary field is $F_i=P_i-(1-H_i)Q_i$. For $q=R^{-1/3}x$ its rescaling $R^{1/3}F(q)$ tends in $C^1$ on bounded boxes to $Gx-H(x)e_2$, where $H(x)=(x_0+x_1+x_3)x_0x_1x_3$. At $x=y/H(y)^{1/3}$ the Jacobian has determinant $-3\det G$ by the matrix determinant lemma and Euler's identity for degree four. A nearby full-support exact root persists for all large $R$. Every rival family has geometric survival, so the immediate and perpetual-wait indifferences give exact terminal Nash and its original uniform payoff. Each hazard is $\Theta(R^{-1/3})$. The grand probability is the product of all four hazards divided by the probability of a nonblank date. ### Passing to a fixed table and an absorption-time limit

Otherwise take $R_n\to\infty$ and profiles with $e_{R_n}+R_np_I\to0$. They also have vanishing regret on the fixed table at $R=1$: reducing that one reward weakly lowers every deviation offer of player 2 and changes its prescribed payoff by $o(1)$. Write $e$ for the maximum regret on this bounded reference. The probability $\nu$ of joint Never obeys $\nu\le e$, by replacing an owner's Never mass by a late finite date; the limiting gain on joint Never is one and all other outcomes converge to their old payoffs. On every date with joint survival bounded below, the original high-table forced offer to 2 bounds $R_n q_0q_1q_3$ from below. Its prescribed conditional payoff is bounded on those dates because $R_np_I=o(1)$. Pasting the deviation after survival gives the uniform upper bound on that offer. In particular, every limiting hazard row at positive reach satisfies $q_0q_1q_3=0$.

The following construction gives the continuous parts as well as the atoms of the limit. On each date of the reference profiles let $S$ be prior joint survival and $h=S(1-\prod_i(1-q_i))$ its unconditional first-exit mass. Represent dates by their successive disjoint intervals of lengths $h$ in $[0,1]$. On each interval spread the masses of its first-exit coalitions uniformly. Extend the cumulative coordinates $X_C^n(t)$ constantly over the final tail of length $\nu$. They are nondecreasing and 1-Lipschitz. After subselection they converge uniformly to $X_C(t)$ with $\sum_C X_C(t)=t$. The absolute remaining native vector
$$
 W(t)=\sum_C Z(C)\,[X_C(1)-X_C(t)]
$$
is Lipschitz and $W(1)=0$. Sorting the date intervals by decreasing length and taking a diagonal subsequence of endpoints and hazards gives a countable family of positive-length limit intervals, with disjoint interiors. On each such interval $(s,t)$ the limiting coalition masses are the product-row masses times $1-s$, the sum is $t-s$, and $W$ is affine. Every other interval after the first $k$ in a profile has length at most $1/(k+1)$.

For a reference date with $S$ bounded below, the actual conditional payoff and each owner's full conditional cap differ by at most $e/S$ by pasting. The conditional prescribed original payoff at an initial mass coordinate $s$ converges to $1+W(s)/(1-s)$, since its joint Never correction is $\nu/S$. At a macroscopic interval's entrance every row's value is at least its forced quit offer $Q_i$ in native units, with equality for every positive hazard. Indeed if the original cap is $B_i$ and original immediate quit offer $1+Q_i$, the loss from assigning the current hazard to that offer is at most the conditional regret. The reference has no sure opening: it has roles $u=0,v=1,a=3,b=2$ and $KN=4>1=mL$. Sole $b$ is excluded by $G_{ab}=2$, and sole $v$ would require $Y=(3+\sqrt{21})/2$, $X=1/Y$, $Z=1+Y$, giving the sure owner the negative advantage $2/Y-2Y$. The other sure sets are excluded by the six-entry theorem. These same tests apply to a limiting sure row: owners facing a sure rival use the limiting two first-date offers; either prospective sole owner $v,b$ can refuse and secure at least its own singleton after a blank by quitting at the next date (both have nonnegative native participating rewards). Thus every macroscopic row is proper and $t<1$. [Sure-opening classification](FIVE_NONSINGLETON_REWARDS_AND_THE_SIX_ENTRY_FRONTIER.md)

Off the interiors of these intervals only singleton derivatives occur almost everywhere. More precisely, for almost every such $s<1$ one has $W(s)\ge0$, $X'_{\{i\}}(s)=0$ whenever $W_i(s)>0$, and the four singleton derivatives sum to one. Here are details for this diffuse part. On $s\le1-\eta$, dates of length $h\le\varepsilon$ have $\alpha=h/S\le\varepsilon/\eta$, hence $q_i\le\alpha$, $|Q_i|\le C\alpha$, and total collision mass on those dates at most $6\varepsilon/\eta$, since the probability of a tie is at most $6\alpha^2$. Their initial $W_i^n$ are at least $-C\varepsilon/\eta-o(1)$ by the immediate offers, and interpolation changes $W$ by at most $C\varepsilon$. In addition,
$$
                  \sum_{\rm dates} S q_i(B_i-1-Q_i)\le e.
$$
Indeed the best-response Bellman recursion prices prescribed mass stopping now by $B_i-1-Q_i$, and prices the waiting mass by the nonnegative loss from waiting using its optimal successor cap. Iterating the resulting gap identity gives the sum bound, including infinite calendars by finite partial sums. On any small-date interval where $W_i$ is bounded below by a fixed positive constant, $B_i-1-Q_i$ is therefore bounded below uniformly for $n$ large and $\varepsilon$ small, so the aggregate mass of singleton $i$ there tends to zero. Formally remove the first $k$ ranked intervals and their limits from the cumulative coordinate measures. The positive residual measures converge weakly as the finitely many affine portions converge uniformly (a zero-length limit contributes zero). Their supports satisfy the preceding estimates with $\varepsilon=1/(k+1)$ on compact subintervals of $[0,1-\eta)$. As $k\to\infty$ they decrease in the limit to the derivative measures off the positive-length intervals. They have total density one there. This gives all the stated diffuse conditions by localization and then $\eta\downarrow0$.

### Eliminating all macroscopic collisions

Throughout $[0,1]$, rows 1 and 2 of $W$ are nonnegative. Their immediate native offers are nonnegative at every actual date, and the endpoint and interpolation estimates above pass this to the limit. At a macroscopic non-solo date containing 2 but omitting 3, row 2's immediate offer vanishes since $q_0q_1q_3=0$. Its passive offer is strictly positive from the singleton rivals in $01$ and its nonnegative successor, a contradiction. Likewise a non-solo date containing 1 but omitting 0 gives row 1 a zero forced offer and strictly positive passive offer from a singleton in $23$. Thus $012$ and $123$ cannot be active supports. In fact 1 and 2 cannot both be active, as they would require both 0 and 3, violating $q_0q_1q_3=0$. No remaining macroscopic row has a negative native quit offer: row 0's negative triple requires 1 and 2, and row 3's negative grand requires 0, 1 and 2. At macroscopic exits that adjoin a diffuse region, values are nonnegative by continuity from diffuse points or other interval entrances. More explicitly any exit below 1 either starts a further interval, is approached on the right by interval entrances, or is approached there by diffuse points of nonnegative value. Thus $W\ge0$ everywhere, including by affine interpolation inside the intervals.

The only remaining triple support is $023$. Its three active values would all be zero. Waiting with a nonnegative successor forces $p_2\ge2p_3$, $p_0\le2p_3$ and $p_0\ge2p_2$ for the three active odds, which is impossible. At an active pair both members get zero. A positive singleton reward from the other member is therefore impossible, since waiting yields that positive contribution with positive probability and a nonnegative successor. Only the unique mutually negative pair $01$ can occur.

At the entrance of a pair $01$ interval, $W_0=W_1=0$. With positive singleton masses $x,y$ and positive pair mass $z$ there, $W_2\ge x+y>0$ and $W_3\ge z>0$, from the passive singleton entries and the offer for joining $01$ respectively. This interval cannot have a positive-length immediate predecessor. A preceding solo 2 or 3 leaves its own next coordinate zero, a preceding solo 0 or 1 would require a negative preceding value in its mate's row, and a preceding $01$ increases both mate coordinates strictly. Nor can such an interval start at a positive time $t$ accumulated from the left. Choose a small left neighborhood on which $W_2,W_3>0$, and a point $s<t$ outside interval interiors there. All intervals starting between $s$ and $t$ are solo 0, solo 1 or pair $01$. Each strictly increases $W_0+W_1$, since only the two singleton masses contribute, with coefficients 2 and 1, and each nonblank product row is proper. Off those intervals only colors 0 and 1 can occur because $W_2,W_3>0$, so the derivative of $W_0+W_1$ there is at least one almost everywhere. Integrating gives $W_0(t)+W_1(t)>0$, a contradiction.

After at most one initial $01$ interval, the remaining positive-length terminal segment consists entirely of diffuse parts and solo intervals. On every solo interval the mover's coordinate stays zero by its zero immediate offer and $G_{ii}=0$. Rescaling the remaining segment yields a continuous singleton path: the derivative masses form simplex weights, $W$ is the integrated remaining singleton payoff, and each positive color has zero coordinate. The matrix $G$ is $R_0$: every column has a negative entry, its off-diagonal entries are nonzero and all its principal triples and $G$ itself are nonsingular. The sign-switch graph has only the directed cycle $023$, whose singleton mass ratios are $1,1/2,1/4$ and whose geometric scale is $1/2$. The omitted row 1 has first-tour sum $-2+1/2+1/2=-1$. The finite path test therefore rejects $G$. This contradiction proves the asserted lower bound. [Continuous singleton path theorem](ALL_FOUR_PLAYER_SINGLETON_PATHS_HAVE_A_FINITE_TEST.md)

### A single-date lower bound and the fixed reference

The aggregate bound also places a compulsory grand event on a single physical date. Write $d_I=\sup_t S_t\prod_iq_{it}$ for the largest date's unconditional grand probability and $h_t=S_t(1-\prod_i(1-q_{it}))$ for its total exit probability. On a date of positive reach its conditional nonblank probability is at least $\max_iq_{it}\ge(\prod_iq_{it})^{1/4}$. Hence its grand fraction is at most $(d_I/S_t)^{3/4}$. The survival intervals of lengths $h_t$ have right endpoints $S_t$, so
$$
 p_I\le d_I^{3/4}\sum_t h_t S_t^{-3/4}\le d_I^{3/4}\int_0^1s^{-3/4}\,ds=4d_I^{3/4}.
$$
Consequently $e_R\le\delta/2$ forces $d_I\ge(\delta/(8R))^{4/3}$. The stationary equilibria above attain the order $R^{-4/3}$ at date one, so this order is also sharp.

At the fixed reference $R=1$, write $d_3$ for the largest unconditional probability on any date of a first exit by at least three players. Then $\inf(e_1+d_3)>0$. Otherwise apply the same compactification directly to a sequence with both quantities vanishing. Every macroscopic row now has support of size at most two because any positive-reach row with three positive limiting hazards has positive unconditional probability of a triple or grand exit on that date. As above, rows 1 and 2 have nonnegative value throughout. A proposed $12$ pair would give active row 2 a zero offer and a positive waiting gain, so no such pair survives. The remaining rows have nonnegative native immediate offers, and the only possible pair is $01$. The same left-neighborhood restriction and singleton path contradiction apply. Thus every sufficiently accurate profile on the reference requires at least three simultaneous positive hazards on one reached date, with a fixed positive probability of an actual three-or-more exit on one date. The accumulating return constructs such approximations using at most three active hazards throughout, while the exact equilibrium obstruction on the same reference requires four for any exact equilibrium.

### Three quitters on one date for a full orientation of the six-entry template

Use the [six-entry template](FIVE_NONSINGLETON_REWARDS_AND_THE_SIX_ENTRY_FRONTIER.md) with strict foreign singleton comparisons, unique mutually negative pair $M=\{u,v\}$ and triple bonus $K=Z_a(M+a)>0$. Allow all six sizes and either implementation of either withdrawal. Suppose $G$ fails the continuous singleton-path test, $G_{bu},G_{bv}>0$ and at least one original own singleton is positive. There exists $\delta>0$, depending on the fixed table, such that every private profile satisfies
$$
 e+\sup_j\Pr(\text{the first exit is on date }j\text{ and has at least three quitters})\ge\delta.
$$
No inequality on the relative magnitudes of the six entries is needed here.

Otherwise take a vanishing-regret sequence along which the displayed supremum tends to zero. Apply the preceding fixed-table absorption-mass limit. Joint Never vanishes by the positive singleton. Every macroscopic product row has at most two positive hazards. No such row contains a sure owner. Two sure owners would form a pure pair: at $M$ the outsider $a$ strictly prefers joining, and at every other pair one owner strictly prefers the other's singleton. A sole sure owner with one proper mixer would make that mixer indifferent between a zero native participating pair reward and a nonzero foreign singleton. A sole sure owner with three quiet rivals is excluded by a negative singleton in its column, since otherwise the column's basis vector gives a constant singleton path. In all cases these deviations face a sure rival.

Rows $v,b$ have nonnegative forced quit offers everywhere. Their remaining payoff coordinates are nonnegative by the endpoint and interpolation bounds of the compactification. A macroscopic pair $vb$ is impossible: one of the two gets a positive singleton from the other, but its immediate offer is zero while waiting earns a positive term and a nonnegative successor. For the remaining supports every row's forced quit offer is nonnegative. Indeed with at most two movers a negative grand entry cannot enter any forced offer, and the sole possible negative participating triple entry belongs to $u$ against $vb$. The same bounds now give $W\ge0$ throughout. The preceding two-player argument rules out every pair except $M$.

At each $M$ interval's entrance $W_u=W_v=0$, $W_a>0$ by $K$ times its positive pair mass, and $W_b>0$ by its positive singleton terms from both mates. On a left neighborhood with $W_a,W_b>0$ only solo $u,v$ and $M$ can contribute: a solo outsider's coordinate stays zero across its entire interval, and the diffuse part has singleton contact. Every contribution there strictly increases $W_u+W_v$ in the forward absorption coordinate, because the only nonzero terms in those two rows are the mutual negative singletons and any proper $M$ interval contains singleton mass. Thus $M$ can occur only as the initial interval. It ends before unit mass, since both movers are proper. The remaining segment is a continuous singleton path, a contradiction.

With the printed census labels and $a=3,b=2$ this test covers witnesses 6, 8, 18, 19 and 21, and open neighborhoods of all five singleton matrices. Their row-2 comparisons to $01$ are respectively $(1,1),(2,1),(1,1),(1,1),(1,1)$. The census verifies rejection of singleton paths at each. This rejection is open: simplex controls along paths for converging matrices have a weakly convergent subsequence through their uniformly Lipschitz cumulative masses, and their remaining payoffs converge uniformly, preserving nonnegativity and singleton contact. With the outsider roles exchanged the three-date theorem supplies an exact period using at most two movers per date throughout a positive range of the new joining bonus, for the same singleton matrices and either implementation of either withdrawal. ### Compulsory grand exits together with a positive stationary regret gap

Take witness 6, with rows $(0,-1,-1,2),(-1,0,-1,2),(1,1,0,-1),(-1,-1,2,0)$, roles $u=0,v=1,b=2,a=3$, and own singletons one. Allow either implementation of each withdrawal: $Z_u(vb)=N$ or $Z_u(uvb)=-N$, and $Z_a(uvb)=L$ or $Z_a(I)=-L$. The other nonzero nonsingletons are $Z_a(uva)=Z_u(I)=Z_v(I)=1$ and $Z_b(I)=R$.

There exists $R_0\ge10$ such that for every $R\ge R_0$, every fixed $L>R$, and all sufficiently large $N$ the following three conclusions hold together. The game has an original uniform-equilibrium payoff. Every sufficiently accurate private profile gives one physical date a probability bounded away from zero of first exit by all four players. Every stationary profile has regret bounded away from zero. The two gaps may depend on the table. Existence follows from the two large-withdrawal constructions at witness 6 with $K=1$. [Large-withdrawal constructions](SIX_ENTRY_EQUILIBRIA_WITH_LARGE_WITHDRAWAL_REWARDS.md) · [Six-entry sure openings](FIVE_NONSINGLETON_REWARDS_AND_THE_SIX_ENTRY_FRONTIER.md) · [Absorption-time compactification and the single-date estimate](#the-price-of-grand-exits-on-the-six-entry-frontier)

**Grand exits.** In fact the grand-date assertion holds for all $R\ge R_0$, $N\ge4$, $L\ge1$, $N>L$. First fix one such table, temporarily without the lower threshold on $R$. Suppose a sequence has regret and largest grand-date mass tending to zero. The single-date estimate $p_I\le4d_I^{3/4}$ gives vanishing total grand mass. Apply the fixed-table absorption-time compactification. Every macroscopic row has support of size at most three. It has no sure mover: two sure movers require $N\le L$, while a sole sure mover on this matrix must be $v$ with all three rivals proper and positive, by the sure-opening proof. These necessary comparisons apply at a limiting date, since the sure owner can secure zero native continuation after a blank by quitting next. The absolute remaining payoffs in rows $b,v$ are nonnegative everywhere by their nonnegative forced offers, the diffuse inequalities and the endpoint interpolation of the compactification.

On a nonsolo date containing $b$ and omitting $a$, the forced value of $b$ is zero and its waiting value strictly positive from a mate's singleton and its nonnegative successor. This excludes every support containing $u,v,b$, since a fourth mover is impossible. Thus even a negative participating grand in row $a$ cannot enter any remaining forced offer, and the same endpoint argument gives nonnegative remaining value for $a$. On a date with support $v,b,a$ all three active forced offers are zero. If their odds are $x,s,w$ in that order, the singleton waiting terms force $s\ge2w$, $w\ge x$ and $x\ge2s$, impossible. It follows that $v,b$ never act together. Even a negative participating $N$ in row $u$ therefore cannot enter any remaining forced offer, and its remaining value is likewise nonnegative. The same cycle excludes support $u,b,a$. Higher passive terms can only increase a waiting value. At a pair other than $uv$, one member has a positive rival singleton, zero forced offer and a nonnegative successor, also impossible. Thus only solos, $uv$ and $uva$ remain. In particular the limiting masses of $vb$ and $uvb$ vanish, since the diffuse part has only singleton exits. This reasoning works on every convergent subsequence.

If the grand-date conclusion failed for unbounded $R_n$, choose a failing table $(R_n,N_n,L_n)$ for each $n$. Pass to a subsequence with fixed implementations. The fixed-table conclusion lets us take one profile per table with regret and
$$R_np_I,\qquad N_n(p_{vb}+p_{uvb}),\qquad L_n(p_{uvb}+p_I)$$
all tending to zero. Take the bounded reference $R=1,N=4,L=1$ with the same implementations. Put $E_n=\sum_{i,C}|Z_i^n(C)-Z_i^0(C)|p_C=o(1)$. At a date of reach $S$, prescribed continuations on the two tables differ by at most $E_n/S$. The only possible upward changes in an immediate quit offer on moving to the reference come from raising the two negative participating marks. For $u$ the increase is at most $N_n q_v q_b(1-q_a)$; for $a$ it is at most $L_n q_u q_v q_b$. Multiplying by $S$ and summing over dates bounds these by the second and third vanishing quantities respectively: the owner's two possible actions partition each relevant rival event. Thus each reference immediate offer exceeds its actual offer by at most $o(1)/S$ regardless of the owner's own hazard. All immediate waiting contributions from nonempty rival exits weakly decrease on the reference, since the changed passive rewards are positive and are reduced.

Only local estimates are needed for the absorption-time compactification at the bounded reference. Joint Never mass is at most actual regret by the common own levels one. On every compact survival prefix $S\ge\eta>0$ the actual conditional best-reply cap $C_i$ is within $e_n/S$ of the actual prescribed value by pasting and hence within $o(1)$ of the reference prescription. There it bounds the reference immediate offer $1+Q_i^0$ up to $o(1)$. Also
$$\sum_t S_t q_{it}(C_i-1-Q_i^0)_+\le e_n+E_n.$$
Indeed the actual Bellman sum bound applies with $Q_i^n$, and grouping by the first coalition gives $\sum_t S_t q_{it}|Q_i^n-Q_i^0|\le E_n$. These estimates give the same entrance inequalities, active equalities and diffuse singleton contact on the reference. For a proper macroscopic row the affine remaining-payoff identity gives the waiting comparison using its successor. At a limiting sure rival, a deviator can wait this date and then never quit; native passive rewards on every table are at least $-2$. The actual first-date nonempty waiting contributions dominate the reference ones, and the vanishing probability of no rival exit at that date contributes at worst $o(1)$ to the lower bound. Thus the supposed date's limiting reference payoffs bound both its forced offers and these waiting offers for every owner facing a sure rival. For such owners the reference prescription is exactly the mixture of these two first-date offers, so its positive-mass actions maximize them. This proves the necessary two-sure comparisons and the other owners' necessary comparisons against a sole sure mover. They require $N\le L$ at two sure movers and all three rivals positive against the sole possible sure $v$, contradictory on this reference with zero grand mass. Finally on $S\ge\eta$ the original conditional row-$b$ payoff is uniformly bounded by $R_n p_I=o(1)$ and its fixed smaller rewards. Pasting its immediate offer bounds $R_n q_uq_vq_a$, so every macroscopic reference row has $q_uq_vq_a=0$. The same support argument applies on the reference, and now also rules out $uva$. This proves $\min(S,C)=0$ at five entries. The six-entry example with a positive stationary regret gap and all fifteen coalitions compulsory on the same physical date attains the joint lower bound. Six is consequently sharp for requiring nonstationarity together with even one simultaneous first exit, a triple, the grand, or all fifteen on a date. For a dense table, let $m_6$ be the sixth-largest absolute centered nonsingleton reward, counting all 44 entries. Keeping the five largest and setting all others to zero changes each profile and unilateral deviation payoff by at most $m_6$, without changing its first-exit law. Hence $\min(S,C)\le2m_6$. This also bounds the minimum using $\inf(e+d_3)$ in place of $C$, where $d_3$ is the largest unconditional probability at a single date of a first exit by at least three owners.

### Both requirements occur on all 116 singleton sign classes

For **each of the 21 printed strict singleton matrices and each of the 95 printed tied matrices**, there is a completion with an original uniform-equilibrium payoff, a positive stationary regret floor, and constants $e_0,c>0$ such that every profile of regret below $e_0$ has some date with unconditional probability at least $c$ of a simultaneous first exit. The date may depend on the profile. Each literal matrix can also be kept at the center of a full 60-dimensional open set of games with all three properties. Thus all surviving sign classes are needed for the behavioral classification. These examples admit equilibrium. For stationary hazards $q$, define the passive field $P_i(q)$ as the expected centered passive reward from the nonempty set of rivals who quit on one date. The immediate centered quit offer is zero. The necessary stationary comparisons at nonzero hazards are $P_i\ge0$ if $q_i=0$, $P_i=0$ if $0<q_i<1$, and $P_i\le0$ if $q_i=1$. If regret tended to zero at a sequence of stationary profiles with a nonzero limiting hazard vector, these comparisons would hold at the limit. With at least two positive limiting hazards this follows by continuity of prescribed, immediate and perpetual-wait payoffs. A sole positive limiting hazard at $j$ is excluded by an owner $i$ with $G_{ij}<0$ who can quit immediately for zero centered. Its prescribed centered payoff tends to $G_{ij}$. A sequence of all-zero hazards has regret at least one. If all hazards tend to zero with positive sum, let $z$ be a subsequential limit after dividing by that sum. When at least two owners have positive hazards at each stage, their rivals absorb geometrically. Prescribed and immediate centered payoffs tend respectively to $Gz$ and zero, and the perpetual-wait payoff for $z_i<1$ tends to $(Gz)_i/(1-z_i)$. Vanishing regret implies $Gz\ge0$ and $z_i(Gz)_i=0$, including a unit coordinate since the diagonal is zero. This violates the printed $R_0$ test. If just one hazard is positive along a subsequence, its negative outsider again strictly gains. Consequently excluding every nonzero solution of the stationary comparisons proves a strictly positive stationary regret gap.

For 103 matrices the verification uses a positive integer vector $b$. On the computational matrix $G\operatorname{diag}(b)$ take additive passive rewards, so that the field is linear in $q$. To recover the literal printed singleton matrix, multiply every passive payoff at a nonempty coalition $T$ by $\prod_{j\in T}b_j^{-1}$. If $q'_j=(q_j/b_j)/(1-q_j+q_j/b_j)$, the new field in row $i$ is the old field at $q'$ times $\prod_{j\ne i}(1-q_j+q_j/b_j)>0$. The coordinate transformation is a bijection of the cube preserving its faces. For the thirteen other matrices the certificate lists integer passive pair and triple rewards on the literal $G$. On these tables the field is generally nonlinear.

The verifier uses only integer sign comparisons on boxes. Choose each owner to be quiet, a proper mixer or sure. When at least one is sure, the fields are multiaffine on the free cube and their vertex values are their Bernstein coefficients. When nobody is sure, choose a maximal positive hazard $q_h=r$, write the free hazards as $q_j=r y_j$, and set $y_h=1$. Divide every field by $r$. This is multiaffine in the free $y_j$ and quadratic in $r$. At a vertex of the $y$ cube write it as $A+B r+D r^2$. Its three Bernstein coefficients in $r$, multiplied by two, are $2A,2A+B,2(A+B+D)$. Thus all starting coefficients are integers after a positive row scaling. The zero-radius endpoint is safe since a nonzero normalized direction satisfying its comparisons would contradict $R_0$. An owner who must weakly prefer waiting excludes a box if its maximum coefficient is negative; an owner who must weakly prefer quitting excludes it if its minimum coefficient is positive. Both tests apply to mixers. Otherwise bisect the box and use the exact Bernstein midpoint coefficients on each half. The verifier terminates with every box excluded.
