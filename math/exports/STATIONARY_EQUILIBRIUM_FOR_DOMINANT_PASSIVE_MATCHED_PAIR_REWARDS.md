# Stationary equilibrium for dominant passive matched-pair rewards in every direction

Split four players into two pairs A and B, with mate m(i). At each live date players privately and independently quit or continue. The first nonempty quitting coalition S receives its vector r(S), and perpetual play receives zero. Set sᵢ=rᵢ({i}) and center each nonempty coalition payoff by Zᵢ(S)=rᵢ(S)−sᵢ. Write Gᵢⱼ=Zᵢ({j}), Gᵢᵢ=0. Suppose each Gᵢⱼ for j in the opposite pair is strictly negative and some y≥0 has Gy strictly positive in every coordinate. No condition on any joining reward is imposed. For each i let Tᵢ be the centered reward to i when precisely its two opposite-pair owners quit. These are four distinct passive reward coordinates.

Let K=maxᵢ|Tᵢ|. For any compact family of G inside this domain, there exist K₀,δ,c,C>0 such that the following condition guarantees an exact stationary terminal Nash equilibrium. Take K>K₀ and require each of the forty other centered nonsingleton entries Zᵢ(S) to satisfy |Zᵢ(S)|≤δK^(|S|−1). These forty entries may vary independently. Each equilibrium hazard is at most C/K and at least three are at least c/K. The same profile gives a fixed uniform-equilibrium payoff at arbitrary signed own levels. The four Tᵢ may have arbitrary signs, and any of them may be zero. Consequently every counterexample in this matching class satisfies

    maxᵢ|Tᵢ| ≤ C₁ max(1, max over the other forty coordinates of |Zᵢ(S)|^(1/(|S|−1))),

with C₁ uniform on the given compact family. For the forty coordinates in a bounded box, this removes the whole exterior of a bounded set of the four passive rewards.

## The endpoint equations at small hazards

For each row i let Qᵢ be the expectation conditional on forcing its Quit, Pᵢ the unconditional contribution to its Wait reward when at least one rival quits on this date, and Hᵢ the probability all its rivals wait. Use centered rewards. At a stationary hazard q put Fᵢ=Pᵢ−(1−Hᵢ)Qᵢ. When there is a surely finite rival under indefinite repetition, waiting forever pays Pᵢ/(1−Hᵢ), and every finite deadline interpolates between this value and Qᵢ by a geometric sum. It suffices to find 0≤qᵢ<1, Fᵢ≥0 and qᵢFᵢ=0. This prices every full private stopping law by taking mixtures of deadlines and Never.

Write kᵢ=−Tᵢ/K and ε=1/K, and decompose k=kᴬ+kᴮ by zeroing the complementary recipient pair in each summand. Use p_A(z)=∏_{i∈A}zᵢ and p_B(z)=∏_{i∈B}zᵢ. For q=εz the exact stage polynomials give

    F(εz)/ε = Gz−kᴬ p_B(z)−kᴮ p_A(z)+L(z).                    (1)

Let θ be the maximum of |Zᵢ(S)|/K^(|S|−1) over the other forty entries. On each fixed bounded z set, L and its first derivatives are O(ε+θ), with constants depending only on that set and the compact family of G. The distinguished passive term in Pᵢ/ε is −kᵢ times the product of the opposite z coordinates times (1−εzₘ₍ᵢ₎). The singleton contributions to Pᵢ/ε give (Gz)ᵢ+O(ε). For every other passive S of size b, its event in Pᵢ has a factor εᵇ times a polynomial bounded with its derivatives. For every participating S of size b≥2, its event in Qᵢ has a factor ε^(b−1), multiplied in Fᵢ/ε by (1−Hᵢ)/ε, bounded with its derivatives. The centered own singleton is zero and none of the four T entries occurs in the corresponding row's Qᵢ. These terms give the asserted uniform estimate with no assumptions about the signs of the forty rewards.

Let Π permute mate columns. Then M=GΠ has positive diagonal (every yᵢ and every matched entry must be positive by Gy>0), strictly negative between the two pairs and zero elsewhere. The vector Πy>0 supplies strict diagonal dominance after column scaling. Writing M=D−N, D its positive diagonal and N≥0, the spectral radius of D⁻¹N is strictly below one by this scaling, and its off-diagonal graph is irreducible. The Neumann series gives M⁻¹≫0 and G⁻¹=ΠM⁻¹≫0.

## The ratios of the two pair products

For uniformity consider any sequence of games with K→∞ and θ→0, and extract limits G,k of their singleton matrices and normalized poles with |k|∞=1. Use U=G⁻¹kᴬ, V=G⁻¹kᴮ. For y with d=p_A(y)+p_B(y)>0 put z=y/d. If t=p_A(y)/d, multiplying (1) by d reduces the complementarity problem to finding nonnegative y,λ with disjoint supports and

    G_n y−(1−t) kᴬ_n−t kᴮ_n+d L_n(y/d)=λ.                    (2)

Thus at prescribed t and λ, the limiting solution is the affine expression (1−t)U+tV+G⁻¹λ. On any compact interval of such affine solutions on which d stays positive, (2) has a continuous nearby solution, converging in C¹ to that expression as n→∞. Indeed the Jacobian in y converges uniformly to G there. Equivalently, solve for y by multiplying by G_n⁻¹ and treating the last term as a contraction on a fixed small ball about the affine solution, then use the implicit function theorem. This works for trial negative coordinates as long as d stays positive; only nonnegative solutions are used as actual hazards.

We first describe what happens if U has a negative coordinate; exchange A,B for the corresponding case of V. In the mate-swapped coordinates x=ΠU the B rows of Mx=kᴬ give x_B=−M_BB⁻¹ M_BA x_A, a strictly positive matrix applied to x_A. Eliminating B gives W x_A=k_A, where W=M_AA−M_AB M_BB⁻¹ M_BA has strictly negative off-diagonal entries. Its inverse is the strictly positive AA submatrix of M⁻¹. The two-by-two inverse formula shows W has positive diagonal and determinant. There is therefore an i in A with Uᵢ<0 and kₘ₍ᵢ₎<0. In fact a negative coordinate in U forces a negative x_A coordinate, say x_m=Uᵢ<0. If k_m≥0, row m forces x_i<0 too, and then k_i<0 since W⁻¹>0 and the right side cannot be nonnegative. Exchange the two labels in that case.

Fix this i. Add λᵢ>0 to the right-hand side at t=0 until Uᵢ+(G⁻¹)ᵢᵢλᵢ=0. At this crossing x_m=0, and the unchanged row m of W yields x_i=k_m/W_mi>0. Consequently all other coordinates are strictly positive and d>0. The slope in λᵢ is strictly positive. On a small interval about the crossing (2) has an actual solution with yᵢ=0 by continuity and the strict opposite signs at the ends. The other three entries remain strictly positive. Now t=p_A(y)/d=0, λ≥0, and (2) gives a three-active equilibrium for the scaled game.

It remains to treat U,V≥0. A nonzero U then has both B coordinates positive, as x_B is a strictly positive matrix times its nonzero nonnegative x_A. Among the A coordinates at most one can vanish. The symmetric statements hold for a nonzero V. If both U,V are nonzero, w(t)=(1−t)U+tV is strictly positive for 0<t<1 and d is positive on the whole closed interval. Use (2) with λ=0 to obtain y_n(t) uniformly close to w(t). At an endpoint where y_n has a negative entry, at most one is negative, it approaches 0, and its index i belongs to the corresponding recipient pair. At t=0, add a small nonnegative slack in that row. The derivative of yᵢ in its own slack approaches (G⁻¹)ᵢᵢ>0; a slack tending to zero makes yᵢ=0, with the other three still positive and with t=p_A(y)/d. The same argument at t=1 uses the B recipient pair. This directly gives an admissible equilibrium in either event.

Otherwise both endpoint vectors are nonnegative. For the continuous curve form the scalar

    f_n(t)=t p_B((y_n(t))₊)−(1−t)p_A((y_n(t))₊),

where the positive part is coordinatewise. Its values at 0 and 1 have opposite weak signs, so it has a zero. Everywhere on the curve at least three entries are strictly positive, uniformly away from zero: this follows from the compactness of the interval and the preceding coordinate counts for w. An interior zero therefore requires all four coordinates positive, and yields t=p_A(y)/d. At an endpoint zero, the vector already is nonnegative and the same ratio identity holds. In all cases λ=0 gives the required solution.

If U≠0 and V=0, take the interval [0,t_R] with p_A(U)/(p_A(U)+p_B(U))<t_R<1. On it w(t)=(1−t)U still has d>0 and at least three strictly positive coordinates. The limiting f(t_R) is strictly positive. At the left endpoint one uses the same small slack if needed, or else f_n(0)≤0. Thus the same zero construction works. When U=0,V≠0 exchange pairs and reverse t. The two columns cannot both vanish because |k|∞=1.

On each extracted sequence the construction has all z bounded and its third-largest coordinate bounded below: the closed trial intervals keep d bounded above and away from zero and the third-largest y bounded below. These subsequence conclusions imply uniform C,c,K₀,δ as asserted. Otherwise choose games with K>n and θ≤1/n having no solution with all z≤n and third-largest z≥1/n, then apply the construction on a convergent subsequence. The counterexample bound follows by contraposition with C₁ at least the maximum of K₀ and δ^(−1/b) for b=1,2,3. Three positive hazards ensure certain geometric rival absorption even if any one player alters its entire law. Thus each signed shift adds its whole constant to every prescribed or unilateral terminal payoff. Uniform geometric absorption under each unilateral replacement for the fixed game gives the finite-average uniform payoff conclusion.

## Why the active set must be selected

Consider A={0,2}, B={1,3}, all matched singleton entries 3 and all crossed singleton entries −1. The inverse has diagonal 2/15, crossed entries 1/5 and matched entries 7/15, verified by multiplication. Set all nondistinguished nonsingleton entries to zero for this calibration. Take T₀=K, T₁=T₂=T₃=0. The limiting complementarity system has z=(6,2,0,2), with zero residual in the three positive coordinates and residual 14 in the quiet coordinate 2. This is the strict three-active crossing in the proof. No full-support stationary solution with hazards O(1/K) exists as K tends to infinity. Indeed any bounded limit of z=Kq for such full-support solutions would satisfy Gz+e₀p_B(z)=0. The strictly positive inverse forces z=0. But with K‖q‖ tending to zero the exact full-support equations divided by ‖q‖ then give G(q/‖q‖) tending to zero, impossible by invertibility. On the reversed ray T₀=−K, any nonzero solution of the limiting system Gz−e₀p_B(z)=λ≥0 with z≥0 must have all four entries positive, since G⁻¹ is strictly positive and its nonnegative input cannot vanish. There the explicit full-support solution is z=25 G⁻¹e₀. Thus even these two opposite points of the direction sphere require different supports at the produced scale.

For a direction with both signs within each recipient pair, take k=(1,−1,−1,1). Neither a uniform sign of the four passives nor nonvanishing of their individual normalized coordinates is a hypothesis of the theorem. The earlier signed passive-coalition ray criterion fixes the orientations of inverse columns. The weighted collision-dominance criterion also allows proper subsets with different signed inverse columns, when the selected favorable coordinates dominate the other coordinates on the weighted scale. The present theorem quantifies over the whole four-dimensional exterior at once and selects the active set, even when individual directions degenerate. The theorem supplies no numerical value of K₀ or δ.

## Interface with the uniform-equilibrium target

The datum required by the checked declaration `quittingGame_fixedProfile_uniformPayoffWitnesses_of_terminalNash_exact` in `UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Terminal/TargetTail/TerminalUniformPayoffSelection.lean` is one behavioral profile with zero terminal regret against all replacements. Equations (1)–(2), the support selection and the stopping-law comparison above produce that datum in the original game. The corresponding target is its original terminal payoff. The exterior and support-selection arguments are ordinary mathematics awaiting formalization. The sufficient conditions concern the stated matching and dominance class. Arbitrary four-player tables remain open.
