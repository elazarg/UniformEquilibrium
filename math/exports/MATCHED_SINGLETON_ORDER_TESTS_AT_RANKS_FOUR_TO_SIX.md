## A bound at every rank from one crossed entry

The normalized crossed entries satisfy a single-coordinate bound on every matched rank failure. Set $q=(N-2)^2$ for $N\ge4$. If some $x_{ij}\ge (q-1)^3+(q-1)$, the all-four-finite test passes at rank $N$ for every completion. To prove this let $M$ be the largest crossed value at $(i,j)$, let $l=m(j),b=m(i)$. A failure would have $R_k<q$ and $U_k>1/(q-1)$ for every $k$. The two-cycle through the edge $b\to j$ gives $x_{lb}<1/M$. If $x_{il}\ge M/q$, another two-cycle gives $x_{jb}<q/M$, contradicting $U_b>1/(q-1)$ since the stated threshold exceeds $q^2-1$. Otherwise $R_i<q$ forces $x_{li}>1/(q-1)$ by its formula. Now $x_{lb}/x_{li}<(q-1)/M<1/q$ and $R_l<q$ likewise forces $x_{bl}>1/(q-1)$. Finally $x_{jb}>1/(q-1)-1/M$ from $U_b$. The four-cycle $b\to j\to i\to l\to b$ has product strictly larger than $M/(q-1)^3-1/(q-1)^2$, at least one at the threshold. This contradicts $\rho(P)<1$. At $N=4$ a single normalized crossed comparison of magnitude at least 30 suffices.
