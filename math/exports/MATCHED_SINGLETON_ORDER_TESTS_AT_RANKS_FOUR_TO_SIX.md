# Matched singleton order tests at ranks four to six

A native four-player quitting table $Z$ has $Z_i(\{i\})=0$ and zero payoff on perpetual play. Write $G_{ij}=Z_i(\{j\})$. At rank $N$ give each player $N$ training and $N$ validation labels. For each owner the cumulative counts differ by at most one at every order cut. Given the training order, the prescribed validation payoff must dominate the offers from every observed training date, literal Never and Late (a deadline after all finite opposing dates). The validation law independently draws one label uniformly for each participating clock. In the test used here all labels of all four players are finite. Any unilateral replacement therefore still faces a finite rival, so adding any signed row constant on all nonempty exits preserves every obedience inequality. Passing levels of unbounded rank is needed for the equilibrium conjecture. [Order formulation and reconstruction](../notes/CODEX_ROOT__INTERLACED_ORDER_TESTS_FOR_ARBITRARY_FOUR_PLAYER_GAMES.md#the-native-absorbing-order-hull-in-56-reward-coordinates)

Let $m(i)$ denote the mate of $i$ in the matching $01,23$. Suppose $G_{i,m(i)}>0$ and both crossed comparisons are strictly negative. Divide each row by its mate reward. Let the crossed magnitudes in normalized row $i$ be $p_i(1\pm d_i)$ in opposite pairs of columns, where $p_i>0$ is their mean. The rank-four all-four-finite native test passes, including every higher-reward completion and every signed row translation, under either of the following conditions.

1. All four means $p_i$ are arbitrary and independent, and $|d_i|\le 1/32$ in every row.
2. $p_0=p_1$ and $p_2=p_3$. The two means are arbitrary and independent, and the crossed magnitudes in a recipient row can occur in any positive proportions.

The twelve original singleton comparisons in the first assertion can vary independently in a full-dimensional region, without any bound on the four row ratios or positive scales. The second assertion allows arbitrarily large imbalance between the two negative entries in each row. These two assertions extend rank four to asymmetric singleton matrices. For a common positive mate reward and a common negative crossed reward, ranks five and six also pass at every reward ratio. An additional criterion below applies to a single extreme crossed comparison at any specified rank.

## The restricted matrices at a failed level

For the proof suppose the absorbing native infimum is positive, since otherwise the four-finite completion theorem applies. To obtain $y>0$ with $Gy>0$, take the strictly positive inverse column on this kernel, then add a sufficiently small multiple of $G^{-1}\mathbf1$. Put $x_{ij}=-G_{ij}/G_{i,m(i)}$ in the crossed positions, and let $P_{m(i),j}=x_{ij}$, zero elsewhere. Then $Py<y$, giving $\rho(P)<1$. In particular every directed edge cycle has product strictly below one, and $G$ has all inverse columns strictly positive by the convergent geometric series for $(I-P)^{-1}$. The inverse-column result supplies the necessary matrix condition for an actual positive gap. At any pivot $k$ write $j,l$ for the opposite pair, and $U_k=x_{jk}+x_{lk}$. The positive triple default on $\{k,j,l\}$ has neutral masses $z_j=x_{lk}z_k,z_l=x_{jk}z_k,z_k=1/(1+U_k)$. Its best single-helper ratio is
$$
R_k=\frac{\max(x_{kj},x_{kl})(1+U_k)}{x_{kj}x_{lk}+x_{kl}x_{jk}}\ \ge\ 1+1/U_k.
$$
The hidden two-owner continuation at rank $N$ works if $R_k\ge(N-2)^2$ for at least one pivot. It uses exactly these triple masses to complete the private continuation, with no restriction on higher payoffs. Consequently a failure at rank four implies $U_k>1/3$ for every $k$. Setting $u=p_0+p_1,v=p_2+p_3$, averaging the two crossed column sums on each side gives $u,v>1/3$.

## The two asymmetric rank-four families

For condition 1 set $\delta=1/32$ and $c=(1-\delta)^{-1}$. Entrywise $P$ dominates $1-\delta$ times the matrix with both crossed coefficients $p_i$ in row $m(i)$. This latter matrix has spectral radius $\sqrt{uv}$, as one sees by grouping its positive eigenvector in the proportions $p_{m(i)}$ within each side. Thus $uv<c^2$. Relabel the pairs so $x=c/(c+u)\le y=c/(c+v)$, and sort the means inside each pair with the smaller mean first. The two scalars lie in the triangle with vertices $(X,X),(1-X,X),(1/2,1/2)$ where $X=3c/(3c+1)$. In normalized rows multiply A's rewards by $x$, B's by $y$. This makes the mate coefficients $x,x,y,y$ and the mean crossed coefficients in order are
$$
 h_A,\quad c(1-x)-h_A,\quad h_B,\quad c(1-y)-h_B,
 \qquad 0\le h_A\le c(1-x)/2,\quad 0\le h_B\le c(1-y)/2 .
$$
The region in $(x,y,h_A,h_B)$ is the convex hull of twelve vertices, obtained by selecting a base vertex together with either endpoint of each displayed interval. Indeed, decompose the base point barycentrically, then independently mix the two endpoint choices in each interval in the proportions $2h_A/[c(1-x)]$ and $2h_B/[c(1-y)]$, constant across base vertices. The mean vector and mate coefficients are affine on this four-dimensional polytope.

For condition 2 write $a=1/p_0,b=1/p_2$ and interchange the pairs so $a\le b$. Here $\rho(P)=\sqrt{uv}=2/\sqrt{ab}$ even at full skew, since each row in a given block has the same sum and Perron's vector can be chosen constant on both pairs. The preceding necessary conditions give $ab>4$ and $a,b<6$. After dividing a row by its original crossed mean and multiplying by $s=1/(a+2)$ in A, $t=1/(b+2)$ in B, its mate coefficient becomes $1-2s$ or $1-2t$ and its crossed mean equals $s$ or $t$. The ordered pair $(s,t)$ belongs to the triangle with vertices $(1/8,1/8),(3/8,1/8),(1/4,1/4)$. Within each recipient row write the two crossed entries as $-s(1+e_i(-1)^j)$ for donor $j$, using $t$ in the other pair. There are sixteen choices of signs of the four $e_i$, including an arbitrary sign choice when $e_i=0$. Independent swaps of labels within each pair preserve the two means and split the sixteen patterns into five orbits. The checker enumerates these orbits and uses one set of priors per representative, testing the two endpoints of each signed interval for its corresponding row. Thus the actual magnitudes can vary independently between zero and twice their respective means.

For each vertex of either polytope the attached exact checker gives a distribution on finite validation words, each using the same six training dates. Each early date $e$ trains owners $0,2$, each late date $f$ trains owners $1,3$, and $x$ trains all four. Every word in a given certificate has the same training pattern, $exexff$ or $fxfxee$, and four validation labels per owner on separate dates. The checker tests discrepancy at every cut. Every tested or assigned first-exit coalition is therefore a singleton. For a given recipient row and deadline $t$, let $H_t(\pi,z,d)$ be its prescribed-minus-offered score for the word distribution $\pi$, vertex parameter $z$ and its signed crossed imbalance $d$. It is linear in $\pi$, affine in $z$ for fixed $d$, and affine in $d$ for fixed $z$. If the actual parameter has barycentric weights $\lambda_a$, choose the order prior $\sum_a\lambda_a\pi_a$. Its score equals
$$
 \sum_{a,b}\lambda_a\lambda_b\,
 \frac{H_t(\pi_a,z_b,d)+H_t(\pi_b,z_a,d)}2 .
$$
The checker proves all displayed coefficients nonnegative for both allowed endpoints of $d$, every row and every test. Since the observation is identical throughout each prior these are the full conditional obedience inequalities. This proves both rank-four assertions. The checker verifies the signs of 6,048 rational coefficients, including 510 exact equalities in the twelve-vertex construction. ## A bound at every rank from one crossed entry

The normalized crossed entries satisfy a single-coordinate bound on every matched rank failure. Set $q=(N-2)^2$ for $N\ge4$. If some $x_{ij}\ge (q-1)^3+(q-1)$, the all-four-finite test passes at rank $N$ for every completion. To prove this let $M$ be the largest crossed value at $(i,j)$, let $l=m(j),b=m(i)$. A failure would have $R_k<q$ and $U_k>1/(q-1)$ for every $k$. The two-cycle through the edge $b\to j$ gives $x_{lb}<1/M$. If $x_{il}\ge M/q$, another two-cycle gives $x_{jb}<q/M$, contradicting $U_b>1/(q-1)$ since the stated threshold exceeds $q^2-1$. Otherwise $R_i<q$ forces $x_{li}>1/(q-1)$ by its formula. Now $x_{lb}/x_{li}<(q-1)/M<1/q$ and $R_l<q$ likewise forces $x_{bl}>1/(q-1)$. Finally $x_{jb}>1/(q-1)-1/M$ from $U_b$. The four-cycle $b\to j\to i\to l\to b$ has product strictly larger than $M/(q-1)^3-1/(q-1)^2$, at least one at the threshold. This contradicts $\rho(P)<1$. At $N=4$ a single normalized crossed comparison of magnitude at least 30 suffices.

## Every symmetric positive matching through rank six

Suppose all mate entries equal $a>0$ and all crossed entries equal $-b<0$, allowing additional independent positive row scales. Set $r=a/b$. Here $\rho(P)=2/r$, so only $r>2$ can have a positive absorbing gap. The triple ratio above is $R_k=1+r/2$ at every pivot. Hence, for any $N\ge3$, the all-finite test already passes when $r\ge2((N-2)^2-1)$. This leaves $[2,16]$ at rank five and $[2,30]$ at rank six. For completeness, its graph state consists of the number of training dates processed and the four validation counts $c_i$ so far. An edge advances one validation count or executes the next scheduled training date at fixed $c$. Both ends must respect the unit discrepancy. A path reaching final counts $N$ therefore gives one legal order. A numerical linear program suggests a mixture of such paths. From the proposed flow the program extracts complete paths and rounds their masses to nonnegative integer weights with positive sum. Every path is then checked for interlacing and scored again with integers. At a new validation label of donor $j$, the increment in recipient $i$'s running passive offer for $i\ne j$ is
$$
 G_{ij}\prod_{k\ne i,j}(N-c_k)/N^3 .
$$
Its contribution to the prescription is this amount times $(N-c_i)/N$. The running offer at each training date is the payoff of stopping there, since no rival validation label is tied there and the own singleton pays zero. The final offer gives both Never and Late. The program multiplies these formulas by $16N^4$ for the listed rational endpoints. Only the integer path weights and rescored complete paths enter the final inequality checks.
