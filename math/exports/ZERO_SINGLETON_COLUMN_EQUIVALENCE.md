# A zero singleton column and the exact translated payoff and cap sets

Let $I$ have four players. A native reward table $Z$ has $Z_i(\{i\})=0$ in every row and zero payoff on live and perpetual play. For $c\in\mathbb R^4$ let $r^c(S)=Z(S)-c$ on every nonempty first-exit coalition $S$, with zero payoff on perpetual play. Write $K(r)$ for the Euclidean closure of the pairs $(U,B)$ of prescribed terminal payoffs and unrestricted unilateral response caps on the actual independent private stopping laws for $r$. Let $K_p(Z)$ require that player $p$'s clock be finite almost surely. Let $K_{\mathrm{abs}}(Z)$ require almost-sure absorption, and $K_{\mathrm{fin}}(Z)$ require four individually finite clocks. Put $A_Z=\min_{K_{\mathrm{abs}}(Z)}\sum_i(B_i-U_i)$. All minima of the closed bounded sets exist. For a pair of vectors, addition of $(c,c)$ means adding $c$ to both payoffs and caps.

Write $F_Z(c)$ for the unrestricted infimum of total terminal regret in the signed game $r^c$, and $A_{Z,p}$ for the native infimum requiring player $p$ to be individually finite. For **every** native table and **every prescribed** player $p$, take $g=Z(\{p\})$. Then
$$
 K(r^g)+(g,g)=K_p(Z).
$$
Moreover, for every $Z$ and every $p$,
$$
 A_{Z,p}=A_Z=F_Z(G_{\cdot p})=\max_{c\in\mathbb R^4} F_Z(c),
 \qquad G_{ij}=Z_i(\{j\}).
$$
Each of the four singleton columns attains the maximum, including when it is positive. Thus a prescribed player's clock can be required to stop almost surely on every native table without changing the minimum total regret. These are equalities of infima. At a fixed positive accuracy the prescribed clock, its distribution and the other strategies can depend on that accuracy.

In particular, proving uniform equilibrium for all signed tables with one prescribed singleton column identically zero is equivalent to the unrestricted four-player conjecture. If the conjecture fails, for one and the same native kernel there are four signed counterexamples $r^{Z(\{p\})}$, one with each possible singleton column identically zero. The column refers to the payoffs of **all four recipients** when $p$ alone quits. Earlier own-singleton normalization fixed four diagonal entries, one from each column.

**Proof of the identity.** Add $g$ to every outcome of $r^g$, including perpetual play. The resulting terminal problem has rewards $Z$ on finite exits and default payoff $g$ when no player stops. This adds $g$ to both $U$ and $B$. On every $p$-finite profile the new prescription agrees with the native one. Replacing any player other than $p$ still gives certain absorption. When $p$ deviates, the only potentially different payoff on perpetual play is $g_p=0$. This proves one inclusion.

Conversely start from any profile in the default-$g$ problem. Move all four marginal finite masses beyond a cutoff $T$ to Never. The sum of the moved masses tends to zero. Coupling each clock to its original draw bounds the change in each prescription and every pure reply uniformly by twice the payoff bound times that sum. Therefore the caps also converge. In the truncated profile replace $p$'s remaining Never mass by an independent uniform draw on $L$ consecutive fresh dates after $T+1$. Leave $T+1$ vacant. Native prescribed payoffs now equal the truncated default-$g$ payoffs exactly. The entire response menu of $p$ has the same monetary values because its rivals' clocks are unchanged and its default is zero.

For $i\ne p$ the offers on dates through $T$ agree. On survival of all rivals through the cutoff, the two previous late options paid $0$ (a solo finite quit) and $g_i$ (Never). In the new calendar the vacant date still pays $0$ and Never pays $g_i$. A date $t$ in or after the new block pays conditionally
$$
 \Pr(V<t)g_i+\Pr(V=t)Z_i(\{i,p\}),\qquad V\text{ uniform on the block}.
$$
Every other part of the offer is the same passive payment from earlier rival exits. The conditional amount is at most $\max(0,g_i)+|Z_i(\{i,p\})|/L$. The best old offer remains accessible. Thus each new native cap lies between its old default-$g$ value and that value plus $|Z_i(\{i,p\})|/L$. All unilateral behavioral replacements are mixtures of deadlines and literal Never because before absorption there is only one surviving history per date. Let $L$ and then $T$ increase. This establishes the reverse inclusion on the exact closed sets. A finite-support construction suffices at each accuracy.

**Consequences on the hard kernels.** Write $G_{ij}=Z_i(\{j\})$. If each row has some weak blocker $j\ne i$ with $G_{ij}\le0$, the existing four-clock completion theorem gives $K_{\mathrm{fin}}(Z)=K_{\mathrm{abs}}(Z)$. Its proof on an absorbing profile keeps a finite owner and uses a diffuse tail of its weak blocker after a truncation. Hence every intermediate set $K_p(Z)$ equals the same carrier. This old completion result concerns one reward table. The equality above identifies its common set with the translated unrestricted carrier of each of four different signed games, including all positive regret minima and all attainable closed payoff/cap pairs. [Four-clock completion, Sections F6–F8](FOUR_FINITE_CLOCKS_AT_ORIGINAL_MINIMA.md)

To prove the scalar identities for arbitrary $Z$, first suppose every row has a weak blocker. The four-clock theorem and the singleton identity give $A_{Z,p}=A_Z=F_Z(G_{\cdot p})$ for each player. For arbitrary $c$, all four individual clocks finite implies absorption even after each unilateral replacement. On those profiles a row translation adds the same constant to the prescription and cap. Hence $F_Z(c)\le A_Z$, proving maximality.

If a row lacks a weak blocker, **every** translated game $r^c$ has a uniform-equilibrium payoff. Indeed, the checked four-player normal-core theorem requires that a game lacking such a payoff have all players in its normal core, and each such player has a distinct weak blocker in its own row. The centered singleton matrix for $r^c$ is exactly $G$, since subtracting its own singleton cancels the row translation. The declarations are `normalCore_eq_univ_of_fourPlayer_not_exists_uniformEquilibriumPayoff` in [AmbientCarrierElimination.lean](../../UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Classification/LCP/ThreeCore/AmbientCarrierElimination.lean), `exists_core_blocker_of_mem_normalCore` in [NormalCore.lean](../../UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Classification/LCP/NormalCore.lean), and `normalizedSoloMatrix_eq_soloReward_sub` in [PreemptionGateDictionary.lean](../../UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Classification/PreemptionGateDictionary.lean). The semantic equivalence `quittingTerminalDebtSumInf_pos_iff_not_exists_uniformEquilibriumPayoff` in [TerminalDebtSumInf.lean](../../UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Terminal/TerminalDebtSumInf.lean) gives $F_Z(c)=0$ for every $c$. In particular every $A_{Z,p}$ vanishes by the singleton identity, and $A_Z=0$ by inclusion. This argument for the scalar equality does not identify the four closed sets $K_p(Z)$ on tables missing a blocker.

For the scalar infimum, even **one** weak blocker anywhere suffices to require all four clocks finite. Choose its recipient $p$. The single-owner completion in Sections F6–F8 of the cited proof needs only that recipient's blocker to complete a $p$-finite profile. It appends the blocker as a second, diffuse finite clock and then places the remaining clocks later. Thus $K_p(Z)=K_{\mathrm{fin}}(Z)$ in this case, and the pointwise scalar identity just proved gives the conclusion. With no blocker it can fail. For example, let $Z_i(S)=1$ when $i$ is absent from $S$ and zero when present. A sole diffuse quitter gives $A_Z=0$. On any all-four-finite profile each player gets one by choosing Never, so total regret equals the expected number of first quitters and has infimum one, attained by distinct deterministic dates. This is the native version of the blocker boundary in [the earlier four-clock theorem](FOUR_FINITE_CLOCKS_AT_ORIGINAL_MINIMA.md).

In particular, start with **any original signed counterexample** $r$, and let $Z_i(S)=r_i(S)-r_i(\{i\})$ on nonempty exits. The maximality inequality on this very kernel implies $0<\inf D_r\le A_Z$. Replacing $r$ by any of its four zero-column translations preserves a positive gap, with exact value $A_Z$. Conversely a prescribed-face positive gap on $Z$ forces $A_Z>0$ on this same kernel and yields all four signed counterexamples. The terminal criterion gives a fixed uniform payoff target when the infimum is zero. The earlier [single-pivot normalization](../../UniformEquilibrium/Diagnostics/Quitting/FinFourSinglePivotNormalization.lean) and [native repair](GLOBAL_LEAST_NEVER_SOURCE_REDUCTION.md) provide a different original counterexample family by changing the own levels.

For the interlaced order formulation this means one may fix the same player $p$ in advance, at every level for all native tables. A strictly failing level on this prescribed face would already settle the original conjecture negatively, even without checking the other three faces. In the positive direction an all-level argument on the fixed face yields absorbing approximations for each table and hence the unrestricted conjecture. Deleting the prescribed player can change the game. For example, pay $p$ zero on every exit containing $p$ and $-1$ on every other exit. Pay each other player one on every exit omitting $p$ and zero on every exit containing $p$. These are signed rewards with an identically zero singleton column for $p$. With $p$ forced to Never, write $m$ for the probability none of the other three stops. Their combined gain from forcing finite quits is $3m$ and $p$ can gain $1-m$ by quitting on the first date. Yet $p$ quitting surely on the first date with everyone else waiting is an exact equilibrium. ## All exact default vectors and further equalities of regret infima

Write c⁺_i=max(0,c_i). For every native table Z,
```
K(rᶜ)+(c,c) = K_fin(Z)   if and only if   (c,c⁺) ∈ K_fin(Z).
```
This characterizes equality for the prescribed payoff and the full response cap together. On an individually finite four-clock profile every player faces a finite rival, including after any replacement, so K_fin(Z) always lies in the translated carrier. AllNever in the default-c problem gives exactly (c,c⁺), proving necessity. Conversely, truncate any profile in that problem to K dates by sending its remote finite mass to Never. On each owner's surviving branch attach its own independent member of a four-finite native tail approximating (c,c⁺), leaving a vacant date K+1 between the two parts. When all four survive the prefix, its native prescribed reward approaches c. Against a fixed owner, past rival exits make the same passive contribution to every post-cut test. Conditional on all rivals surviving, the supremum of post-cut and vacant-date offers tends to c⁺_i, exactly the supremum of the old finite-solo and Never alternatives. The earlier offers agree. This also preserves cap lower bounds, because each tail's full cap is the supremum of actual dated replies and Never. Conditional branches with zero probability carry no contribution. Independent prefix/tail concatenation and boundedness prove equality after taking the cutoff and approximation limits.

There is a weaker sufficient condition for equality of the scalar infima. Suppose (w,b)∈K_fin(Z), w≥c and b≤c⁺ coordinatewise. The same construction weakly increases each limiting prescription and weakly decreases each limiting cap relative to the default-c profile. Since every resulting native calendar absorbs, the unrestricted regret infimum for rᶜ is at least A_Z. The reverse inequality holds whenever K_fin(Z)=K_abs(Z), in particular under the preceding full-row weak-blocker assumption. Thus the infimum equals A_Z there. On kernels missing a weak blocker, both infima vanish for every default by the normal-core argument. This comparison allows w_i>c_i and does not assert equality of the two payoff-and-cap sets.

A direct test uses any coalition $S$ with at least two members. Set $w=Z(S)$ and assume $Z_i(S\mathbin{\triangle}\{i\})\le w_i^+$ for each $i$, where the triangle toggles membership of $i$. Have the members of $S$ stop surely on date two and all others strictly afterward, with the first date vacant. The first-exit payoff is $w$, and the full cap is $\max(0,w_i,Z_i(S\mathbin{\triangle}\{i\}))$. This gives exact default $w$ and the scalar comparison for its downward shifts.

**Simultaneous diffuse hazards.** Choose a simplex vector z, put D={i:z_i>0}, and w=Gz. Suppose w_i≤0 for i∈D. If |D|≥2, the pair (w,w⁺) belongs to K_fin(Z) without any blocker assumption. Let the members of D use constant hazards h z_i and send h down to zero. Prescriptions tend to w since simultaneous exits have conditional probability O(h) given a rival or joint exit. Every player faces a positive rival hazard. Its complete cap is the maximum of its forced immediate offer and its native payoff on waiting forever: every finite deadline interpolates between them under geometric survival. The former tends to zero, the latter to w_i/(1−z_i). This gives w_i⁺ for each owner, since w_i≤0 when z_i>0. Any outside clock left at Never can be made individually finite on a sufficiently late date without affecting the limit: every response faces at least one of the two scheduled finite rivals, including after the tested clock is removed. For |D|=1 the singleton identity above gives (w,w⁺) under the weak blocker for its owner.

Hence the default w gives exact carrier equality. Under the full-row weak-blocker assumption every c with c_i=w_i where w_i>0 and c_i≤w_i elsewhere gives unrestricted regret infimum precisely A_Z. The reductions in different nonpositive coordinates can be arbitrarily large and independent. On the canonical matched singleton matrix, with mate rewards 3 and crossed rewards −1, take equal z on the crossed owners 0,1 and zero elsewhere. This gives w=(−1/2,−1/2,1,1). For example c=(−2,−3,1,1) is outside the four downward regions obtained from single columns, since no positive singleton entry there equals 1. The corresponding original game has both positive and negative own levels. This example distinguishes the sets of shifts without assuming a counterexample.

**Two ordered solo phases.** Let 0<θ<1 and p≠q. First let p alone stop with total probability θ uniformly over a long fresh interval; if it does not stop there it waits. In a disjoint later interval let q alone stop uniformly with total probability one. Write w=θG_{·p}+(1−θ)G_{·q}. The complete cap limits are
```
b_p=max(0,G_pq),    b_q=max(0,θG_qp),
b_i=max(0,θG_ip,w_i)   for i outside {p,q}.
```
Indeed an owner's offer between the intervals collects the first interval's passive contribution; within either diffuse interval it interpolates between the endpoint offers up to a vanishing collision error. Row p sees only the second interval and row q only the first. A weak blocker for q permits four-finite completion of this q-finite profile as above. It follows that (w,w⁺) is attainable, and the same downward gap conclusion applies, when G_pq≤0 and G_ip>0 implies G_iq≥0 for each outside i. This ordered construction also permits G_qp>0 and w_q>0, even though q stops in its last interval.

## Implementable defaults need not form a convex set

For a native table $Z$, let $S(Z)$ be the vectors $c$ for which $(c,c^+)$ belongs to the all-four-finite payoff/cap carrier, with positive part taken coordinatewise. Start with the reference table $Z^0_0(C)=\mathbf1_{C=\{1\}}$, $Z^0_1(C)=\mathbf1_{C=\{0\}}$ and both other rows zero. For a finite-clock profile put $D=\min(T_2,T_3)$ and $E_i=\{T_i<D\}$ for $i=0,1$. Waiting forever weakly dominates each prescribed clock in these two rows. On $E_0\cap E_1$ replacing the earlier owner by Never earns an additional unit, and on a tie both owners gain a unit. Consequently
$$
 d_0+d_1\ \ge\Pr(E_0\cap E_1)\ \ge\Pr(E_0)\Pr(E_1)\ \ge U_0U_1.
$$
The middle inequality follows by conditioning on $D$: the two conditional probabilities are nondecreasing functions of the same random threshold. On the reference table all payoffs are nonnegative. Thus $S(Z^0)$ consists precisely of the two coordinate segments from zero to $(1,0,0,0)$ and $(0,1,0,0)$. To realize any point of one segment, let one donor stop before the first dummy with the desired probability and otherwise later, while the other donor always stops after that dummy. All four individual clocks can have finite support.

The obstruction persists under strict matrix conditions appearing at the counterexample frontier. For concreteness let $0<\varepsilon<\delta<1/100$ and take the two ordered mate entries $G_{01}=G_{10}=1$, the other two $G_{23}=G_{32}=\delta$, and all eight crossed entries $-\varepsilon$. Require $|Z_i(C)|<1/100$ for every nonsingleton $C$. The singleton matrix is $R_0$ and has degree one, positive determinant and strictly positive inverse. Indeed the two mate-difference directions have eigenvalues $-1,-\delta$; on vectors constant on each mate pair the matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-2\varepsilon\\-2\varepsilon&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Thus $\det G=\delta(\delta-4\varepsilon^2)>0$. Inverting this two-by-two block and the two difference directions gives positive entries everywhere: in each diagonal block its constant-mode contribution strictly exceeds the magnitude of the off-setting difference-mode contribution. All two- and three-element principal minors are nonzero (a triple determinant is $2\varepsilon^2$ times its mate entry), and every singleton column has a negative outsider. This proves $R_0$. At complementarity offset $-\mathbf1$, $Gx\ge\mathbf1$ forces every mate active, giving the unique transverse solution $G^{-1}\mathbf1$ and degree $+1$.

Both $c^0=G_{\cdot0}$ and $c^1=G_{\cdot1}$ lie in $S(Z)$ by singleton translation and weak blockers in all rows. Their midpoint $m=(1/2,1/2,-\varepsilon,-\varepsilon)$ cannot lie there. Indeed $\|Z-Z^0\|_\infty\le1/100$, so on the same clocks every payoff and full cap differs by at most $1/100$. A sequence realizing $(m,m^+)$ would yield $\liminf U_0^0 U_1^0\ge .49^2$ but $\limsup(d_0^0+d_1^0)\le .04$ on the reference table, violating the displayed product bound. The margins and strict blocker/matrix conditions persist in a neighborhood of any fixed such table. This can occur on proved equilibrium games even with all higher rewards strictly negative. For example set each grand payoff to $-1/400$ and each pair or triple payoff to $-1/200$. All four quitting on the first date is an exact equilibrium for every signed row translation, since waiting would receive the strictly smaller complementary triple payoff. Thus midpoint failure concerns this default-carrier construction itself.
