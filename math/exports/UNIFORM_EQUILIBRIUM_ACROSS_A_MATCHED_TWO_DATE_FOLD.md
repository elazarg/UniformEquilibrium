For each row take s_i=r_i({i}) and ℓ_i>0, and put Z_i(S)=(r_i(S)−s_i)/ℓ_i on nonempty coalitions. Fix the normalized canonical matched-pair table, with mate singleton 3, crossed singleton −1, own singleton 0, matched participant M=8/3, crossed participant caps −1+1/10 and −1−1/10 in cyclic order, and, for each player i, passive reward T_i=Z_i(O(i)) at its opposite matched pair O(i) with joining triple payoff Z_i({i}∪O(i))≤T_i. ## Closing one periodic block

Take δ>0 sufficiently small. The strict finite bounds above persist by compactness and continuity. In particular all resets at time 1520 lie strictly in the small strip with positive centers, and the delayed .57 and .80 cones remain ordered from their comparison at 1502 and 1500.

Let B be the closed rectangle of (r,s) above. Use the center coordinate u, stable vector z and section graph u=U(z) from the finite estimates. Extend U to all stable values by componentwise clipping to this radius and denote the extension by Ū. Use the even phase for this graph. Write H(u,z)=u−Ū(z), which is positive on the upcoming side.

Set a=u_c−3R. From the common even entrance time onward use the auxiliary map obtained by replacing the center of each full transfer (û,ẑ) by max(a,û) and retaining ẑ. The map is continuous and preserves the strip up to its top entrance bound. In fact the original full transfer preserves the strip if it does not hit the floor. If û<a then the preceding center u is negative, with |u|≤|a|. Its new stable norm is at most 3.9(u²+δ)<4(a²+δ), by the strict stable estimate and its persistence at the perturbed fold. The center decreases before clipping. Once H≤0 at an even phase, its center is at most u_c+R. If it is in [u_c−R,u_c+R], the next unclipped full step decreases the center by more than 2R because .5(u_c+R)²>2R. If it is below u_c−R it stays below u_c−R. Thus H is strictly negative at every later even phase. If an even terminal state has H=0, all its transfers were genuine and its stable vector lies within the section cube. The same construction applies with the two transported charts on the perturbed raw tables.

For each (r,s)∈B, take the reset with y=.80 and let n(r,s) be its first even full-date count with H≤0. These integers have a uniform upper bound by the positive strip drift for fixed δ>0. Take their maximum n and a pair (r₀,s₀) attaining it. At n every .80 reset has H≤0. At n−2 the .80 reset from (r₀,s₀) is still upcoming. This reset is upcoming through count n−2 in the following coordinate sense: its even states have H>0 and u>u_c−R, and if an intervening odd center were at or below u_c−R the next even state could not be upcoming, by monotone descent.

Every .57 reset has H>0 at count n. Indeed, the certified difference from .57 at 1502 to .80 at 1500 lies in the positive cone even when their (r,s) inputs differ: each term was separately enclosed over the entire B. Propagate this ordering, comparing the .57 reset at k to the selected .80 reset at k−2. Both genuinely follow the full transfers until the latter reaches n−2. More explicitly, a step from ordered points in the convex chart gives strictly ordered images before clipping. The smaller center, on the selected .80 path, exceeds u_c−R>a up to n−2. Hence its larger .57 counterpart has no clipping either, proving the order at n. If the .57 state there were on the passed side, its center and the .80 center at n−2 would both belong to [u_c−R,u_c+R]. Both stable vectors then lie in the section cube. The earlier sign DF<0 on the positive cone contradicts this ordering of sides, since F is positive on the passed side and negative on the upcoming side.

For every (r,s,y)∈B×[.57,.80] take its clipped orbit to n and call the result (u,z). Let z₀ be z clipped to the section cube. Apply the 1500-date exit map to (U(z₀),z₀) and compute the two next solo reset values (r′,s′). They lie in B for every input, and depend continuously on it. The continuous self-map
    (r,s,y) ↦ (r′,s′, clip_[.57,.80](y+H(u,z)))
has a fixed point by Brouwer. At its lower y face H>0 and at its upper y face H≤0, so every fixed point satisfies H=0. Hence its exit genuinely starts at (u,z) and every full step was genuine. The two solo dates at the output recover exactly the same (r,s), so the physical block closes and repeats with period n+1500+2. The full and solo comparisons above hold at every phase. Each player faces rival absorption with a geometric bound, even after any unilateral replacement. Iterating the one-step inequalities shows terminal Nash against complete private replacements, including Never. ## The period on the diagonal

For the diagonal parameter δ=t−τ let e be the center tangent and ℓ the left unit covector normalized by ℓe=1. The constants A=−ℓ∂_t g, B=−ℓD²g[e,e]/2 are positive; in the displayed chart they are approximately .75944718 and .94652104. Throughout the strip put v=ℓ(V−V*). Taylor expansion using ℓDg=ℓ and |z|=O(u²+δ) gives
    v′−v = −Aδ−Bv²+O(|v|³+δ|v|+δ²).
The initial v is uniformly positive after the common finite entrance, while its section values are uniformly negative. For fixed small ρ>0 the time outside |v|≤ρ is uniformly bounded as δ decreases to zero by the strict strip drift. On each step inside that band the integral of 1/(Aδ+Bv²) from v′ to v equals 1+O(ρ+δ), uniformly: the relative change of the denominator over a step is O(ρ+δ), as follows from |v′−v|=O(δ+v²). The integral across the whole band, multiplied by √δ, tends to π/√(AB); at its two ends one partial step contributes O(1). Let ρ decrease to zero. The resulting periods satisfy
    lim_{δ↓0} √δ · (n+1500+2) = π/√(AB) ≈ 3.70540727 .
This counts dates in the selected calendar. The single solo date for player 0 per block makes its length the least period of this particular hazard profile.

## Independent variation of the four passive rewards

For completeness, take the two-date physical full-joint map F as a composition of the two alternating one-date maps. At the critical table it is g², and has a simple unit eigenvalue with three strictly stable others. Nonzero parameter and quadratic center projections for g remain nonzero for g² (both double). Finite-dimensional Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction for the fixed-point equation, followed by the ordinary implicit function theorem on the scalar residual and its u derivative, gives a smooth fold surface through the table. Parametrize the three tangential parameters by θ, and take δ along the direction that increases the two high entries together, measured from that surface. On δ<0 small there are proper two-date roots with strict quiet guards, and on the surface there is the fold root itself.

On each nearby fold table recenter the two alternating coordinate charts at its two periodic phase states. Use the smooth first chart center eigenvector, and as the other first column take its image under the intervening one-date derivative. Since the two-date eigenvalue is exactly one, each of the two one-date derivatives takes its preceding center column exactly to the next one. Keep the three complementary columns close to their displayed base values. Thus at δ=0 each alternating map has first derivative along its center coordinate exactly (1,0); there is no residual linear forcing in the three stable components. For small θ the strict derivative bounds yielding the strip, cone and opposite-side drift persist with slightly weaker constants, by continuity of the charts, first and second derivatives and parameter derivative. The strict finite entrance/exit, transversality, relative phase and reset-inclusion inequalities persist as well, by the physical two-phase maps' smooth dependence on θ and δ. Times compared always have the same parity, and the exit transfer has even length. The preceding backward-return argument therefore covers all sufficiently small θ and δ>0, proving the neighborhood assertion.

## Relation to existing coverage

For a separating family, take T=(−1,−1,τ+ε,τ+ε) with ε>0 small enough for this theorem and τ+ε<−3/10, and attain the predecessor crossed caps. Both matched pairs are split.
