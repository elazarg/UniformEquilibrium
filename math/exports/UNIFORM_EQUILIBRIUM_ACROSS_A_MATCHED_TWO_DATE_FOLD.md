# A native return across the matched two-date saddle-node

For each row take s_i=r_i({i}) and ℓ_i>0, and put Z_i(S)=(r_i(S)−s_i)/ℓ_i on nonempty coalitions. Fix the normalized canonical matched-pair table, with mate singleton 3, crossed singleton −1, own singleton 0, matched participant M=8/3, crossed participant caps −1+1/10 and −1−1/10 in cyclic order, and, for each player i, passive reward T_i=Z_i(O(i)) at its opposite matched pair O(i) with joining triple payoff Z_i({i}∪O(i))≤T_i. Other nonsingleton entries are arbitrary. Let τ≈−0.333198490 be the two-date fold value defined below. There exists an open neighborhood of (−1,−1,τ,τ) in all four independent passive rewards T on which every such game admits a private terminal equilibrium and a fixed original uniform-equilibrium payoff. The neighborhood's radius is existential. The proof allows arbitrarily many consecutive joint dates and does not impose a periodicity condition on the resulting infinite strategy.

## The local full-joint operation

Set c=1/3. At a fully mixed matched date, indifference determines its mate's hazard p=f(Y)=Y/(Y−c) from the scheduled player's child value Y<0. For the line T=(−1,−1,t,t), interchanging the groups after each backward step allows the same operation g. ## Why the sections give an infinite private strategy

First hold the other parameters fixed on the displayed line and take δ>0 sufficiently small. All finite comparisons in 1–3 persist by compactness and continuity, including the graph in 2, which keeps the same inequalities and the same strictly interior reset images. Use the entrances at any common even time at least 1520. The finite difference between the delayed y=.57 path and the y=.80 path stays in K: the comparison began at dates 1502 and 1500 inside the small box and persists there under each full step. On the baseline the strip capture at 75 and positivity ensure that all the intermediate full guards up to 1520 persist at nearby parameters, and the states remain strictly inside the small strip at that finite time. Local cone and drift now apply until the section is reached.

Given any eligible reset state W, vary y continuously in [.57,.80]. Regard a small-strip point as having passed the section if u<u_c−R, or if it lies inside the section box with u≤U(z). Values u>u_c+R are on the upcoming side. A continuous sign test everywhere is u−U(π_Z z), where π_Z clips each stable coordinate to [−Z,Z]. Near a zero the strip lies inside that cube, so clipping has no effect there. At the u levels where the graph is used the invariant strip gives |z|<Z by taking δ sufficiently small. While the family is on the upcoming side it stays inside the chart. The drift is at least .55δ, so there is a first even full-step count m for which some y reaches or passes the section. The intermediate odd step can only decrease u, and its overshoot is less than 1.2(u²+δ). One extra date just below u_c stays in the small chart. Thus the states used through count m are defined and feasible, and depend continuously on y. The y=.57 state at count m is still on the upcoming side. Otherwise it would be ahead of the section while the y=.80 state at m−2 was still behind it. But their difference (.57 at m minus .80 at m−2) belongs to K. If the two points were on the indicated sides in this order, both u coordinates would necessarily be in [u_c−R,u_c+R]; their connecting segment stays within the z cube, contradicting the strict sign of DF on K. Therefore some y at time m lands exactly on F=0 by continuity. Now take the 1500 further full steps of 2. By 3 their output is another eligible W. All scheduled and quiet comparisons throughout the resulting backward block hold.

The return is a correspondence on eligible states; it need not select one common m or a continuous value of y. Start with any W from the nonempty returning section and prepend successive valid blocks. For each sufficiently large finite number of blocks this gives a bounded string of phase values and four independent hazards. Select a diagonal subsequence of the chronological strings (from their new first dates) to obtain an infinite calendar and bounded phase values. Both groups alternate and all positive hazards are uniformly bounded away from zero and one for the fixed game: finite entrances and exits are compact, the central strip is compactly within the full-joint region, and the two solo rates lie in the stated compact proper intervals. Every player faces rival absorption with a uniform geometric bound even after any replacement. The limiting one-step inequalities are closed, and its bounded phase values are the actual unique terminal values by the geometric tail. This proves terminal Nash against complete private replacements including Never, and the fixed uniform target in the raw original game with arbitrary signed singleton levels.

## Independent variation of the four passive rewards

For completeness, take the two-date physical full-joint map F as a composition of the two alternating one-date maps. At the critical table it is g², and has a simple unit eigenvalue with three strictly stable others. Nonzero parameter and quadratic center projections for g remain nonzero for g² (both double). Finite-dimensional Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction for the fixed-point equation, followed by the ordinary implicit function theorem on the scalar residual and its u derivative, gives a smooth fold surface through the table. Parametrize the three tangential parameters by θ, and take δ along the direction that increases the two high entries together, measured from that surface. On δ<0 small there are proper two-date roots with strict quiet guards, and on the surface there is the fold root itself.

On each nearby fold table recenter the two alternating coordinate charts at its two periodic phase states. Use the smooth first chart center eigenvector, and as the other first column take its image under the intervening one-date derivative. Since the two-date eigenvalue is exactly one, each of the two one-date derivatives takes its preceding center column exactly to the next one. Keep the three complementary columns close to their displayed base values. Thus at δ=0 each alternating map has first derivative along its center coordinate exactly (1,0); there is no residual linear forcing in the three stable components. For small θ the strict derivative bounds yielding the strip, cone and opposite-side drift persist with slightly weaker constants, by continuity of the charts, first and second derivatives and parameter derivative. The strict finite entrance/exit, transversality, relative phase and reset-inclusion inequalities persist as well, by the physical two-phase maps' smooth dependence on θ and δ. Times compared always have the same parity, and the exit transfer has even length. The preceding backward-return argument therefore covers all sufficiently small θ and δ>0, proving the neighborhood assertion.

## Relation to existing coverage

For a separating family, take T=(−1,−1,τ+ε,τ+ε) with ε>0 small enough for this theorem and τ+ε<−3/10, and attain the predecessor crossed caps. Both matched pairs are split. Each contains a reward below −1/2 and its other reward is below 1/3. Thus neither the earlier two-observer interval, the middle cube nor the cone with an observer ≥1/3 in each pair covers these tables. Each pair's mean exceeds −2/3 because τ>−1/3, putting them outside the pair-mean construction as well. They also lie below the explicit long-period segment [−3/10,−1/10]. The sharp fold criterion rules out every nonzero proper two-date matched schedule here, including asymmetric rates and three-player support. A sure matched mover is defeated by its predecessor at the stated cap equalities. The present return supplies exact native play throughout a full neighborhood containing these tables. It does not establish a minimum period for other calendars.
