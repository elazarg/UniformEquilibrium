# Finite order tests near a strictly positive singleton kernel

Write $G_{ij}=r_i(\{j\})-r_i(\{i\})$. Fix a hollow matrix $G_0$ with $G_0z=0$ for a positive probability vector $z$. Suppose $G_0$ has rank three, a left kernel generator with no zero coordinate, and a negative off-diagonal entry. Use the maximum entry norm. At every rank $N$ the four-finite order test passes for every completion of the 44 higher rewards and every signed choice of own levels throughout the following balls, with constants independent of $N$.

* The radius is $c/N^2$ if no nonzero nonnegative diagonal matrix $D$ makes $DG_0$ skew-symmetric. In particular this holds at every strictly signed such matrix with a reciprocally negative pair.
* The radius is $c/N$ if some skew-symmetric matrix $A$ with $A\mathbf1=0$ satisfies $\sum_j A_{ij}(G_0)_{ij}<0$ in every row. All 21 remaining strict sign classes and all 95 remaining classes with ties contain centers for this linear-radius conclusion, using the positive-triple variant below.

Thus near a given center of the second kind, at distance $\delta>0$, a failing rank must exceed $c/\delta$. Both results concern exact feasibility at the specified finite level. Proving existence on a fixed nearby game requires unbounded levels. ## Hiding small changes of rates

Let $x$ be a probability vector near $z$, $d_i=1-x_i$, $W=Gx$, and $f(d,t)=\int_0^t e^{-du}\,du$. All rate denominators below are uniformly positive. In each construction a common deterministic set of training dates is chosen for the four owners. A random scenario specifies four independent clocks with continuous distributions. Independently for each owner take the $N$ validation quantiles at $(k+U_i)/N$, $0\le k<N$, with $U_i$ uniform on $(0,1)$. Their count through time $t$ rounds the scenario's true cumulative count $NF_i(t)$ either up or down. A uniformly selected validation label, averaged over its shift, has exactly the scenario clock's physical law. No validation clocks of different owners tie, and none ties a training label, almost surely. Continuous time here is a device for sampling a finite weak order; only these orders and their probabilities enter the test. Every trained deadline and the final Never/Late comparisons have only singleton scores. The training observation hides the scenario. Mixing orders in this test makes no assumption of public randomization in a quitting game.

First consider the hypothesis on $D$. The linear map from real weights $b_{ij}=b_{ji}$ on distinct pairs to $(\sum_j b_{ij}(G_0)_{ij})_i$ has a strictly positive vector in its image. Otherwise separation of a linear subspace from the open positive orthant yields $\lambda\ge0$, $\lambda\ne0$, with $\lambda_i(G_0)_{ij}+\lambda_j(G_0)_{ji}=0$ for every pair. This gives the forbidden $D$. Start at the positive weights $z_i z_j$, whose image is zero, and add a small multiple of a preimage of that strictly positive vector. Normalize to pair probabilities $p_{ij}$. In a fixed neighborhood of $G_0$ we then have $S_i=\sum_j p_{ij}G_{ij}\ge\gamma>0$. For a strictly signed matrix every nonzero nonnegative skew symmetrizer would have all diagonal entries positive, which is impossible on a reciprocal pair of the same sign.

Sample a pair $i,j$ with this law and a fair sign. Use independent exponential clocks with rate vector $X=x\pm\eta(e_i-e_j)$, where $\eta=e/N$. Take the deterministic training dates at the midquantiles of each nominal exponential law of rate $x_i$. Uniformly in time, changing a rate by $\eta$ changes a marginal cumulative probability by at most $C\eta$, by differentiating $1-e^{-xt}$. Choose $e>0$ so that $Ce<1/2$ and all sampled rates are positive. The training count differs from $NF_i(t)$ by less than one, including the left limits. Thus the validation grid interlaces with the training grid. The expected native prescription is $W_i$ since rates always sum to one. The offer at time $t$ conditional on $X$ is $(GX)_i f(1-X_i,t)$. Averaging over the pairs and signs gives the following exact prescription minus offer:
$$
 W_i[1-\mathbb E f(1-X_i,t)]
 + \frac{\eta S_i}{2}\,[f(d_i-\eta,t)-f(d_i+\eta,t)].
$$
The final bracket is at least $2\eta\int_0^t u e^{-d_i u}\,du$ by $\sinh v\ge v$ for $v\ge0$. At $t\ge1/2$, including infinity, the integral has a uniform positive lower bound and the coefficient of $W_i$ is bounded. Below $1/2$ that coefficient is positive and at most one. Each training date is at least $1/(2N)$, so the integral is at least a positive constant times $N^{-2}$ there. Consequently every tested gain is strictly negative when
$$
 -cN^{-4}\le W_i\le cN^{-2} \qquad\text{for all } i.
$$

## Oriented periodic changes of rates

Now assume the condition on $A$. In the hyperplane $\mathbf1^\perp$ choose a piecewise linear loop $\beta$ based at zero with $\int\beta_i\,d\beta_j$ a positive multiple of $A_{ij}$. Indeed a tangent skew matrix is a sum of $uv^T-vu^T$ on tangent vectors. Trace the parallelograms $0,u,u+v,v,0$ in succession and scale the entire path down. Parameterize it on $[0,1]$, scaling it far enough that its velocities $a=\beta'$ have magnitude below $\min_i z_i/4$ in each coordinate. Extend periodically. In a fixed neighborhood of $G_0$ one has $\sum_jG_{ij}\int\beta_i\,d\beta_j\le-\gamma$ in every row for a fixed $\gamma>0$.

Choose one fair sign $\sigma$ for the order prior. At physical time $s$ let the independent clocks have rates $x_i+\sigma a_i(s/\ell)$, where $\ell=e/N$. The sum of the rates is one. The survival of clock $i$ is $\exp(-x_is-\sigma\ell\beta_i(s/\ell))$. For training, round each nominal exponential midquantile **up** to the next multiple of $\ell$, retaining a midquantile already on a boundary. The nominal cumulative count differs from the rounded integer count by at most $1/2+N\ell\max_i x_i$. The actual marginal distribution differs from nominal by at most $C\ell$ in either scenario. A sufficiently small fixed $e$ gives interlacing as above. Every visible finite date completes a whole number of loops.

The mean prescription is again $W_i$, by integrating the instantaneous reward against total survival $e^{-s}$ and then averaging the sign. Against a time $t$ the mean offer is
$$
 \int_0^t e^{-d_i s}\big[W_i\cosh(\ell\beta_i(s/\ell))+
             (Ga(s/\ell))_i\sinh(\ell\beta_i(s/\ell))\big]ds .
$$
At a complete loop boundary, also at infinity, this equals $f(d_i,t)(W_iB_i+D_i)$ by geometric summation. Here $B_i,D_i$ are the averages on one loop weighted by $e^{-d_i s}$ and normalized by its integral. Uniformly $B_i=1+O(\ell^2)$ and
$$
 D_i=\ell\sum_jG_{ij}\int_0^1\beta_i\,d\beta_j+O(\ell^2)\le-\gamma\ell/2 .
$$
These follow directly by substituting $s=\ell u$ and expanding bounded functions on $[0,1]$. If $-cN^{-2}\le W_i\le cN^{-1}$ with $c$ sufficiently small, the bracket $W_iB_i+D_i$ is at most $-\gamma\ell/4$. Positive or zero prescriptions dominate this offer strictly. Negative prescriptions do so as well, since $f(d_i,t)$ is bounded below by a positive constant times $1/N$ at every visible date. This proves the stronger band.

## Passing from rates to matrices

If the native absorbing regret infimum vanishes, the nearby negative singleton entry gives four-finite approximations and hence passage at every level. Otherwise the strict inverse-column theorem gives a player $p$ with $G^{-1}e_p>0$ and $\det G>0$. Use this column divided by its sum for $x$. Every column of $\operatorname{adj}(G_0)$ is a nonzero multiple of $z$ by the two kernel hypotheses. Each of the four normalized adjugate columns is therefore smooth nearby and equals $z$ at $G_0$. Uniformly $x-z=O(\|G-G_0\|)$ and $0\le Gx=O(\|G-G_0\|)$ componentwise. The upper sides of the two bands give the asserted balls. With four finite clocks each tested deviation still faces a finite rival, so row shifts preserve the payoff comparisons. [Inverse-column condition](FIN4_STRICT_INVERSE_COLUMN_AND_ALL_END_MINIMUM_REDUCTION.md) · [Finite-clock completion](ZERO_SINGLETON_COLUMN_EQUIVALENCE.md)

The same two-sided matrix argument works if just one principal triple of $G_0$ has positive determinant, even when some coordinates of the left kernel vanish. Its omitted adjugate column is a positive multiple of $z$, since its diagonal entry is that positive determinant. Keep this index throughout the nearby positive-determinant branch. The positive triple already forces rank three for the matrix with the given positive kernel. Symmetric matrices have $\sum_{ij}A_{ij}G_{ij}=0$ for every skew $A$, making the pair-probability proof applicable at centers outside the loop test.

## Centers in every remaining sign class

The linear-radius theorem reaches **all 21 strict classes and all 95 classes with one, two or three ties** in the singleton sign census. Here the ball is in the full twelve-dimensional matrix space, also at a center with ties. The exact checker constructs a balanced matrix in every class with a positive principal triple and a suitable circulation. In a balanced row, distribute the same integer total between its positive and negative entries, keeping each indicated magnitude positive. Totals from $\{2,3,4,6\}$ suffice. Write $A_1,A_2,A_3$ for the basis of skew matrices with zero row sums obtained by freely choosing entries $01,02,12$. Form the four-by-three matrix $M_{ik}=\sum_j(A_k)_{ij}G_{ij}$. The checker accepts when the alternating three-row minors of $M$ have both signs. They then span the left kernel, so the image contains a strictly negative vector: a vector with strictly positive coordinates can be made orthogonal to a normal of mixed signs. The corresponding linear combination of the three basis matrices is the required $A$. Strictness also gives relatively open sets of such centers among balanced matrices on the same face. [Exact sign and center checker](../experiments/FIN4_SINGLETON_SIGN_CLASSES.py)

In every strict class the linear-radius construction reaches nearby matrices that pass the published determinant, degree and inverse-column tests and fail the full continuous singleton-path test. The checker requires nonzero deleted-triple determinants at the center and chooses $h$ with positive deleted determinant and positive determinants on all triples required by the degree test at $e_h$. Increase a positive entry of row $h$ by $\delta>0$. Then $G_\delta\mathbf1=\delta e_h$ and $\det G_\delta$ equals $\delta$ times that positive deleted determinant, because the cofactors in row $h$ of the balanced center are equal. For sufficiently small $\delta$ all selected triple signs persist. There is no nonzero homogeneous complementary vector, since every principal block of size two or larger is invertible and every singleton has a negative outsider. The sign graph admits no strict four-cycle. For each potential triangle the checker rules out its scale or one outsider comparison at the center. The scale is positive and cannot equal one because its triple is nonsingular, so these failures persist for small $\delta$. Whether these nearby matrices admit a uniform-equilibrium payoff for every completion of the higher rewards remains open.
