# A uniform-equilibrium payoff requiring every first-quitter coalition

## Statement

Four independent players have one live history at each positive date. The first nonempty set of quitters $T$ pays a finite vector $r(T)$ at absorption. Each preceding live stage, the decision stage and perpetual continuation pay zero. A unilateral behavioral replacement can use any private law of its first attempted quit along the live history, including Never. Write $e$ for the maximum gain in original expected terminal payoff over all such replacements.

Let $s_i$ be player $i$'s own singleton reward and use the zero-diagonal matrix
\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}0&-8&6&-2\\9&0&2&-10\\-7&15&0&-4\\14&-3&-4&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Start from native rewards $r_i(T)-s_i$ equal to $\sum_{j\in T}G_{ij}$ for a nonparticipant and its negative for a participant. Give 0 an additional $L$ on every $T\supseteq013$ and 1 an additional $K$ on every $T\supseteq123$, writing strings for sets. At 0123 alone add another $J_0$ to 0, $J_2$ to 2 and $N$ to 1. All five parameters are finite and nonnegative.

1. For every fixed $L,K$, all sufficiently large $J_2$, every $J_0\ge0$, and all sufficiently large $N$ depending on the preceding parameters, there is a fixed original uniform-equilibrium payoff $s+7x e_2$, $1/1000<x<16/7$. Here $s$ is any real vector and $e_2$ is the unit vector at player 2.
2. Take $s=(1,1,1,1)$. There is a finite $L_0$ such that for each $K\ge L\ge L_0$, all independently sufficiently large $J_0,J_2$ at that fixed $L,K$, and then all sufficiently large $N$, the following conclusions hold together. A uniform-equilibrium payoff exists. There is no Nash opening at the punishment continuation with a sure quitter, no exact terminal Nash equilibrium even among aperiodic profiles, and a positive regret floor at every fixed period bound. There are constants $c,\delta>0$ for each table such that every original profile with $e\le\delta$ has one date at which **each of the fifteen nonempty coalitions** has unconditional probability at least $c$ of being the first exit. This date may depend on the profile. The target and the prescribed or deviating calendar have no role in the constants.

3. In the range of the second assertion, each such table is in the closure of a **nonempty open subset of the full 60-dimensional reward space** on which those same conclusions hold. Here the thresholds on the parameters can be increased as in the proof. All 60 entries can vary independently within that open set. The constants for first-exit probabilities and regrets are for each fixed table.

Consequently even a construction that chooses which one of the fifteen coalitions to omit separately at every table cannot cover all Fin4 games. At these rewards approaching equilibrium forces simultaneous use of all four independent clocks on a positively reached date. A pure grand-coalition opening would realize only one outcome, and the no-sure result excludes such an opening. The finite profiles used below approximate one target for each fixed table.

## Constructing the return from the rewards

Write \(g=Gq,\ H_i=\prod_{j\ne i}(1-q_j)\). Prefixing a row q to future native values w gives Wait outputs \(w'_i=g_i+H_i w_i\). The Quit offers are \(-g_i+B_i\) with
\[
 B=((L+J_0q_2)q_1q_3,\ (K+Nq_0)q_2q_3,\ J_2q_0q_1q_3,\ 0).
\]
The construction keeps \(w_3=0\) throughout its multiactive arm and ties the two offers for each active player.

**Entry.** Start at \((0,0,7x,0)\) in the stated closed shooting interval. Prefix solo-0 with cumulative hazard probability \(s=x/(1+x)\), reaching \((0,9s,0,14s)\) and then prefix solo-2 with cumulative exit probability \(14s/(4+14s)\), reaching \((U,V,0,0)=x(21,16,0,0)/(1+9x/2)\). Solo blocks can be split to arbitrary mesh, all intermediate values being between the endpoint values. Put \(U_m=336/79,\ c=16/21,\ k=3/14,\ \alpha=9k/10\).

On a fully active row, set \(q=(kp+2h/7,p,h,y)\) so \(g_3=0\). For inputs \((U,V,D,0)\) the three other ties are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 H_0 U&=(L+J_0h)py+16p-12h+4y,\\
 H_1 V&=(K+Nq_0)hy-18q_0-4h+20y,\\
 H_2 D&=J_2q_0py-27p+4h+8y .
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
For the first row \(D=0,\ V=cU\) and U ranges on a compact subinterval of \((0,U_m]\). Write \(y=Y/J_2,\ h=(J_2/N)T\). After cancellation the equations are smooth in \(1/J_2,J_2/N,J_0/N\), and their common zero-parameter limit is triangular. In particular the extra term in the first equation is \((J_0/N)pTY\).
\[
 p=U/(16+U),\qquad kp^2Y=27p,\qquad kpTY=(1-kp)V+18kp.
\]
All three roots are strictly positive with uniformly invertible Jacobian on the compact seed interval. The inverse function theorem and finite covering give a continuous nearby root when \(J_2\) is sufficiently large and then \(N\) is sufficiently large for the chosen \(J_2,J_0\). Local continuations agree by uniqueness in a neighborhood of the compact limiting graph. The output \((U',V',D',0)\) tends uniformly to \((8p,\ 9kp+(1-kp)V,\ 13.5p,\ 0)\). Here \(V'/p\le9k+c(16+U_m)<18\) in the limit. Consequently \(10U'>2V'\) and \(10D'>4V'\) with uniform strict margins on the seed interval for those finite parameters. Prefix a solo-3 block of total exit probability \(V'/(10+V')\). Its effect is
\[
 (U',V',D',0)\longmapsto
 \left(\frac{10U'-2V'}{10+V'},0,\frac{10D'-4V'}{10+V'},0\right).
 \tag{2}
\]
The new U,D are positive. By uniform convergence, at the first full row we may assume \(h\le p/100,\ y\le p/4\), giving \(U-U'\ge7p\).

**Subsequent returns to \(V=0\).** All inputs now have \(0<U\le U_m\), \(0\le D\le15\). First try the 013 row \(h=0,\ y=\alpha p\), tying 0,1,3. Determine p uniquely and continuously from the strictly increasing first equation of (1). Write \(p_A(U)\) for this root. Its output has \(V'=0\). The silent 2 can Wait if and only if \(D\ge F_A(U)\), where
\[
 F_A(U)=\frac{J_2k p_A^2(\alpha p_A)-27p_A+8\alpha p_A}
 {(1-kp_A)(1-p_A)(1-\alpha p_A)}.
 \tag{3}
\]
For smaller D we construct a fully active row and apply (2).

First hold both grand premiums \(J_0,J_2\) fixed and put \(h=t/N\). At \(1/N=0\) equations (1) with \(V=0\) give
\[
 p=p(y)=\frac{U-(U+4)y}{16+U+(L-U)y},\quad
 t=\frac{18kp-20y}{kpy},\quad
 D=F_U(y)=\frac{J_2k p^2 y-27p+8y}{(1-kp)(1-p)(1-y)}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The physical range is \(0<y\le \alpha p(y)\), whose upper endpoint is \(y_A=\alpha p_A\). On this interval p is positive and strictly decreasing, and \(p\le U/(16+U)<3/14\). Also \(F_U(0)<0,\ F_U(y_A)=F_A(U)\).

For all sufficiently large \(J_2\), every level crossing \(0\le F_U(y)\le15\) on this interval has strictly positive derivative in y, uniformly in the following sense. For small enough p (depending on the fixed L), the first equation and \(y\le\alpha p\) imply small U, hence \(-1/2<p_y<0\) by (4). The derivative of \(p^2y\) is then at least \((1-\alpha)p^2\), and the derivative of \(-27p+8y\) is positive. The denominator has logarithmic derivative \(-kp_y/(1-kp)-p_y/(1-p)-1/(1-y)<0\) upon decreasing the small-p cutoff, since \((1+k)/2<1\). This proves the assertion at nonnegative numerator. On the remaining strip p is bounded away from zero. Derivatives of \(p(y)\) and of the denominator are bounded independently of \(J_2\) there. At a crossing the numerator bound \(J_2k p^2y\le15+27p\) implies \(y=O(1/J_2)\), giving \((p^2y)'=p^2+O(1/J_2)\) bounded below. The positive derivative of the numerator grows linearly in \(J_2\) while the denominator term in the quotient derivative is bounded since \(F_U\le15\). These two regions prove the claim. Hence, if \(D<F_A(U)\) and \(0\le D\le15\), equation (4) has a unique interior root, with \(t>0\). At equality use \(t=0,y=y_A\). Indeed two crossings of the same level, all strictly increasing, are impossible.

For each fixed large \(J_2\) these solutions, including the seam, form a compact graph bounded away from \(p=0,y=0\). In fact \(F_A(U)\ge0\) requires \(p_A^2\ge(27-8\alpha)/(J_2k\alpha)>0\), bounding U below. At any root \(F_U(y)\ge0\) then keeps y away from zero. All physical hazards are bounded away from one. To perturb (4) to finite N, use \(1/N,t,p,y\) in (1). The first two equations solve for p,t locally at fixed U,y since at the limit the first increases strictly in p and the second has t derivative \(kpy>0\). The last equation has the strictly positive level derivative just proved. The inverse function theorem on the compact graph gives continuous solutions uniformly close, with convergent first derivatives, for all sufficiently large N at these fixed \(J_0,J_2\). At \(D=F_A(U)\) the finite solution still has \(t=0,\ y=y_A\), since (1) then coincides exactly with (3). At the limiting seam \(dt/dD<0\), since along (4), \(dt/dy=-18/y^2+20p_y/(kp^2)<0\). Its sign persists on a neighborhood of the compact seam. Thus for \(D<F_A(U)\) the continuation has \(t>0\) near the seam and also on the complementary compact subregion by positivity of its limiting t. Taking sufficiently large N ensures \(0\le h\le p/100,\ y\le p/4\) on all chosen rows. On the 013 branch these bounds hold directly.

For a full row out of \(V=0\), the new value \(V'=g_1=B_1/2\) is nonnegative and at most \(9q_0+2h<3p\). On the 013 branch \(V'=0\). In either case \(U'=B_0-g_0>7p,\ D'=H_2D+13.5p-2h-4y>12p\) and \(U-U'=(1-H_0)U-g_0\ge7p\). Therefore (2) is permissible, preserves nonnegative intermediate silent values and gives strictly positive U,D and is the identity when \(V'=0\). D stays at most 15 since every Wait update averages the child and passive rewards at most 15, including during the reset. U decreases at least \(7p\) in each row-plus-reset. At fixed parameters \(U\le C_{L,J_0,J_2}p\) uniformly by the first equation of (1) and the stated bounds on h,y,p (on the continued graph p remains below \(1/4\)). Consequently U goes to zero geometrically and \(\sum_{j\ge n}p_j\le U_n/7\). D changes by at most \(Cp\) per row or reset because its bounded Wait recursion is Lipschitz in the aggregate rival hazard and \(V'\le3p\). The completed output is \((0,0,R(x),0)\) and is continuous because each step is continuous by pasting at (3), and the tail errors are uniform at fixed finite parameters. Once U is small enough only 013 occurs, by (3).

**Shooting.** For \(x=10^{-3}\), \(\sum p\le21x/7=3x\) across all multiactive rows, including the initial one. Their aggregate hazards sum to at most \(2\sum p\). In each associated solo-3 block the sum of the split hazards is at most \(-\log(1-V'/(10+V'))\le V'/10\). Since initially \(V'\le V+3p\), thereafter \(V'\le3p\), all these blocks cost at most \((16x+3\sum p)/10\). The combined aggregate hazard sum is below \(9x\). Throughout these phases \(U\le21x,\ D\le13.5\sum p\le41x\), because solo-3 resets decrease D. Take \(v=(891,-413,587,559)/587\), so \(vG=(0,0,2284/587,0)\). At any of these rows \(v(w'-w)=vGq-\sum_i v_i(1-H_i)w_i\). The total loss is at most \((2\cdot21+41)9x^2\), since \(w_3=0,\ v_1<0\). Thus
\[
 R(x)\ge12103x/[587(1+9x/2)]-747x^2>7x.
\]
At \(x=16/7\), \(R(x)\le15<7x\). The intermediate value theorem supplies a fixed point.

**Original uniform play.** Truncate the infinite prefixing construction after n multiactive rounds, split all solo blocks to date hazards of mesh at most \(1/n\), reverse into physical order and repeat the period. The assigned values satisfy the Wait and prescribed recursions except at one seam of size \(\delta_n\to0\). On each solo date the owner ties, each silent Wait output is nonnegative, and every deviating Quit offer has magnitude at most \(\max |G_{ij}|/n\). No premium enters a solo date's offer. All multiactive comparisons are exact. Under every unilateral replacement the opponents absorb per period with probability at least a common \(c_x>0\) from the original solo-0 and solo-2 blocks. Thus summing seam errors in survival probability costs at most \(\delta_n/c_x\) for adherence and any deviation. A deviator's first attempted Quit on the unique all-Continue history incurs at most the extra single-date offer margin. Conditioning on this possibly infinite random deadline covers every replacement. The fixed initial target is approached and terminal regret tends to zero. Restoring own levels adds exactly $s_i$ to each of player $i$’s comparisons since the deleted calendar absorbs almost surely. At each fixed period the same geometric absorption bound is uniform in deviations and yields convergence of all sufficiently long finite-horizon payoffs to their terminal payoffs. This proves the stated original uniform payoff.

## Forcing first exits at three and then four clocks

All remaining arguments use $s_i=1$. For each of the intermediate and final tables a half hazard at every date guarantees the original payoff 1. The two immediate native absorbing contributions from the two actions sum to the nonnegative joining premium and a half hazard absorbs almost surely. All-Continue opponents give the opposite punishment bound, so the punishment vector is exactly $\mathbf1$.

At the table without premiums, the stage comparison Wait minus Quit at punishment divided by two is $D=Gq$. At a sure player it must be nonpositive, at a quiet player nonnegative and at a mixer zero. There is no sure Nash opening there. If $q_3=0$ then $D_3\ge0$ gives $14q_0\ge3q_1+4q_2$. At $q_0=1$, $D_1>0$ forces $q_1=0$ and $D_2<0$ forces $q_2=1$, contradicting $D_0\le0$. At $q_1=1$, $D_0<0$ forces $q_0=1$. At $q_2=1$, $D_1>0$ forces $q_1=0$ and $D_0>0$ forces $q_0=0$, contradicting $D_3\ge0$. If $q_3>0$, then $D_3\le0$ implies $q_0\le(3q_1+4q_2)/14\le1/2$. Hence $D_0\ge0$, $q_2>0$, $q_1<3q_2/4$ and $D_1\ge0$. A sure 3 would have $D_1\le9/2+2-10<0$. Only 2 can be sure. If $q_2=1$ then 3 mixes, $q_0=(3q_1+4)/14>0$ also mixes and $q_1=(6-2q_3)/8>1/2$. This yields $D_2>-7/2+15/2-4=0$, impossible.

If a row with a sure player is Nash at child best-response values above punishment, lowering those values to punishment preserves its Nash comparisons. Only a sole sure player's Wait can depend on the child values and lowering them favors its Quit. In particular a first sure date on an exact calendar would give a sure opening at punishment. The same argument applies to the final table below.

**An unavoidable simultaneous first exit at the unboosted table.** Suppose baseline regrets $e$ and tie probabilities $\tau$ both tend to zero. For the live history prior to date $t$ write $S_t$ for survival, $b_t$ for the vector of unrestricted best response caps and $W_t$ for the prescribed payoff. These are original units. Let $F(w,q)$ denote the prescribed one-date expectation using $w$ if nobody quits. The nonnegative one-step loss $\ell_t=b_t-F(b_{t+1},q_t)$ satisfies $\sum_t S_t\ell_t\le e$ componentwise by telescoping the prescribed recursion and using $b_t\ge W_t$. Following the strategy up to $t$ and then optimizing also gives $\|b_t-W_t\|_\infty\le e/S_t$. All caps are at least 1 by the constant half hazard. Joint Never mass is at most $e$ because if the limiting survival is positive then conditional prescribed payoff tends to zero and the conditional cap is at least 1.

Write $\eta=e+\tau+1/n$ along the sequence, $\sigma=\sqrt\eta$, and stop just after survival first falls below $\sigma$. There is such a cut for large $n$. Every hazard up to it is at most $1-\gamma$ for fixed $\gamma>0$ and large $n$. Otherwise compactness at the bounded child caps $b_{t+1}\ge\mathbf1$ and $\ell_t\le e/\sigma$ gives a limiting Nash row with a sure quitter. Lowering its continuation to the punishment levels keeps it Nash as in the sure-opening argument. The final survival is at least $\gamma^4\sigma$. With $a_t=\sum_i q_{it}$ and $H=\sum_t a_t$ through the cut, the log survival bound $-\log(1-q_{it})\le q_{it}/\gamma$ gives $H\to\infty$.

Put mass $a_t/H$ on the marked rows $(w,q,z)=(b_{t+1},q_t,q_t/a_t)$, skipping blank dates. Take a weak limit on the compact marked space. Every limiting $w=\mathbf1+Gx$ for $x$ in the probability simplex with $Gx\ge0$. Indeed, the future conditional probability of a tie or Never is at most $(\tau+e)/(\gamma^4\sigma)$, and the child cap differs from the actual conditional payoff by at most $e/(\gamma^4\sigma)$. Write $a=\sum_i q_i$. The empirical mean of the nonnegative $\ell_t/a_t$ is at most $e/(\sigma H)$ componentwise. Since $b_t$ is the best of the two current actions using the child cap, any positive-rate limiting support row is Nash at $w$. The mean of the normalized tie probability has the same bound with $\tau$ in place of $e$, so any such row has just one active player. On a zero-rate row $z_i>0$ implies $w_i=1$ because a strictly greater continuation would give a strict Wait advantage and positive normalized loss for the positive proportion assigned to Quit. These assertions use compactness locally at points with strict inequalities.

For every polynomial $V$ the mean of $(V(F(w,q))-V(w))/a$ is zero in the limit. The expression extends continuously in $(w,a,z)$ to $a=0$ by $\nabla V(w)\cdot\sum_j z_j(r(\{j\})-w)$, by expanding the product probabilities. Replacing $V(F)$ by $V(b_t)$ before division by $H$ costs at most a fixed Lipschitz constant times $4e/\sigma$. On a blank row the corresponding telescoping difference has the same summed error bound. The remaining sum telescopes to bounded endpoints, proving the identity.

Here is an explicit contradiction to this mean-zero identity. In simplex coordinates put $Y_i=\{x:Gx\ge0,(Gx)_i=0\}$, $D_iU=\nabla U(x)\cdot(e_i-x)$, where $e_i$ is the coordinate vector. At a positive solo row of player $i$ with proper hazard $h$, its indifference gives $w_i=1$. The output is $\mathbf1+Gx'$ with $x'=(1-h)x+he_i$. This output is the limit of pre-date caps (the unnormalized row losses vanish), so $Gx'\ge0$ and $(Gx')_i=0$. Convexity puts the segment from $x$ to $x'$ in $Y_i$. At a zero-rate row $x\in Y_i$ whenever $z_i>0$.

Use $y=(6x_0-2,8x_1-2,5x_2-2)$, and define $U$ by the following coefficients on the monomials $y^\alpha$, $1\le|\alpha|\le3$, in ascending lexicographic order on the triples $\alpha$.

```
145,170,51,27,321,201,-61,47,-23,0,58,22,102,246,-36,-13,-54,71,-7
```

Use six vertices $A,B,C,D,E,F$, in this order, obtained by dividing each of the following vectors by its coordinate sum.
\[
 (388,304,559,461),\ (244,356,587,337),\ (45,21,28,0),\ (25,42,56,0),\ (312,268,891,459),\ (60,28,189,0).
\]
The vertex lists for $Y_0,Y_1,Y_2,Y_3$ are $ABCD,ABE,ACEF,BDEF$. They result by solving $\sum x_j=1,(Gx)_i=0$ and each pair of tight rows from $x\ge0,Gx\ge0$, keeping nonsingular feasible solutions. For $P=D_iU$ evaluate its symmetric affine polarization on triples of these vertices with repetition by

 \frac16\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subset\{1,2,3\}}(-1)^{3-|S|}|S|^3 P\left(\frac{\sum_{j\in S}v_j}{|S|}\right).

The four maxima are respectively $-20852908491/5017776128$, $-4055857013/1179868608$, $-5487168/21253933$, $-1882523941/1031797300$. Barycentric expansion bounds $D_iU<-1/4$ throughout every $Y_i$.

Take $V(w)=U(G^{-1}(w-\mathbf1))$, which is defined since $\det G=4568$. At a proper positive solo row its normalized increment is $h^{-1}\int_0^h D_iU((1-t)x+te_i)/(1-t)\,dt<-1/4$. Thus $\inf(e+\tau)>0$ on the baseline, which implies the fixed tie bound used above.

**A paid coalition must quit first.** Use the baseline consequence that for some $\delta>0$ every profile of sufficiently small baseline regret has probability at least $\delta$ of two or more first quitters. This was just proved for the unboosted table. Let $C\ge1$ bound baseline absolute original payoffs and let $h=\delta/24$. On the compact set of $q\in[0,1]^4$ where two hazards are at least $h$ and $q_i[(Gq)_i]_+=0$ for every $i$, there is an $a>0$ with $\max(q_1q_3,q_2q_3)\ge a$. Indeed if $q_3=0$ then no pair of $012$ can be the support, since each has a participant whose comparison with the other is positive. The triple $012$ has $(Gq)_1>0$ and $q_1>0$. If $q_3>0$ and $q_1=q_2=0$, the pair $03$ has $(Gq)_3>0$. These exhaust all supports with vanishing maximum. If the compact set is empty use any $a>0$, as the limiting row below would already contradict emptiness.

Take $L$ so large that $La/2>2C+1$ and fix any $M=K/L\ge1$. At the intermediate table carrying just $L,K$, suppose profiles had regret $e\to0$ and $p=\Pr(T\in\{013,123,0123\})\to0$. Every deviator's baseline payoff is bounded above by its modified payoff, while the prescribed payoff gain due to the modification is at most $MLp$ per player. Thus baseline regret $e^0\le e+MLp\to0$. There is a date of survival $S\ge\delta/2$ on which two hazards are at least $h$. Otherwise every date before survival falls below $\delta/2$ has conditional tie probability at most $6h\max q_i$, which is at most $6h$ times the conditional exit probability, and subsequent dates together have total mass at most $\delta/2$. This would give tie mass at most $6h+\delta/2<\delta$. Pass to convergent hazard rows along selected dates. Baseline immediate Quit has native value $-(Gq)_i$ whereas the player's baseline cap is at least 1 in original units. The conditional regret is at least $q_i[(Gq)_i]_+$ because the prescribed mixture assigns $q_i$ to immediate Quit, while the prescribed Wait branch cannot exceed its cap. Since a player can follow the profile to this date before switching, this quantity is at most $e^0/S$. The limiting row therefore lies in the compact set above.

On a subsequence $q_1q_3$ or $q_2q_3$ stays at least $a/2$. In the first case outsider 0 receives a bonus of at least $La/2$ by quitting immediately, in the second outsider 1 receives at least $MLa/2$. In either case the conditional baseline Quit offer is at least $-C$, while the modified conditional prescribed payoff is at most $C+MLp/S$. Thus following the profile to this date and then quitting gives unconditional gain at least $S(La/2-2C)-MLp$, bounded away from zero. This gives a positive lower bound on paid first-exit probability at all sufficiently small regrets for each fixed finite modified table.


**The grand coalition.** For fixed $L,K$ in the preceding range write $d>0$ for its lower bound on first-exit probability in $\mathcal B=\{013,123,0123\}$ at small regret. Let $R\ge1$ bound the intermediate rewards. Suppose at the final table that original regret and grand first-exit mass $m$ both tend to zero. Removing the three extra grand rewards can only decrease a deviator's terminal payoff, and decreases any prescribed payoff by at most $m\max(J_0,J_2,N)$. Thus intermediate regret tends to zero. Dates of pre-date survival below $d/2$ contribute at most $d/2$ in total. At some earlier date with survival $S\ge d/2$, the conditional probability of $\mathcal B$ exceeds $(d/4)a$, with $a>0$ the conditional probability of any exit. Otherwise summing with survival weights would contradict mass at least $d$ in $\mathcal B$. That probability is at most $q_0q_1q_3+q_1q_2q_3$, so one product is at least $da/8$. Since each hazard is at most $a$, the product is at most $a^3$ and hence at least $h=(d/8)^{3/2}$. Player 2 or 0 can act on that date to collect expected extra grand reward at least $h\min(J_0,J_2)$. Following until then and quitting gains at least
\[
 S\bigl(h\min(J_0,J_2)-2R\bigr)-m\max(J_0,J_2,N).
\]
For $h\min(J_0,J_2)>2R$ this contradicts vanishing regret. Therefore grand first-exit mass has a positive lower bound $d_*$ at all sufficiently small regrets on each fixed finite final table. This is an actual outcome probability under independent private play.

## Excluding sure openings and exact profiles

Take $K\ge\max(L,1220)$ as we can by increasing $L_0$. At the punishment continuation the four Wait-minus-Quit differences divided by two are

    D₀=g₀−(L+J₀q₂)q₁q₃/2,        D₁=g₁−(K+Nq₀)q₂q₃/2,
    D₂=g₂−J₂q₀q₁q₃/2,             D₃=g₃,   g=Gq.

If q₃=0 every joining premium vanishes. The unboosted argument rules out a sure opening there. On q₃>0, one has D₃≤0 hence q₀≤(3q₁+4q₂)/14≤1/2, then D₀≥0 yields q₂>0, q₁<3q₂/4. Consequently D₁≥0, yielding

    q₃(K+Nq₀)≤4+18(3·3/4+4)/14<12.1.

Thus q₃<1/100 and the only possible sure agent is 2, giving q₂=1. Then 3 mixes, so q₀=(3q₁+4)/14≥2/7 also mixes and q₃<43/N. Its D₀ tie gives q₁=(6−2q₃)/(8+(L+J₀)q₃/2)→3/4 as N→∞, uniformly as long as (L+J₀)/N→0. Substituting in D₂ gives a strict positive lower bound even at a supposed sure 2. As $J_2$ is fixed while $N$ tends to infinity, the last joining term in $D_2$ tends to zero. This contradiction excludes sure openings.

**One common date for all fifteen outcomes.** On dates reached with probability at least $d_*/2$ all four hazards are at most $1-\gamma$ for a fixed $\gamma>0$ and all sufficiently small regrets on this table. Otherwise take a sequence of such dates with a hazard tending to one. Restart regret tends to zero by following until that date, and the bounded child best-response values have a convergent subsequence above punishment. Vanishing one-step loss at those caps would give a sure Nash row at the limiting continuation, hence at punishment by the lowering argument.

At some such date the product $\prod_i q_i$ exceeds $(d_*/4)a$, with $a>0$ the conditional probability of any exit. This follows by the same survival-weighted summation as above. The product is at most $a^4$, so it and each individual $q_i$ are at least $h_*=(d_*/4)^{4/3}$. Independence now gives every particular nonempty $T$ unconditional first-exit probability at least $(d_*/2)\min(h_*,\gamma)^4$ on this one date. This supplies the common lower bound in the statement.

The argument for exact profiles and bounded periods is given together with the perturbation result below.
