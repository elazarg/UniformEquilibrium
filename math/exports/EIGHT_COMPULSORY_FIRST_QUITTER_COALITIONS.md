# Exactly eight first-quitter coalitions and a uniform payoff with 21 unrestricted rewards

There is a nonempty open set in the original 60-dimensional reward space with the following properties. Each game has a uniform-equilibrium payoff. For each game there exist $e_0,c,C>0$ such that every private behavioral profile of terminal regret $e\le e_0$ satisfies all three conclusions.

1. At one date, each of the seven nonempty subsets of $\{0,1,3\}$ has unconditional probability at least $c$ of being the first quitting coalition.
2. Player 2 quits alone at the first exit with total probability at least $c$.
3. The total probability of all seven first exits in which player 2 quits together with anyone else is at most $Ce$.

Consequently **every** limiting first-exit law as regret tends to zero has exactly the same eight coalitions in its support. There is no exact equilibrium even with an aperiodic calendar. The existence proof applies more broadly. It gives explicit open sets in 39 tested reward coordinates on which all 21 remaining entries can take arbitrary real values independently. The necessity statements hold on the described open subset of the full reward space. Here regret is the greatest gain from replacing one player’s complete stopping rule, including choosing Never.

Two further conclusions apply to specified families. On the central tables indexed by a large premium $L$, the total mass of triple 013 has order $L^{-1}$ and the largest possible unconditional mass it can receive on one date has order $L^{-3/2}$. Collecting half the triple mass requires order $\sqrt L$ dates, and this order is attained by profiles with arbitrarily small regret. On the signed singleton line described below, existence holds for every real value of the observer reward against player 3, with the same 21 independently unrestricted entries. The asymptotic bounds concern the specified central tables.

For arbitrarily accurate finite-word implementations, the remaining large-\(u\) dates of the first \(J\) periods then carry at least half of \(m\); their number is at most \(K\sqrt L\).

In particular the maximum one-date mass has the matching order for the constructed profiles: at least one of those at most $K\sqrt L$ dates carries at least $m/(2K\sqrt L)\ge c/(2K L^{3/2})$. This is an attainment statement for a family of arbitrarily small-regret profiles. The upper bound holds for every sufficiently small-regret profile at the fixed table.
