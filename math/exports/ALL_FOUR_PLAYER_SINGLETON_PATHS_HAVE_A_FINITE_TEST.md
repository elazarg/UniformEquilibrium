# Triangles and quadrilaterals decide continuous singleton paths on every four-player matrix

Let Γ be any hollow four-by-four real matrix, including zero comparisons and singular principal matrices. A singleton path means a measurable x:[0,1]→Δ({0,1,2,3}) with W(t)=∫_t^1 Γx≥0 for every t and x_i W_i=0 almost everywhere for each i. The path exists exactly when one of the following finite tests succeeds.

1. There is p∈Δ with Γp≥0 and p_i(Γp)_i=0 for all i. This is finite linear feasibility after choosing the support. It yields the constant control x=p.
2. There is a cyclic list of three or four distinct pure labels, with 0<ρ<1, positive lengths d_0,...,d_(k−1) of sum 1−ρ, and vectors

       W_0=(Σ_j d_j Γ_.c_j)/(1−ρ),      W_(j+1)=W_j−d_j Γ_.c_j.

   Require every W_j≥0 and (W_j)_(c_j)=0. Repeating the tour with factors ρ^n gives a path, by affine interpolation. For each list these conditions are polynomial after multiplying by 1−ρ. Only the fourteen labelled cyclic orders up to rotation need be considered. Once test 1 has failed, the necessity argument produces a word in which every switch p→j has Γ_jp≥0≥Γ_pj, with at least one comparison strict. This defines a graph with at most one direction per pair, containing at most two directed triangles and one directed quadrilateral. At zero comparisons one may retain the polynomial feasibility tests even when a determinant coefficient vanishes. In the absence of zero comparisons there are at most four candidate scales: the matrix of active zero equations has entries Γ_(c_j,c_h) above its diagonal and ρΓ_(c_j,c_h) below. Its determinant divided by ρ has degree k−2 with nonzero constant and leading coefficients, since the increasing and decreasing cyclic permutations are the unique terms with one and k−1 lower factors. Thus each triangle gives at most one real scale and the quadrilateral at most two. No principal minor need be assumed nonsingular.

These alternatives decide the continuous singleton branch for every four-player matrix. In the quitting game there are independent behavioral stopping rules, zero live and perpetual-Continue payoffs, and arbitrary finite real absorbing rewards r_i(A) for a nonempty quitting coalition A. Set s_i=r_i({i}) and Γ_ij=r_i({j})−s_i. Whenever the path test succeeds, the original game has a uniform-equilibrium payoff against complete behavioral deviations. This holds for arbitrary higher rewards and arbitrary signed s_i. The checked consumers and their input data are specified below. When the path test fails the existence of unrestricted uniform equilibrium is a separate question.

## Dynamics on the tetrahedron

Assume there is a path and test 1 fails, so Γ is R0. Write z(t)=∫_t^1 x≥0. In log survival time θ=−log(1−t), p=z/(1−t) stays in Δ, p'=p−x, Γp≥0 and x_i(Γp)_i=0. There is a recurrent entire such trajectory: boundedness and uniform Lipschitz continuity provide an entire trajectory by translating to later times and taking limits on compact intervals. These constraints are closed under local uniform convergence, since ∫x=∫p−[p] and each inactive coordinate stays absent on compact subsets where its limiting slack is positive. The compact space of entire trajectories is invariant under shifts. In a minimal nonempty compact invariant subset the positive omega-limit of each trajectory is the entire subset, giving recurrence in the topology of uniform convergence on compact intervals. No uniqueness of the limiting state equation is required.

Put w=Γp/Σ_i(Γp)_i. The denominator is positive by R0 and the zero active row puts w on the sphere formed by the boundary of the nonnegative unit-sum tetrahedron. On compact intervals use z=e^(−θ)p and physical mass parameter dt=e^(−θ)dθ: W=Γz moves with derivative −Γx. In the open face where just W_j is zero, j is the sole color. The projected path there is a straight, nonconstant segment. Constancy would make Γ_.j proportional to w. A nonnegative proportionality gives a homogeneous complementary unit vector, and a negative one gives a nonzero full nonnegative kernel vector by adding a positive multiple of z to e_j. Both contradict R0.

A vertex with W=α e_l, α>0 and S=I\{l}, can occur only if Γ_SS has no nonzero nonnegative kernel vector. Otherwise extend such a vector u by zero off S, so Γu=β e_l. For β≥0 the normalized u solves test 1, while for β<0 the nonzero nonnegative vector u−(β/α)z is killed by Γ. Likewise the full nonnegative kernel is trivial. Thus the needed cone-kernel tests are automatic at exactly the reachable vertices.

## Behavior along an open edge

At a point of the open edge E with W_i=W_j=0 only these two colors can occur nearby. On such a neighborhood the two physical derivatives are −Γ_ij x_j and −Γ_ji x_i. There are three cases.

* If both coefficients are nonzero, each coordinate is monotone and its zero set is an interval. The interior of a zero interval for one row has zero mass of the other, and outside only the other can move. Simultaneous zeros cannot persist. An open-edge hit of a two-sided trajectory is an isolated crossing from the i-face to the j-face or conversely. The direction i→j requires Γ_ji>0>Γ_ij; same-sign entries cannot give a crossing.
* If Γ_ji=0 and Γ_ij≠0, a visit to E forces W_j=0 on a time neighborhood. W_i is monotone, so only the j-face and E can be present. On a positive-length edge interval only i can move. If Γ_ij>0, the only interior junction is entry from the j-face; an exit into that face is impossible. If Γ_ij<0, only exit to the j-face is possible. Any visit has a nontrivial edge-interval side. Swap i,j for the other one-zero pattern.
* If both coefficients vanish, a visit stays on E in a neighborhood. Both colors may be used while it stays there, but no open facet can join E at an interior point. Any entrance or exit from the edge is through a tetrahedron vertex.

Motion along a feasible edge interval in the latter two cases has a fixed strict orientation. For any tangent-allowed probability vector u (only i in the one-zero case, or any mixture of i,j when both entries vanish), Γu vanishes on i and j. With σ determined by dσ=dt/Σ W, the normalized tangent is

    F_u(w)=−Γu+(Σ_h(Γu)_h)w .

This never vanishes on E∩Γ(R⁴_+), including feasible endpoints in the closed edge. Otherwise Γu=λw. If λ≥0, u is homogeneous complementary; if λ<0, use w=Γz' for z'≥0 to produce a nonnegative full kernel vector u−λz'. The feasible subsegment is convex, and so is the set of tangent controls. Continuity and nonvanishing in the one-dimensional edge line force the same strict orientation for every such u and every feasible position on the same edge. The original edge motion is strictly monotone, even when the two controls vary measurably.

There are locally finitely many intervals of constant *stratum* (open face or open edge) on a two-sided trajectory, allowing isolated vertex points. Away from vertices the preceding local cases prove this. Near a vertex e_l suppress l by continuity. It is the only possible vertex in that neighborhood and is attained at most once; two occurrences of W_S=0 would give a nonzero integrated nonnegative kernel vector on S. In either punctured side, a slide on a nonstrict edge cannot both start after and end before the ends of that time interval: that would require a vertex, or two opposite interior junctions of which at most one direction is allowed. Consequently an infinite accumulation of stratum changes at the vertex would require infinitely many successive strict face crossings among the three available labels, after discarding at most two end slides. The directed crossing graph would have to be a directed triangle. All its off-diagonal entries are nonzero in S, and the adjugate of this three-by-three oriented triangle is strictly positive (opposite signs on each two-cycle and like signs in each off-diagonal cofactor). Its determinant cannot vanish by triviality of the reachable nonnegative kernel. Its inverse has one sign. Yet at this interior time the integrated controls on a short left and a short right interval give nonzero nonnegative u,v with Γ_SS u≥0, Γ_SS v≤0 respectively. One of these is impossible. This establishes local finiteness on every compact interval of the entire trajectory.

## A repeating direction and its number of switches

An injective recurrent projected trajectory cannot cross a strict open edge. If it did, choose a short subarc J about the crossing with endpoints never visited (these strict crossings are locally finite in time). All crossings have the same direction. Successive hits by a simple curve of such a transversal in a sphere are strictly ordered along it. Indeed, join two consecutive hits A<B by the reverse chord BA of J to make a Jordan curve. Right after B the trajectory is in the component toward which it crosses the chord. It cannot leave across the chord, since all crossings point into that component, nor across its own past. The portion of J before A is in the other component and the portion beyond B is in its current component, as seen in the two transverse crossing disks. Therefore any later hit is beyond B, with order reversed if B<A. Recurrence gives later hits arbitrarily close to the first, a contradiction.

An injective trajectory visits each of the four vertices at most once. After those visits, in the absence of strict edge crossings, a nonstrict edge interval can neither start nor end at two successive interior junctions, by the local edge cases. Thus such an entire future must eventually stay in just one face or just one edge. Staying in a face forces p'=p−e_j there, so boundedness gives p=e_j and test 1. If it stays in the edge E of i,j then p(θ)=∫_θ^∞ e^(θ−s)x(s)ds is supported in {i,j}. Its Γp is nonnegative with both those rows zero, again test 1. Consequently the recurrent trajectory has a repeated w.

Between two equal directions there are finitely many stratum pieces. They are locally injective: the interior of each pure facet or edge piece is strictly monotone on a line. At a junction different neighboring open strata have disjoint interiors. Two consecutive pieces in the same open face cannot pass through a lower stratum and return to the face, since the movement in the common pure direction makes every other unnormalized coordinate affine and nonnegative. Consecutive pieces on one edge cannot turn around at a vertex because all their tangents have the same strict orientation. Thus on this compact interval a finite cover gives an injectivity radius in time. Choosing two equal directions with the least positive separation gives a Jordan loop. A loop with no stratum junction is impossible by strict one-dimensional motion.

Each strict edge contributes at most one interior junction of the loop. The crossings of a Jordan curve by a segment alternate sides and strict-edge transitions have fixed direction. Each one-zero edge contributes at most one interior junction as well. If entry from the face is allowed, an edge piece starting at an interior junction can end only at the forward endpoint vertex fixed by the edge orientation. If exit is allowed, its piece can start only at the fixed backward endpoint vertex. Two distinct such interior junctions would therefore visit the same vertex twice. A two-zero edge has no interior junctions. There are six edges and the Jordan loop visits each of the four vertices at most once. It has at most ten stratum junctions and therefore at most ten pieces cyclically. If a loop starts in the middle of a piece, the initial and final parts are merged at the end of the construction. This merge is valid on an edge because the replacement below may use the same pure color on both parts.

On each edge piece the constant pure color tangent to it (choose either one when both are available) can traverse the identical normalized subsegment in the original direction. Its vector field F is continuous and bounded strictly away from zero on that closed feasible subsegment, by the nonvanishing argument above. Its σ traversal time is finite. The corresponding positive radial scale satisfies d(ΣW)/dσ=−(ΣΓ_.i)ΣW, and dt/dσ=ΣW, so the entire pure piece is traced by spending a finite positive physical mass. This replacement uses the same normalized endpoints, while its physical duration and scale may change. On pure-face pieces follow the observed path scaled to start at the new radial size. The final unnormalized point is therefore ρW_0 with ρ>0. Let d≥0 collect the nonzero mass spent across the whole loop. We have Γd=(1−ρ)W_0 and W_0=Γz_0 for some z_0≥0 from the original trajectory. If ρ≥1, then d+(ρ−1)z_0 is a nonzero nonnegative full kernel vector. Thus ρ<1. Taking geometric copies reconstructs the actual tails from their singleton masses, since their sum is Γd/(1−ρ)=W_0. It is unnecessary that the replacement use the same individual mass budget as the trajectory segment it replaced. Rescale all masses so that their first tour sums to 1−ρ. This yields a finite periodic pure path of the indicated bounded complexity. We next show that its cyclic word, after adjacent identical labels are merged, meets the sharper test 2.

## Counting switches after moving edge segments into their assigned faces

Use the simple normalized polygon of the actual traversals just obtained. It is the same oriented geometric curve as before replacing the edge controls. Give every segment its pure owner. On any switch p→j with distinct owners, the j row is zero at the junction and was nonnegative on the incoming p segment. Thus Γ_jp≥0. Similarly the p row starts at zero on the j segment and stays nonnegative, so Γ_pj≤0. They cannot both vanish. Otherwise j is zero throughout the incoming p segment, and p throughout the outgoing j segment, putting both segments entirely on E_pj. At an interior junction there they would be parts of a single maximal double-zero slide, which received just one pure label. At a tetrahedron vertex they would traverse the same edge toward and then away from its endpoint, revisiting points of the Jordan curve. If the physical cut divides one slide, its two fragments are assigned the same owner. Merging adjacent equal labels preserves all distinct-owner transitions and the actual circle of mass intervals.

The following small perturbation is purely topological and preserves the cyclic owner switches. Around each of the finitely many original junctions take a disjoint small disk meeting only its two incident arcs; at an edge-interior point take the usual two incident face sectors, and at a vertex use its three incident sectors. Away from the disks the finitely many nonincident compact arcs are disjoint at positive distance. Move each piece lying along a tetrahedron edge slightly into the interior of its assigned incident face, inside disjoint thin strips outside the disks. Retain the open-face parts inside their own sectors. The displaced arc ends lie in their assigned sectors on each disk boundary. Join ends in the same sector by a simple arc there. For ends in different sectors, the two sectors share a radial edge, also in a tetrahedron-vertex disk since exactly three faces are incident; join through that edge once, avoiding the center, the remaining sector and all other edges. Since only these two curve ends meet each disk, this preserves a single simple closed curve. The new curve never visits a vertex and crosses the open edge E_pj exactly once for each p→j or j→p switch. No reward or dynamics is evaluated on the perturbed curve.

For a fixed open edge all its crossings by this curve have the unique orientation Γ_jp≥0≥Γ_pj with one strict. Traversing an oriented edge from one endpoint to the other, successive transverse crossings of a Jordan curve on the oriented sphere must alternate in sign: each crossing changes membership in its two complementary components. Consequently no edge is crossed more than once. After merging identical successive labels the original cyclic word therefore gives a directed closed trail with no repeated undirected edge. It is nontrivial, since only one owner would imply a nonnegative singleton column from Γd=(1−ρ)W_0. Any repeated label would split a closed trail into at least two edge-disjoint simple directed cycles, each of length at least three. Two triangles on four vertices share an edge, and a triangle and a quadrilateral or two quadrilaterals would require more than the six available edges. Thus the word is itself a simple triangle or quadrilateral and its original geometric masses pass test 2.

## A path using a genuine edge interval

Take, in order 0,1,2,3,

    Γ = [0,0,−4,8; 8,0,−3,2; 2,8,0,−3; −1,−2,8,0].

The full determinant is −3840. The four three-by-three principal determinants in lexicographic order are −256,−128,116,110. Every singleton has a negative outsider. Every pair principal is invertible except 01, where a possible nonnegative kernel vector is supported only on player 1 and its slack in row 3 is negative. Hence the matrix is R0. For word 0123 take d=(8,4,2,1)/16, ρ=1/16. The four opening vectors are

    (0,4,3,0),   (0,0,2,1/2),   (0,0,0,1),   (1/2,3/8,0,0).

Direct substitution verifies all boundary requirements. During the second interval both the 0 and 1 values are zero and the projected point travels on their common edge to a vertex. This example illustrates the boundary geometry at a zero comparison.
