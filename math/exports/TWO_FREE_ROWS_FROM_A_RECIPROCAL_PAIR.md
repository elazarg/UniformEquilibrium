# Uniform equilibrium with two unrestricted rows from reciprocal endpoint comparisons

At a stationary row of a four-player quitting game let $H_i$ be the probability all rivals wait, $P_i$ the passive native payoff contribution on nonempty rival exits, and $Q_i$ the forced native quit offer. Center each nonempty payoff by the recipient's own singleton $s_i=r_i(\{i\})$, writing $Z_i(T)=r_i(T)-s_i$ and $G_{ij}=Z_i(\{j\})$ with zero diagonal. Set $F_i=P_i-(1-H_i)Q_i$. With a positive rival hazard, Never pays $P_i/(1-H_i)$ and finite deadlines interpolate between it and $Q_i$. A stationary cube complementarity root (zero $F_i$ at interior hazards, nonnegative at zero and nonpositive at one) with at least two positive hazards is exact against every stopping law. Uniform geometric rival absorption preserves signed row translations and gives its terminal payoff as a fixed uniform-equilibrium payoff.

## Two complete payoff rows at arbitrary reward scales

Choose two owners $a,b$, and let $z\in[0,1]^2$ be the hazards of the remaining owners, called leaves. Assume only
$$
F_a(q_b=0,z)\le0,\qquad F_b(q_a=1,z)\ge0\qquad\text{for every }z.            \tag{1}
$$
Then the game has a uniform-equilibrium payoff for every choice of the two leaves' entire thirty original reward entries and all signed own-singleton levels. Each condition tests only its own recipient's table. A finite sufficient list is $Z_a(T)\le0$ and $Z_a(T\cup\{a\})\ge Z_a(T)$ on every nonempty leaf subset $T$, together with $Z_b(\{a\}\cup T)\ge Z_b(\{a,b\}\cup T)$ on every leaf subset including the empty set. At $q_b=0$ these comparisons give $Q_a\ge P_a$ and $P_a\le0$, so $F_a\le H_aP_a\le0$. At $q_a=1$ the second guard is itself equivalent to the four displayed pure comparisons, by multilinearity.

Prescribe $a$ to quit surely and $b$ to wait. The two leaves play a mixed Nash equilibrium at a date on which $a$ surely quits and $b$ waits. Each leaf and owner $b$ face certain rival absorption immediately, and the second guard verifies $b$'s incumbent action. If any leaf uses positive hazard, the first guard verifies the sure action of $a$ against every deadline and Never, by the stationary endpoint formula. If both leaves wait, the first guard implies $G_{ak}\le0$ for each leaf $k$ by its first-order term on that singleton ray. Add a stationary leaf hazard $\epsilon>0$ to one such $k$, leaving $a$ sure. The native prescription and immediate offer of $a$ tend to zero while its native Never offer equals $G_{ak}\le0$. Every other player faces $a$ surely and its terminal gains converge to those of the original one-date actions. Every unilateral change faces a positive rival hazard, so all original row constants are paid. The prescriptions approach the same fixed solo-$a$ payoff and terminal regrets tend to zero. For every fixed positive $\epsilon$, geometric absorption uniformly over replacement strategies transfers those terminal bounds to all sufficiently large finite averages. The argument also works with any finite number of leaves, by the same finite-game Nash existence theorem.

**An explicit separation.** Take core $0,1$ and leaves $2,3$, with matching $01,23$. In either core row the native passive payoff is $3$ with the mate present and $-1$ on any other nonempty rival coalition. Joining pays $2$ with the mate and $8$ on every nonempty set without the mate. In each leaf row the passive/quit offers against a singleton mate are $3,2$, respectively. Against exactly one core, with or without the other leaf, the passive payoff is $-1$. Joining changes this by $+1$ when owner 2 faces a present mate or owner 3 faces an absent mate, and by $-1$ otherwise. Against both core owners the passive payoff is $3$, and joining pays $8$ without the mate or $2$ with it. Perpetual play pays zero and the own centered singleton is zero; use $s_i=1$ for the following comparisons. Every required guard holds, and the two leaves facing a single core play matching pennies.

No first-date equilibrium has two sure owners. If a core is sure, its mate strictly waits, and both leaves must mix in their two-player game. If both leaves are sure, owner 3 would strictly prefer to wait, at every mixture of the two core actions. There is no nonempty pure Nash first-exit coalition: with a single core otherwise alone, leaf 3 joins; with a single leaf otherwise alone, either core joins. The all-Never profile is also unstable. The earlier alternating-phase two-free-row test fails for every role assignment. In a tested core row take zero hazards in the scheduled phase and, in the other phase, hazard $1/2$ on the mate and on one leaf, zero on the other. This gives $P_p=5/4$, $Q_p=3$, $H_p=1/4$ and hence $A=5/4>0$, $B=-1<0$ in the earlier implication. In a tested leaf row take the two core hazards near one with no mate hazard: $A\to3$, $B\to-5$. Each row also fails the one-recipient positive-passive condition and its origin-component restriction along the mate-only ray, where $P=3q$, $Q=2q$ and $F=3q-2q^2>0$ for $0<q\le1$. The strict raw certificate and these separations persist on an open set in all sixty original entries. ## Interior core hazards under both endpoint comparisons

There is a further stationary conclusion on the matrix branch relevant to a possible counterexample. Suppose both core recipients satisfy the two strict walls $F_i(q_m=0,z)<0$ for all $z\ne0$, $F_i(q_m=1,z)>0$ for all $z$, with $m$ the other core owner. Assume $G_{im}>0$ and $G_{ik}<0$ for the leaves $k$. At each $z\ne0$ the stationarity residual is a polynomial of degree at most two in the mate's hazard, so these signs force a unique simple crossing in $(0,1)$. At $z=0$ it equals $t(G_{im}-t Z_i(\{i,m\}))$, strictly positive for $t>0$. Uniqueness and compactness extend the two selected mate hazards continuously over the closed square as $b(z)$ with $b(0)=0$. The implicit function theorem gives $Db(0)=B=-G_{CC}^{-1}G_{CL}>0$, with $C=\{a,b\}$, $L$ the leaves.

Put $M=G_{LL}+G_{LC}B$. If $M$ is $R_0$ and the degree of $y\mapsto\min(y,My)$ at zero differs from one, there is an exact stationary equilibrium with both core hazards strictly between zero and one and a positive leaf hazard. Indeed $f(z)=F_L(b(z),z)$ has derivative $M$ at zero. Extend it to the ambient plane using the implicit-function extension near zero. The total degree of $z-\operatorname{proj}_{[0,1]^2}(z-f(z))$ on a disk containing the square is one, by homotoping the projected term to a constant inside the square. Near zero this map is $\min(z,f(z))$, with the same local degree as the linear minimum by rescaling. The extra root supplies the stated profile. If $M$ is not $R_0$, a nonzero complementary $y\ge0$ lifts to $(By,y)$ complementary to $G(By,y)=(0,My)$ and gives the constant singleton path. [Singleton paths](ALL_FOUR_PLAYER_SINGLETON_PATHS_HAVE_A_FINITE_TEST.md)

The reduced-degree test always succeeds when $M$ is $R_0$ and $G$ satisfies the necessary hard-matrix conditions $G\in R_0$, $\det G>0$ and $G^{-1}e_h>0$ for some $h$. In fact $\det M=\det G/\det G_{CC}<0$. If a diagonal of $M$ is negative, choose $d>0$ with its other component large enough that the corresponding singleton is a strict complementary solution at target $-d$. Choose the target generically to avoid ties. Homotope $\min(y,My+t d)$ on a large disk for $0\le t\le1$; $R_0$ and homogeneity bound all roots along this path. The origin contributes $+1$ at the final target, every feasible singleton contributes $-1$ by its negative diagonal, and any full solution contributes $-1$. Hence the degree is at most zero. With no negative diagonal the off-diagonals have the same nonzero sign. If both are negative then $-M^{-1}d>0$ is a full root cancelling the origin. If both are positive, a zero diagonal violates $R_0$. In the only remaining case $M$ is strictly positive. Neither leaf inverse column can then be positive, since the diagonal of the leaf block $M^{-1}$ is negative. For a positive core inverse column with index $i$ and mate $m$, elimination gives $M x_L=-G_{Lm}/G_{im}$ at $x=G^{-1}e_i>0$. Both leaf comparisons to $m$ are negative. Since $M_{kk}=(G_{LC}B)_{kk}>0$ for both leaves and $B>0$, both of their comparisons to $i$ are positive. The entire singleton column $i$ is nonnegative, contradicting $R_0$. ## An exterior theorem with an arbitrary second leaf

For this result assume only that $G_{CC}$ is invertible, $B=-G_{CC}^{-1}G_{CL}>0$, and the Schur matrix $M$ has negative diagonal, positive off-diagonal and negative determinant. The last signs give $R_0$ by direct support enumeration and local degree $-1$: in the homotopy $\min(y,My-s\mathbf1)$, $0\le s\le1$, all roots stay bounded, and at $s=1$ neither a singleton nor the origin is possible. The sole root is the strictly positive $M^{-1}\mathbf1$, with negative active determinant. These singleton conditions hold on the hard branches of the reciprocal pair with distinct dependents and the chain, and for the positive matching with a positive inverse column. [Reciprocal and chain reductions](RECIPROCAL_CORE_AT_RANK_FOUR.md) · [Matched matrix branches](EIGHT_SIGNED_REWARDS_ON_MATCHED_PAIRS_AND_JOINING_TRIPLES.md)

Fix either leaf $i$ and a core member $j$. Designate $J=Z_i(\{i,j\})$ and $R=Z_i(\{i,a,b\})$. Both signs and scales are independent, with $K=\max(|J|,\sqrt{|R|})$. The other leaf's eleven higher entries are entirely arbitrary, even at fixed $K$. Bound the twenty-two core higher entries and the other nine higher entries of row $i$ by $\delta K^{|T|-1}$ at each coalition $T$. Uniformly for $K\ge K_0$ the game has an exact stationary equilibrium with maximum hazard at most $C_0/K$ and third-largest at least $c_0/L_0$, where $L_0=\max(1,\max_{j,|T|\ge2}|Z_j(T)|^{1/(|T|-1)})$. The constants $K_0,\delta,C_0,c_0$ depend only on $G$ and can be taken uniformly on compact subsets of the matrix domain. The pair entry can also be the passive $J=Z_i(\{a,b\})$. Taking a pair and triple in each dependent row gives a four-dimensional exterior with all four rewards independently signed and scaled: choose the leaf attaining the largest weighted scale $K$. When the other forty entries lie in the same weighted error box, $L_0=K$ for large $K$, so at least three hazards are at least $c_0/K$. The existing eight-reward result on positive matchings designates passive pairs in its quadratic terms.

Here are the degree and uniformity details. It suffices to consider any sequence $K\to\infty$ with the thirty-one weighted errors tending to zero. Pass to limits of $(J/K,R/K^2)=(t,u)$, with maximum absolute limit one. Set $x=Kq$, $y=x_L$. On bounded real $x$ sets the scaled core fields $KF_C(x/K)$ approach $G_{CC}x_C+G_{CL}y$ in $C^1$. In the designated leaf row the corresponding field approaches
$$
(Gx)_i-t x_j\sum_{r\ne i}x_r-u x_a x_b\sum_{r\ne i}x_r .
$$
In the passive-pair version replace the quadratic term by $+t x_a x_b$. A passive size-$d$ event contributes $d$ rival factors; a participant event has $d-1$ and the additional $1-H_i$. All other factors and the derivatives of the scaled polynomials are bounded on these sets. The arbitrary leaf's higher payoffs enter none of these three rows.

On any fixed disk in $y$ the exact core zeros admit a differentiable selection $x_C=b_K(y)$, with $b_K(0)=0$ and $Db_K\to B$ uniformly. Indeed $x_C\mapsto x_C-G_{CC}^{-1}KF_C(x/K)$ is a contraction tending in $C^1$ to $By$ on a fixed-width tube around that graph. Thus both selected core coordinates are bounded above and below by positive constants times $\|y\|$ for nonnegative nonzero $y$ in the disk. Each actual reduced leaf row $f_{r,K}(y)=KF_r((b_K(y),y)/K)$ has derivative $M_{r,\cdot}$ at zero for every fixed table, regardless of its higher entries. The local degree of the reduced minimum at zero is consequently $-1$.

On the nonnegative unit directions, evaluating at $x_C=By$ makes $x_j\sum_{r\ne i}x_r$, $x_ax_b$ and $x_ax_b\sum_{r\ne i}x_r$ all strictly positive, even on the coordinate rays. The highest nonzero homogeneous term in the designated row therefore has constant strict sign. Choose a sufficiently large finite radius $D$ so this holds for its full limit on the nonnegative semicircle at radius $D$. The actual $f_{i,K}$ has that same sign for all large $K$ there. If the sign is negative no complementary boundary point exists, and homotoping the designated row to $-1$ gives degree zero on the disk. If it is positive, the only possible complementary boundary point is $D e_k$ for the other leaf $k$. If $f_{k,K}(D e_k)=0$, that point already supplies a root. Otherwise homotope the designated row to $+1$, then the other row to the constant sign of $f_{k,K}(D e_k)$. No boundary zero occurs. The resulting disk degree is zero if that sign is negative and one if it is positive. Since the origin has degree $-1$, every case produces a nonzero root with $\|y\|\le D$. This argument uses no bound on the unselected leaf's field.

At such a root $q_C$ is comparable to $\|q_L\|$. A root cannot have $0<\|q\|\le c/L_0$ for arbitrarily small $c$: there $F(q)=Gq+O(c\|q\|)$ in all rows by the same monomial bounds with $L_0$ replacing $K$. The vanishing core equations give $q_C=Bq_L+O(c\|q_L\|)$, and the leaf complementarity conditions, divided by $\|q_L\|$, contradict $R_0$ of $M$ in the limit $c\to0$. This proves the lower bound on two core hazards and one leaf hazard. The radius argument on each limiting coefficient tuple proves common $K_0,\delta,C_0,c_0$ by contradiction with a sequence having $K>n$, weighted errors $<1/n$, and no solution with maximum at most $n/K$ and third-largest at least $1/(nL_0)$. The positive absorption estimates at the beginning transport the exact profile and its payoff to the original game.

**The grand coalition at the controlling leaf.** Fix $0<\eta\le1$. One may instead take $K=\max_{|T|\ge2}|Z_i(T)|^{1/(|T|-1)}$ over the entire controlling row. Require the normalized grand entry $w=Z_i(C\cup L)/K^3$ and the normalized triple $u=Z_i(\{i,a,b\})/K^2$ to have the same sign and magnitudes at least $\eta$. All nine remaining entries of this row may range over their full weighted scales, and the other leaf's eleven entries are again unrestricted. Only the core entries retain the small weighted bound. The same stationary conclusions follow, with constants depending also on $\eta$. Indeed on a nonnegative leaf direction $v$ of unit norm write $x=(Bv,v)$ and let $k$ be the other leaf. The controlling limiting residual at radius $D$ has quartic term $-D^4 w v_k x_a x_b\sum_{r\ne i}x_r$. Its cubic terms are $-D^3 u x_a x_b\sum_{r\ne i}x_r+O(D^3 v_k)$, since every other cubic event in that row requires the other leaf. The lower terms are $O(D^2+D)$. The common positive factor $x_a x_b\sum_{r\ne i}x_r$ is uniformly bounded below on the closed unit arc. Thus the quartic dominates the $v_k$-proportional error and the triple dominates the lower-degree terms at a sufficiently large fixed radius, including $v_k=0$. This gives the strict outer sign needed in the preceding proof.

For a comparison take mate entries $3$, crossed entries $-1$, core $01$ and leaf 2 controlling. For $n\to\infty$ put $Z_2(012)=n^2$, $Z_2(0123)=A n^3$, $Z_3(0123)=-n^6$ for any fixed $A\ge1$, and set all other native higher entries to zero. The grand/triple case applies with $K=A^{1/3}n$, $\eta=A^{-2/3}$. Every pair joining entry is zero, so no assignment of roles produces a large pair for the one-joining theorem. For the weighted collision criterion, any proper active support places the negative grand reward outside its favorable set, while full support permits only positive inverse-column orientations on this matrix. Its adverse scale is therefore at least $n^2$ in every case, against a favorable scale at most $A^{1/3}n$. The matched eight-coordinate and the single-recipient two-signed criteria also place the negative grand outside their distinguished coordinates. Taking $Z_2(0123)=0$ instead already separates the unrestricted-other-leaf pair/triple theorem from these earlier criteria. For the grand/triple extension itself, arbitrarily large fixed $A$ removes any common small weighted bound on the controlling row's grand entry relative to its triple scale. These comparisons address the quoted sufficient conditions, without claiming that the example escapes every other equilibrium construction. ## Seven strict singleton chambers for the exterior

The exterior applies on the unresolved matrix branch of every surviving strict sign class possessing a core with reciprocal entries of the same sign and both core rows seeing the leaves with the opposite sign. In the published list the canonical masks are
$$
(2,1,1,2),\ (2,1,1,4),\ (2,1,1,6),\ (2,1,8,4),\ (2,1,8,5),\ (2,5,8,5),\ (2,1,9,6).
$$
Checking the six two-element core choices for each of the 21 census witnesses gives eligible cores $13,03,13,03$ or $12,12,23,03$, respectively, in those witnesses' original labels. Only $(2,5,8,5)$ has a negative reciprocal core. [Singleton sign census and positive-axis degree test](TWENTY_ONE_SINGLETON_SIGN_CLASSES_FOR_FOUR_PLAYER_QUITTING_GAMES.md)

Relabel the leaves as $0,1$ and core as $2,3$. For $\varepsilon=1$ or $-1$ write
$$
G=\begin{pmatrix}
0&A&u&v\\D&0&s&t\\-\varepsilon g&-\varepsilon h&0&\varepsilon i\\
-\varepsilon j&-\varepsilon k&\varepsilon l&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad g,h,i,j,k,l>0.
$$
Then $B=((j/l,k/l),(g/i,h/i))>0$ and $\det M=-\det G/(il)<0$. Suppose $G$ is $R_0$ of degree one with positive determinant and some positive inverse column, as required at a counterexample. If leaf axis 0 has a positive inverse column, the first column of $M^{-1}$ gives $M_{11}<0$, $M_{10}>0$. Both core owners see the leaf with sign $-\varepsilon$ and their mate with sign $\varepsilon$. The positive-axis degree test gives $\det G_{023}=-ilM_{00}>0$; the negative determinant of $M$ now implies $M_{01}>0$. Exchanging the leaves handles axis 1. The degree test extends to possibly singular principal triples: $R_0$, its degree and strict inverse-column positivity persist in a neighborhood, and a zero required triple determinant can be perturbed negatively there by changing one axis-row entry with nonzero cofactor. A further generic perturbation would contradict the strict positive-axis degree test.

If a core column is positive, label its index 2. Up to interchanging leaves or core members, the two leaf sign pairs at their core entries in the six positive-core chambers are $(--,--)$, $(--,-+)$ or $(-+,+-)$. For the negative core the type is $(-+,+-)$. A leaf with both core entries negative already has negative Schur diagonal. A leaf with $\varepsilon u>0>\varepsilon v$ has a same-sign reciprocal pair with 3, which sees axis 2 with the opposite sign in both rows. The degree test again forces its Schur diagonal negative. If these arguments cover both leaves, $\det M<0$ forces the off-diagonals to have equal sign, and the positive solution of $Mx_L=(-\varepsilon v/i,-\varepsilon t/i)$ makes that sign positive since at least one right-hand entry is positive.

Otherwise the sole uncovered leaf, labelled 0, has $\varepsilon u<0<\varepsilon v$, while $\varepsilon t<0$ and $M_{11}<0$. The second component of $Mx_L$ gives $M_{10}>0$. If $M_{00}\ge0$, the first component forces $M_{01}<0$. Positive inverse diagonal at 2 means $\det G_{013}=-\varepsilon(Atj+vDk)>0$, hence $D<-Atj/(vk)$. Also
$$
A<-uk/l-vh/i,\qquad v g/i\ge-uj/l .
$$
Since $t/v<0$, substitution yields $M_{10}=D+sj/l+tg/i<sj/l+tjh/(ik)=(j/k)M_{11}<0$, a contradiction. Thus $M_{00}<0$ and the determinant forces $M_{01}>0$. All seven classes satisfy the exterior's matrix hypothesis on their remaining branch. This conclusion concerns the exterior and the stationary hazards it produces. Higher rewards outside both that exterior and the endpoint conditions remain unrestricted conjecture cases.
