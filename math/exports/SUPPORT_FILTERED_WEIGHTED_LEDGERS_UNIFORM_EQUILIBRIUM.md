# Support-filtered weighted ledgers produce uniform equilibria

Ordinary mathematics; no new checked Lean theorem is asserted.

## Exact statement

Let I be finite with |I|>=2. The game has finite real terminal rewards
r_i(S) for every nonempty S, zero live/Never payoff, and nonnegative own
singleton levels s_i=r_i({i})>=0. Strategies are independent private complete
stopping laws; all unrestricted unilateral behavioral deviations are priced.

Let A be a nonempty FINITE index set. For each a in A choose a nonzero
nonnegative recipient weight vector w^a and let M_a={i:w_i^a>0}. Call a
nonempty coalition D safe when

    M_a is NOT contained in D for every a in A.             (1)

Define

    H_a(S)=sum_i w_i^a[r_i(S)-s_i],
    J_a(S)=sum_(i outside S)w_i^a[r_i(S+i)-r_i(S)].

Here S+i means S union {i}. A product root is a vector q in [0,1]^I
of independent current Quit probabilities. Its support is
support(q)={i:q_i>0}, and its coalition probabilities are

    p_q(S)=product_(i in S)q_i product_(i outside S)(1-q_i).

Its forced-Quit payoff Q_i(q) is the expected reward r_i(T union {i}),
where T is the independent opponent quitter set generated by q.

Assume:

1. H_a(S)>=0 for every a and every safe S, AND for every singleton S.
2. J_a(S)<=0 for every a and every nonempty proper S.
3. At every product root q with nonempty SAFE support D, some active owner
   has forced-Quit payoff Q_i(q)<=s_i.

**Theorem.** The infimum of total full terminal Nash debt over actual absorbing
profiles is zero. The original game therefore has a fixed uniform-equilibrium
payoff.

A finite sufficient version of condition 3 assigns to every safe D a
nonnegative vector b^D supported on D, of total mass one, with

    sum_(i in S)b_i^D[r_i(S)-s_i]<=0
       for every nonempty S subset D.                    (2)

Point masses recover a flat participant on each safe support. No condition
of either kind is required on a support containing even ONE M_a.

Only an antichain of distinct weight supports is needed. Delete any ledger
whose support strictly contains another family support, and retain only one
ledger at each repeated support. A coalition contains some original support
if and only if it contains a retained inclusion-minimal support. Thus the
safe domain and its product-low tests are unchanged, while all retained
ledger inequalities continue to hold. This proves that such deletion
preserves every hypothesis and gives a finite support-pattern search.

If every M_a has at least two members, every singleton is safe and the extra
singleton clause in condition 1 is automatic. For a single ledger, the
theorem is exactly the single-support-sensitive criterion:
its only potentially unsafe singleton is its own one-hot support, and that
singleton has H=0 identically. Thus the family theorem is a true extension.

The version with arbitrary singleton supports but without the extra singleton
H clause is not proved here. The clause supplies the simultaneous small-prefix
comparison below; the one-ledger exception alone does not preserve the other
ledgers.


The conclusion means that one vector v exists such that for every epsilon>0
there is an independent behavioral profile and a finite horizon threshold
whose sufficiently long average payoffs are epsilon-close to v and against
which every unilateral behavioral deviation gains at most epsilon. The vector
v is fixed before epsilon; the profile and threshold may depend on epsilon.

## Conjecture-facing change

A positive weighted cap margin rules out every nonsure Nash root activating
all positive-weight owners. With several ledgers, a root is excluded as soon
as it activates the support of ANY one ledger. Hence the preservation and
product-low tests are needed only on the remaining supports. In particular,
proper coalitions may have negative total reward for every nonnegative
weight, and some omitted proper supports may have strictly positive Quit
premiums for every active owner.

The single-ledger version with all weights positive recovers the criterion
that tests H on every nonempty proper coalition and product-lowness on every
proper support. Sparse and simultaneous supports remove those tests on
additional coalitions. The complete raw example below also lies outside the
named previous reward criteria audited there. Arbitrary Fin4 remains open.

## Strategic inputs

The inputs are only the finite reward table and the displayed reward
inequalities. The product-low condition is a quantified one-stage reward
test; condition (2) gives a finite linear sufficient test on each required
support. Neither version assumes a continuation value, profile, cap,
absorption rate, root selection, punishment, or periodic word.

The proof produces an actual absorbing seed, uses finite mixed Nash
existence at each continuation, and constructs arbitrarily small debt in
the closure of ACTUAL absorbing payoff/cap pairs. Closure is used only for
compact minimization: actual absorbing approximants supply the strategies
at the end. No response supremum is assumed attained and no closure point
is executed as a behavioral law. All strategic inputs are produced.

## Definitions and assumptions

At the first nonempty quitting coalition S every player receives r_i(S),
and this reward persists. Before absorption the reward is zero. Literal
Never means all players continue forever and pays zero. Private randomization
is independent. Before stopping the only live public history is that everyone
continued, so a behavioral strategy is equivalent to a private law on the
nonnegative integer dates plus a separate Never label. Against fixed
opponents, a unilateral behavioral replacement has such a complete law too.
Its payoff is an average of the pure finite-deadline and Never payoffs;
their supremum is the FULL behavioral response cap. The proof includes every
finite date, arbitrarily late dates, and literal Never. It assumes neither
a finite calendar nor a public correlation device.

An absorbing profile has probability one of a finite first quitting date.
The terminal debt d_i is the full response cap minus prescribed terminal
payoff. It is nonnegative. Zero infimum of total debt over actual absorbing
profiles supplies terminal approximate equilibria at every positive accuracy.

## Proof

### Full-cap prefix identities and the absorbing carrier

Let K_abs be the closure of pairs (U,B) from actual absorbing profiles,
where U is prescribed terminal payoff and B is the full unrestricted response
supremum. The finite reward bound makes this carrier compact and nonempty.
It retains B>=U. Set d_i=B_i-U_i and E=max_i d_i.

For a product prefix q write

    c=product_i(1-q_i),  alpha_i=product_(j different from i)(1-q_j),
    Q_i=E[r_i(A+i)],  b_i=E[r_i(A)1_(A nonempty)],
    C_i=b_i+alpha_i B_i,
    Delta_i=q_i(C_i-Q_i)_+ +(1-q_i)(Q_i-C_i)_+.

Here A is the random opponent quitter set at the prefix. Complete-law
linearity and the split into Quit now or Continue with an arbitrary old
replacement give

    U_i'=q_i Q_i+(1-q_i)(b_i+alpha_i U_i),
    B_i'=max(Q_i,C_i),
    d_i'=c d_i+Delta_i,
    E'<=cE+max_i Delta_i.                                  (3)

These formulas preserve actual absorption and are continuous in (U,B), so
they preserve K_abs. They do not require attainment of any old response cap.

For each a put L_a(B)=sum_i w_i^a(B_i-s_i). Algebra gives simultaneously

    L_a(B')=cL_a(B)+sum_(S nonempty)p_q(S)H_a(S)
                              +sum_i w_i^a Delta_i.       (4)

A full Nash root of the finite Boolean game with all-Continue vector B has
Delta_i=0 for EVERY owner. It therefore satisfies E'=cE.

Condition (2) implies condition 3 by the exact finite identity

    sum_(i in D)b_i^D q_i(Q_i-s_i)
      =sum_(S nonempty)p_q(S)sum_(i in S)b_i^D[r_i(S)-s_i].

The right side is nonpositive; positivity of every active premium would
make the left side positive. This supplies the stated finite raw adapter.

### Completely one-hot families and a common strict seed

Let F be the set of owners i for which some family weight is supported on
the singleton {i}. If F=I, choose any owner j to quit at date zero and let
everyone else Never. For i different from j, its one-hot joining inequality
at {j} says

    r_i({i,j})<=r_i({j}).

Every response except joining at zero receives the passive singleton reward,
so i cannot improve. Owner j can receive only its singleton s_j by a finite
quit or zero by Never, and s_j>=0. This is an actual absorbing exact Nash
profile. No carrier argument is needed in this case.

Henceforth F is proper. First impose the auxiliary strictness condition

    H_a({j})>0 whenever sum_i w_i^a>w_j^a.                 (5)

The only exception for a particular a is when its weight is one-hot at j;
there H_a({j})=0 identically. Choose j outside F. Condition (5) is strict
for this same j in EVERY ledger. The actual sole-j absorbing profile has

    B_j=s_j,
    B_i=max(r_i({j}),r_i({i,j})) for i different from j.

Thus all L_a(B)>=H_a({j})>0. If its debt E is zero, we are done. Otherwise
fix the positive constants lambda_a=L_a(B)/E at this common seed.

For e>0 define the compact nonempty simultaneous chamber

    C_e={(U,B) in K_abs:
          min_i(B_i-s_i)<=e,
          L_a(B)>=lambda_a E for EVERY a}.                (6)

If K_abs contains a diagonal, actual absorbing approximants already give
the theorem. Otherwise take e sufficiently small that, for every positive
singleton H_a({i}),

    lambda_a e<H_a({i}).                                 (7)

The finitely many tests permit one common e. We prove min_(C_e)E<=e.

### Nontrivial exact roots

At any positive-E point of C_e, every L_a is positive. Consider any full
Nash root q. If some q_i=1, (3) gives a diagonal point of K_abs and the
theorem follows. Otherwise every q_i<1.

If its support D contains some M_a, every positive-weight owner for that
ledger is active and strictly mixed. Dividing its Nash indifference by
alpha_i and summing with w_i^a gives

    L_a(B)=sum_(empty!=S proper)product_(j in S)[q_j/(1-q_j)]J_a(S)<=0,

a contradiction. The zero-weight owners need not be active. Hence every
remaining nonzero root has safe support D.

Condition 3 supplies an active i with Q_i<=s_i, and supported Nash
optimality gives B_i'=Q_i. Every realized nonempty root coalition is a
subset of the safe D and is therefore safe. Conditions 1 and (4) imply,
FOR EVERY a,

    L_a(B')>=cL_a(B)>=lambda_a cE=lambda_a E'.

Thus the root preserves the same simultaneous chamber and strictly decreases
E. This uses each ledger separately. Replacing the family by its sum would
exclude only supports containing the union of the M_a and would lose this
union of exclusions.

### Escape from the uniquely All-Continue root

Suppose min_(C_e)E>e and choose a minimizer. The preceding root exclusion rules out every
nonzero full Nash root. Finite Nash existence leaves All Continue as the
unique original root, so B_i>=s_i for every i. Put

    m=min_i(B_i-s_i),  0<=m<=e<E.

If m>0, choose i attaining it and use a sole-i prefix with small quitting
probability p>0. All other owners' strict Continue margins persist. The
only defect is Delta_i=pm, B_i'=B_i, and

    E'<=(1-p)E+pm<E.

For each ledger separately, (4) gives

    L_a(B')-lambda_a E'
      >=(1-p)(L_a(B)-lambda_a E)
          +p[H_a({i})+(w_i^a-lambda_a)m].                (8)

If H_a({i})>0, (7) makes the bracket positive. Otherwise (5) says this
particular ledger is one-hot at i. Then L_a(B)=w_i^a m>=lambda_a E and
E>m give w_i^a>lambda_a, so its bracket is again positive. All inequalities
hold simultaneously. The singleton may be unsafe because some other ledger
is one-hot there; condition 1 explicitly supplies its H signs for every
ledger. This is the reason for the added singleton guard. The prefix remains
in C_e and contradicts minimality.

It remains that m=0. Let T={i:B_i=s_i}; T is nonempty. Since each L_a(B)>0
and every margin is nonnegative, M_a is not contained in T for ANY a. Thus
T is safe. It contains no owner in F, and (5) consequently gives

    H_a({k})>0 for every a and every k in T.               (9)

Choose k in T. Add a bonus delta>0 to k's Quit action in the auxiliary
finite Boolean game, and select any mixed Nash root q^delta. As delta tends
to zero, all such roots tend to zero: a nonzero subsequential limit would
be an original nonzero Nash root. For small delta all owners outside T
are inactive, by their strict Continue margins. Hence

    support(q^delta) is a subset of T and is safe.

The perturbed root is nonzero and q_k^delta>0. If it were zero, the tied
owner k would profit from its bonus; if q_k were zero at a nonzero root,
that root would also be original Nash. In the ORIGINAL game its defects
are exactly Delta_k=delta q_k and Delta_i=0 for i different from k.

Safe product-lowness supplies an active i with Q_i<=s_i. Original endpoint
indifference at the other active owners and the bonus at k give
min_i(B_i'-s_i)<=delta. Choose delta<=e. All realized root coalitions are
safe, and for small delta p_q({k})>=q_k/2. For every a, (4) yields

    L_a(B')>=cL_a(B)+(H_a({k})/2)q_k
             >=lambda_a(cE+delta q_k)>=lambda_a E'

when delta<=min_a H_a({k})/(2lambda_a). Also 1-c>=q_k>0 and delta<E imply
E'<=cE+delta q_k<E. This original-game prefix preserves C_e, a contradiction.

Therefore min_(C_e)E<=e for every sufficiently small e. Points of K_abs and
their actual absorbing approximants give total full debt tending to zero.

### Weak inequalities and a fixed payoff target

Increase each passive singleton reward by epsilon>0, leaving every own
singleton and participant reward unchanged. For every a and singleton {j},
H_a increases by epsilon(sum_i w_i^a-w_j^a), and J_a decreases by the same
quantity. All larger-coalition H and J values are unchanged. The original
singleton H guard therefore gives (5) simultaneously for the entire family.
Every safe H guard and every joining guard persists.

Forced-Quit payoffs depend only on participant entries, so all safe
product-low tests and certificates (2) are unchanged. The supports M_a and
the set F are also unchanged. Apply the strict-source proof, or its explicit
F=I case, to these nearby tables. Entrywise error epsilon changes each
prescribed payoff and full response cap by at most epsilon, hence each debt
by at most 2epsilon. The strategy space of absorbing profiles is unchanged.
Letting epsilon decrease to zero proves the claimed original absorbing
debt infimum. The terminal-Nash-all-errors endpoint selects one fixed uniform
payoff target; it is not allowed to vary with accuracy.


## Boundary tests

### Finite families cannot always be replaced by one weight

For a four-player algebraic control take w^1=(1,1,0,0) and w^2=(0,0,1,1).
Their supports are 01 and 23. Safe nonempty supports are exactly the four
singletons and four cross pairs 02,03,12,13.

Define a native table Z by the following complete rules:

- At each singleton, its owner receives zero and every outsider receives 1.
- At pairs 01 and 23, every recipient receives -1.
- At each cross pair, participants receive -1 and outsiders receive 1.
- Every triple and the grand coalition give every recipient -1.

Use the actual raw game r_i(S)=Z_i(S)+1/2, with live/Never payoff zero.
Its own singleton levels are +1/2. All calculations below concern the
centered premiums Z and unchanged joining differences.

For each family weight, singleton H is 1 when the quitter belongs to its
support and 2 otherwise. On a cross pair, each ledger sees one participant
at -1 and one outsider at +1, so H=0. Every safe support has a flat
participant. At a singleton, J is -2 when it contains one weighted owner,
and -4 when it contains none. At a cross pair, the sole missing weighted
owner's joining gap is -2. At 01 or 23 the opposite ledger has J=0, and
at a triple the remaining owner's gap is zero. These exhaust all proper J
tests. Hence the family theorem applies.

For ANY proposed single nonzero nonnegative weight v, its support must be
contained in 01 to exempt the strictly negative H_v(01) from the single
theorem's H test. It must simultaneously be contained in 23 to exempt
H_v(23). The two pairs are disjoint, so no such support exists. This rules
out every alternative single weight, not only the two displayed ones or
their convex combinations. In particular any nontrivial convex sum of the
two displayed weights has full support and fails both negative-pair H tests.

This control establishes strict theorem-hypothesis separation. It is already
covered by global participant-flatness (all native participant entries are
nonpositive), and its singleton matrix has determinant -3. It is used only
to test strict inclusion of the hypotheses, rather than additional coverage
beyond the previously established existence criteria.

    Gamma^(-1) = (1/144) [[ 5,15, -3,-35],
                         [13, 3,-15, 17],

#### Every sure-quitter root fails for every actual tail cap

Fix a sure owner i, so q_i=1. Each other owner's current-stage payoff
comparison is Q_j-P_j, independent of the tail. Solving these comparisons
gives the following complete fibers of possible Nash choices by the other
three owners.

| Sure owner | Forced choices of the other owners | Owner i's Continue-minus-Quit value |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | q_1=0, q_3=1, q_2 in [0,1] | 7-6q_2 >=1 |
| 1 | q_0=0, q_2=1, q_3 in [0,1] | 4-3q_3 >=1 |
| 2 | q_1=0, q_0=1/3, q_3=1/2 | (5+B_2)/3 >=4/3 |
| 3 | q_0=0, q_1=1, q_2 in [0,1/2] | 2-2q_2 >=1 |

Here is an exhaustive derivation, including the boundary choices.

Write g_j for the signed Quit-minus-Continue gap in this census, separately
from the nonnegative Nash defects Delta_j used in the proof.

* With q_0=1, player 1 has gap
  -1-2(1-q_2)(1-q_3)<0, so q_1=0. The remaining gaps are
  g_2=2(1-q_3), g_3=2(1+q_2). Hence q_3=1 and q_2 is arbitrary.
* With q_1=1, player 0 has gap
  -1-4(1-q_2)(1-q_3)<0, so q_0=0. Now
  g_2=4(1-q_3), g_3=-2(1-q_2). If q_2<1 the latter forces q_3=0,
  making g_2>0, a contradiction. Thus q_2=1 and q_3 is arbitrary.
* With q_2=1, player 1 has gap
  -1-3(1-q_0)(1-q_3)<0, so q_1=0. Now
  g_0=1-2q_3, g_3=-2+6q_0. The endpoint best-response conditions
  exclude either variable being 0 or 1, so both gaps vanish and
  (q_0,q_3)=(1/3,1/2).
* With q_3=1, player 0 has gap
  -1-6(1-q_1)(1-q_2)<0, so q_0=0. Now
  g_1=1-2q_2, g_2=-6(1-q_1). If q_1<1 the latter forces q_2=0,
  contradicting player 1's choice. Thus q_1=1 and its gap requires q_2<=1/2.

The displayed own deviations then follow by direct substitution into
P_i+alpha_i B_i-Q_i. In the third row, Q_2=-2/3, P_2=1,
alpha_2=1/3. The other rows have alpha_i=0. The lower bound B_i>=-1
proves the strict improvement in every row.

This excludes **every** sure-quitter root for every actual full continuation
cap and its closure, including tails selected differently on different
branches. It does not exclude tails or roots with all q_i<1.

There is also a positive approximation gap near the sure faces. For an arbitrary actual raw profile, let q be its initial product root,
(U,B) its continuation payoff/full-cap pair, and c=product_i(1-q_i).
Let delta_i(q,B) be player i's finite root-game Nash defect with
all-Continue reward B. Exact full-cap prefix semantics gives

    d_i(initial profile)=delta_i(q,B)+c(B_i-U_i)>=delta_i(q,B).

Every B lies in a fixed compact reward box and satisfies B_i>=-1/2.
Subtracting 1/2 from every outcome of this finite root game preserves its
Nash defects and gives the native comparison with cap >=-1. Our census
excludes zero defect on the compact union of sure faces. Thus the
continuous function max_i delta_i has a strictly positive minimum there,
and remains uniformly positive in a fixed neighborhood of those faces.
Consequently arbitrary-tail approximate equilibria cannot have their
initial maximum hazard tend to one. This conclusion allows arbitrary
behavioral continuations; it is stronger than a stationary boundary test.

This comparison includes the implemented instant-punishment consumer, not
only stationary repetition of a chosen root. The predicate
`HasQuittingPunishmentVectorNashRootWithSureQuitter` and the theorem
`quittingInstantPunishmentεEquilibriumExistence_iff_sureQuitterPunishmentVectorNashRoot`
in `UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Classification/InstantPunishmentSureQuitterCharacterization.lean`
characterize that branch by a sure root at the actual punishment vector.
Each coordinate of such a vector is at least the minimum participant
reward, since immediate Quit guarantees that minimum against every opponent
profile. Our comparison excludes every such vector, whether or not its
coordinates are jointly realized by one continuation.

#### Exhaustive stationary-support exclusion

Consider an absorbing stationary profile with hazards q in [0,1]^4,
q!=0. Let U_i be its native payoff and N_i its literal Never response value.
For an active owner with 0<q_i<1, an exact stationary Nash profile must satisfy

    U_i=Q_i,
    F_i:=(1-alpha_i)Q_i-P_i=0.                         (1)

Indeed stationary mixing assigns positive probability to each finite
deadline. Since each such payoff is bounded above by the full cap, exact
Nash requires every supported deadline to attain that cap; alternatively
use the two current-action Bellman comparisons. The sure-quitter census
above removes all q_i=1 cases. It remains to exhaust the fifteen nonempty
positive supports.

**Singletons.** For sole active owner j=0,1,2,3 choose respectively the
inactive blocker i=2,2,0,1. Its prescribed payoff Gamma_ij is respectively
-3,-5,-2,-1, whereas immediate Quit gives q_j Z_i(ij), with pair entry
respectively -1,-1,-1,0. The latter is strictly larger for every 0<q_j<1.

**Pairs.** For support ij, equation (1) gives
Gamma_ij=q_j Z_i(ij). Every pair except 02 has a directed singleton entry
Gamma_ij>0 but participant Z_i(ij)<=0. Pair 02 would require q_2=2 from
-2=-q_2. Thus all six pair supports fail.

**All supports containing 01.** The two weighted owners have Q_0,Q_1<=1.
Every opponent coalition containing 1 pays owner 0 at least 2, and every
remaining opponent outcome, including empty, pays it at least -2. Thus
P_0>=4q_1-2. Similarly P_1>=3q_0-1. Equation (1) implies P_i<=1 and hence

    q_1<=3/4,       q_0<=2/3.                          (2)

Condition on owners 2 and 3 both continuing. The conditional immediate
weighted reward is

    f(q_0,q_1)=2q_0+4q_1-8q_0q_1.

On the rectangle in (2), its vertex values are 0,4/3,3,1/3. Bilinear
interpolation shows f>0 when both variables are positive. Every coalition
containing owner 2 or 3 has H>=0. Since both these owners have hazards <1,
the unconditional immediate weighted reward is strictly positive, and
therefore U_0+U_1>0.

On the other hand, put z_j=q_j/(1-q_j). Divide the active-owner Bellman
identity by the probability that all its opponents continue, then use
U_i=Q_i and Z_i({i})=0. This gives

    U_i=sum_(nonempty S not containing i) z_S[Z_i(S+i)-Z_i(S)],

where z_S=product_(j in S) z_j. Summing for i=0,1 gives
U_0+U_1=sum_(nonempty proper S) z_S J(S)<=0, a contradiction. This
excludes supports 01,012,013,0123, with any zero hazards of the other owners.

**Support 123.** Put a=q_1, b=q_2 and u=a+b-ab. Direct expansion gives

    F_3=2ab-u-u^2.

Here 0<u<1 and u>=max(a,b), so ab<=u^2 and F_3<=u^2-u<0.

**Support 023.** Set x=q_0,y=q_2,z=q_3 and q_1=0. The three active equations
are exactly

    f0=2y-6z+4yz-(y+z-yz)^2=0,
    f2=3x-5z+3xz-(x+z-xz)^2=0,
    f3=(x+y-xy)(-x-y+5xy)+3x-y-xy=0.                  (3)

The elementary inequalities in the elementary calculation below prove (3) has no solution in
(0,1)^3. All nonempty supports and all sure-quitter
boundaries have now been excluded.

#### A positive regret bound throughout the stationary class

The exclusion strengthens from exact roots to approximate stationary
strategies. Write d_i(q) for the full native terminal debt of an absorbing
stationary profile, and E(q)=max_i d_i(q).

**Proposition.** There is eta>0 such that every stationary profile of the
actual game r has E_r(q)>=eta.

Suppose first that absorbing native stationary profiles q^n satisfy
E(q^n)->0. Pass to a convergent subsequence q^n->q*. If q*!=0, the prescribed
absorbing payoff is continuous there: its numerator is a polynomial and its
denominator is 1-product_j(1-q_j)>0. Every finite-deadline response payoff
is also continuous in q. Hence every finite deadline at q* is bounded by
U_i(q*). Native Never is their limit, as proved in the centering argument above, so it is
bounded as well. The limiting profile is therefore an exact native
stationary Nash profile, contradicting the stationary-support exclusion above.

If q*=0, put m_n=sum_i q_i^n>0 and pass to a further subsequence
x^n=q^n/m_n->x in the probability simplex. Bounded rewards imply

    Q_i(q^n)->0,      U_i(q^n)->(Gamma x)_i.

The latter follows because the one-stage reward numerator is
Gamma q^n+O(m_n^2), and its absorption denominator is m_n+O(m_n^2).
The immediate-Quit inequalities Q_i-U_i<=d_i yield Gamma x>=0.

There is also the exact recursion identity

    q_i(U_i-Q_i)=(1-q_i)(1-alpha_i)(N_i-U_i).          (4)

If all opponents have zero hazard, take N_i=0; both sides are zero. In
every case N_i-U_i<=d_i, and (1-q_i)(1-alpha_i)<=m_n. Dividing (4) by m_n
and taking the limit gives x_i(Gamma x)_i<=0. Together with nonnegativity
this is the forbidden nonzero homogeneous complementary vector from
the R0 calculation above. Thus no sequence of absorbing stationary profiles has E->0.

Their infimum regret is consequently strictly positive. By the centering
identity, the same holds for all absorbing stationary profiles of r. The
only remaining stationary profile is all-Never, whose actual regret is
1/2. Taking the smaller of these two bounds proves the proposition.

No numerical value of eta is asserted. The argument excludes every
stationary approximation family, including hazards tending to zero and
limits of exact stationary equilibria in nearby reward tables. It does
not imply a positive unrestricted regret gap: the new H/J producer proves
that nonstationary absorbing profiles attain arbitrarily small debt.
For the reward-closure assertion, changing the finite table by at most
epsilon in supremum norm changes each prescribed payoff and each full cap
by at most epsilon, uniformly over all profiles. Hence it changes each
debt by at most 2 epsilon. The positive stationary gap therefore persists
through a sufficiently small neighborhood of this table.

#### Elementary exclusion of support 023

Work in 0<x,y,z<1 and assume the three equations (3). The first two
polynomials satisfy

    partial_z f0=-6+4y-2(y+z-yz)(1-y)<0,
    partial_x f2=3+3z-2(x+z-xz)(1-z)>0,
    partial_z f2=-5+3x-2(x+z-xz)(1-x)<0.

The middle derivative is positive because x+z-xz<1 gives the lower bound
1+5z>0. We first show that f0=f2=0 forces x>y/2.

For every 0<y<1,

    f0(y,3y/10)=y(20-49y+78y^2-9y^3)/100>0.

Indeed the polynomial in parentheses is at least 20-49y+69y^2,
whose leading coefficient is positive and whose discriminant is negative.
As f0 decreases in z, its zero must satisfy z>3y/10. If y<=4/5, then

    f2(y/2,3y/10)=-y^2(9y^2-96y+76)/400
                 <=-31y^2/2500<0.

The bound uses that 9y^2-96y+76 decreases on [0,4/5] and has value
124/25 at 4/5. Monotonicity of f2 in both variables implies that a zero
with z>3y/10 cannot have x<=y/2.

If y>=4/5, use instead

    f0(y,y/3)=y^2(-4+8y-y^2)/9>0,
    f2(y/2,y/3)=-y(6+7y-10y^2+y^3)/36<0.

The first bracket is increasing on [4/5,1] and is positive at 4/5.
The second is at least 6-3y>0 because y^2<=y. Consequently z>y/3,
and the same monotonicity argument again gives x>y/2.

Finally f3 strictly increases in x on the unit square. Its x derivative
is affine in x, and its endpoint values are

    partial_x f3(0,y)=3(2y^2-y+1)>0,
    partial_x f3(1,y)=1+9y-4y^2>0.

At the necessary lower bound x=y/2,

    f3(y/2,y)=y p(y)/4,
    p(y)=2-11y+18y^2-5y^3.

For y<=1/2, p(y)>=(31/2)y^2-11y+2>0: this quadratic has
discriminant -3. For y>=1/2, put v=y-1/2 in [0,1/2]; then

    p(y)=3/8+13v/4+21v^2/2-5v^3>0.

Therefore x>y/2 implies f3(x,y)>0, contradicting its required zero.
This completes a proof using only polynomial identities and inequalities.



## Adapter and consumer

For a given raw table, choose a finite family of nonnegative weights and
check the H and J inequalities and nonnegative own singletons. On each safe
support either check the quantified product-low reward condition or produce
the finite supportwise linear certificate (2). The prefix and compact-chamber
proof then supplies actual absorbing profiles with max debt arbitrarily
small. Their complete-law realization and full-cap identity supply terminal
behavioral approximate Nash profiles. The named terminal-Nash-all-errors
theorem selects one fixed uniform payoff. Neither normalization nor a reward
shift is required for this adapter. A shift used in a boundary example is
accounted for explicitly and is not an invariance assertion at AllNever.

## Scope and nonclaims

No claim is made that every four-player table admits the weights or the
restricted product-low tests. Negative own singletons are not covered by
this theorem without a separate legitimate reduction. No universal bound on
calendar length or period is supplied. A diagonal of the absorbing semantic
closure gives actual approximate equilibrium; it need not give an actual
exact equilibrium. The stationary exclusion in the example is a restricted
strategy-class comparison, and the example itself HAS uniform equilibrium
by the theorem. The general four-player conjecture remains open.
