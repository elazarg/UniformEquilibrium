# Cyclic subgame-perfect approximation on the remaining quitting-game branch

Work with n≥2 finite players, original bounded terminal rewards r, and zero at Never. Write s_i=r_i({i}), Γ_ji=r_j({i})−s_j and J_ji=r_j({i,j})−s_j for j≠i. For a behavioral calendar let u be its payoff, b its coordinatewise full unilateral best response, and e=max_i(b_i−u_i). A sure head means an original first stage with at least one unit hazard, with its optimality conditions evaluated at the original upper punishment value μ_i in case a sole sure i withdraws. A limiting punishment cap suffices. For each outsider the withdrawal continuation is immaterial because another player is sure.

Suppose the following hold.

* Some s_i>0.
* There is no sure head.
* For each owner i and every t∈[0,1], some j≠i has t J_ji>Γ_ji.

On this class existence of a UE payoff is equivalent to existence, for each ε>0, of a proper finite periodic independent calendar having full terminal regret at most ε after every phase. There are constants b>0 and κ>0 depending only on the raw game. Its period can be chosen as a nonempty concatenation of finite blocks, each having full survival ≥b and, for EACH deleted owner i, opponents' survival ≤1−κ. From any phase, a finite interval ending at a block cut has full survival ≥b² and every deleted survival ≤1−κ (take the current remainder and, if necessary, one more complete block). A prescribed limiting vanishing-regret payoff can also be preserved by allowing a finite prefix followed by a periodic tail.

Under the original singleton-path atom theorem's extra hypotheses giving a single date with actual joint first-exit probability ≥a in every small-regret calendar, each block can instead end in such a date. Every phase of the resulting periodic calendar has a future date (possibly the present phase) carrying conditional joint first-exit probability ≥a. The construction preserves independent private randomization and includes the Never deviation.

## Two elementary uniform bounds

Let d(x) be the original stage conditional joint survival. If initial regret is e and survival to date t is S_t>0, restarted regret is ≤e/S_t. Following the original hazards to t, then switching to a nearly best response, proves this player by player. If S_∞>0, the conditional probability of any future exit tends to zero, each date's hazards tend to zero, payoffs tend to zero and the immediate quit offer to an owner with positive s tends to s. Thus e≥s S_∞, also valid when S_∞=0.

There exist e_p>0, γ>0 such that every first stage of an e_p-Nash calendar has all q_i≤1−γ. Otherwise take e→0, q→q* containing a unit hazard. Every player facing a sure rival in the limit has both endpoint payoffs tending to the head Quit and Wait offers, independently of the tail because its multiplier tends to zero. The mixed payoff is within e of both supported optimum comparisons, so in the limit a positive action probability implies the action is optimal. If there is a unique sure i, its payoff tends to its head Quit offer and waiting now then optimizing is worth at least the head waiting exit contribution plus its no-opponent-exit probability times μ_i. Thus the limiting profile satisfies exactly the sure head inequalities, a contradiction. Approximate upper punishment attainment causes no change to the inequalities.

Put M=max(1,max|r|), and Δ=min_{i,t∈[0,1]} max_{j≠i}(t J_ji−Γ_ji)>0. Choose θ=κ=min(1/4, Δ/(16M)). Take d_0>0 smaller than θ e_p, θs and Δ/4. In any calendar of initial regret ≤d_0 stop just after the first date for which the joint survival drops below θ. The date exists by the S_∞ bound. Its initial reach is ≥θ, so it has all q_i≤1−γ by restart. The full prefix survival is hence ≥b=θγ^n. It is also <θ. Suppose deleted survival for owner i on this prefix exceeds 1−κ. The chance of any outside player attempting to quit within the prefix under the independent complete clocks is <κ. The chance the first outcome is the singleton {i} within the prefix is therefore ≥1−θ−κ, and each U_j differs from r_j({i}) by ≤2M(θ+κ). For j≠i the immediate Quit payoff differs from s_j+q_i(0)J_ji by at most 2Mκ, by coupling away all other opponents. Taking a j attaining the margin gives regret at least Δ−2Mθ−4Mκ>d_0. This proves simultaneous contraction under all deletions, at positive full survival, on one finite block. The properness bound also applies to every earlier date in the block since its reach was ≥θ.

## An actual near return gives a cyclic strategy

Fix h>0, and partition [−M,M]^n in K boxes of sup diameter ≤h. Choose d∈(0,d_0] and an original calendar with e≤b^K d (arbitrarily small initial regrets exist by the terminal characterization). Apply the block bound successively K times. Each block endpoint has original reach ≥b^K, and every intermediate date has reach at least the final endpoint, so all restarts through that endpoint have regret ≤d. Two out of K+1 cut payoff vectors fall in the same box. Let m<n be corresponding date indices and x=u(n); then ||u(m)−x||∞≤h. The strictly positive length word [m,n) consists of full blocks.

For this word let F be the prescribed affine payoff map on terminal continuation values. In row i let T_i be the full finite-word cap map on scalar continuation rewards. At each date the latter chooses the better of Quit and Wait plus the continuation. Composition over the word proves T_i is monotone, is Lipschitz with constant at most the word's deleted survival, and contains every unilateral decision before the endpoint. The prescribed row is Lipschitz with full survival constant. Each constant is ≤1−κ since the word includes a full block. F(x)=u(m). Moreover
    u_i(m) ≤ T_i(x_i) ≤ T_i(b_i(n))=b_i(m) ≤ u_i(m)+d.
The lower bound uses within-word adherence and the upper bound uses monotonicity and the definition of the actual full cap. Thus F and T have unique fixed points u*,b* satisfying
    ||u*−x||∞ ≤ h/κ,   ||b*−x||∞ ≤ (h+d)/κ.
These fixed points are the actual periodic payoffs and caps of the repeated word. Joint and every deleted survival vanish geometrically across periods, uniformly over every stopping strategy of the deleted player; the finite-block truncation tail bounds prove the assertion even for Never.

For every phase l∈[m,n), the original and periodic payoff vectors are F_[l,n)(x) and F_[l,n)(u*) respectively. The original cap is T_[l,n)(b(n)); the periodic cap is T_[l,n)(b*). All these suffix maps are 1-Lipschitz. Using T_[l,n)(x)≤b(l)≤u(l)+d gives periodic regret at that phase ≤d+(2h+d)/κ. This controls every phase simultaneously, even if the unconditional reach of l is very small, because initial e was chosen only after K and b had been fixed. Select h,d sufficiently small for any target ε. Conversely periodic initial small regret for all ε implies UE by the existing terminal selection theorem.

To retain an original limiting payoff, play the finite original prefix [0,m) then the periodic word. The periodic payoffs and caps at m differ from u(m) by at most h+(h+d)/κ; the same suffix-map estimates control every prefix phase as well. This preservation refers to a limiting payoff from the original terminal calendars.

## Optional single-joint-date strengthening

Suppose a,e_a>0 ensure every e_a-Nash calendar has some date with ACTUAL probability of first exit by at least two at least a. End each block immediately after such a date. Its pre-date reach relative to the block start is ≥a. Starting with regret ≤min(e_a,a e_p), it has q_i≤1−γ, as do all earlier dates, so the block survival is ≥aγ^n. Every deleted survival is ≤1−a since every such realized first exit includes a player other than the deleted owner. Conditional on survival from a later starting phase of the same block, the probability of this joint first exit at its final date can only increase: divide the original block mass by the pre-phase reach, a number ≤1. This also covers the very last phase of a whole period (it is the distinguished date), and a new period opens with the next regular block. The periodization proof applies with these constants.

## Scope and nearby work

In the conjecture's residual branch, μ_i≤s_i is already known. If the scalar condition fails at t=0 a nonnegative singleton column gives a nonzero homogeneous complementary solution, a covered branch. At t>0, the stationary comparison B_j=P_j−(1−H_j)Q_j on the sole i axis equals t(Γ_ji−t J_ji); B_i=0. The one-owner stationary-comparison disposition with μ_i≤s_i covers precisely such a supported root, including t=1. Genuine sure heads and all s_i≤0 likewise have original UE dispositions. See math/exports/JOINT_RECIPIENT_AND_REVERSE_DEGREE_UNIFORM_EQUILIBRIUM.md and its sources. The theorem thus covers every table left after these raw exclusions, with no pair guard or singleton-path assumption. It asks for arbitrarily long periods and does not show such periods exist in a hard game.

Earlier finite-menu early-absorption necessity only bounds full reach and allows one exceptional deleted clock and a punishment completion. The verified quantile hierarchy has bounded finite support and an explicit Never atom but only initial best-response comparison. Solan–Vieille (2001), Thm 1.2, produces cyclic subgame-perfect ε-equilibria under extra reward hypotheses; the period production is unconditional on prior UE there. Project singular circuit stitching (Noether RM35) starts with supplied exact common-port circuits and two charged owners. Here every hard-branch sequence of small initial-regret calendars supplies the renewable cuts, simultaneous contraction after deleting any player, and a return by compactness. This is a necessary and sufficient reduction, not a new raw-table existence class.

## Polynomial bound on the physical period

For 0<ε≤1 the periodic word above may be chosen with at most C_G ε^{−(n+1)} physical dates, hence C_G ε^{−5} for n=4. In the base theorem a valid C_G uses only the raw margin Δ, M and n. In particular the properness constant γ, which entered only the required initial accuracy, does not enter the length bound. The absolute-value bound M applies to every possible finite-block continuation x used in this section.

Put L=n+1/θ, since on each threshold block the sum of individual hazards before its final date is at most minus the logarithm of its pre-final survival, at most −log θ. The last date contributes at most n. For the optional joint-date version replace θ by a in L and κ by a in the following formulas. Keep the distinguished final date as an individual cell even when its hazard is small.

Write h=κε/12 and take d≤min(d_0,h) in the previous argument. The returned word contains at most K=ceil(2M/h)^n blocks (subdivide the closed payoff cube into K disjoint sets of diameter ≤h, including the outer boundary). Its full regret at every phase is at most ε/3 before the following compression. Let η=κε/(12M(L+2)).

In each block keep dates of summed hazard >η as individual cells. Within each intervening run greedily form maximal consecutive cells with summed hazard ≤η. Two consecutive maximal small cells in a run together have summed hazard >η. If the run has total hazard A, it therefore contains at most 1+2A/η such cells. There are at most L/η big dates and at most one more run than big dates. Counting the separately kept final date (a split at the last endpoint adds at most one cell) gives at most 2+4L/η cells altogether. Aggregate each cell C to the single-date hazards q*_i=1−∏_{t∈C}(1−q_i(t)). This preserves every player's marginal survival across the cell and hence all joint and deleted survivals across complete blocks. All compressed hazards stay proper. In the guarded case the distinguished final date and its pre-date reach from the beginning of each retained cell are unchanged.

Here is a direct uniform coupling estimate for any suffix beginning at a cell boundary, evaluated at any continuation x in [−M,M] (coordinatewise for the prescribed map). Sample independent complete clocks over the block and aggregate each clock into its cell. For any small cell with total individual hazard R≤η, the chance that two or more different players' clocks are in it is at most R²/2 by independence and a union bound. This estimate also applies to the clocks excluding a prescribed deviator. Conditional on reaching the relevant suffix, sample afresh from its given hazards for this bound. Summing the probabilities over completed small cells costs at most ηL/2. Absent this event, all completed cells produce identical outcomes. At any large singleton date, all original ties are preserved exactly.

For prescribed play this already controls the entire difference by MηL. For a player's pure deadline inside a small cell R, an opponent acting anywhere inside that single cell has probability at most R≤η. Absent that event, quitting at any of its dates and quitting on the compressed date give the same comparison. Conversely choose any date in the cell for a compressed deadline. The endpoint action of waiting throughout the word matches in both models. The differences are bounded by
    E=Mη(L+2)
for every supported deadline in both directions. Full caps are the suprema over such pure deadlines, including waiting to the assigned endpoint continuation, so all compressed suffix cap maps and prescribed payoff maps differ from the corresponding original maps by at most E, uniformly in x.

Take the maximum of the old-versus-new periodic payoff errors across the finitely many block boundaries, row by row. The one-block bound gives D≤E+(1−κ)D, so D≤E/κ. The same argument applies to full cap fixed points. At starts of internal cells, the suffix bound and suffix Lipschitz constant at most one give cell-phase error at most E+E/κ for each payoff and cap. The full subgame regret rises by at most 2E(1+1/κ)≤ε/3. Thus the claimed period bound is
    ceil(2M/h)^n ceil(2+4L/η).
For rational rewards all constants in this base bound have explicit rational upper bounds, using the piecewise-linear one-dimensional minimizations for Δ. The guarded variant may depend on a separately supplied atom constant. No finite table-independent exact period follows.

## Further source comparison

Simon (2007), The structure of non-zero-sum stochastic games, Theorem 3(ii), DOI 10.1016/j.aam.2006.07.002, already identifies a cyclic F_ε-orbit condition under stationary/instant branch exclusions. Literature/Simon2007.lean records the exact conditions in CyclicOrbitCondition and theorem3_corrected_of_firstCrossingExtraction. There the orbit is supportwise locally rational and has positive quitting mass somewhere in the period. The extraction gives no polynomial physical period in error and the definition imposes neither a uniform deletion contraction nor a joint-survival lower bound on elapsed blocks from every phase. Proposition 3 converts generated orbits to some approximate equilibrium, potentially changing the calendar (CyclicOrbitCondition.hasQuitApproximateEquilibria). We do not claim the bare cyclic search principle as new. Solan–Vieille (2001), Theorem 1.2, is stated in Literature/SolanAndVieille2001.lean; it gives cyclic subgame-perfect approximate equilibria under A.1 and A.2. The unrestricted finite-support quantile hierarchy already gives finite initial-regret exclusion queries on every table and the early-absorption packet already gives a positive-singleton equivalence. The distinction here is a polynomial physical-period bound for cyclic full-regret comparisons from every phase, with simultaneous deleted-survival contraction and positive full survival on renewable blocks, and (when applicable) an actual common joint-absorption date witness from every phase.

## A finite payoff graph for the signed residual quitting-game branch

Use the hypotheses and one-block estimates above. Take the computable constants M, κ=θ and L=n+1/θ from its base theorem. No lower bound on full survival appears in the tests.

Fix 0<h≤1 and partition [−M,M]^n into K=ceil(2M/h)^n boxes of sup diameter ≤h. Choose any representative per box, including its outer boundary by a deterministic partition convention. With rational input take a rational M,h and rational representatives. Each point is matched to the representative of its own box. Write V for these K representatives.

At a date q let H_i=∏_{j≠i}(1−q_j), c=∏_j(1−q_j), p_i be its unconditional payoff contribution from all exits under prescribed play, P_i the unconditional exit contribution when i waits (zero for no opponent exit), and Q_i the payoff when i quits. All rewards in these expressions are original. The prescribed operator is z↦p_i+c z. The response operator is z↦max(Q_i,P_i+H_i z). For a finite chronological word use their backwards compositions F_{l,i},T_{l,i} from the suffix starting at date l to the word end; subscript zero means the full word.

Define v→w if there is a proper word of between one and
    D(h)=ceil(2+4LM(L+2)/h)
dates satisfying the following, in every row i:
    ∏_t H_i(q_t) ≤1−κ;
    |F_{0,i}(w_i)−v_i| ≤3h;
    T_{0,i}(w_i) ≤v_i+4h;
    T_{l,i}(w_i)−F_{l,i}(w_i) ≤5h at each suffix l.
Each edge query is independent and semialgebraic. Use a finite disjunction over the possible lengths. A zero date inserted on the right offers an additional forced quit and can increase the cap when w_i<s_i. With the disjunction each query has O_G(h^{-1}) real variables by introducing a pair of continuation values per row and date, including maxima as semialgebraic constraints. The number of vertices is O_G(h^{-n}) and the number of candidate edges is at most K². No bound is asserted on the cost of quantifier elimination.

**Claim.** The residual game has a UE payoff iff the graph contains a directed cycle for every h=1/m, m≥1. Equivalently, it has no UE payoff iff at some such scale there is an integer rank on V strictly increasing along every edge. In a counterexample every sufficiently fine grid has that property.

For necessity choose K consecutive actual blocks by the main theorem's restart argument so every conditional regret through them is ≤h (choose the input regret after K). Their K+1 cut payoff vectors have representatives v_0,...,v_K. In the kth original block ending in actual payoff x and next representative w, ||x−w||∞≤h. Its starting payoff differs from v by ≤h. Thus its prescribed value at w differs from v by ≤2h. Its response cap at x is bounded above by the true cap at the block start since the latter uses the unrestricted response at the block end. At w this gives T_i(w_i)≤v_i+3h. At every internal date T_{l,i}(w_i)−F_{l,i}(w_i)≤3h, since the true conditional regret is ≤h and both maps are 1-Lipschitz. Compress each block by the small-cell construction with η=h/[M(L+2)], giving ≤D dates. Every retained suffix map changes by at most h and every deleted survival stays the same. Thus successive representatives form actual directed edges. Repetition among K+1 vertices supplies a cycle.

Conversely, take any directed cycle and an actual word witnessing each of its edges. Repeat their chronological concatenation. At every block boundary with annotated value v the actual periodic payoff u and true cap b obey
    ||u−v||∞ ≤3h/κ,    b_i−v_i ≤4h/κ.
Indeed take maxima of the errors over all cycle boundaries and invoke the corresponding edge slack and contraction by 1−κ. Geometric vanishing of all deleted survivals identifies the cap recursions with actual unrestricted caps, including Never. At an internal phase of an edge leading to vertex w, its true cap minus payoff is bounded by
    T_{l,i}(w_i)−F_{l,i}(w_i) + 4h/κ + 3h/κ ≤5h+7h/κ,
by monotonicity and 1-Lipschitzness. Thus any graph cycle produces an actual periodic calendar with this full regret bound from every phase. Cycles at arbitrarily small meshes imply a UE payoff by the terminal selection theorem. If there is a positive terminal gap g, any graph with 5h+7h/κ<g is acyclic. Topological sorting gives the rank with integer values 0,...,K−1; any such rank conversely excludes cycles.

Relative to direct all-phase verification of a period of O_G(h^{−(n+1)}) dates, the graph factors the single existential search into independent O_G(h^{−1})-date block queries and finite graph operations. The unrestricted initial-regret quantile clock hierarchy already gives O(h^{−1}) finite-date queries and finite nonexistence obstructions. The graph exposes the strict order on continuation-payoff regions that a counterexample must carry. Simon (2007) Theorem 3 and the proof of finite approximate-orbit closure already use compact return; neither its cyclic condition nor the quantile hierarchy states this priceable block graph criterion. No game has been shown to have an acyclic graph on this residual branch.
