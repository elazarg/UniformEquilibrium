# Prescribed signs and the native limit of the four-player escape obligation

If any four-player quitting game lacks a uniform-equilibrium payoff, the counterexample search can prescribe **which recipients gain at every exit and which lose at every exit**. For each nonempty proper partition into winners \(W\) and losers \(L\), a single native table produces the existing fixed-threshold tail-escape entrance and source-preserving uniform-escape packet under arbitrarily large signed row shifts of that sign pattern. The first marked coalition occurs in unedited play, and every later original restart exceeds the entrance threshold. It suffices to exclude these escaping sources for any one chosen partition. The role-specific limits below describe what has to be excluded.

Native means all own singleton, live and perpetual-Continue rewards are zero. The table \(Z\) has four independent private stopping laws on the natural dates and Never. Its first nonempty quitting coalition absorbs. Fix \(M\ge1\) bounding the native rewards. Let \(U_i,B_i,e_i,N_i\) denote the prescribed terminal payoff, supremum against all unilateral replacements, payoff of the literal Never reply, and original marginal probability of Never. Write \(d_i=B_i-U_i\), \(\Delta_i=B_i-e_i\), \(D_Z=\sum_i d_i\), \(\nu=\prod_iN_i\) and \(N_{-i}=\prod_{j\ne i}N_j\). Form the compact Euclidean closure of these actual native tuples. Finite deadlines escaping to infinity do not change the meaning of the retained Never marginals.

Assume \(A=\inf_{\text{actual absorbing profiles}}D_Z>0\). This premise follows from an arbitrary counterexample. The single-pivot normalization yields a counterexample with one own singleton one and three zero. Subtract one from all nonempty rewards of the pivot recipient. On an absorbing profile this subtraction preserves its regret, as both its prescribed payoff and every finite reply lose one, and the finite-reply supremum dominates literal Never at both nonnegative own levels. This proves the premise for the resulting native table. Positive total-debt infimum and absence of an original uniform-equilibrium payoff are equivalent. Fix \(q=|W|\), positive \(\omega_i\) for \(i\in W\) summing to one, and positive \(c_j\) for \(j\in L\). Add \(a\omega_i\) to every nonempty reward of winner \(i\), and subtract \(Kc_j\) from every nonempty reward of loser \(j\), with zero live and Never rewards. On this original table write \(m(K,a)\) for the infimum of total regret. Consider native carrier points \(X\), with marginal coordinates \(n_i\), and real \(\rho\) satisfying
\[
 \prod_{i\in W}n_i=0,\quad d_i=0\ (i\in W),\qquad
 n_j>0,\quad d_j=n_j\Delta_j\le c_j\rho,\quad
 (1-n_j)(c_j\rho-d_j)=0\quad(j\in L).                 \tag{1}
\]
The minimum \(\rho_*\) of these feasible \(\rho\) exists and is positive. For every scale \(K^{1-1/q}\ll a\ll K\), meaning both successive ratios tend to infinity,
\[
 K m(K,a)/a\longrightarrow\rho_* .
\]
On every sequence within \(o(a/K)\) of the infimum, \(K\nu\to\rho_*\) and every joint native limit attains (1) at \(\rho_*\). Conversely each such minimizing native point arises along actual near-minimizers of that accuracy. Thus all native regret on a losing row comes from its own Never actions. Each loser with positive finite mass has \(d_j=c_j\rho_*\). At least one winner has zero original Never mass. With a single loser it carries all the native debt, which is at least \(A\). Every minimizing point yields a translation of the native table at which all approximate equilibrium first-exit laws tend to perpetual continuation. With a single winner, generic choices of the three losing weights force at least two losing rows to bind and at least one losing recipient to participate with positive probability in the first exit.

## The scaling limit

For arbitrary signed own levels \(s_i\), every finite reply gains \(s_i\), the prescribed payoff gains \(s_i(1-\nu)\), and the literal Never payoff gains \(s_i(1-N_{-i})\). In the native game late finite replies tend to the Never payoff. Therefore
\[
 d_i^{Z+s}=d_i+s_i\nu+[-\Delta_i-s_i N_{-i}]_+ .
\]
This also proves that every such table with some \(s_i>0\) lacks UE under \(A>0\): vanishing original debt would give \(\nu\to0\) and then \(D_Z\to0\). Redirect a smallest Never marginal to a finite date to obtain an absorbing profile. One payoff changes by at most \(2M\min_iN_i\) with its cap fixed, and the other three payoffs and caps change by at most the same bound by coupling. Consequently \(A\le D_Z+14M\nu^{1/4}\).

Let \(Y_K\) denote the game at \(a=0\). At every profile the signed objective is exactly \(D_{K,a}=D_{Y_K}+a\nu\). To seed (1), make all losers play Never. The restricted game on \(W\) has at most three players. Raise all its nonempty rewards by one for those players; it then has a private uniform-equilibrium payoff. The terminal Nash criterion gives profiles with vanishing total regret in that raised game. Their regret is \(\sum_W d_i+q\prod_W N_i\); taking a native accumulation point gives the two winner conditions. All losers have \(n_j=1\), so \(d_j=\Delta_j\) and any \(\rho\ge2M/\min c_j\) works. All winners' native debts tend to zero. The native contribution of played finite deadlines to each loser's debt, \(d_j-N_j\Delta_j\ge0\), is bounded above by original total debt, since all finite replies shift identically while the full cap can exceed the shifted finite cap. In the limit \(d_j=n_j\Delta_j\). The signed finite and Never tests give \(d_j\le c_j\rho\) and \(d_j-\Delta_j+c_j\rho(1/n_j-1)\le0\); substitution gives equality \(d_j=c_j\rho\) when \(n_j<1\). ## Selecting the actual escape at the signed table

For fixed \(K\), put \(g(s)=K m(K,s)\). It is the minimum of the affine functions \(K D_{Y_K}+sK\nu\) on a fixed compact carrier. Hence it is concave, has \(g(0)=0\), and \(g(s)/s\) is nonincreasing. On \([K^{1-1/(2q)},K^{1-1/(4q)}]\) this ratio is bounded above and below uniformly by positive constants, by the preceding limit (or by its seed and repair estimates). This interval contains an increasing number of consecutive windows \([b,16b]\) with disjoint interiors. On some such window its decrease is at most \(\ell/4096\), where \(\ell=g(b)/b\). Write \(d=4b\), \(\kappa=\ell/128\). Minimize
\[
 g(s)-\ell s+\kappa(s-2d)^2/d
\]
on \([d/4,4d]\). The first two terms lie between \(-\ell d/1024\) and zero, so the minimizer \(a\) has \(|a-2d|<d/2\). Smooth lower contact and concavity imply differentiability there, with \(v=g'(a)\ge127\ell/128\), and
\[
 g(a-u)\ge g(a)-vu-\kappa u^2/d \qquad(a-u\in[d/4,4d]).
\]
Every actual sequence approaching \(m=m(K,a)\) satisfies \(K\nu\to v\), by testing each compact minimizer's affine function on both sides. Fix \(0<z<1\). After any date through which the unedited reference has cumulative exit probability at least \(z\), the rescaled conditional Never mass \(w\) of every later restart is at least \(K\nu/(1-z)\); eventually \(w-v\ge9vz/10\). Test this actual tail at the lower price \(a-u\), \(u=3az/4\). Using \(g(a)\le a\ell\) gives
\[

     >(5/8)K m z^2 .                                    \tag{2}
\]
All these later dates have genuine reach at least \(v/(2K)>0\) at each fixed \(K\).

## All finite moves, the Never bonus, and compulsory participation

At a minimizer of (1), put \(\lambda_i=0\) for winners and \(\lambda_j=c_j\rho_*/n_j\) for losers. For this comparison only, pay \(\lambda_i\) for an owner's *entire choice* of Never, including when rivals stop. Its prescribed augmented payoff equals its augmented cap:
\[
 U_i+\lambda_i n_i=\beta_i:=\max(B_i,e_i+\lambda_i).
\]
For winners this is their native equality. A loser with \(n_j<1\) has \(\lambda_j=\Delta_j\); a pure-Never loser has \(U_j=e_j\) and \(\Delta_j=d_j\le\lambda_j\).

Consider a finite timing game with any number of dates, the native exit rewards, and continuation payoff \(\beta\) to everyone if all wait through the block. Its only Nash profile is perpetual continuation through the block. To see this, let \(x\) be any product of marginal laws on those dates and the option to wait through the block. Let \(t_i\) be player \(i\)'s probability of waiting through, \(C=\prod_i t_i\), \(H_i=\prod_{k\ne i}t_k\), and \(P_i\) the passive expected reward for rivals exiting in the block, counting zero if none exit. Prefix the native point by these laws and set \(\lambda'_i=H_i\lambda_i\). Prefixing actual approximants yields the prefixed native carrier point. An in-block deadline has its ordinary payoff. The best finite tail reply and literal Never have augmented offers \(P_i+H_i B_i\) and \(P_i+H_i e_i+\lambda'_i\), respectively. Their maximum is \(P_i+H_i\beta_i\), exactly the in-block payoff for waiting through. The prescribed payoff under the new bonus also equals the prescribed payoff of the block with child \(\beta\), since the tail's prescribed augmented payoff equals its cap. Consequently each new augmented regret equals its finite block regret, including off-path comparisons.

If \(x\) is Nash and \(C=0\), then \(\lambda'_i n'_i=C\lambda_i n_i=0\) for all players and hence native debt vanishes at the absorbing prefixed point, contradicting \(A>0\). If \(0<C<1\), the winners retain zero debt and their product of marginal Never masses remains zero. The losers have \(n'_j=t_jn_j>0\) and \(\lambda'_j=c_j C\rho_*/n'_j\). For any native point and bonus \(\lambda\ge0\), the corresponding augmented regret decomposes into three nonnegative terms:
\[
 d-n\Delta +(1-n)(\lambda-\Delta)_++n(\Delta-\lambda)_+.
\]
Vanishing implies (1) at \(C\rho_*\), contradicting minimality. A one-date Nash equilibrium exists, so blank is its unique equilibrium and \(\beta_i\ge0\).

This rules out even asymptotic equilibrium with positive limiting exit probability in the original infinite quitting game whose nonempty rewards are \(Z_i(T)-\beta_i\) and whose Never payoff is zero. Indeed suppose original profiles have total regret tending to zero and exit probability bounded below. Truncate each profile by moving all finite marginal mass after a sufficiently late date to Never, changing every prescribed payoff and deviation cap by \(o(1)\) by coupling. Add \(\beta_i\) to every outcome. Against a truncated profile, a finite reply later than the truncation gives own singleton zero when all rivals wait through, while waiting through in the finite game gives \(\beta_i\ge0\). Thus its finite-block regrets tend to zero. Prefix these blocks as above and pass to limits of the prefixed native tuples and all marginal survivals. The limiting augmented regrets vanish by continuity. If \(C\to0\) all prescribed bonus payments tend to zero, giving an absorbing zero-debt native limit. Otherwise \(0<\lim C<1\), and the limiting tuple satisfies (1) at \((\lim C)\rho_*\). Both conclusions are impossible. AllNever itself is Nash in the translated game, so its set of limiting approximate-equilibrium first-exit laws consists exactly of perpetual continuation.

Take a single winner \(p\). Write \(\Gamma_{jp}=Z_j(\{p\})\) and \(J_{jp}=Z_j(\{p,j\})\) for distinct owners. The least feasible coefficient when all losers play only Never in the native limit equals
\[
 \rho_{\rm solo}=\min_{0\le h\le1}\max_{j\ne p}\frac{(hJ_{jp}-\Gamma_{jp})_+}{c_j}.
\]
In this sector \(p\) absorbs, each outsider's prescribed and Never payoff equals \(\Gamma_{jp}\), and an actual approximant's opening hazard of \(p\) tends on a subsequence to some \(h\). Testing the opening and Never gives the lower bound. Conversely make only \(p\) act with stationary hazard \(h>0\). Every finite deadline of \(j\) interpolates between \(hJ_{jp}\) and \(\Gamma_{jp}\); its full cap is their maximum. For \(h=0\), take a limit of positive hazards, retaining \(n_p=0\).

Choose the weights so the positive numbers \(-\Gamma_{jp}/c_j\) are pairwise distinct. No minimizing native point can lie in this solo sector. Otherwise \(\beta_p=0\) and \(\beta_j=\Gamma_{jp}+c_j\rho_*\), so at most one other coordinate is zero. With only one zero, a small solo hazard of its owner gives a nonblank Nash row. With two zeros \(i,j\), write \(L_{ij}=J_{ij}-\Gamma_{ij}\). If either \(L_{ij}\le0\) or \(L_{ji}\le0\), a small solo hazard of the corresponding rival leaves the zero recipient willing to wait, and the other two players strictly wait. Otherwise lower the two zero continuation coordinates to \(-\delta\), keeping the positive ones fixed. In a fixed neighborhood of the blank row the two positive owners strictly wait, uniformly for small \(\delta\). Each of the two others then strictly prefers Quit, since its Wait-minus-Quit difference is \(-\delta(1-x_j)-L_{ij}x_j<0\). No equilibrium there can approach blank. Existence and compactness yield a nonblank Nash equilibrium at the original \(\beta\), a contradiction in every case.

Consequently \(\rho_*<\rho_{\rm solo}\). Compactness gives a uniform positive lower bound on the largest losing finite mass \(1-n_j\) over all native minimizers. Such a losing owner must also participate positively in the original first exit. Coupling its clock to Never on the same rival trials gives \(|U_j-e_j|\le2M\Pr(j\text{ belongs to the first quitting coalition})\). At the limit \(U_j-e_j=(1-n_j)c_j\rho_*/n_j\). This proves the positive uniform participation bound, after extracting its label if necessary, on every sufficiently accurate intermediate-price minimizing sequence. The losing owner need not be a member of the particular marked coalition supplied at each fixed price.

At least two different losing constraints bind at every minimizing point for these generic weights. If two losing players have finite mass, (1) already forces both equalities. Otherwise let \(j\) be the sole such loser and write \(n=n_j\in(0,1)\). Along actual approximants make the pivot surely finite and the other two losers surely Never by moving \(o(1)\) marginal masses. Keep \(p\)'s law and \(j\)'s conditional finite law, but fix a slightly smaller Never mass \(n'<n\) for \(j\), passing again to a native limit. Against an absent \(j\), all unilateral payoff offers and the prescribed payoff of \(p\) are zero. Hence \(p\)'s regret scales by \((1-n')/(1-n)\) and stays zero in the limit. The offers of \(j\) stay fixed and its prescribed conditional finite payoff still equals its finite cap, since \(d_j=n\Delta_j\) and \(n<1\). It now binds at \(\rho'=n'\Delta_j/c_j<\rho_*\). Each remaining loser is pure Never and its debt varies by at most \(4M|n'-n|\) by coupling. Were both of these constraints initially slack, they would remain feasible for sufficiently close \(n'<n\), contradicting minimality. Thus every limiting optimal source has at least two positive native debtor rows, each attaining its weighted allowance.
