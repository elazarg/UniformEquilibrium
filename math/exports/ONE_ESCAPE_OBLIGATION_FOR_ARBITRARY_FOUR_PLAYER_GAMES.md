# One escape obligation suffices for the four-player quitting-game conjecture

**Theorem.** If any signed four-player quitting game has no uniform-equilibrium payoff, some fixed four-player game admits a genuine \(\texttt{FinFourUniformEscapePacket}\) of the source-preserving completion atlas. It is produced directly from the earlier fixed-threshold \(\texttt{TailEscapeSubsequence}\) entrance, with all later restarts exceeding its prescribed threshold. Its source coalition \(T\) can be chosen with at least two participants and a strictly positive net participant surplus defined below. At each of the retained marks, play of the **unedited reference profile** quits exactly at \(T\) with probability bounded away from zero. Its all-Continue continuation through that mark and every later date have original reach bounded away from zero, and every such later restart has total debt larger than the same table's global infimum by one fixed positive amount. The full forced-pair, payer and singleton targets retain their required literal post-mark spine.

In fact there is an open ball in the full 60-dimensional space of original reward tables on which every table admits both stated packets, so a rational or generic table can be chosen there.

The conclusion about the completion packet and positive reach holds at the **original** table, with no change of reward entries, whenever at least one compact minimizing joint law has positive probability of Never. The source law is chosen to maximize that probability among joint minima. No uniqueness or differentiability assumption is needed for this same-table statement. It asserts a positive escape floor; the numerical threshold of the earlier tail-escape entrance uses the table selection below.

Consequently it suffices to exclude these uniform-escape packets, or to prove that every table admitting one has a uniform-equilibrium payoff. A separate consumer for minimum-return packets is unnecessary for this existential reduction. It is still possible for other table or source choices to admit minimum-return packets. This is a reduction of the universal conjecture, whose escape obligation remains open.

All games here use zero live and Never rewards. Private behavioral randomization is independent. A profile has four stopping laws on natural dates and Never; the first nonempty quitting coalition determines the terminal payoff. Every unilateral deviation may replace the owner's full law. A uniform-equilibrium payoff is a fixed target before the tolerance; the terminal debt criterion used below is the original game's equivalent all-tolerances condition.

## 1. The source coalition and its physical row

Fix a four-player table without an original uniform-equilibrium payoff. Write \(M\ge1\) for a common bound on absolute rewards, with zero Never payoff, \(s_i=r_i(\{i\})\), \(U,B\) for the prescribed terminal value and unrestricted unilateral cap, and \(D=\sum_i(B_i-U_i)\). The infimum \(m=\inf_\sigma D(\sigma)>0\) is over all original behavioral profiles. For a nonempty coalition put
\[
 J(T)=\sum_{i\in T}(r_i(T)-s_i)-(|T|-1)m.
\]
Set \(b=\min\{m/4,m^2/(32M)\}\), \(h=\min\{1/2,b/(4M)\}\) and \(c=bh/(352M)>0\).

**Actual source statement.** Let \(p\) be any original calendar with \(D(p)\le m+\varepsilon\), where \(0\le\varepsilon\le\min\{b/8,bh/4\}\). Let \(t\) be its *first* date with conditional probability of any exit greater than \(h\). This date exists. The sum of the previous conditional exit probabilities is at most \(4\varepsilon/b\), the calendar reaches \(t\) with probability at least \(1-4\varepsilon/b\), and its actual restart there still has total debt at most \(m+\varepsilon\). At this very date some coalition \(T\) of size at least two has \(J(T)>b/2\) and conditional probability at least \(c\). In particular the unconditional probability of precisely that coalition's first exit on this date is at least \(c/2\).

On the *same* date, either the literal next continuation has debt at least \(m+cm/2\), or a player can gain at least \(cm/16\), evaluated from the beginning of \(p\), by changing only its action at \(t\) to a pure endpoint and then following the original continuation. The date, coalition and gain are all statements about the actual calendar; the benefited player may differ from the quitting participants.

**The near-minimum cap bound.** At any tail with \(D\le m+b\) each cap satisfies \(B_i-s_i\ge m/4\). For a direct check prefix an ordinary mixed Nash root of the finite game with continuation \(B-m\mathbf1/2\). Let \(z\) be the probability of any exit at that root, \(q_i\) its hazards and \(H_i=\prod_{j\ne i}(1-q_j)\). Raising the continuation back to \(B\) makes a prescribed Quit lose at most \(mH_i/2\) per unit of its assigned weight, and never makes a prescribed Wait worse relative to Quit. Thus the new total debt is at most \((1-z)(m+b)+(m/2)\sum_i q_iH_i\le(1-z)(m+b)+(m/2)z\), since the disjoint singleton probabilities sum to at most \(z\). Every genuine prefix has total debt at least \(m\), so \(z<1\) and \(z/(1-z)\le2b/m\). At a proper Nash root each player can Wait. Its Wait inequality gives \(B_i-s_i\ge m/2-2M(1-H_i)/H_i\ge m/2-4Mb/m\ge m/4\).

In fact on the *entire same neighborhood* one has
\[
 B_i-s_i>m+b\qquad\text{for all }i.                         \tag{1}
\]
If \(B_k-s_k\le m+b\), prefix just \(k\) repeatedly with fixed hazard \(u=m/(16M+2m)\). Each other player \(j\)'s Wait advantage against its cap is at least \((1-u)m/4-2Mu=m/8\); the owner strictly prefers Wait since its cap exceeds \(s_k\). Its cap is unchanged by this prefix. The exact total-debt update is consequently \((1-u)D+u(B_k-s_k)\le m+b\), so induction never leaves the stated neighborhood. Each outsider's successive cap is the affine update \(v\mapsto u r_j(\{k\})+(1-u)v\), and hence \(r_j(\{k\})\ge s_j+m/4\) for every outsider. This contradicts the necessary full \(R_0\) singleton matrix. Indeed if column \(k\) of \((r_i(\{j\})-s_i)_{ij}\) had no negative entry, its unit vector would solve the nonzero homogeneous complementarity problem. This argument uses no saturation or common selection across reward tables.

**Deleting small rows.** At a parent with \(D\le m+\varepsilon\), the immediate Quit value \(Q_i\) is at most \(s_i+2Mz\), where \(z\) is its row's conditional exit probability. If \(z\le h\), \(1\) implies \(B_i-Q_i>m+b/2\); the cap chooses Wait. Write \(D^+\) for debt on the actual next continuation. The exact cap recursion gives \(D=(1-z)D^++\sum_iq_i(B_i-Q_i)\ge(1-z)D^++z(m+b/2)\). Thus \(D^+\le D-(b/4)z\), since \(D\le m+b/8\). Telescoping before the first large row proves the asserted bound on cumulative absorption, and the union bound gives its reach. If all dates were small, the entire sum of their conditional exit probabilities would be finite. The tail probabilities of a future exit and therefore the tail prescribed values would tend to zero. But at every such restart \(U_i\ge B_i-D>s_i+b-\varepsilon\). Some \(s_i>0\), since otherwise AllNever is an exact equilibrium. This proves existence of the claimed first date.

**The participant inequality at the physical row.** At the date just found let \(P(T)\) denote the conditional first-exit-at-this-date probabilities, and \(z=\sum_{T\ne\varnothing}P(T)>h\). For every player's root at the *child cap*, the cap defect contains at least its prescribed Quit weight times the gap from that action to the parent cap. Consequently
\[
 D\ge (1-z)m+\sum_iq_i(B_i-Q_i).
\]
The elementary identity \(\sum_iq_iQ_i=\sum_{T\ne\varnothing}P(T)\sum_{i\in T}r_i(T)\) uses independence at this date. Using \(1\) and \(D\le m+\varepsilon\) gives
\[
 \sum_{T\ne\varnothing}P(T)J(T)\ge b\sum_i q_i-\varepsilon
      \ge 3bz/4 .                                             \tag{2}
\]
Singletons contribute zero and \(J(T)\le8M\). The conditional mass of coalitions with \(J(T)>b/2\) is therefore at least \(bz/(32M)\). There are eleven nonsingletons, giving a single qualifying \(T\) with probability at least \(c\). Every member of \(T\) has hazard at least \(c\). With at least two such members, every player's opponents have survival \(H_i\le1-c\) at this row.

For the claimed physical edit, take the root regret \(e_i^U\) of replacing the current mixed action by the better pure endpoint *at the prescribed child payoff* \(U_i^+\). Improving the continued branch from \(U_i^+\) to its full cap \(B_i^+\) raises the maximum of the two endpoints by at most \(H_i d_i^+\), where \(d_i^+=B_i^+-U_i^+\). Hence \(d_i\le e_i^U+H_i d_i^+\) and
\[
 m\le D\le \sum_i e_i^U+(1-c)D^+.
\]
If \(D^+<m+cm/2\), some \(e_i^U\ge cm/8\). Changing precisely that player's action at this date and using its original behavior thereafter realizes the gain multiplied by the original reach, at least \(1/2\). A root defect evaluated against the child *cap* would not alone justify this edit.


The full-debt prefix identity used here is implemented as `quittingTerminalSemanticDebtSum_prefix_eq_continueMass_mul_add_capDefect` in `UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Classification/LCP/ThreeCore/CapDebtBellmanReduction.lean`. The singleton \(R_0\) conclusion is `finFour_singleton_r0Degree_eq_one_of_no_uniformPayoff` in `UniformEquilibrium/Diagnostics/Quitting/FinFourSingletonDegreeCriterion.lean`.

## 2. A same-table criterion and a universally available table

At a fixed counterexample table let \(H\) be the compact closure of actual simultaneous payoff, cap and whole outcome-law tuples. Among its points of minimum total debt take a point \((z,\mu)\) maximizing the Never coordinate, and write \(v_*=\mu(\mathrm{Never})\). Suppose \(v_*>0\). For every \(\xi>0\), compactness yields \(\alpha(\xi)>0\) such that every actual profile with \(\nu\ge v_*+\xi\) has \(D\ge m+\alpha(\xi)\), with \(\nu\) its true joint Never probability. If the closed slice is empty, any positive constant suffices.

Let actual reference profiles satisfy \(\nu(p_n)\to v_*\). Whenever their probability of absorption through a selected date is at least a fixed \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), survival \(S_j\) to any later date \(j\) satisfies \(v_*/2\le S_j\le1-\lambda\) eventually. The true conditional Never probability at every such date fulfills
\[
 \nu(\operatorname{Spine}(p_n,j))=\nu(p_n)/S_j
        \ \ge\ \nu(p_n)/(1-\lambda)
        \ >\ v_*+v_*\lambda/[2(1-\lambda)].
\]
Thus every such later restart has a uniform positive objective excess and positive unedited reach. The key source requirement is convergence of the actual unedited reference profiles' Never probabilities to the chosen maximum, which Section 4 verifies.

Start from any hypothetical four-player counterexample. The checked single-pivot normalization, `nonempty_finFourSinglePivotNormalization_of_no_uniformPayoff` in `UniformEquilibrium/Diagnostics/Quitting/FinFourSinglePivotNormalization.lean`, produces from any hypothetical counterexample a game \(R\) with one positive singleton equal to one, three zero singletons, and still no uniform-equilibrium payoff. Its full total-debt infimum is strictly positive by `quittingTerminalDebtSumInf_pos_iff_not_exists_uniformEquilibriumPayoff` in `UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Terminal/TerminalDebtSumInf.lean`. Subtract one from the pivot recipient's reward on each nonempty coalition, leaving zero live and Never payoff. The resulting game \(Z\) has all own singletons zero. On every almost surely absorbing profile all four debts are unchanged: the pivot's prescribed payoff and each finite pure response decrease by one, while the finite-response supremum already dominates literal Never in both games. For the latter, late finite responses differ in the limit from Never by the own singleton times the probability of opponents' joint Never. Thus
\[
 A=\inf_{\text{actual absorbing profiles}}D_Z>0 .
\]

Write \(M_Z=\max(1,\max_{i,S}|Z_i(S)|)\). In \(Z\), at any actual profile let \(N_i\) be player \(i\)'s original Never mass and \(\nu=\prod N_i\). Redirecting its smallest marginal Never mass to any finite date gives an actual absorbing profile. That player's prescribed payoff changes by at most \(2M_Z\min_i N_i\) and its cap is unchanged. Each of the three other payoffs and caps changes by at most the same bound by coupling, uniformly over pure responses. Consequently
\[
 A\le D_Z+14M_Z\nu^{1/4}.
\]
For \(0<t<A/16\) raise every reward on a nonempty coalition by \(t\) for all recipients, producing a game \(r_t\), also written \(r\) for fixed \(t\). Every finite response gains \(t\), every prescribed payoff gains \(t(1-\nu)\), and the finite-response supremum dominates literal Never in both games by the same late-deadline argument. Thus
\[
 D_r=D_Z+4t\nu,\qquad \eta=(A/(28M_Z))^4 .
\]
Either \(D_Z\ge A/2\) or \(\nu\ge\eta\). Hence \(m=\inf D_r\ge\min(A/2,4t\eta)>0\), a true counterexample table by the same total-debt criterion. AllNever gives \(m\le4t\), so \(D_r\le m+A/4\) implies \(D_Z\le 4t+A/4<A/2\) and \(\nu\ge\eta\). Thus every such raised game is a genuine counterexample with the displayed Never floor.

A joint minimum exists by compactness, and the uniform lower bound on actual near-minimum \(\nu\) passes to it. Thus even an arbitrary choice \(0<t<A/16\) supplies the positive-Never hypothesis for the same-table criterion. On this constructed table the reach to every date of any near-minimizing unedited profile is at least \(\eta\).

## 3. A quantitative choice of the raise

The freedom to select \(t\) gives the precise floor needed for the earlier tail-escape entrance. Put \(K=\overline{\{(D_Z(p)/4,\nu(p)):p\text{ actual}\}}\) and \(f(s)=\min_{(x,v)\in K}(x+sv)\), so the actual raised minimum is \(m(s)=4f(s)\) for \(s\ge0\). In the own-zero native game AllNever is exact and \(f(0)=0\). Set \(v_0=\min\{v:(0,v)\in K\}>0\), using the absorbing repair bound of Section 2. Then \(0\le f(s)\le v_0s\) for \(s\ge0\) and \(f(s)/s\to v_0\) as \(s\downarrow0\): minimizers have \(x\le v_0s\), so all cluster points have \(x=0\) and \(v\ge v_0\).

Choose \(\kappa=v_0/128\) and \(a>0\) sufficiently small that \(4a<A/16\) and \(\sup_{0\le s\le4a}|f(s)-v_0s|<\kappa a/4\). Minimize \(f(s)-v_0s+\kappa(s-2a)^2/a\) on that interval. Its minimizer \(t\) lies in \((3a/2,5a/2)\), since at \(2a\) the objective is nonpositive and outside this inner interval it is positive. Consequently \(f\) has a smooth quadratic lower support at the interior point \(t\). By concavity its two one-sided slopes agree with the support slope \(v=v_0-2\kappa(t-2a)/a\ge127v_0/128\). For every \((x,w)\in K\) minimizing at \(t\), \(f(t+d)\le f(t)+wd\) for increments of either sign, hence \(w=v\). All actual near-minimizing sequences on this one raised table have \(\nu\to v\). We also have, for \(0\le u\le t\),
\[
 f(t-u)\ \ge\ f(t)-vu-\kappa u^2/a.                       \tag{3}
\]

Write \(m=4f(t)>0\). Fix any \(z\in(0,1)\). On any actual near-minimizing sequence, at a date by which the cumulative probability of exit is at least \(z\), the joint Never probability \(w\) of every strictly later restart is at least \(\nu(p)/(1-z)\). Hence \(w-v\ge(9/10)vz\) eventually, uniformly over those dates and restarts. The same statement holds for all actual profiles in some sufficiently small fixed objective neighborhood depending on \(z\), by compactness of \(K\). The payoff identity for every actual continuation gives \(D_{r_t}/4\ge f(t-u)+uw\). Insert \(u=tz\) in (3). Since \(\kappa t/a\le5v_0/256\), \(v\ge127v_0/128\) and \(m\le4v_0t\), we obtain
\[
 D_{r_t}(\text{every later restart})-m
 \ \ge\ 4t z^2[(9/10)v-\kappa t/a]\ >\ \tfrac34\,m z^2 .  \tag{4}
\]
The unedited probability of reaching any of these restarts is at least \(\eta\). In particular on every actual minimizing sequence the first substantial row from Section 1 has a funded coalition and reach tending to one. Use \(z=h/2\) in (4) for a permanent, table-uniform excess after that very row.
