# Every four-player quitting game passes the third interlaced order test

Every signed four-player quitting game passes the exact rank-three test for terminal obedience. On every native table (all four own singleton payoffs zero), one may prescribe **any** player whose training and validation labels must all be finite. If even one foreign singleton payoff is weakly nonpositive in native units, one can require **all four** players' labels to be finite. The other 44 rewards are arbitrary. In particular the 56-dimensional, reward-independent convex hull for absorbing native play contains zero at rank three for every collection of nonnegative, observation-dependent test weights. The corresponding unrestricted 60-dimensional signed hull also contains zero. The earlier universal constructions covered ranks one and two. A counterexample needs a failing level of at least four. [Order test and equivalence for all levels](../notes/CODEX_ROOT__INTERLACED_ORDER_TESTS_FOR_ARBITRARY_FOUR_PLAYER_GAMES.md)

At level $N$, each owner has $N$ training labels and $N$ validation labels on a common ordered set of dates, with finite ties and a separate Never. At every cut the two counts for that owner must differ by at most one. An order prior is obedient if, conditional on each positive-probability training observation, the prescribed validation payoff is at least the offer from every observed date, literal Never and Late. To score a validation payoff or offer, draw one validation label uniformly and independently for each clock used. Late waits through every finite opposing date and receives the owner's singleton payoff if no opponent quits. A prior is only a witness for a finite test. Private approximate equilibria of arbitrary accuracy require feasibility at unbounded ranks.

## Filling a continuation without observing the two hidden dates

Let $Z$ have zero own singletons, and let $w$ be a vector attainable as the limit of native prescribed payoffs on four individually finite private clocks whose complete unilateral caps converge to $w^+=\max(w,0)$ coordinatewise. These clocks can have finite support at each accuracy: move the mass beyond a cutoff to a later finite date and bound every payoff and pure response change by coupling the clocks. Give the original finite exits rewards $r(S)=Z(S)-w$ and perpetual play zero. Assume some $k$ has $s_k=-w_k>0$, some distinct $j$ has $s_j=0$, and all other own rewards are nonpositive. For $N\ge3$ suppose
$$
 r_k(\{j\})\le -(N-1)(N-3)s_k. \tag{1}
$$
Then the **all-four-finite native test** passes at this $N$.

Put $h=1/(N-1)$. In the signed game, on two successive hidden dates let $k$ and then $j$ alone quit with independent probabilities $h$, with any surviving owner going to Never. Write $v$ for the resulting payoff. Player $k$ receives $h s_k+(1-h)h r_k(\{j\})$. Never pays $h r_k(\{j\})$ and Late adds $(1-h)s_k$ to this. Subtracting Late from the prescription gives $(2h-1)s_k-h^2r_k(\{j\})\ge0$ by (1). For $j$, prescription, Never and Late agree because its own level is zero and it moves last. Each other owner is prescribed Never; Late adds its nonpositive own payoff multiplied by rival survival. Thus both tests are dominated for every owner.

Prepend one observed date. At that date use an ordinary mixed Nash equilibrium $q$ of the one-stage game with continuation $v$ when nobody quits. The product clocks on this head and the two hidden dates now dominate the three tests at the observed date, Never and Late. Indeed the head Quit offer is a stage action. The latter two offers are at most the stage Wait offer by the just-proved tail comparisons conditional on rival survival. Give owner $i$ exactly $K_i=\min(N,\lfloor Nq_i\rfloor+1)$ training labels at this head, with no training label on either hidden date. Write $F_i(t)$ for the true unconditional distribution function. An independent systematic validation grid has cumulative count $\lfloor NF_i(t)\rfloor$ or $\lceil NF_i(t)\rceil$ at each cut, and a uniform choice from that grid has exactly the physical clock law. These counts differ from $K_i$ by at most one through both hidden dates. Indeed if $a=\lfloor Nq_i\rfloor\le N-2$, then $N(q_i+(1-q_i)h)\le a+2$ by substituting $(a+1)/N$ for $q_i$ in this increasing expression. The endpoint $a\ge N-1$ follows from the count bound $N$. This head construction extends the earlier signed two-owner test to the two inactive players' nonpositive own levels. [Earlier hidden-tail calculation](../notes/CODEX_ROOT__INTERLACED_ORDER_TESTS_FOR_ARBITRARY_FOUR_PLAYER_GAMES.md#a-two-owner-hidden-tail-and-unbounded-first-failure-levels-on-a-hypothetical-counterexample-kernel)

In native payoff units the preceding experiment has default $w$ on perpetual play. Replace each old Never draw by that owner's independent clock from one fixed approximating native continuation for $(w,w^+)$. All continuation dates are after both hidden dates. On earlier rival exits a new post-cut test receives the same passive payment as the old Late and Never tests. Conditional on all rivals reaching the continuation, its offer is at most $w_i^++\varepsilon$. The previous two tests have respective conditional values $0,w_i$. Consequently the better of those two old tests dominates **every newly observed date**, up to $\varepsilon$. The original head test and prescribed payoffs change by at most $\varepsilon$, because on reaching the continuation its prescribed payoff is within $\varepsilon$ of $w$ and old finite deadlines are unchanged. The new literal Never and Late are also controlled by the continuation cap. All comparisons are against the physical product laws.

It remains to exhibit the interlacing without disclosing the hidden dates. Keep $K_i$ labels at the head and none on the two hidden dates. From the first fresh continuation date onward set the deterministic cumulative training count to
$$
 T_i(t)=\max\{K_i,\lfloor N F_i(t)\rfloor\},
$$
where $F_i$ now denotes the full appended physical law. These counts are nondecreasing, reach $N$ at a finite date, and differ from every independent systematic validation count by at most one. Indeed $K_i\le\lfloor N F_i(t)\rfloor+1$ throughout. The training observation is deterministic, so a test at any of its ranks earns its actual physical offer after averaging over the validation shifts. The resulting all-finite priors have obedience excess tending to zero. At fixed $N$ the space of weak orders is finite and the unnormalized conditional obedience inequalities are closed. A limiting prior passes exactly. This construction uses the physical law of the head experiment; an arbitrary feasible prior at a translated default need not supply such a law.

## All four-player singleton matrices at rank three

Write $G_{ij}=Z_i(\{j\})$. First take strict off-diagonal signs. If the native absorbing infimum of total regret is zero, absorbing approximations exist with each prescribed finite owner, and with all four owners finite if there is a weak blocker anywhere. Their quantile grids pass at every rank on the corresponding faces. Otherwise the zero-column translation produces a signed counterexample on this kernel, so it has one of the 21 strict sign types in the census and every row has a blocker. [Prescribing and completing clocks](ZERO_SINGLETON_COLUMN_EQUIVALENCE.md) · [Strict sign census](TWENTY_ONE_SINGLETON_SIGN_CLASSES_FOR_FOUR_PLAYER_QUITTING_GAMES.md)

In twenty of the classes there is a column $j$ with one negative recipient $k$ and two positive recipients. The singleton default $w=G_{\cdot j}$ is attainable with caps $w^+$ on four finite clocks by the zero-column theorem: the translated all-Never prescription gives precisely this pair in native default units, and row $j$ has a blocker for four-clock completion. The signed game has just one positive own level, $s_j=0$ and $r_k(\{j\})=0$. This gives (1) with $N=3$.

In the remaining class each player has a mutually positive mate and both crossed comparisons are negative. A positive absorbing infimum implies $\det G>0$ and $G^{-1}e_k>0$ for some $k$. Remove the mate of $k$ to form a triple $S$. The established matched default takes $z$ proportional to $-G_{SS}^{-1}e_k>0$ on $S$, with sum one, and zero outside. Then $w=Gz$ has $w_k<0$, the two other coordinates on $S$ zero, and the omitted mate coordinate positive. A diffuse independent race on $S$ realizes the required finite-clock payoff/cap limit $(w,w^+)$. Since $z_k>0$ and $G_{kk}=0$, at least one of the two neutral helpers $j\in S\setminus\{k\}$ has $G_{kj}\le w_k/(1-z_k)<w_k$. Thus $r_k(\{j\})=G_{kj}-w_k<0$, which meets (1) at rank three. [Matched default and inverse-column calculation](ZERO_SINGLETON_COLUMN_EQUIVALENCE.md#two-families-with-one-positive-own-singleton)

This proves the prescribed-owner test for every strict native table and its four-finite version whenever a blocker exists. Passing any of these fixed-rank tests is closed in the rewards by compactness of the finite-dimensional order simplex. Approximate a boundary table by strict ones, preserving a chosen weak blocker as a negative comparison when required. Finally, all-four-finite native orders remain obedient under every signed row translation, since after any single replacement there is still a finite rival. If there is no blocker anywhere, the four-player normal-core criterion already gives a uniform-equilibrium payoff for every signed translation, hence a passing unrestricted prior. This proves all of the stated rank-three assertions, including the convex-hull versions by finite separation.

The continuation argument also gives an explicit higher-rank range in the matched class. For the given $w,k$, it suffices that either neutral helper satisfy $G_{kj}\le(N-2)^2 w_k$, which is (1) in native units. For example, take all mate entries one and all crossed entries $-c$, $0<c<1/2$. Then $w_k=-2c^2/(1+2c)$ by the two neutral row equations. At $N>3$ the bound holds for $c\le1/[2((N-2)^2-1)]$, independently of all higher-coalition rewards. This passes rank four for every $0<c\le1/6$. Outside these bounds the same tests can still pass by other constructions. Universal rank four and the unbounded-rank conjecture remain open.
