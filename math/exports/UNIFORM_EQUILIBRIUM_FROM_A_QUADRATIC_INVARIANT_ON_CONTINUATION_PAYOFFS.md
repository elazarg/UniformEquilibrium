# A quadratic invariant for absorbing four-player equilibria

Label the four players modulo four and put $m(i)=i+2$. Fix a symmetric invertible hollow matrix $G$ with $G_{i,m(i)}>0$ and all other off-diagonal entries strictly negative. Assume $G^{-1}$ is strictly positive entrywise. For a nonempty coalition $A$ let $w_0(A)=1_{0\in A}$. For each $j\ne0$ let $w_j(A)=1$ if $j\in A$ and $m(j)\notin A$, and zero otherwise. Thus 0 has priority over 2 when both quit, whereas 1 and 3 cancel each other when both quit. Take the centered reward table

$$Z^0(A)=G w(A).$$

There is an open neighborhood of this table in **all 56 independent native reward coordinates** on which an exact absorbing native terminal Nash equilibrium exists. In particular the infimum $A_*$ of total native terminal regret over absorbing profiles is zero. Every choice of the four signed own singleton levels there admits a uniform-equilibrium payoff. This result includes an open family on which stationary profiles cannot approximate equilibrium on the unit raise, no sure opening is possible at any bounded continuation, and the continuous singleton path test fails. Each player uses an independent private stopping law on natural dates and a separate Never outcome. A nonempty first quitting coalition pays its reward, and ongoing play pays zero. A deviation can replace a player's entire law. All proofs here are ordinary mathematics.

## Backward invariance for actual Nash rows

Use the fixed reference matrix $G$ even when all rewards vary. At a product row $q$ write $C=\prod_i(1-q_i)$, $r=1-C$ and put $x_0=q_0$, $x_j=q_j(1-q_{m(j)})$ for $j\ne0$. At a child payoff $v=Gy$ the centered reference parent is $G(Cy+x)$. The forced Quit offer $Q^0_i(q)$ sums $G_{ij}x_j$ over the two crossed rivals $j\notin\{i,m(i)\}$, with an additional $G_{20}x_0$ precisely for $i=2$. For any Nash row, including sure and boundary rows, a positive $x_i$ implies $i$ is active and its prescribed payoff equals its true Quit offer. Symmetry gives the exact scalar comparison

$$2x^\top Q^0-x^\top Gx=-2\sum_{i<j,\ \{i,j\}\ne\{0,2\}}|G_{ij}|x_ix_j\le0.$$

Suppose $\|Z-Z^0\|_\infty\le\epsilon\le1$. With $M=1+\max_{i,S}|Z_i^0(S)|$, begin at a terminal vector $v_0=Gy_0$ of norm at most $M$. Every parent in any backward chain of Nash rows then has norm at most $M$ by convexity. At a genuine Nash row for the perturbed table write $u=Gy'$ for the parent. One has

$$y'=Cy+x+b,\qquad \|b\|_\infty\le\|G^{-1}\|_\infty\epsilon r.$$

Put $E(y)=y^\top Gy$. Since $G$ is symmetric,

$$E(y')=C^2E(y)+2(x+b)^\top u-(x+b)^\top G(x+b).$$

For $x_i>0$, the true Quit offer equals $u_i$ and differs from $Q_i^0$ by at most $\epsilon$. Each $x_i\le r$. The preceding scalar comparison and uniform bounds therefore give a constant $D$, depending only on $G,M$, with

$$E(y')\le C^2E(y)+D\epsilon r.\tag{1}$$

Also $|x|_1\ge h r$ for some $h>0$ uniformly on all Nash rows at all $\|v\|_\infty\le M$ and all sufficiently small perturbations. At blank, as $q\to0$, the ratio tends to one, since $r=\sum_i q_i+O((\sum_i q_i)^2)$ and $|x|_1$ has the same expansion. At the reference table, if $x=0$ and $q$ is nonblank then $q_0=q_2=0$ and owners 1 and 3 quit surely. In the presence of its sure mate, owner 1's joining gain is strictly negative, equal to $-G_{13}$: all crossed contributions cancel and waiting selects the mate. This precludes every such Nash row independently of the continuation. Compactness and closed one-row Nash inequalities give the uniform bound by contradiction. Decrease $h$ if necessary so $h\le1$.

Choose $y_0=t(e_0+e_1)$ with fixed $t>0$ sufficiently small that $\|v_0\|_\infty\le M$ and, for each $i$, $v_{0,i}$ exceeds by a fixed margin the native punishment obtained by scheduling one hostile rival surely at the first date and everyone else at Never. The cap against that punishment at the reference table is $G_{ij}<0$, since both joining and waiting give this value. It remains strictly below $v_{0,i}$ for nearby rewards. Here $y_0\ge0$ and $E(y_0)<0$.

Iterating the affine recursion gives $y_i\ge-\|G^{-1}\|_\infty\epsilon$, because $y_0\ge0$ and $r=1-C$. It also gives $\sum_i y_i\ge h_0:=\min(2t,h/2)>0$ for sufficiently small $\epsilon$, using $|x|_1\ge hr$. All these $y$ are bounded since their $Gy$ are. Finally (1) gives $E(y)\le D\epsilon$ throughout, since $C^2+1-C\le1$. These bounds imply there is a $\gamma>0$ such that $\min_i(Gy)_i\le-\gamma$ throughout each chain for all sufficiently small perturbations. Otherwise a limit as $\epsilon\to0$ would yield $y\ge0$, $\sum_i y_i\ge h_0$, $Gy\ge0$ and $E(y)\le0$. The inverse positivity gives $y=G^{-1}(Gy)\gg0$ and hence $E(y)>0$. On this compact set of child continuations a Nash row has $r$ uniformly bounded below. In fact a converging sequence with $r\to0$ would give blank Nash at a limit continuation with a negative coordinate, where immediate Quit pays zero.

## Exact native play and signed rewards

Starting at $v_0$, prepend an arbitrary Nash row repeatedly, which is always possible in the finite game. The resulting length-$n$ private block is Nash against all deadlines and waiting through at $v_0$ by backward induction. Write $r_0>0$ for the uniform lower bound on one-row absorption. At every physical date $t$ in the block the prescribed survival through that date is at most $(1-r_0)^t$.

Read the blocks in physical order and select a diagonal subsequence on which every fixed date's hazards converge. The resulting infinite calendar absorbs surely, by the same survival bound. Its native payoff $u^*$ is the limit of the block payoffs: the first $t$ dates converge and the bounded contribution beyond them is at most $M(1-r_0)^t$ in each calendar. Every fixed unilateral deadline is also priced correctly on taking this limit, since only its finite physical prefix enters. A native deadline tending to infinity yields the literal Never payoff by bounded convergence: if any rival stops, it eventually stops before the deadline; when all rivals choose Never, a lone finite quit and perpetual play both pay zero. These comparisons exhaust mixed stopping laws. Thus the infinite calendar is an **exact absorbing native terminal Nash equilibrium**. It stays exact at every nonnegative row raise because the prescribed absorbing payoff gains the whole row constant and a deviation gains at most that constant.

For arbitrary signed row levels the same fixed target $u^*$ shifted by the levels is a uniform-equilibrium payoff. In each approximating block on the chosen subsequence take an owner $k$ of smallest individual survival, at most $s^{1/4}$ if $s$ is total survival. After the block schedule the designated hostile rival of $k$ surely at the next date and all three remaining owners surely on the following date. For $k$ the full native cap does not increase: the tail cap is less than $v_{0,k}$. For any other owner the probability all its opponents survive the block is at most $s^{1/4}$ because they include $k$, so its full cap increases by at most $2Ms^{1/4}$. All prescribed payoffs change by at most $2Ms$. In particular $A_*=0$, with exponentially decaying terminal regret as the block length grows. Every player now faces a rival surely finite by date $n+2$, even under unilateral replacement. Consequently every signed row shift leaves all regrets exactly unchanged. For a fixed repaired block the discrepancy between terminal and finite-average payoffs tends uniformly to zero over these replacements, because every outcome absorbs by $n+2$. Selecting a block with small regret and its prescribed payoff close to $u^*$, then taking all sufficiently long horizons, proves the fixed-target claim. This argument imposes no quantitative convergence rate to $u^*$ on the chosen subsequence. ## A finite test for more general reward columns

The same construction has a finite algebraic test independent of the matching formula. Choose an invertible hollow singleton matrix $G$ and, for each nonempty $S$, a nonnegative vector $b^S$ supported on $S$, with $b^{\{i\}}=e_i$. Set $Z^0(S)=G b^S$ and $M=1+\max|Z^0_i(S)|$. Choose a positive diagonal $D_0$, a symmetric matrix $H\le D_0 G$ entrywise, and $\theta\ge0$. Require $z^\top Hz>\theta$ at every simplex point with $Gz\ge0$. Give a nonzero $y_0\ge0$ with $y_0^\top H y_0\le\theta(\sum_i y_{0,i})^2$, $\|Gy_0\|_\infty\le M$, and for each $i$ a rival $j$ with $\max(G_{ij},Z^0_i(\{i,j\}))<(Gy_0)_i$.

Write $x=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}\Pr_q(S)b^S$ and $L(x)=\sum_i x_i$. For every $q$ which is a one-date Nash row at the reference table for **some** $v\in[-M,M]^4$, impose

$$q\ne0\Longrightarrow x\ne0,\qquad 2x^\top D_0Q^0(q)-x^\top Hx\le\theta L(x)^2.$$

All inputs and tests concern finitely many real variables, with each one-date best-reply inequality polynomial. They imply the same open-neighborhood and signed-level conclusion. In the matching example use $D_0=I$, $H=G$, $\theta=0$, and the seed and punishments constructed above.

Indeed $x_i>0$ still implies that owner $i$ is active; also $\|x\|_1=O(r)$ and $L(x)/r\to1$ at blank. On nearby Nash rows $L(x)\ge hr$ by compactness. Their normalized violation of the second test, divided by $r$ on nonblank rows, tends uniformly to zero from above as $Z\to Z^0$. Near blank its numerator is $O(r^2)$ since own singletons vanish. Away from blank this follows by compactness and continuity of one-date Nash inequalities. The affine recursion and its $O(\epsilon r)$ residual still imply $y_i\ge-O(\epsilon)$ and $L(y)\ge h_0>0$ on every backward chain. For $E_\theta(y)=y^\top Hy-\theta L(y)^2$, expansion about $Cy$ now gives

$$E_\theta(y')\le C^2 E_\theta(y)+2x^\top D_0u-x^\top Hx-\theta L(x)^2+O(\epsilon r).$$

In fact $x^\top H y'\le x^\top D_0Gy'+O(\epsilon r)$ by $H\le D_0G$, $x\ge0$ and $y'_i\ge-O(\epsilon)$. The omitted cross term $-2\theta C L(y)L(x)$ is nonpositive. On the support of $x$ the parent $u$ equals the perturbed Quit offer. The normalized violation estimate therefore gives $E_\theta(y')\le C^2 E_\theta(y)+o(1)r$, uniformly through every chain. As before, $E_\theta(y)\le o(1)$, whereas a limiting nonnegative $y$ of positive total with $Gy\ge0$ gives $E_\theta(y)>0$ by the simplex test. This proves the absorption bound, and the preceding diagonal and terminal punishment arguments apply unchanged.

## A calibration beyond stationary and continuous singleton profiles

Take $G_{i,m(i)}=3$ and $G_{i,i\pm1}=-1$. After swapping mate columns the result is $3I$ minus the adjacency matrix of a four-cycle; its inverse is strictly positive by the geometric series. Set $a_{20}=1+d^2$, put $a_{i,m(i)}=c$ in the other three matched directions, and use $a_{i,i+1}=1-d$, $a_{i,i-1}=1+d$ on crossed pairs. For distinct players $i,j$, $a_{ij}$ is the factor that $i$'s presence applies to $j$'s weight. Use the native reward

$$Z_i(A)=\sum_{j\in A}G_{ij}\prod_{k\in A\setminus\{j\}}a_{kj}.$$

Choose $c>0$ sufficiently small, then $d>0$ sufficiently small for that fixed $c$. These tables are in the open family just proved. They retain singleton differences $G$ and have participant pair rewards $J_{ij}=a_{ij}G_{ij}$. We prove a strict stationary regret floor on the original unit raise $1+Z_i(A)$.

For a stationary row write $H_i=\prod_{k\ne i}(1-q_k)$ and $b_{ij}=q_j\prod_{k\ne i,j}(1-q_k+a_{kj}q_k)$. Then $Q_i=\sum_j G_{ij}a_{ij} b_{ij}$ and the expected passive head reward is $P_i=\sum_j G_{ij} b_{ij}$. When $H_i<1$, the sign of immediate Quit minus literal Never is the sign of

$$F_i=(1-H_i)Q_i-P_i=\sum_{j\ne i}G_{ij}b_{ij}[(1-H_i)a_{ij}-1].\tag{2}$$

Consequently $q_i>0$ requires $F_i\ge0$, with equality if $0<q_i<1$. For a row with a sure rival, $F_i$ is also the ordinary one-stage joining gain at every continuation, since $H_i=0$.

Suppose some $i\ne0$ quits surely. Its mate $m$ strictly waits: $a_{mi}=c$, $b_{mi}\ge(1-d)^2$, and the negative matched gain dominates the two crossed terms of size $O(d)$. Its successor $s=i+1$ then strictly waits. The only remaining rivals of $s$ are $i$, whose contribution is negative of order $d$ since $a_{si}=1+d$, and its own mate. The latter contribution is nonpositive except when $s=2$, in which case it is at most $O(d^2)$. Here $b_{si}$ is bounded below independently of the remaining hazard because the sure owner's own mate waits. The predecessor $p=i-1$ then faces only $i$ and strictly joins since $a_{pi}=1-d$. But $i$ facing only the sure $p$ strictly waits.

If instead 0 is sure, for $i=1$ or $3$ one has $b_{i0}\ge(1-d)$ and $b_{i2}\le c(1+d)$. Thus 1's adverse crossed term from 0 exceeds its favorable crossed gain from 2; its mate term is nonpositive. This forces 1 to wait. Against 3 the two crossed signs are reversed, and its mate is now absent, so 3 quits surely. This is the previously excluded case $i\ne0$. Consequently no Nash row is sure at any continuation. For each bounded continuation set this exclusion persists near the chosen table by compactness.

At $d=0$, consider any proper nonblank stationary equilibrium with at least two active owners. Put $x_j=q_j\prod_{k\ne j}(1-q_k+a_{kj}q_k)$, so its payoff is $(Gx)_i/(1-C)$. For every $i$, $Q_i\le(Gx)_i$: the hostile factors equal one, and the mate's positive contribution is weakly reduced when $i$ is forced to quit. Equality holds when the mate is absent. An active $i$ must be indifferent. If it has no active mate, this gives $(Gx)_i=0$ while its only active rivals are hostile. If the support is just one matched pair, each active gets $(Gx)_i>0$, inconsistent with indifference since $C>0$. In full support the same inequality forces $Gx\le0$, impossible since $G^{-1}>0$. These exhaust the supports closed under mates. A lone actor cannot be the proper nonblank limit of stationary Nash rows at $d\downarrow0$ if its limiting hazard is strictly below one: a crossed inactive opponent has prescribed value $-1$ and immediate offer $-q_i>-1$.

A sequence of nonzero proper stationary roots at $d\downarrow0$ must therefore tend to blank or to the sure boundary. At blank, normalizing total hazards to a simplex vector $z$, all inactive inequalities and active equalities from (2) give $Gz\ge0$ and $z_i(Gz)_i=0$. Indeed $F_i=-(Gq)_i+O(\|q\|_1^2)$ uniformly, and if only one owner acts its positive-join predecessor strictly improves. Since $G^{-1}>0$ these complementarity conditions are impossible: a nonzero $Gz\ge0$ would give $z\gg0$ and hence $Gz=0$. If $q_i\to1$ for $i\ne0$, its mate is absent eventually: the gain (2) for the mate has a strictly negative matched term even in the limit. Whenever an active owner $i$ has no mate, faces some rival and $H_i>d/(1+d)$, all its crossed terms in (2) are nonnegative and some is strictly positive. Thus for a proper root $H_i\le d/(1+d)$; a crossed hazard must approach one along a subsequence. If neither of the two approaching one is owner 0, their mates both wait eventually. The only active owners are a crossed pair $i,j$, whose two equalities in (2) require $q_ja_{ij}=q_ia_{ji}=1$, impossible because one factor is $1-d$.

It remains to exclude $q_0\to1$. If $q_2=0$, the same argument gives a crossed hazard approaching one; its mate waits, reducing again to the impossible two-owner case. Take $q_2>0$. If one of $q_1,q_3$ has a positive limit, its mate waits eventually: the negative matched term persists while $H_i\to0$ for those two owners. If $q_1=0$ and $q_3$ has a positive limit, the only terms in $F_3$ are from 0 and 2. Here $b_{30}\ge1-o(1)$ and $b_{32}\le c+o(1)$ because the senior 0 suppresses 2. The total is strictly positive, consisting of nonnegative $H_3(b_{30}+b_{32})$ plus $(1-H_3)d(b_{30}-b_{32})>0$. If $q_3=0$ and $q_1$ has a positive limit, then
$$F_2=(1-H_2)(3d^2 b_{20}-d b_{21})-H_2(3b_{20}-b_{21}).$$
Both terms are nonpositive and the first is strictly negative: $b_{20}\to1$, while $b_{21}$ is bounded away from zero and at most $1+o(1)$. This contradicts $q_2>0$.

Finally suppose $q_1,q_3\to0$. Here $b_{20}\to1$, $b_{21}+b_{23}=o(1)$. From $F_2\ge0$ it follows
$$H_2(3b_{20}-b_{21}-b_{23})\le(1-H_2)(3d^2 b_{20}+d(b_{21}+b_{23}))=o(d).$$
Thus $1-q_0=o(d)$ and $H_1=o(d)$. In $F_1$ the matched term is nonpositive; the crossed terms sum to $H_1(b_{10}+b_{12})+(1-H_1)d(b_{12}-b_{10})<0$ because $b_{10}\to1$ and $b_{12}\le c+o(1)$. This forces $q_1=0$. The preceding strict $F_3>0$ then forces a sure 3, again impossible. This proves nonexistence for all sufficiently small strictly positive $d$ at fixed $c$.

At each such $c,d$ an approximating sequence of stationary profiles on the unit raise likewise cannot exist. A nonsure nonblank limit with at least two active owners is a stationary root by continuity of all stationary prescribed payoffs and deadline or Never tests wherever each recipient has an absorbing rival. With one limiting actor $i$, its predecessor's prescribed value tends to $-1$ natively and its immediate offer tends to $-(1-d)q_i>-1$. A sure limit gives a sure Nash row at a bounded subsequential continuation by the unit-raise one-step comparisons: these compare a deviation only at the first date followed by the old profile. Centering these comparisons is legitimate at a nonblank limit with sure play, since every nonblank stationary prescribed profile absorbs and the one-step deviations followed by it also absorb. At a blank limit the exactly blank unit-raised profile already loses one. Otherwise its prescribed native payoff tends to $Gz$ and immediate Quit gives $Gz\ge0$. Whenever $0<z_i<1$, its native Never payoff tends to $(Gz)_i/(1-z_i)$, forcing $(Gz)_i=0$. For $z_i=1$ the same conclusion follows from the zero diagonal. This contradicts the same complementarity condition. Thus the stationary regret infimum is positive. Its positivity is open by the uniform Lipschitz estimate for payoffs and all complete deviation menus.

All singleton columns of $G$ have a negative entry. Each reciprocal pair has the same strict sign, so the directed sign graph contains no arcs and the continuous singleton triangle and quadrilateral tests fail. All three-by-three principal determinants are nonzero: each triple contains one positive pair and two negative pairs, with nonzero product. The full matrix is nonsingular as above. Both members of either matched pair have strictly positive joining rewards, so they give a proper supported row with strictly positive Quit offers for both participants at arbitrarily small hazards. Thus the local low-offer condition and the all-active-positive participation premise in the positive-offer welfare theorem fail. No pair has two positive joining **increments** $J_{ij}-G_{ij}$: the matched pair 13 has two negative increments, 02 has opposite signs, and each cross has opposite signs. The proper-pair-at-the-positive-minimum test therefore fails at every labeling. These strict failures persist on a neighborhood. The proof does not require a prescribed period or an assumed form for the global minimum.

The payoff-domain orientation criterion also fails on the support $S=\{0,2\}$. The two positive active Quit numerators are $J_{02}z_2$ and $J_{20}z_0$ at any positive odds. The indifference map on this support is $\Phi=(L_{02}z_2,L_{20}z_0)$, where $L_{02}=3(c-1)<0<L_{20}=3d^2$. Its determinant is $-L_{02}L_{20}>0$, violating the required nonpositive orientation for a pair. At small odds its signed payoff projection lies in the reward hull: mix the origin, the singleton of 1 with projection $(-1,-1)$, and the singleton of 0 with projection $(0,3)$. Positive small coefficients produce the displayed negative and positive entries. The canonical first-action domain always contains zero at the blank action and the orientation test uses an open neighborhood, so this same pair also violates that adapter. The determinant, positive offers and interior of the projected triangle are stable under perturbation. The supportwise low-or-charge and negative-orientation producers likewise fail on that pair: its two members can both earn strict Quit premiums and its two joining increments have opposite signs. All these are comparisons with named tests.

For the support-filtered weighted-ledger theorem, the mandatory singleton tests require every nonzero candidate recipient weight $w\ge0$ to satisfy $w^\top G\ge0$. Then $w^\top=(w^\top G)G^{-1}$ is strictly positive. Thus both matched pairs are safe supports under any finite family of such weights. Their two positive joining rewards violate the required low-offer condition. This argument persists when the independent singleton entries vary with strictly positive inverse.

At the calibrated tables, and in a neighborhood of each one, the very same infinite calendar constructed by diagonalization is an exact terminal equilibrium for every signed row raise. To see this, the actual rows of the limiting calendar are Nash at their bounded children, by finite comparisons and the uniform prescribed-survival bound also from every fixed cut. No owner quits surely at any one date, so every finite cut has positive reach. If all rivals of some $k$ had positive Never probability, their total remaining finite mass conditional on a sufficiently late cut would tend to zero. Prescribed play still absorbs surely there. Its conditional payoff would approach the singleton column of $k$. But $i=k-1$ has $\min(0,J_{ik})>G_{ik}$, a strict inequality retained under perturbation of the actual singleton and pair entries. Its immediate Quit offer at the cut is at least $\min(0,J_{ik})-o(1)$ since all other rivals apart from $k$ have vanishing conditional hazard. This contradicts its conditional Nash comparison. Thus every owner faces a surely finite rival, and every response receives the same whole signed shift. Moreover the rival survival tail bounds the time to absorption uniformly over that owner’s deviations, so the calendar itself yields uniform asymptotic incentives for the fixed shifted target.
