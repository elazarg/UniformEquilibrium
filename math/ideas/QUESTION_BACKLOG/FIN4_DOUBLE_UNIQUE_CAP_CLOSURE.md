# Consume the paired unique-all-Continue cap obstruction

## Mathematical data

Let a four-player hard-residual source produce two actual profiles:

1. a singleton-base paid/reset source; and
2. its literal owner repair.

Each profile carries a source-matched paid row, a zero-debt reset coordinate,
a positive opponent-incidence coordinate, a complete terminal law, and the
same positive global minimum reference $D_*>0$.

Assume that, at each profile's unrestricted cap vector, all-Continue is the
unique exact product root.

The two cap vectors and terminal laws are not assumed equal.

## Question

Prove one of:

1. the paired unique-cap configuration is incompatible with the four-player
   hard residual or positive global minimality;
2. it yields terminal approximate Nash profiles or a uniform-equilibrium
   payoff;
3. it yields a positive cumulative admissible-payoff near-return by an
   operation other than exact prefixing at the frozen caps;
4. it regenerates a strictly smaller well-founded source state; or
5. an explicit four-player reward table realizes the complete configuration
   and has a fixed positive exploitability gap against every behavioral
   profile.

## Exact obstruction

Every finite exact cap-prefix stack above either profile is a literal list of
all-Continue roots.  It has zero absorption and fixes that profile's complete
terminal semantics.  Thus further exactification at the same cap cannot spend
the paid row, enter a lower reset face, or create return charge.

A solution must use the relation between the source and its owner repair, the
retained paid/reset laws, global positive minimality, or another nonlocal
operation.  Unique-root geometry by itself is insufficient: zero-minimum
tables can have the same local geometry and an exact equilibrium elsewhere.

## Nonanswers

- another all-Continue prefix stack;
- treating the two caps or laws as equal;
- a local root-defect moat without chronological use;
- a regression with global minimum zero; or
- renaming the paired inert state without a consumer or finite-rank edge.
