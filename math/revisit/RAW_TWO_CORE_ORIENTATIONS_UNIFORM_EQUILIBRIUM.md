# A raw three-date equilibrium theorem for two orientations of a core pair

At each nonempty exit S write Z_i(S)=r_i(S)−r_i({i}). The native payoff from quitting alone is zero. Own singleton levels are arbitrary signed numbers, live and Never pay zero and each player uses independent private behavioral randomization. For a nonempty rival subset T put P_i(T)=Z_i(T), Q_i(T)=Z_i(T+i), Γ_ij=P_i({j}) and J_ij=Q_i({j}). Set Γ_ii=0. A positive scaling of the X and Y native rows is understood in the following tests.

In row and column order X,Y,U,V suppose

    Γ = [ 0   σ   −σ b_U  −σ b_V ]
        [ 1   0     −d_U    −d_V ]
        [ e_U f_U      0     h_U ]
        [ e_V f_V     h_V      0 ],

where σ is either −1 or +1, all b_j,d_j are strictly positive, and all six entries e_i,f_i,h_i in the last two rows are arbitrary real numbers. Require:

* In row X, −σ P_X({U,V})≥0. If σ=−1, there is k_X∈[0,1] with Q_X(T)≤k_X P_X(T) on each of the seven nonempty rival subsets T. If σ=+1, require Q_X(T)≤0 on all seven, and additionally Q_X(T)≤P_X(T) on the three nonempty twin subsets.
* In row Y, P_Y(T)<0 on the three nonempty twin subsets. There is k∈[0,1) with k P_Y(T)≤Q_Y(T)≤0 on these three subsets, and J_YX≤1−k.
* In each twin row i, with opposite twin j, require J_iX≤0 and J_ij≥Γ_ij.

All other entries are arbitrary. Then the original game has a uniform-equilibrium payoff. If Γ is R0 with minimum-map degree one there is an exact terminal Nash calendar of three dates giving a fixed original uniform payoff. For other Γ the existence conclusion follows by contraposition of the prior four-player no-UE necessity of R0 and degree one, recorded in JOINT_RECIPIENT_AND_REVERSE_DEGREE_UNIFORM_EQUILIBRIUM.md, section “The index and the original decisions.” Positive diagonal row scaling preserves the homogeneous complementary zero set, and a homotopy through positive diagonal scales preserves the R0 degree. Along the constructed calendar all unilateral replacements face almost-sure rival absorption, which restores each original signed level and permits each scaling to be undone.

Both sign choices have interior in the full sixty-dimensional reward space. All displayed nonsingleton inequalities can be made strict with fixed k_X,k in (0,1). The six singleton entries of the two twin rows impose no sign condition. In the σ=−1 orientation the entire asymmetric strict diamond is included. In particular it covers additive passive rewards with independent joining multipliers k_X,k_U,k_V∈[0,1], k_Y∈[0,1/2] when e_i<0, f_i>0 and h_i<0. For this specialization take k=k_Y. The σ=+1 orientation includes the singleton matrix of SHARP_SIMULTANEOUS_ACTIVITY_UNIFORM_EQUILIBRIUM.md with X=1,Y=2,U=0,V=3, by replacing its higher rewards as required by the tests. This last inclusion concerns the singleton matrix, not the specific higher-reward calibration.

## Eliminating the two core coordinates in the degree

Let H be the twin principal block of Γ (zero diagonal, H_ij=h_i for i≠j) and put L_ij=e_i d_j+f_i b_j+H_ij. Suppose Γ is R0 with degree one, meaning its homogeneous minimum map min(z,Γz) has only its zero root and has local Brouwer degree one there. In particular some e_i<0: otherwise Γ's X column is nonnegative and the X basis vector is a nonzero homogeneous complementary root. A nonzero homogeneous L-solution m lifts to (d·m,b·m,m) for Γ, with both core coordinates positive and both core residuals zero. Thus L is R0.

With the convention Bz≥t, z≥0, z_i((Bz)_i−t_i)=0 a regular solution has index sign(det B_SS) on its active support S, with empty determinant one. Choose a generic twin target t with both entries in (1,2). Each L-solution is regular: for each support exclude the proper affine subspaces where a singular principal is consistent, an active coordinate vanishes, or an outside slack vanishes. Consistency for a singular principal requires the target on its active rows to lie in its proper image; the outside-slack condition uses the free outside target coordinate. R0 bounds the solution set along homotopies of bounded targets, by normalizing a sequence escaping to infinity. Thus degree can be evaluated by summing the local indices at t.

Choose u,v>0 sufficiently small, and for the full matrix set the target to (u,v,w_U,w_V), where w_i=t_i+e_i v+σ f_i u. Take u,v small enough that each w_i>1/2, u<min b_j/max(1,max|L_ij|), and (max_i |h_i|)u/min b_j<1/2. At any full solution the Y inequality X−d·m≥v forces X>0. X's equality is σ(Y−b·m)=u. For σ=+1 this already gives Y>0. For σ=−1, if Y=0 then b·m=u, and at a leaf with e_i<0 the left side e_i X+h_i m_j is at most |h_i|u/min b_j<1/2<w_i. Thus Y>0 in both cases. Consequently X=v+d·m and Y=σ u+b·m. The twin inequalities are precisely Lm≥t, with the same complementarity on m. Conversely every L-solution gives two strictly positive core variables by these formulas: since Lm≥t, ||m||_1≥1/max(1,max|L_ij|), whence b·m>u. This proves bijectivity including empty root lists. At active twins S the full principal determinant is det([[0,σ],[1,0]]) det L_SS=−σ det L_SS by the Schur determinant formula. All outside slacks match; therefore every full solution is regular and the two degrees differ only by the factor −σ, so the reduced degree is nonzero (equal to −σ).

## Solving the core recursions from raw cells

Use hazards A=(x,0,0,0), B=(0,y,p_U,p_V), C=(0,0,r_U,r_V), with H_p=∏(1−p_i), H_r=∏(1−r_i). For a pair of twin hazards z let V(z) be −σ times X's expected passive native exit contribution against z alone, and let φ(z) be −σ times its expected passive payoff when Y is sure and the two twin hazards are z. Empty exit counts zero in V. Thus V(z)≥0 and is strictly positive for a nonzero proper pair of twin hazards, while φ(0)=−1. Write E(z) for minus Y's expected passive exit contribution against z alone and K(z) for minus its forced Quit offer there, including zero at an empty rival subset. Consequently E is strictly positive except at 0 on the closed twin cube and 0≤K≤kE. Set

    A_0=V(p)+H_p V(r),          y=A_0/(A_0−φ(p)),
    x=[E(r)+(E(p)−K(p))/H_p+K(p)H_r]/[(1+K(p))H_r].

Use the bounded open domain of z=(p_U,p_V,r_U,r_V) in (−1,1)^4 on which the positive parts satisfy φ(p)<0 and E(r)+(E(p)−K(p))/H_p<H_r. Its closure has H_p,H_r uniformly positive. Indeed H_r≥E(r) and H_p≥E(p)−K(p)≥(1−k)E(p). If one of the relevant hazards tended to one, E at its limit would be strictly positive while the corresponding product would vanish. On the closure A_0−φ(p)>0: either p=r=0, when −φ(0)=1, or A_0>0 by properness. Thus x,y extend continuously, both are in [0,1], both are positive away from zero in the nonnegative part, and every nonnegative boundary point has x=1 or y=1.

The always-wait native values of X on A,B,C are 0,0,−σ V(r); those of Y are x−(1−x)K(p), −K(p), (E(p)−K(p))/H_p. Direct substitution gives N_t=b_t+h_t N_next, where b_t is that row's passive exit contribution and h_t is its rival survival. The scheduled X,Y quit offers tie. X's B passive exit contribution is −σ[(1−y)V(p)+yφ(p)]=σ(1−y)H_p V(r). For σ=−1 this is nonpositive, and the seven averaged cap conditions price its B offer at most zero and its C offer at most V(r). For σ=+1 the B offer is nonpositive by the seven raw bounds; on C the twin-subset bounds give a forced offer at most its passive contribution −V(r). Y's C offer −K(r) is ≤N_YC. The C recursion gives N_YA=[E(r)+(E(p)−K(p))/H_p]/H_r≥E(p)−K(p), hence (1+K(p))x≥E(p). Put a=J_YX≤1−k. Since 1−a+K(p)≥k(1+K(p)) and kE(p)≥K(p), we have (1−a+K(p))x≥K(p). Thus its A offer ax≤N_YA. These formulas apply at the root and at any nonzero trial point in the closed domain.

## Twin comparisons and the index

For each twin use the exact three-date Never-minus-Quit residuals on B,C multiplied by D_i=1−∏_{v=A,B,C}h_iv. Explicitly F_it=b_it+h_it b_i,next+h_it h_i,next b_i,nextnext−D_i Q_it, also defined when D_i=0. As p,r→0 write m=p+r. Then A_0=b·m+O(||m||²), A_0−φ(p)=1+O(||m||), x=d·m+O(||m||²) (the two first-order K terms cancel), and y=b·m+O(||m||²). Each twin's passive contribution has linear part e_i x at A, f_i y+h_i p_j at B and h_i r_j at C; its forced offer is O(||m||) at every date and all h_it=1+O(||m||). Thus both of i's slots have linearization (Lm)_i with uniform quadratic remainder. The four-dimensional derivative duplicates the two rows and columns of L. It is R0 since nonnegative hazards collapse by addition to a homogeneous L-solution; the only collapse to zero is zero itself. Its degree equals that of L by giving one copy per owner a generic target and the other target one unit lower: since the row values are identical, only the higher target can be active and the lower copy is strictly slack. The regular local indices coincide. This is the duplicate-index argument in TWO_UNRESTRICTED_RECIPIENTS_UNIFORM_EQUILIBRIUM.md. The extension min(z,F(z⁺)) has the same local degree. Interpolating the linear input from z to z⁺ cannot create a nonzero homogeneous root since any negative coordinate cannot be a zero of the minimum map. The R0 lower bound on the unit sphere absorbs the quadratic remainder.

For each twin let u_i=f_i−J_iY and v_i=P_i({Y,j})−Q_i({Y,j}). First assume u_U u_V≠0 and that the simultaneous linear equations

    (1−p_j)u_i+p_j v_i=0   (i=U,V)

have no solution p∈[0,1]^2 with φ(p)=0. This is dense even when all other raw entries are fixed. The intercept and endpoint u_i,v_i are independent, unconstrained coordinates for each twin (vary its joining payoffs at {Y} and {Y,j}). If both slopes v_i−u_i are nonzero, the solution is p_j=−u_i/(v_i−u_i). Clearing denominators in φ(p) yields a polynomial in the four free coordinates which is not identically zero: set both u_i=0 and both v_i=1, giving φ(0)=−1. Excluding this polynomial, the two intercept zeros and the two slope zeros gives the asserted dense set.

Shift both C residuals by subtracting the same T≥0 and take the componentwise minimum with z, leaving B residuals unshifted. As in the independent-factor diamond proof, no boundary root is possible. At x=1 choose a twin with e_i<0. Its C residual F_iC=(Γ_ij−J_ij)r_j+(1−r_j)e_i is strictly negative. At y=1 we have φ(p)=0 and F_iB=(1−p_j)u_i+p_j v_i. If exactly one B twin acts, its residual equals u_i≠0 and fails complementarity. If both act, the two equations above fail at φ(p)=0. Neither can be absent simultaneously since φ(0)=−1. Negative boundary coordinates cannot be roots of a minimum map. For T larger than the uniform upper bound of the C residuals on the closure there are no roots, while at T=0 zero has local degree −σ. Homotopy and excision give a nonzero complementary root at T=0.

For nongeneric twin payoffs approximate the four free entries by generic ones, maintaining all raw conditions and the identical Γ and core domain. Roots stay uniformly away from zero by the reduced duplicate R0 bound and uniform quadratic expansion near the fixed original data. Compactness supplies a nonzero root in the limiting closure, possibly with y=1. The x=1 exclusion still holds. Consequently x,y>0 and each owner has a positive rival hazard every period. For a twin, its C inequality N_iC≥J_ij r_j gives (1−r_j)N_iA≥(J_ij−Γ_ij)r_j≥0; hence its A offer J_iX x≤N_iA. Every occupied twin slot ties by complementarity. This completes the one-date optimality checks. The always-wait values bound the payoff of every unilateral strategy by iteration and are attained under the prescribed hazards. Geometric rival absorption uniformly controls the residual tail for all replacements including Never. It gives uniform finite-average convergence to the same original payoff, proving the assertions.

## Comparison with earlier producers

The exact three-date conclusion for that entire positive-determinant branch follows here as well. Write A=2f−1/2; the reduced matrix has diagonal A and both off-diagonals A−g. Its eigenvalues g and 2A−g are positive when det Γ=g(4f−g−1)>0 in the present normalization. Positive definiteness gives R0 and degree one for L by a homotopy to the identity through positive definite matrices. For this symmetric case Γ is R0: a nonzero homogeneous solution would need X>0 (otherwise the Y and X inequalities successively force m=0 and Y=0) and m≠0 (otherwise X's equality forces Y=0 and a twin inequality fails). This preserves the exact symmetric conclusion as well as allowing all independent asymmetric magnitudes and nonlinear higher payoffs here.
