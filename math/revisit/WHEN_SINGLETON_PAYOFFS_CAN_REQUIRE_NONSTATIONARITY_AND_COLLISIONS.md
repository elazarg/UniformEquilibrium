# When singleton payoffs can require both nonstationarity and simultaneous quitting

Fix all sixteen original singleton rewards, with centered comparisons $G_{ij}=r_i(\{j\})-r_i(\{i\})$. There exists a higher-reward completion with an original uniform-equilibrium payoff that requires both nonstationarity and a positive probability of simultaneous first exit on one physical date in every sufficiently accurate profile **if and only if** some own singleton is positive and $G$ is $R_0$, has minimum-map degree one, and admits no continuous singleton path. More precisely, the stationary regret infimum is strictly positive and there exist $c,\varepsilon>0$ such that every profile of terminal regret at most $\varepsilon$ has a date with simultaneous first-exit probability at least $c$. The prescribed singleton rewards themselves are kept fixed. Necessity follows from the behavioral reduction; when all own singletons are nonpositive, perpetual waiting is an exact stationary equilibrium. The matrix conditions imply positive determinant and the sign-selected inverse columns on one of the 21 strict or 95 tied sign classes. [Behavioral reduction](SHARP_SPARSITY_FOR_COMPULSORY_GRAND_EXITS.md) · [Singleton census, inverse signs and finite controls](TWENTY_ONE_SINGLETON_SIGN_CLASSES_FOR_FOUR_PLAYER_QUITTING_GAMES.md) · [All-matrix singleton-path test](ALL_FOUR_PLAYER_SINGLETON_PATHS_HAVE_A_FINITE_TEST.md)

Here $G$ is hollow and $R_0$ means that $\min(x,Gx)=0$ only at zero, with coordinatewise minimum. The degree is the local Brouwer degree of this map at zero. A continuous singleton path is a measurable probability vector $x(t)$ on $[0,1]$ such that $W(t)=\int_t^1Gx(s)\,ds\ge0$ and $x_i(t)W_i(t)=0$ almost everywhere. Absence of a path already implies $R_0$, since a normalized nonzero complementary vector would give a constant path. Thus for a positive own level there are two matrix tests. If either fails, every completion admits stationary profiles of vanishing regret or profiles of vanishing regret with zero probability of simultaneous first exit. If both pass, this theorem constructs a completion that excludes both kinds of approximations. For comparison, the same singleton data always permit a completion with a pure stationary equilibrium and zero collisions: choose a positive-own player $j$ to quit alone on date one and set each rival's reward for joining $j$ no higher than its payoff at $\{j\}$.

For rational inputs the path verifier's `--behavioral` option decides this alternative. On a path-free matrix it enumerates the sixteen linear pieces of the minimum map. For each invertible piece $B$ it counts its determinant sign when $B^{-1}(1,t,t^2,t^3)^\top$ satisfies all its strict comparisons for sufficiently small $t>0$. Cramer's rule gives the sign of the first nonzero comparison coefficient. This target misses the images of all singular pieces and walls for small positive $t$, since each lies in a proper linear subspace. Positive homogeneity allows the target to be scaled into the local degree neighborhood. The degree is the sum of the signs counted. ## Constructing the higher rewards

For sufficiency keep all 28 centered participating nonsingleton rewards equal to zero. Only the 16 passive nonsingleton entries are used in the construction. As in the fixed orbit calibrations, supportwise lowness gives an actual uniform-equilibrium payoff, the protected path converse gives the claimed single-date collision bound and $R_0$ excludes vanishing stationary hazards. A sole positive limiting hazard is excluded by its negative singleton outsider. On at least two positive limiting hazards every unilateral replacement faces rival absorption and all stationary comparisons are continuous, independently of the own levels. Thus it suffices to exclude nonzero stationary comparison solutions. A small downward perturbation of all centered nonsingleton participating rewards retains both positive gaps, by uniform payoff continuity, and makes the sufficient lowness inequalities strict. This also realizes the fixed singleton rewards at an interior point of a full-dimensional set of such games.

All remaining payoff formulas use centered rewards. Write $a_{i,T}$ for the passive reward at rival pair $T$, and $b_i$ for the passive reward at the triple omitting $i$. On active support $S$ set $t_i=(1-q_i)/q_i\ge0$. Write $F_i$ for the unconditional one-date passive offer divided by the product of the active rival hazards. Active comparisons require $F_i\le0$ and $t_iF_i=0$. On a triple $S$,
$$
\sum_{i\in S} v_i t_i F_i
=\sum_{j\in S}(v^\top G_{SS})_j\prod_{k\in S-j}t_k
 +\sum_{i\in S}v_i a_{i,S-i}t_i .
$$
A triple can use any strict signs for its three passive entries when one of its rows has weakly nonnegative singleton comparisons on that triple and its passive entry is positive: that owner's $F_i$ is strictly positive for every $t$. Otherwise choose nonzero row weights $v_i$ with $v^\top G_{SS}\ge0$ and give the passive entries their signs. The identity excludes all nonzero $t$. At $t=0$, a positive passive entry or $b_o<0$ for the omitted owner excludes the pure triple.

On the full support choose $e>0$, $w=G^{-\top}e$ with no zero component, and set $b_i=\operatorname{sign}(w_i)$. The corresponding identity is
$$\sum_iw_i t_iF_i=\sum_j e_j\prod_{k\ne j}t_k+
\sum_{|T|=2}\left(\sum_{i\notin T}w_i a_{i,T}\right)\prod_{k\notin T}t_k+
\sum_i |w_i|t_i .$$
Call a pair $T=ij$ adverse if both singleton comparisons are nonpositive. The finite sign test below ensures some outside passive entry is negative and some outside entry has the sign of its full weight on each adverse pair. The two recipients can coincide. Increase the magnitude at a matching entry to make the corresponding full quadratic coefficient positive. At all other pairs use just their prescribed signs times $\epsilon>0$. If a nonzero full root exists along $\epsilon\downarrow0$, some negative quadratic of largest absolute magnitude is $O(\epsilon)t_k t_l$ for a nonadverse complementary pair $T$. Comparing this with the positive linear terms gives $t_k,t_l\gtrsim1/\epsilon$. Comparing with the two positive cubic terms containing both variables gives $t_i=O(\epsilon)$ for $i\in T$. The physical hazards converge to the pure pair $T$, where one member strictly prefers its rival singleton. Its unscaled passive offer remains positive in the limit, contradicting its active comparison. A pure grand has a positive $b_i$ since every forced positive inverse column has positive full weight.

On support $ij$ the active numerators are $G_{ij},G_{ji}$. A positive comparison excludes the entire support; with both negative both owners must be sure and the assigned negative passive entry excludes it. If $G_{ij}=0>G_{ji}$ then $j$ is sure and $i$ can mix. The test assigns some outsider $h$ both $a_{h,ij}<0$ and $G_{hj}\le0$, precluding its waiting inequality at every $t_i\ge0$. If both comparisons vanish it uses either one negative passive recipient with both singleton comparisons nonpositive, or negative passive entries for both outsiders. In the latter case, for $(t_i,t_j)\ne(0,0)$ the two outsider linear parts cannot both be nonnegative: the vector with coordinates $(t_j,t_i)$ at $(i,j)$ would give a nonzero homogeneous complementary vector for $G$. At $(0,0)$ the pure-pair test applies. This proves the boundary checks for the actual tied matrix.

## Certifying the signs on the four supports of size three

For each triple the checker enumerates the direct row test and signed row weights of magnitudes $M^2,M,1$ in all orders. A leading positive nonzero summand in each nonzero column suffices for large $M$, and an identically zero column imposes no constraint. Where a triple still needs a choice, each column of its adjugate has one nonzero sign among its entries and at least one nonzero entry. This is checked by expanding the two-by-two minors. For $u=\operatorname{adj}(G_{SS})^\top\mathbf 1$ use $v=u$ or $-u$ according to the determinant sign. At determinant zero choose the negative orientation. The resulting weighted singleton sums are $|\det G_{SS}|\mathbf1^\top$, including zero. The checker tests both signs where the determinant monomials have opposite signs. A strictly signed inverse column fixes the sign of its diagonal cofactor. If both determinant signs are possible, an actual zero can use the negative branch.

An all-negative adjugate choice needing $w_o<0$ occurs only when the triple has a nonnegative nonzero adjugate and nonpositive determinant. At determinant zero the outsider's inverse column vanishes on its own diagonal and restricts to a nonzero vector in the kernel of the triple. That kernel is spanned by a nonnegative column of its nonzero adjugate. The inverse column cannot be nonnegative by $R_0$, so it is nonpositive and hence $w_o<0$ for every $e>0$. At negative determinant choose $e$ close to the outsider axis, using the negative full inverse diagonal. If two choices are required, the checker verifies that the complementary pair has opposite strict signs, so Jacobi's identity makes the corresponding two-by-two inverse principal determinant positive. With two negative diagonal entries, a positive combination of its rows is strictly negative in both coordinates: if both off-diagonals are positive the two ratio bounds overlap by the positive determinant, and otherwise at most one bound restricts the ratio. An actual singular requirement in this pair holds independently of that choice.

More generally the checker uses all previously forced positive and nonpositive full inverse columns to constrain $w$. With no required concentration it may choose any coordinate axis to approach. At a chosen axis $p$, the sign of $w_j$ near it is fixed whenever all nonzero monomials of the cofactor giving $(G^{-1})_{pj}$ have the same sign. Perturb the target into the positive orthant, avoiding the finitely many hyperplanes of zero full weights. For each admissible target choice and every remaining sign vector it tests $w^\top G>0$ column by column at the level of signs, then searches the finite list of triple choices for the pair and triple conditions above. One target must succeed for all of its remaining full sign vectors. This works for 20 of 21 strict classes, 45 of 46 one-tie classes, 38 of 39 two-tie classes, and all ten three-tie classes. The only exceptions are witness 14 and the one- and two-tie faces treated below. [Exact sign calculation](../experiments/FIXED_SINGLETON_BEHAVIORAL_COMPLETIONS.py)

## The remaining strict chamber

At witness 14 write the literal singleton matrix as
$$
G=\begin{pmatrix}0&-m&-g&c\\-n&0&a&-d\\f&k&0&-s\\l&o&-b&0\end{pmatrix}
$$
with all twelve letters strictly positive. Assume the necessary singleton conditions for the two behavioral requirements. For any generic $e>0$, $w=G^{-\top}e$ has $w_0,w_1>0$ by the two forced inverse columns and all $w_i\ne0$. At least one of $w_2,w_3$ is positive by column 0 of $w^\top G$. Simultaneously exchanging $0,1$ and $2,3$ preserves the displayed signs, so take $w_2>0$. Both $\det G_{123,123}$ and $\det G_{023,023}$ are positive by the positive inverse diagonals at 0 and 1 and $\det G>0$.

In each row below the first three entries give the centered passive rewards at rival pairs in lexicographic order and the fourth gives the passive reward at the rival triple. Use zero centered participating rewards and retain the prescribed original own singleton rewards.
$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
0 &K&-C\epsilon&-c_0\epsilon&\delta\epsilon^2\\
1 &K'&-C'\epsilon&\epsilon&1\\
2 &A&-D'\epsilon&B\epsilon&-\epsilon^3\\
3 &-B_0&K''&-L&\operatorname{sign}(w_3).
\end{array}
$$
Take $B,C',D',K''>0$ and $0<c_0<w_1/w_0$. Choose $B_0,L>0$ so that $2\sqrt{B_0L/d}>o/d+1$, and $A,K',K>0$ so the weighted coefficients at $01,02,12$ are strictly positive. These are $w_2A-w_3B_0$, $w_1K'+w_3K''$, $w_0K-w_3L$. The coefficient at $23$ is $\epsilon(w_1-w_0c_0)>0$. Take $C>cB/s$ and $0<\delta<(C-cB/s)/d$. All choices precede $\epsilon\downarrow0$.

On $012$ the three passive pair signs are positive, and one can take positive triple weights with row 2 largest, then 1, then 0. On $013$ all three are negative, and weights negative in decreasing magnitude order $1,0,3$ give strictly positive weighted column sums. The excluded owner 2 joins the pure triple. On $023$ the signs in increasing owner order are $(-,-,+)$, the signs of the column sums of its adjugate. This follows by expanding its two-by-two minors, and the determinant is positive. The preceding triple identity excludes all these supports. For $123$ the active inequalities are
$$
 a t_3-dt_2+\epsilon\le0,\qquad k t_3-s t_1+B\epsilon\le0,\qquad o t_2-b t_1-L\le0 .
$$
The first forces $t_2>0$, so the second binds and gives $t_1=(k t_3+B\epsilon)/s>0$. The first then binds as well. Substituting in the third gives $(oa/d-bk/s)t_3+\epsilon(o/d-bB/s)-L<0$ for all sufficiently small $\epsilon$, since $dbk-aso=\det G_{123,123}>0$. Thus $t_3=0$, $t_2=\epsilon/d$, $t_1=B\epsilon/s$. At this candidate owner 0's passive offer, divided by the product of its rival hazards, is $\epsilon^2(\delta-C/d-c_0B/s+cB/(sd))<0$. It strictly prefers to join. The pure pairs $01,23$ have negative passive offers for outsiders 3 and 0, respectively. Every other pair has a member with a positive comparison against its mate. Nonpure pair and sole-owner supports are excluded as before.

On the full support the four active passive polynomials are
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_0={}&-mt_2t_3-gt_1t_3+ct_1t_2+Kt_3-C\epsilon t_2-c_0\epsilon t_1+\delta\epsilon^2,\\
F_1={}&-nt_2t_3+at_0t_3-dt_0t_2+K't_3-C'\epsilon t_2+\epsilon t_0+1,\\
F_2={}&ft_1t_3+kt_0t_3-st_0t_1+At_3-D'\epsilon t_1+B\epsilon t_0-\epsilon^3,\\
F_3={}&lt_1t_2+ot_0t_2-bt_0t_1-B_0t_2+K''t_1-Lt_0+\operatorname{sign}(w_3).
\end{aligned}
$$
Suppose there are roots along $\epsilon\downarrow0$. In the weighted identity all negative terms are bounded in absolute sum by $C_1\epsilon t_2(t_0+t_1+\epsilon^2)$ for a fixed $C_1$. If $t_2=0$ its positive linear terms force all remaining $t_i=0$, contradicted by $F_1=1$. Thus $t_2>0$ and $F_2=0$. The positive $01$ quadratic and the cubic monomials containing $t_2t_3$ imply $t_3=O(\epsilon)$, by comparing $t_2t_3(1+t_0+t_1)$ to that negative bound. The $t_0t_1t_2$ cubic gives $t_0t_1\le C_2\epsilon(t_0+t_1+\epsilon^2)$, so $\min(t_0,t_1)=O(\epsilon)$. In fact $t_1=O(\epsilon)$. It suffices to check $t_0=O(\epsilon)$, when $F_1\le0$ implies $t_2\ge1/(nt_3+dt_0+C'\epsilon)\gtrsim1/\epsilon$. The inequality $F_0\le0$ gives $(ct_2-gt_3-c_0\epsilon)t_1\le (mt_3+C\epsilon)t_2$, proving the claim.

If $t_3=0$, $F_2=0$ forces $t_0>0$ and $t_1<B\epsilon/s$. Then $F_0=0$ gives $t_2\le\delta\epsilon/(C-cB/s)<\epsilon/d$. But $F_1\le0$ requires $t_2\ge(1+\epsilon t_0)/(dt_0+C'\epsilon)\ge\epsilon/d$ for small $\epsilon$.

Hence $t_3>0$ and $F_3=0$. If $t_0\to0$, the same $F_1$ bound gives $t_2\to\infty$, whereas $F_3/t_2\to-B_0$. On a subsequence with $t_0$ bounded below, $F_2=0$ forces $t_1>0$ since $B\epsilon t_0>\epsilon^3$. Consequently $F_1=0$, and
$$
t_2=\frac{1+\epsilon t_0+(at_0+K')t_3}{dt_0+C'\epsilon+nt_3}.
$$
This is $O(1/t_0+\epsilon)$. If $t_0\to\infty$ then $F_3/t_0\to-L$. Otherwise pass to $t_0\to u\in(0,\infty)$, giving $t_2\to1/(du)$. The limiting $F_3$ is $o/d-B_0/(du)-Lu+\operatorname{sign}(w_3)<0$ by the choice of $B_0,L$. These contradictions exclude all full supports for sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$. The stationary gap and the collision and existence conclusions now follow from the common argument. Thus the behavioral singleton conditions are sharp at every literal matrix in all 21 strict sign classes.

## The two remaining tied faces

In the checker's zero-based canonical order face 28 with one tie has the displayed W14 signs except $f=0$, and face 23 with two ties has $f=o=0$. On the first face columns $0,1,3$ are forced positive, so exchanging $0,1$ and $2,3$ as above leaves just $o=0$ and gives $w_2>0$. The same four-row construction works in these labels. With $o=0$, the weak pair $13$ permits owner 3 to mix against sure owner 1, but outsider 0 has $G_{01}=-m<0$ and $a_{0,13}=-C\epsilon<0$. All comparisons on triple $123$ and on full support remain valid with $o=0$, including the strict final bound.

On the two-tie face all four inverse columns are forced strictly positive. Keep $f=o=0$ and change $K'$ in row 1 to $-\epsilon$. Now the full quadratic coefficient at $02$ is $w_3K''-w_1\epsilon>0$ for sufficiently small $\epsilon$. Owner 1 strictly joins every active $02$ pair, since owner 0 must be sure, $G_{10}=-n<0$ and $a_{1,02}=-\epsilon$. The $13$ test remains as before. Triple $012$ is excluded by $F_2=A+kt_0>0$ at every $t$. On $023$ the signs $(-,-,+)$ still have strictly positive weighted column sums: take the absolute values at rows 2, 0 and 3 in strictly descending powers of a large number. All remaining triple comparisons are identical. The full equation $F_1$ now has $-\epsilon t_3$ in place of $K't_3$. Since $t_3=O(\epsilon)$ follows from the same weighted inequality, its numerator lower bound $1$ becomes $1-O(\epsilon^2)$, which suffices in both places where $t_2$ must diverge. At $t_3=0$ the equations are identical, and the positive-limit and unbounded-$t_0$ arguments use the same limits. This completes the physical construction on every strict and tied sign stratum at each admissible magnitude.
