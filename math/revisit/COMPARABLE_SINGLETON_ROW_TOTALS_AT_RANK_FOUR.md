# The fourth order test from comparable positive and negative singleton totals

Write $G_{ij}=r_i(\{j\})-r_i(\{i\})$ and $G_{ii}=0$. In each row let $P_i=\sum_j(G_{ij})_+$ and $M_i=\sum_j(-G_{ij})_+$. Choose any marked recipient $h$. The unrestricted fourth order test passes for every choice of the 44 nonsingleton rewards and all four signed own rewards if
$$
   20 M_i\le21 P_i,\qquad 20 P_i\le21 M_i\quad(i\ne h),\qquad
   20 M_h\le21 P_h .
$$
The three pairs of inequalities apply only to the unmarked recipients. There is no upper bound on $P_h$. The result gives the all-four-finite test whenever the matrix has a weak blocker, meaning $G_{ij}\le0$ for some $i\ne j$. Under the displayed hypotheses a weak blocker always exists, since each unmarked row either vanishes or contains a negative entry. At rank four an order prior tests each recipient against every training date, literal Never and Late, conditional on the pooled training observation. Feasibility at this fixed rank alone does not establish a uniform-equilibrium payoff. [Order criterion](../notes/CODEX_ROOT__INTERLACED_ORDER_TESTS_FOR_ARBITRARY_FOUR_PLAYER_GAMES.md)

In particular, if any strictly positive column of $G^{-1}$ has largest to smallest coordinate ratio at most $21/20$, the four-finite test passes. Indeed normalize the column to $y>0$, so $Gy$ is zero off its positive axis $h$. The two weighted foreign totals then agree in each unmarked row, giving the two displayed inequalities upon bounding each $y_j$ by its extreme coordinates. In row $h$ the positive weighted total exceeds the negative one, giving the stated lower bound. More generally, the same argument applies to any $y>0$ with $(Gy)_{-h}=0$ and $(Gy)_h\ge0$, with no invertibility assumption.

All 21 strict integer calibrations and all 95 tied calibrations from the singleton census therefore pass rank four for arbitrary completions. Each of those matrices has exactly three row sums zero and one positive. These strict inequalities on a positive and negative total give open singleton neighborhoods of all the calibrations. The theorem concerns arbitrary distributions of rewards within each sign in a row. The previously established rank-dependent neighborhoods of positive-kernel centers use a matrix distance and may apply at every fixed rank. The criterion here concerns rank four, includes every calibration at its actual nonsingular matrix, and permits unbounded surplus even after normalizing the marked negative total. ## A single prior for each sign pattern and marked recipient

First work in one of the remaining strict chambers with a two-positive row. There are 17 such chambers and four choices of $h$ in each one. All rows have strictly positive $P_i,M_i$. The certificate uses one training observation and a probability distribution on interlaced orders with sixteen distinct validation dates disjoint from the training dates. Let $F_{ij}(t)$ be the probability that $j$ is first on the prescription minus the probability that it is first before tested date $t$ after deleting player $i$, drawing one validation label independently and uniformly from each clock. Include $t=\infty$ for Never and Late. Every scored exit is a singleton and the prescribed-minus-tested gain in native units is $\sum_{j\ne i}G_{ij}F_{ij}(t)$.

For each of the 68 chamber and recipient choices the checker gives one prior satisfying all of the following inequalities at every tested rank, for every positive donor $p$ and negative donor $n$ in row $i$:
$$
  20F_{ip}(t)-21F_{in}(t)\ge0,\qquad 21F_{ip}(t)-20F_{in}(t)\ge0.
$$
It also verifies $F_{hp}(t)\ge0$ separately for every positive donor of the marked row. Thus $uF_{ip}(t)-F_{in}(t)\ge0$ for every $20/21\le u\le21/20$. Set $u_i=P_i/M_i$ for each unmarked row, and $u_h=\min(P_h/M_h,21/20)$. To recover a row, sum the generators $u_i e_p-e_n$ with weights $G_{ip}(-G_{in})/P_i$. This gives its entire negative part and a fraction $u_i M_i/P_i$ of each positive entry. Any remaining positive part occurs in the marked row, where each corresponding comparison was checked. The same prior therefore serves every allowed matrix in this chamber with this choice of $h$.

The checker uses the census representatives only to enumerate signs. In each chamber all four choices of $h$ are checked directly, including the eight choices that cannot support a positive determinant axis in the census tree test. Its numerical flow selects candidate weights on paths. The verification decomposes the flow into full words, checks all training and validation counts at each cut, enumerates all $4^4$ literal independent draws on each word, and accepts integer masses of positive total only after every complete gain inequality passes. The graph excludes any word allowing $h$ to be first with one of its positive donors second. Each retained word has $F_{hp}(t)\ge0$ since a first exit by $p$ after deleting $h$ can occur only if $p$ was already first. The verifier checks these surplus comparisons separately on every accepted word. The 68 priors use 790 weighted complete words and satisfy 7,376 integer generator inequalities. The remaining four strict census chambers each have exactly one positive donor per row and already pass the four-finite test for their entire sign chambers. Strict signs outside the census cannot have positive native absorbing regret infimum, since that would give a signed counterexample on the same singleton matrix. The weak-blocker completion then gives the four-finite test. Finite rivals also ensure that signed row translations preserve every comparison. Finally, matrices with ties satisfying the displayed row inequalities are limits of strict matrices satisfying them. In a row with both positive and negative totals, adjust their magnitudes slightly to put the total ratio in the interior first, then perturb the zeros. An unmarked zero row can be approximated by a strictly mixed row with equal positive and negative totals. A marked zero row permits the same operation. A marked row with only positive entries and zeros has a strict margin in its sole inequality, and a marked row with $M_h>0$ has $P_h>0$ by the hypothesis. All approximations can keep the signed own levels and the 44 higher payoffs fixed. The finite prior simplex is compact and all obedience inequalities are continuous in the table, so its limit gives the conclusion.

### What a higher-rank certificate must contain on the same calibrations

All 116 census calibrations pass the fourth order test. For the 17 strict calibrations with a two-positive row, the word certificates above use only singleton first exits. Higher ranks on the very same singleton matrices require a different kind of order.

For every hollow matrix $G$ which fails the finite singleton-path test, give every player own singleton reward one and set all 44 centered nonsingleton rewards to zero. There exist $c(G)>0$ and $N_0(G)$ with the following property. At every $N\ge N_0$, in **each positive-probability training observation of every obedient prior** there is one finite rank and a pair of owners such that each puts at least $c(G)N$ training labels there. In **every** compatible validation extension of that observation each of those same two owners puts at least $c(G)N-2$ validation labels at that same rank. This applies also to ordinary signed order tests with Never permitted. Thus a uniform bound on multiplicities per owner and date cannot suffice at unbounded levels, even when singleton orders suffice at a low level.

Indeed the zero participating rewards satisfy the pair protection hypothesis of the previous continuous-path converse. It gives $e,d>0$ such that every original profile of maximum regret at most $e$ has a date with unconditional probability at least $d$ of a simultaneous first exit. For a passing signed prior the empirical training profile in every positive observation has maximum regret at most $48M/N$, where $M\ge1$ bounds the original table. Choose $N_0$ so this is at most $e$. One of the six pairs contributes at least $d/6$ to the unconditional simultaneous-exit probability at that date. This probability for a specified pair is at most either owner's individual probability of that date, so its two empirical multiplicities are each at least $Nd/6$. The two cumulative counts within each owner differ by at most one just before and just after the date, proving the claimed validation bound with $c(G)=d/6$. This transfers a previously established physical collision requirement to a deterministic constraint on the extensions of an observed order. There is an explicit four-owner version. On the cyclic matrix with four grand rewards $R\ge100$ the earlier bound is $e+Rp_I\ge1/1000$. For $N\ge96000(R+1)$ the training profile in each occupied cell therefore has $p_I\ge1/(2000R)$. If its four marginal atoms at a date are $a_i(t)$, then $p_I=\sum_t\prod_i a_i(t)$. At each date $t$ choose an owner $i(t)$ of smallest atom. The events that the three other owners all select $t$ are disjoint across different dates, since any two sets of three owners intersect. Independence thus gives $\sum_t\prod_{j\ne i(t)}a_j(t)\le1$. Consequently $p_I\le\max_t\min_i a_i(t)$. Some common training date has at least $N/(2000R)$ labels of **each of the four owners** and at least $N/(2000R)-2$ of each on every validation extension.
