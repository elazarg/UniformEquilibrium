# Finite order tests near a strictly positive singleton kernel

Write $G_{ij}=r_i(\{j\})-r_i(\{i\})$. Fix a hollow matrix $G_0$ with $G_0z=0$ for a positive probability vector $z$. Suppose $G_0$ has rank three, a left kernel generator with no zero coordinate, and a negative off-diagonal entry. Use the maximum entry norm. At every rank $N$ the four-finite order test passes for every completion of the 44 higher rewards and every signed choice of own levels throughout the following balls, with constants independent of $N$.

* The radius is $c/N^2$ if no nonzero nonnegative diagonal matrix $D$ makes $DG_0$ skew-symmetric. In particular this holds at every strictly signed such matrix with a reciprocally negative pair.
* The radius is $c/N$ if some skew-symmetric matrix $A$ with $A\mathbf1=0$ satisfies $\sum_j A_{ij}(G_0)_{ij}<0$ in every row. All 21 remaining strict sign classes and all 95 remaining classes with ties contain centers for this linear-radius conclusion, using the positive-triple variant below.

Thus near a given center of the second kind, at distance $\delta>0$, a failing rank must exceed $c/\delta$. Both results concern exact feasibility at the specified finite level. Proving existence on a fixed nearby game requires unbounded levels. ## Hiding small changes of rates

Let $x$ be a probability vector near $z$, $d_i=1-x_i$, $W=Gx$, and $f(d,t)=\int_0^t e^{-du}\,du$. All rate denominators below are uniformly positive. In each construction a common deterministic set of training dates is chosen for the four owners. A random scenario specifies four independent clocks with continuous distributions. Independently for each owner take the $N$ validation quantiles at $(k+U_i)/N$, $0\le k<N$, with $U_i$ uniform on $(0,1)$. Their count through time $t$ rounds the scenario's true cumulative count $NF_i(t)$ either up or down. A uniformly selected validation label, averaged over its shift, has exactly the scenario clock's physical law. No validation clocks of different owners tie, and none ties a training label, almost surely. Continuous time here is a device for sampling a finite weak order; only these orders and their probabilities enter the test. Every trained deadline and the final Never/Late comparisons have only singleton scores. The training observation hides the scenario. Mixing orders in this test makes no assumption of public randomization in a quitting game.

First consider the hypothesis on $D$. The linear map from real weights $b_{ij}=b_{ji}$ on distinct pairs to $(\sum_j b_{ij}(G_0)_{ij})_i$ has a strictly positive vector in its image. Otherwise separation of a linear subspace from the open positive orthant yields $\lambda\ge0$, $\lambda\ne0$, with $\lambda_i(G_0)_{ij}+\lambda_j(G_0)_{ji}=0$ for every pair. This gives the forbidden $D$. Start at the positive weights $z_i z_j$, whose image is zero, and add a small multiple of a preimage of that strictly positive vector. Normalize to pair probabilities $p_{ij}$. In a fixed neighborhood of $G_0$ we then have $S_i=\sum_j p_{ij}G_{ij}\ge\gamma>0$. For a strictly signed matrix every nonzero nonnegative skew symmetrizer would have all diagonal entries positive, which is impossible on a reciprocal pair of the same sign.

Sample a pair $i,j$ with this law and a fair sign. Use independent exponential clocks with rate vector $X=x\pm\eta(e_i-e_j)$, where $\eta=e/N$. Take the deterministic training dates at the midquantiles of each nominal exponential law of rate $x_i$. Uniformly in time, changing a rate by $\eta$ changes a marginal cumulative probability by at most $C\eta$, by differentiating $1-e^{-xt}$. Choose $e>0$ so that $Ce<1/2$ and all sampled rates are positive. The training count differs from $NF_i(t)$ by less than one, including the left limits. Thus the validation grid interlaces with the training grid. The expected native prescription is $W_i$ since rates always sum to one. The offer at time $t$ conditional on $X$ is $(GX)_i f(1-X_i,t)$. Averaging over the pairs and signs gives the following exact prescription minus offer:
$$
 W_i[1-\mathbb E f(1-X_i,t)]
 + \frac{\eta S_i}{2}\,[f(d_i-\eta,t)-f(d_i+\eta,t)].
$$
The final bracket is at least $2\eta\int_0^t u e^{-d_i u}\,du$ by $\sinh v\ge v$ for $v\ge0$. At $t\ge1/2$, including infinity, the integral has a uniform positive lower bound and the coefficient of $W_i$ is bounded. Below $1/2$ that coefficient is positive and at most one. Each training date is at least $1/(2N)$, so the integral is at least a positive constant times $N^{-2}$ there. Consequently every tested gain is strictly negative when
$$
 -cN^{-4}\le W_i\le cN^{-2} \qquad\text{for all } i.
$$

## Oriented periodic changes of rates

Now assume the condition on $A$. In the hyperplane $\mathbf1^\perp$ choose a piecewise linear loop $\beta$ based at zero with $\int\beta_i\,d\beta_j$ a positive multiple of $A_{ij}$. Indeed a tangent skew matrix is a sum of $uv^T-vu^T$ on tangent vectors. Trace the parallelograms $0,u,u+v,v,0$ in succession and scale the entire path down. Parameterize it on $[0,1]$, scaling it far enough that its velocities $a=\beta'$ have magnitude below $\min_i z_i/4$ in each coordinate. Extend periodically. In a fixed neighborhood of $G_0$ one has $\sum_jG_{ij}\int\beta_i\,d\beta_j\le-\gamma$ in every row for a fixed $\gamma>0$.

Choose one fair sign $\sigma$ for the order prior. At physical time $s$ let the independent clocks have rates $x_i+\sigma a_i(s/\ell)$, where $\ell=e/N$. The sum of the rates is one. The survival of clock $i$ is $\exp(-x_is-\sigma\ell\beta_i(s/\ell))$. For training, round each nominal exponential midquantile **up** to the next multiple of $\ell$, retaining a midquantile already on a boundary. The nominal cumulative count differs from the rounded integer count by at most $1/2+N\ell\max_i x_i$. The actual marginal distribution differs from nominal by at most $C\ell$ in either scenario. A sufficiently small fixed $e$ gives interlacing as above. Every visible finite date completes a whole number of loops.

The mean prescription is again $W_i$, by integrating the instantaneous reward against total survival $e^{-s}$ and then averaging the sign. Against a time $t$ the mean offer is
$$
 \int_0^t e^{-d_i s}\big[W_i\cosh(\ell\beta_i(s/\ell))+
             (Ga(s/\ell))_i\sinh(\ell\beta_i(s/\ell))\big]ds .
$$
At a complete loop boundary, also at infinity, this equals $f(d_i,t)(W_iB_i+D_i)$ by geometric summation. Here $B_i,D_i$ are the averages on one loop weighted by $e^{-d_i s}$ and normalized by its integral. Uniformly $B_i=1+O(\ell^2)$ and
$$
 D_i=\ell\sum_jG_{ij}\int_0^1\beta_i\,d\beta_j+O(\ell^2)\le-\gamma\ell/2 .
$$
These follow directly by substituting $s=\ell u$ and expanding bounded functions on $[0,1]$. If $-cN^{-2}\le W_i\le cN^{-1}$ with $c$ sufficiently small, the bracket $W_iB_i+D_i$ is at most $-\gamma\ell/4$. Positive or zero prescriptions dominate this offer strictly. Negative prescriptions do so as well, since $f(d_i,t)$ is bounded below by a positive constant times $1/N$ at every visible date. This proves the stronger band.

## Passing from rates to matrices

If the native absorbing regret infimum vanishes, the nearby negative singleton entry gives four-finite approximations and hence passage at every level. Otherwise the strict inverse-column theorem gives a player $p$ with $G^{-1}e_p>0$ and $\det G>0$. Use this column divided by its sum for $x$. Every column of $\operatorname{adj}(G_0)$ is a nonzero multiple of $z$ by the two kernel hypotheses. Each of the four normalized adjugate columns is therefore smooth nearby and equals $z$ at $G_0$. Uniformly $x-z=O(\|G-G_0\|)$ and $0\le Gx=O(\|G-G_0\|)$ componentwise. The upper sides of the two bands give the asserted balls. With four finite clocks each tested deviation still faces a finite rival, so row shifts preserve the payoff comparisons. [Inverse-column condition](FIN4_STRICT_INVERSE_COLUMN_AND_ALL_END_MINIMUM_REDUCTION.md) · [Finite-clock completion](ZERO_SINGLETON_COLUMN_EQUIVALENCE.md)

The same two-sided matrix argument works if just one principal triple of $G_0$ has positive determinant, even when some coordinates of the left kernel vanish. Its omitted adjugate column is a positive multiple of $z$, since its diagonal entry is that positive determinant. Keep this index throughout the nearby positive-determinant branch. The positive triple already forces rank three for the matrix with the given positive kernel. Symmetric matrices have $\sum_{ij}A_{ij}G_{ij}=0$ for every skew $A$, making the pair-probability proof applicable at centers outside the loop test.

## Centers in every remaining sign class

The linear-radius theorem reaches **all 21 strict classes and all 95 classes with one, two or three ties** in the singleton sign census. Here the ball is in the full twelve-dimensional matrix space, also at a center with ties. The exact checker constructs a balanced matrix in every class with a positive principal triple and a suitable circulation. In a balanced row, distribute the same integer total between its positive and negative entries, keeping each indicated magnitude positive. Totals from $\{2,3,4,6\}$ suffice. Write $A_1,A_2,A_3$ for the basis of skew matrices with zero row sums obtained by freely choosing entries $01,02,12$. Form the four-by-three matrix $M_{ik}=\sum_j(A_k)_{ij}G_{ij}$. The checker accepts when the alternating three-row minors of $M$ have both signs. They then span the left kernel, so the image contains a strictly negative vector: a vector with strictly positive coordinates can be made orthogonal to a normal of mixed signs. The corresponding linear combination of the three basis matrices is the required $A$. Strictness also gives relatively open sets of such centers among balanced matrices on the same face. In every strict class the linear-radius construction reaches nearby matrices that pass the published determinant, degree and inverse-column tests and fail the full continuous singleton-path test. The checker requires nonzero deleted-triple determinants at the center and chooses $h$ with positive deleted determinant and positive determinants on all triples required by the degree test at $e_h$. Increase a positive entry of row $h$ by $\delta>0$. Then $G_\delta\mathbf1=\delta e_h$ and $\det G_\delta$ equals $\delta$ times that positive deleted determinant, because the cofactors in row $h$ of the balanced center are equal. For sufficiently small $\delta$ all selected triple signs persist. There is no nonzero homogeneous complementary vector, since every principal block of size two or larger is invertible and every singleton has a negative outsider. The sign graph admits no strict four-cycle. For each potential triangle the checker rules out its scale or one outsider comparison at the center. The scale is positive and cannot equal one because its triple is nonsingular, so these failures persist for small $\delta$. ## Which positive-kernel centers are left

Let $G_0z=0$ with $z>0$ of sum one. A nonzero row already has a negative and a positive foreign entry. At **every** such four-player center with no identically zero row, at least one of the two rate constructions applies, without rank or left-kernel hypotheses. Indeed, if the pair comparison fails, let $D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)\ne0$ be nonnegative with $DG_0$ skew-symmetric. Set
$$
 A=-\operatorname{diag}(z)D G_0\operatorname{diag}(z).
$$
It is skew, has zero row sums, and in row $i$ its contraction against $G_0$ is $-\lambda_i z_i\sum_j z_j(G_0)_{ij}^2<0$ wherever $\lambda_i>0$. With $S=\operatorname{supp}\lambda$, skew symmetry implies $(G_0)_{S,S^c}=0$. If $|S|=1$, its row is zero. If $|S|=2$, both rows vanish because their internal hollow block has a positive kernel. Hence at most one owner is outside $S$. On a sole outside owner $o$, the foreign entries of the row are nonconstant by positivity of $z$ and the nonzero-row hypothesis. Pick two distinct rivals with different entries. A skew triangle circulation through them and $o$ can be oriented so its contraction on row $o$ is negative. Add a sufficiently small positive multiple to $A$ to keep the other strict inequalities. This proves the alternative.

Taking $x=z$ directly in the rate estimates, one gets a **full two-sided matrix ball** of radius $c/N^4$ in the pair case, and $c/N^2$ in the loop case, regardless of the rank or left kernel. These priors use only singleton outcomes and have strict obedience at the given level. The better positive-side radii $c/N^2$ and $c/N$ apply to the one-sided conditions on $Gx$ as before, and to full matrix balls under the normalized-adjugate hypotheses. In particular every rank-three center with a nowhere-zero left kernel has a full $c/N^2$ ball without a separate skew-symmetrizability proviso. If $DG_0$ were skew, then $Dz$ would be a nonzero vector of the left kernel, forcing $D$ invertible; a real skew matrix cannot have odd rank. More generally at a rank-three center without a zero row, the case $|S|=3$ also cannot occur: its sole outside column would vanish by hollowness, so both $e_o$ and $z$ would belong to the right kernel. Thus the pair criterion always applies in that case, with its improved two-sided radius whenever a positive principal triple is available.

**The missing one-zero-row neighborhood is equivalent to the full question.** Fix $N$. Consider local centers $G_0$ with exactly one zero row $h$, $G_0 z=0$ for $z>0$, and positive $h$-deleted principal determinant. The following are equivalent.

1. Every signed four-player table passes the unrestricted rank-$N$ test.
2. Every native table with some weak blocker passes the all-four-finite rank-$N$ test.
3. At every indicated center, some positive-radius matrix ball passes the all-four-finite rank-$N$ test for every choice of the 44 higher rewards.
4. For each such center, each fixed choice of the other three entire native payoff rows, and each restored row $R_h$ whose singleton row obeys $R_h z>0$, arbitrarily small positive multiples of $R_h$ admit an all-four-finite rank-$N$ prior, on the resulting one-row ray from the zero payoff row. The three other singleton rows are those of $G_0$.

The first two statements are equivalent by the fixed-rank zero-column completion theorem: translate at a singleton whose owner has a weak blocker for 1⇒2. For 2⇒1 finite rivals preserve signed translations, and tables with no blocker are already solved by the normal-core result. Each indicated center has a strictly negative entry (some nonzero row has zero positive-weight mean), so a small ball has a blocker. Thus 2 implies 3, and 3 implies 4.

If 2 fails then the native absorbing regret infimum is positive: its vanishing and the one weak blocker would give all-four-finite approximating calendars. By the strict inverse-column theorem choose $h$ with $G^{-1}e_h>0$, $\det G>0$, and let $z$ be that column normalized to sum one. Erase the entire recipient $h$ payoff row. The other three singleton rows remain independent and annihilate $z$, so the resulting singleton matrix $G_0$ has rank three and a positive kernel, while the deleted triple has determinant $\det G\,(G^{-1})_{hh}>0$. The restored row has $R_hz>0$. For any $t>0$, replace the original recipient row by $tR_h$, including all its higher payoffs; the other rows do not change. Every order obedience inequality in that row scales by $t$, and all other inequalities stay identical. The very same rank therefore fails along this entire ray as $t\downarrow0$. This contradicts 4. In particular universal four-player UE is equivalent to the indicated one-row local property at every rank. The center itself passes by its positive homogeneous kernel. The neighborhood or ray assertion at the zero payoff row is the unsettled step. Even the analogous full-ball claim at the all-zero singleton matrix would by positive scaling of all rows imply universal passage at that rank.

The other three rows can be made **strictly** obedient at every tested rank in one common training observation at such a center. Apply the pair-weight separation argument to those three rows alone. An obstructing diagonal $D$ would be supported away from $h$. Its ambient left null vector $Dz$ has zero $h$ coordinate, but the left kernel is generated by $e_h$. Hence no obstruction exists. The exact pair perturbation therefore gives all three strict margins. The restored row still has its own obedience inequalities, unaffected in sign by its scale.

**One recipient's polar test.** Fix the three complete native rows at such a center. On the finite alphabet of four-finite pooled orders, let $P_N$ be the compact polytope of priors obeying all three rows' actual conditional tests. It is nonempty by the preceding strict construction. Only present cells require strictness there. For each nonnegative collection $\lambda_{E,t}$ of weights on the remaining owner $h$'s replacement times, allowed to depend on the full training observation $E$, let $d_\lambda(\pi)$ be the weighted sum of that owner's expected displacement vectors on $\pi$, using indicators of the corresponding observations. The 14 coordinates are the changes in probabilities of the eleven nonsingletons and the three foreign singletons; own singleton is unscored. Let $u$ vanish on nonsingletons and equal $z_j$ at the foreign singleton $j$. Then the ray property in statement 4 is equivalent, at these fixed three rows, to
$$
  d_\lambda(P_N)\cap\{-t u:t\ge0\}\ne\varnothing
   \qquad\text{for every }\lambda\ge0.
$$
Indeed for one restored reward vector $R$ the required feasibility on $P_N$ is equivalent to $\min_{\pi\in P_N}R\cdot d_\lambda(\pi)\le0$ for every $\lambda\ge0$, by the finite alternative separating the complete vector of conditional gains from the negative orthant. Now require this for every $R$ with $R\cdot u>0$ and interchange the two universal quantifiers. A meeting with the indicated ray suffices. If the compact hull $d_\lambda(P_N)$ misses the ray, strict separation gives $R\cdot u\ge0$ and $\min_{\pi\in P_N}R\cdot d_\lambda(\pi)>0$. A sufficiently small perturbation of $R$ yields $R\cdot u>0$ while preserving strict separation, since $u\ne0$. All three priced rows' conditional constraints remain enforced in each individual prior. A prior may vary with the one remaining tester.

Two endpoint observations are particularly restrictive for a separating tester. Make $h$ surely quit at the first date and let the other three owners play a one-stage mixed equilibrium given this action, with any waiting player scheduled finite later. Every replacement by those owners faces the sure $h$ and their true complete caps equal their two stage offers. Use independent systematic validation grids and deterministic rounded training grids for this fixed finite product. If $h$'s positive tests there only use its first date, then $d_\lambda=0$. Thus a separator must charge a later time or Never at the observations of every such first-day equilibrium construction.

For the opposite end, fix any $p\ne h$. In the three-player restricted native game on the other owners there are approximate equilibria with prescribed clock $p$ individually finite. Indeed use three-player existence on the singleton-default translation of $p$, then its direct dimension-free private diffuse $p$ completion; its proof only compares finite deadlines against the default on rival survival. Truncate finite tails and clip $p$ to have bounded finite support with vanishing uniform response error. Append $h$ after all original finite dates, therefore after every potential $p$ exit. If $p$ has a weak blocker among its two subgame rivals, first use this blocker for the usual finite-clock completion within the three-player subgame: completing it after $p$'s cutoff controls $p$'s late offers, and the last remaining owner can then be added. In this case all three are finite before $h$. If $p$ has no such blocker, its two internal singleton entries are positive and $G_{ph}<0$ from the balance of its nonzero row. Append $h$ first by an independent uniform draw over an arbitrarily long fresh finite block, then complete the other two subgame owners' missing masses still later. Player $p$'s additional postcut pure offers are bounded by its old native Never offer up to a vanishing joining probability: before the fallback it receives own zero, after it receives the nonpositive $G_{ph}$, and only a tie with the diffuse $h$ can exceed zero. The other two tested subgame owners already face the surely finite $p$, so their complete caps and the subgame prescription converge to their original ones. All four physical clocks are finite, their training ranks may be the floor quantiles (including the final physical date of $p$'s support), and $h$ is strictly after $p$ in both lists. Waiting forever or using a date strictly later than $p$'s last training rank reproduces $h$'s assigned first coalition on every validation draw. If the tester uses only such dates on these observations, then its displacement vanishes, while the three subgame rows' conditional violations tend to zero. Taking a limit of finite-alphabet priors yields $0\in d_\lambda(P_N)$. In particular testers using only $h$'s own labels and Never cannot separate, however the weights depend on the full observation. A separator has to test entry by $h$ at or before the terminal date of each such $p$-finite subgame construction. These are necessary conditions on a finite-rank tester, not a converse.

**A quantitative test on every balanced training grid.** Independently sample the validation clocks from exponential laws of rates $z$. For each owner choose its deterministic $k$th training label anywhere with nominal cumulative probability strictly between $(k-1)/N$ and $k/N$; different owners may share dates. Interlacing holds at every cut because the deterministic count differs from the nominal cumulative count by less than one. With independent systematic validation shifts the three priced rows have zero actual prescriptions and zero offers at every training date and Never, by $G_0 z=0$. Write $E$ for the resulting observation and set $t=\infty$ for a Never or Late test. The $j$th foreign singleton component of $d_\lambda$ is exactly
$$
 z_j\Big(\sum_t\lambda_{E,t}\frac{1-e^{-(1-z_h)t}}{1-z_h}-\sum_t\lambda_{E,t}\Big);
$$
all eleven other components vanish. Therefore every potential separator has positive total weight on **every** such observation, and its weighted mean of $e^{-(1-z_h)t}$ on each grid must be strictly less than $z_h$. Tied ranks on different owners have the same value and their weights can be coalesced.

**Which interior ranks a separating tester must request.** Fix $N\ge2$, a nonempty proper set $H$ of recipients and its complement $J$. Suppose some $p\in J$ has a weak foreign singleton blocker anywhere in the native table. The all-four-finite rank test passes if every recipient in $H$ may request any $H$-owned label, any owner's first or last label, Never, Late, or any date weakly after the earliest last label of $J$, while all recipients in $J$ keep their full conditional tests. In the one-recipient polar above, $H=\{h\}$ gives $0\in d_\lambda(P_N)$ for every tester supported on these requests, including arbitrary observation-dependent weights. This cut statement needs no positive-kernel hypothesis.

In the restricted native game on $J$, choose an approximate equilibrium with the prescribed $p$ clock finite. The at-most-three-player theorem applies to the translated $p$ singleton column, and the dimension-independent diffuse completion at that column gives the finite clock as above. Truncate finite tails and bound $p$'s support at vanishing uniform response cost. If $p$ has a blocker within $J$, complete that blocker's missing mass first on a fresh diffuse block, then the remaining $J$ clocks, then $H$. Otherwise append a blocker from $H$ on the diffuse block first, then all other missing clocks. Every $H$ clock is strictly after all possible $p$ exits. Other $J$ owners face the original finite $p$. Conditional on all its rivals surviving the cutoff, $p$'s new late offers are at most its old native default of zero up to the diffuse tie error. Thus the four-finite suffix has complete regret at most $\varepsilon$ in every $J$ row. Prepend one head date where each owner may quit or use its suffix. Draw independent systematic training and validation grids for all the resulting marginals. Move each first training label to the head and each last to a vacant marker just beyond that owner's fixed suffix support, chosen before varying the head probabilities. These moves preserve interlacing. Before the old first label the training count was zero and the validation count at most one. After the old last label the counts were $N$ and at least $N-1$. The modified intervals are disjoint. A date at or after the earliest $J$ marker is passive for every $h\in H$ because one rival has certainly exited. Every interior $H$ label is at the head or after all possible $p$ exits. Thus every allowed request for $H$ gives its owner's head or passive first-coalition vector.

For any fixed nonnegative tester weights on these requests, let $m_h$ and $L_h$ denote the expected head and total weight of $h\in H$. Both depend continuously on the tentative head probabilities. For fixed training shifts the finite-support quantile lists are locally constant except at a finite set of shift thresholds, and the validation grids are independent of the observation. Use the head-choice maps $q_h=(m_h+\delta/2)/(L_h+\delta)$ for $\delta>0$, together with the ordinary mixed best-reply correspondences for $J$ in the head game. Kakutani's theorem and a limit as $\delta\downarrow0$ give $L_h q_h=m_h$ simultaneously. Against these rivals, the weighted requests and the weighted prescription of $h$ therefore give the same entire first-coalition vector. Each $J$ owner has complete regret at most $\varepsilon$ by its head best reply and suffix guarantee. Compactness of the finite order simplex as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ preserves all $J$ inequalities and all these zero displacement vectors. Applying the finite alternative to all allowed conditional gains gives the stated simultaneous feasibility.

On a table where every row has a weak blocker, this excludes a whole class of global separation certificates. Form a directed graph from any tester whose total weighted gain is strictly positive on every prior. Discard Never, Late and requests tied to any first or last label. For each remaining positively weighted request of $i$, choose any owner $j$ with a label at that rank and include $i\to j$ if $i\ne j$. **This graph must be strongly connected for every choice at ties.** A proper nonempty set $H$ without an outgoing edge would have only the allowed requests above. In particular, for every such cut a separator must sometimes ask from $H$ for a rank carrying exclusively interior labels of $J$, after the common head and before every $J$ marker. For this refinement one may count any unprotected request provisionally as passive when defining $m_h,L_h$. If none of them receives positive weight on the resulting observations, the actual displacements also vanish there.

**Each donor must be tested by a negative recipient.** Fix a column $p$ having a nonempty negative recipient set $B_p=\{i\ne p:G_{ip}<0\}$, and suppose row $p$ has a weak blocker. Use $H=B_p$ and let $p$ alone quit uniformly on a long first suffix block. Put its weak blocker alone on a second diffuse block, then the other owners later. Player $p$ earns zero, and its complete cap tends to zero by the blocker. Every $i\notin B_p\cup\{p\}$ earns $G_{ip}\ge0$ and can obtain only a mixture of zero and $G_{ip}$ up to a vanishing tie error. Now every interior label except $p$'s lies at the head or after $p$ has exited. The same joint head calculation forces some $h\in B_p$ to request a strictly interior label of $p$ alone. On a possible counterexample matrix every column has a negative entry. A separating graph with four edges must therefore be a directed Hamilton cycle whose four entries are negative. Among the 21 surviving strict sign chambers, $(2,5,3,4),(2,5,8,5),(2,4,9,5),(2,12,9,6)$ have no such cycle and require at least five distinct directed pairs. Of the others, fourteen have exactly one eligible cycle and three have two. These counts follow by checking the six cyclic orders starting with owner zero in each sign representative.
