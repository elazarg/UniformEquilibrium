# Continuous-point audit of `AGKRS_3_4.md`

Reviewer: `CODEX_CAUCHY`

## Verdict

The atom-free-neighborhood sentence in the original Section 4.3 is false, but
the proposed small-mass-interval repair is valid.  After making the
original/completed-profile distinction explicit, it proves the continuous
support clause SP.2(b) from ordinary vanishing-error equilibria, even when
jumps accumulate at the continuous point.

This audit concerns only the forward S.1/S.2/S.3 implication and only its
continuous-point step.  It does not review the discretization into S.3, the
terminal-jump construction, or any converse implication.

## Exact claim checked

Normalize the never payoff to zero.  Let `x^k` be behavioral
`epsilon_k`-equilibria, where `epsilon_k -> 0`.  Complete them at dates tending
to infinity to obtain absorbing profiles `bar x^k`, and assume their absorption
paths converge weakly to an absorption path `pi`.  The completion changes an
event of probability `rho_k -> 0`.

Fix a player `i` and `t in T(pi) \ {1}`.  Put

$$
a_i=r_i(\{i\}),\qquad g=\gamma_t^i(\pi)-a_i.
$$

The claim is

$$
\dot\pi_t(\{i\})>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g\le 0.
$$

Together with the separately proved Quit-now inequality `g >= 0`, this is
exactly SP.2(b) with zero error.

## Fully quantified small-mass proof

Let `M > 0` bound the absolute value of every terminal reward.  The case
`g <= 0` is done, so suppose `g > 0`.  Write `b=1-t>0`.

### 1. The point `t` has no atom

Since `t in T(pi)`, the total cumulative mass at `t` is `t`.  The absorption
path inequality gives total mass at least `s` for every `s<t`.  Taking left
limits shows that the total left mass at `t` is at least `t`; monotonicity makes
it at most the value `t`.  Hence the total jump at `t` is zero.  Every
coordinate jump is nonnegative, so every coordinate has zero mass at `t`.

In particular, the total absorption measure `mu` and the singleton-`i`
measure have no atom at `t`.

### 2. Choose one interval with quantitatively small mass

The assumption `dot pi_t({i}) > 0`, where the derivative is the lower right
derivative used in Definition 4.13, gives numbers `lambda>0` and `h_0>0` such
that

$$
\pi_s(\{i\})-\pi_t(\{i\})\ge\lambda(s-t)
\qquad(t<s<t+h_0).
\tag{1}
$$

There are only countably many atoms among the finitely many coordinate
measures.  Since `mu({t})=0`, choose a common continuity point `r` with

$$
t<r<\min\{t+h_0,\,t+b/4\},
\tag{2}
$$

$$
r-t<\frac{gb}{256M},
\qquad
\mu([t,r])<\frac{gb}{256M}.
\tag{3}
$$

The last requirement follows from continuity from above of the finite measure
`mu` at the atom-free point `t`.  Notice that no atom-free neighborhood is
being asserted.

Let `mu_k` be the total absorption measure of `bar x^k`.  Weak convergence and
the atom-free endpoints imply

$$
\mu_k([t,r])\longrightarrow\mu([t,r]).
\tag{4}
$$

For player `i`, define the signed terminal reward measures

$$
\nu_i^k(A)=\sum_S r_i(S)\mu_{k,S}(A),
\qquad
\nu_i(A)=\sum_S r_i(S)\mu_S(A).
$$

Coordinatewise weak convergence and the absence of an atom at `t` also give

$$
\nu_i^k([t,1])\longrightarrow\nu_i([t,1]).
\tag{5}
$$

For all sufficiently large `k`, therefore,

$$
\mu_k([t,r])<\frac{gb}{128M},
\qquad
\left|\nu_i^k([t,1])-\nu_i([t,1])\right|<\frac{gb}{128}.
\tag{6}
$$

We may also require

$$
\rho_k<\frac{gb}{128M}
\tag{7}
$$

and that the artificial completion clock is larger than `r`.

### 3. Uniform control over every source row in the interval

Consider any genuine stage `n` of `x^k` whose pre-stage absorption clock is

$$
u=1-s_n^k\in[t,r].
$$

The same stage occurs unchanged in `bar x^k`, because the completion clock is
larger than `r`.  Let `p_n^k` be its conditional one-stage absorption
probability.  Its unconditional stage mass is the atom of `mu_k` at `u`, so

$$
s_n^kp_n^k\le\mu_k([t,r]).
$$

As `s_n^k=1-u >= 1-r > b/2`, (6) yields

$$
p_n^k<\frac{g}{64M}.
\tag{8}
$$

Let `Q_n^k` be player `i`'s payoff from Quitting surely at this stage.  This
differs from `a_i` only if an opponent also Quits.  That probability is at
most `p_n^k`; hence

$$
|Q_n^k-a_i|\le 2Mp_n^k<\frac g{32}.
\tag{9}
$$

Let `bar V_n^k` be the prescribed conditional payoff in the completed
profile.  For an absorption path generated by an absorbing profile, the
pre-stage conditional value has the exact reward-measure formula

$$
\bar V_n^k=\frac{\nu_i^k([u,1])}{1-u}.
\tag{10}
$$

Since `t` has no atom and its total mass is `t`,

$$
\gamma_t^i(\pi)=\frac{\nu_i([t,1])}{b}.
\tag{11}
$$

The local numerator change satisfies

$$
\left|\nu_i^k([u,1])-\nu_i^k([t,1])\right|
\le M\mu_k([t,r]).
\tag{12}
$$

Using (2), (3), (6), `1-u>b/2`, and
`|nu_i([t,1])| <= Mb`, the elementary quotient estimate gives, uniformly in
every such `n`,

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\bar V_n^k-\gamma_t^i(\pi)|
&\le
\frac{M\mu_k([t,r])+
 |\nu_i^k([t,1])-\nu_i([t,1])|}{1-u}
 +\frac{M(r-t)}{1-u}\\
&<\frac g{32}+\frac g{64}<\frac g{16}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

The constants are deliberately loose; making the two bounds in (6) smaller
if needed gives the displayed strict final inequality.

Let `V_n^k` now denote the prescribed conditional payoff in the original
profile `x^k`, which is the value entering the refusal lemma.  The completion
changes outcomes on an event of unconditional probability at most `rho_k`.
Conditioning at a stage of reach at least `b/2` gives

$$
|V_n^k-\bar V_n^k|\le\frac{2M\rho_k}{s_n^k}<\frac g{32}.
\tag{14}
$$

After harmlessly tightening the numerical choices in (3), (6), and (7),
(9), (13), and (14) imply the uniform bad-Quit inequality

$$
V_n^k-Q_n^k\ge \frac g2
\tag{15}
$$

for every genuine source date whose pre-stage clock belongs to `[t,r]`.

### 4. One refusal deviation removes all singleton mass in the interval

Apply the finite-set refusal lemma to arbitrary finite subsets of those dates.
Since `x^k` is an `epsilon_k`-equilibrium against complete behavioral
deviations, (15) gives

$$
\frac g2\sum_{n:\,1-s_n^k\in[t,r]}s_n^kq_{i,n}^k
\le\varepsilon_k.
\tag{16}
$$

Increasing finite exhaustion proves (16) for the countable set of all such
dates.  The completed profile agrees with the original at all of them, and
its completion atom lies beyond `r`.  Its singleton-`i` mass in `[t,r]` is
therefore bounded by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{k,i}([t,r])
&=\sum_{n:\,1-s_n^k\in[t,r]}
 s_n^kq_{i,n}^k\prod_{j\ne i}(1-q_{j,n}^k)\\
&\le\sum_{n:\,1-s_n^k\in[t,r]}s_n^kq_{i,n}^k
\le\frac{2\varepsilon_k}{g}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$

Weak convergence at the two atom-free endpoints yields

$$
\mu_i([t,r])=0.
\tag{18}
$$

But (1) gives

$$
\mu_i([t,r])
=\pi_r(\{i\})-\pi_t(\{i\})
\ge\lambda(r-t)>0,
$$

a contradiction.

Thus a positive singleton right derivative forces

$$
\gamma_t^i(\pi)\le a_i.
$$

This proves SP.2(b).  No neighborhood free of jumps is needed.

## Checks on the proposed repair

The quotient argument in
`notes/CODEX_ROOT__AGKRS_REFUSAL_COMPACTIFICATION_REPAIRS.md` is sound after
two details are made literal:

1. the denominator displacement is `u-t`, controlled by choosing the interval
   short (it is also bounded by the total path-mass increment because an
   absorption path satisfies total mass at least the clock); and
2. the tail quotient first computes the value in the completed absorbing
   profile, after which the `rho_k/(1-r)` conditional coupling transfers it
   back to the original approximate equilibrium used by the refusal lemma.

These are quantitative bookkeeping points, not further hypotheses.

## Failed wording retained as a boundary example

A continuous point can be approached from the right by jumps at times
`t+2^{-m}` with masses of order `2^{-2m}`.  Every right neighborhood then
contains a jump, while its total mass still tends to zero.  This refutes the
atom-free-neighborhood wording and illustrates exactly why the finite-measure
argument above is the correct replacement.
