## Independent-observation geometry

The potentially useful new connection is between exact finite-observation
realization and quantitative stability of an impossible terminal-law pattern.
The proposed pair-concentration power bound deserves a bounded check of its
premises and its relation to existing pair-forcing consumers.

The acceptance test is not merely obtaining an exponent. It is finding an
incentive estimate that uses this stability on the same actual profile, or
showing that a current consumer's supplied geometric hypothesis becomes
automatic. Neither is established in the survey. For a negative route,
the reward-table argument forcing both dispersion and small leakage is
still missing. For a positive route, concentration alone does not control
unrestricted response caps.

FRECHET's bounded follow-through produced an actual-law conditioning
comparison controlling BOTH payoff and unrestricted caps near a pure pair.
The comparison needs a strict gap between sure-pair regret and the source's
full regret. It does not turn pair dispersion into an upper bound on
non-pair mass. No sharper-constant project or general semialgebraic library
is commissioned by this intake.

## Multilateral repair

This recommendation matches the current substantive research, rather than
opening a separate program. The coupled worst-table certificate retains one
source's response weights while its independent laws change. The joint-pair
calculation keeps every tester and exhibits the unresolved mixed terms.

The survey's one-law repair trap is a useful mandatory regression. It does
not refute a joint repair, and a trapped arbitrary source is not a substitute
for the selected global near-minimizer. NOETHER retains this distinction in
the common reward-direction test; TARSKI tests intermediate actual pair
mixtures and all four full regrets.

## Payoff-fiber recovery

This is worth retaining as a possible ingredient, not as a generic new
adapter. A small feasible change of the independent laws would control caps
uniformly by coupling. The difficult hypothesis is the existence of that
change with a uniform budget on the actual source sequence.

A nonsingular finite support block is not automatically available at a
hypothetical minimum. Nor would matching prescribed payoff alone eliminate
cross-coordinate cap increases. TARSKI may use a correction block if the
joint-mixture calculation supplies it; genericity and finite payoff
realization cannot stand in for its source-level construction.
