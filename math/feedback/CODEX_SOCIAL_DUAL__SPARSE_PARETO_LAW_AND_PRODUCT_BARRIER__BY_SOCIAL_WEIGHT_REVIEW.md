# Independent review: sparse Pareto law and product barrier

Reviewer: SOCIAL_WEIGHT_REVIEW

Verdict: **REVISE.**

Theorem 2.1, the total support bound of four, the sharpness example, the
one-root support calculation, Lemma 4.1, and the strategic regression are
mathematically correct.  The revision is needed for two scope issues:

1. the Never refinement in Theorem 3.1 is ambiguous and must not claim that

2. the conjecture-facing discussion omits a stronger source-attached Pareto

## Theorem 2.1

The exact alternative is correct.  With

\[
G=\{\bar r(\omega)-s:\omega\in\Omega\},
\]

strict separation says exactly that either a vector \(\theta>0\) is
nonpositive on every generator or the cone of $G$ contains a nonzero
nonnegative vector.  In the second arm, normalizing its nonnegative conic
coefficients turns the Never generator $-s$ into an ordinary Never mass and
gives a probability law with

\[
u-s\in\mathbb R^I_{\geq0}\setminus\{0\}.
\]

The converse and exclusivity calculations are also correct.

The phrase “weak Pareto maximality” is convention-dependent.  The exact
order statement should be preferred: there is no $u\in\mathcal F$ with
$u\geq s$ and $u\ne s$.  Some literature calls that strong Pareto
efficiency rather than weak Pareto maximality.

## Conic support and its sharpness

Conic Carathéodory in \(\mathbb R^4\) gives a representation of the same
nonzero cone vector using at most four generators.  Normalization therefore
gives a Pareto law on at most four outcomes.  The general $n$-player bound
is $n$, not the affine $n+1$, exactly as claimed.

The Never bullet needs clarification.  The valid statement is:

> The compressed witness has total support at most four; if Never belongs to
> that compressed support, then at most three members of its support are
> nonempty coalitions.

Conic Carathéodory does not by itself show that an arbitrary witness having
positive Never mass can be compressed to four outcomes while retaining
Never.  Retaining one prescribed generator may require a separate argument
and is not proved in the note.  Read literally as a conditional count on the
compressed witness, the bullet is correct but tautological.

The sharpness table is valid.  The four displayed vectors are

\[
g_k=(4,-1,-1,-1)
\]

up to coordinate permutation, and their uniform mean is
$(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)$.  Any law on at most three outcomes omits some displayed
vector $T_\ell$.  At coordinate \(\ell\), every included displayed vector
has value $-1$, while every nondisplayed outcome has value at most zero.
If any displayed vector has positive mass, this coordinate is negative; if
none does, the moment is coordinatewise nonpositive.  Hence no such law can
weakly dominate zero with one strict coordinate.  This proves genuine
four-outcome sharpness.

The final summed-weight calculation also correctly proves that no positive
welfare separator exists for this table.

## Product and behavioral nonrealizability

The one-root support calculation is exact.  Sure quitters form a fixed core
$K$, fractional quitters generate its Boolean interval, and with no sure
quitter the nonempty support is the punctured Boolean cube of the fractional
set.  In Fin4 its possible nonzero cardinalities are $1,3,7,15$.

Lemma 4.1 is also correct under ordinary independent behavioral strategies.
Because absorption has total mass one, some natural date is the first date
with positive unconditional absorption.  Reach before that date is one and
the earlier roots are all Continue.  At the selected root every singleton
probability is zero:

* with no sure quitter, any positive quit probability would create a
  positive singleton atom, so absorption would be zero;
* with exactly one sure quitter, that player's singleton atom is positive;
* therefore at least two players quit surely.

Absorption is certain at this first active date and every supported coalition
contains the same fixed pair.  The four displayed pair coalitions have empty
intersection, so their uniform law is not generated by any ordinary
behavioral profile.  “Zero Never mass” and “total absorption one” are
redundant assumptions, but this does not affect the proof.

The note is appropriately cautious about closure: Lemma 4.1 applies to exact
zero singleton/Never masses and does not by itself exclude approximation by
laws with small leakage.

## Strategic regression

The two-player table in Section 5 is correct.  The pure pair outcome gives
$(2,2)>s=(1,1)$, but either player obtains $3$ by leaving the pure pair.
Using $u=(2,2)$ as a continuation makes all Continue a strict one-row root,
but an actual all-Continue profile has Never payoff zero.  Thus the example
cleanly separates a realizable Pareto law from Nash compatibility and from a
fixed-point continuation.

## Missing positive-minimum provenance

The most important correction is conjecture-facing rather than algebraic.
At a positive ordinary global minimum $z_*=(U,B)$, the checked singleton
margin gives

\[
D_*\leq B_i-s_i.
\]

Since $B_i=U_i+d_i$,

\[
U_i-s_i\geq D_*-d_i\geq0.
\]

For $n\geq2$, summing the right side gives

\[
\sum_i(D_*-d_i)=(n-1)D_*>0,
\]

so $U\geq s$ and $U\ne s$.  Moreover,
`exists_terminalSemanticLawCarrier_lift` and
`terminalSemanticLawCarrier_rewardMoment` in
`TerminalSemanticResetIncidenceReturn.lean` supply a joint-carrier law
\(\mu\) whose literal limiting reward moment is this same $U$.  That law is
the limit of actual behavioral outcome laws along one realizing subsequence.

Therefore a hypothetical positive-minimum counterexample already has a
source-attached Pareto law.  Conic Carathéodory can reweight at most four
outcomes—indeed, they may be selected from the positive support of this
limiting law—but that compression generally changes the law weights and its
reward moment along the positive Pareto ray.  It loses the literal law and
chronological provenance.

The note should distinguish these two objects:

* the joint-carrier Pareto law, which retains source-limit provenance but is
  not sparse in general; and
* the at-most-four-outcome conic compression, which is sparse but generally
  is not the source law and need not be behavioral-realizable.

This correction does not create a product/Nash consumer.  It does prevent
the reader from inferring that the only Pareto witness available in the hard
branch is the abstract nonrealizable law from Theorem 2.1.

## Quantitative source-attached strengthening

There is also a useful literal-law estimate.  Let \(\theta\geq0\), let at
least two coordinates be positive, put

\[
c=T_\theta-M_\theta>0,
\]

and let \((U,B,\mu)\) be a supplied joint ordinary minimum point.  The global
costate inequality gives

\[
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}
 \mu(\omega)\,
 \theta\mathbin\cdot(\bar r(\omega)-s)
\geq cD_*.
\]

Writing $K=|\Omega|$, some literal outcome satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mu(\omega)\,
\theta\mathbin\cdot(\bar r(\omega)-s)
\geq\frac{cD_*}{K}.}
\]

The product is positive, so this co-realizes positive source-law mass and a
positive weighted surplus at one outcome.  If all reward coordinates are
bounded in absolute value by $R>0$, then with
$T_\theta=\sum_i\theta_i$,

\[
\theta\mathbin\cdot(\bar r(\omega)-s)\leq2RT_\theta
\]

for a finite coalition.  Hence, provided the selected outcome is finite,

\[
\mu(\omega)\geq
\frac{cD_*}{2KRT_\theta},
\qquad
\theta\mathbin\cdot(\bar r(\omega)-s)
\geq\frac{cD_*}{K}.
\]

If $A_\theta=\theta\mathbin\cdot s\geq0$, Never has surplus
$-A_\theta\leq0$, so the positive selected outcome is necessarily a finite
coalition.  These constants require $R>0$ and $T_\theta>0$; both are
automatic in the stated positive-minimum, two-positive-weight setting after
choosing a positive reward bound.

This estimate may connect the social chamber directly to the minimum-atom
atlas.  It is stronger for provenance than an arbitrary reward-moment
selection, although it still supplies no same-date Nash inequality.
