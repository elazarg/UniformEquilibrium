## Exact contribution

For a four-player table, fix the 56 reward coordinates other than the four
own-singleton rewards. An explicit nonzero degree-144 polynomial in those
coordinates excludes equal strictly positive complete debts at every
date-zero product root with at least two sure quitters. Mixed optional
players and probability-boundary cases are included.

Combining this algebra with the existing all-player tie theorem at a positive
minimum of maximum regret gives strict separation from every such screened
root on a selected singleton fiber. Reward robustness and rational density
produce that fiber from any hypothetical counterexample. No favorable
screened root, generic input table, or additional strategic certificate is
assumed. The maximizing own-singleton coordinates need not be rational.

At a positive maximum-regret minimum, a law with no singleton mass first has
zero Never mass. The exact existing strict-margin realization theorem then
turns it into an unpadded screened root with the same prescribed payoffs,
unrestricted caps, and law, contradicting the polynomial exclusion.
Compactness gives the stronger actual-profile conclusion: every sufficiently
accurate near-minimizer has a common positive total prescribed singleton-mass
floor. This also holds uniformly on positive-gap portions of the fixed fiber.

The common-calendar construction can retain that floor on the same selected
profiles and weights. Its later proof uses the new fiber's data, not the
original membership-stretch identity. The construction replaces that old
ancestry; it does not preserve it by assertion.

Thus the zero-singleton minimum-law branch can be excluded for deciding
counterexample existence. This is not a claim about every original table,
nor a proof that generic games have equilibria. It supplies no fixed-date
singleton floor, response-singleton floor, or post-response cap control.

## Additional result and remaining issue

The independent harmonic argument at maximum-regret minima also passes. It
implies strict own-singleton payoff margins and a worst unit-cube minimum
regret strictly below one third. This numerical restriction is separate from
the main source reduction and does not make the regret vanish.

The remaining strategic question is to improve the full worst-player regret
of the singleton-bearing source while controlling every player's changed
response cap. That consumer is not supplied by the manuscript.

The companion's exact archived results were reproduced by the algebra
reviewer without extracting files or invoking its file-writing main block.
The mathematical proofs, rather than these finite checks, support the
universal conclusions. The source and archive were not edited.

The short manuscript's final proof link uses an absent
`screened_root_reduction/` directory; its correct target is the sibling full
proof linked above. This is a presentation issue, not a mathematical gap.
