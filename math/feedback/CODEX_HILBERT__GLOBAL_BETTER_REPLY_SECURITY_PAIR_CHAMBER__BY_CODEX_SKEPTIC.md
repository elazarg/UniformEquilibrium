## 1. Claim and original theorem actually checked

The game has finitely many nonempty players, bounded terminal rewards,
zero joint-Never payoff, and one independent complete stopping law per
player on T=ℕ∪{∞}. The strategy space is P(T) with its weak topology.
Under sᵢ=rᵢ({i})≥0 for every i and sⱼ>0 for at least one j, the claim is

    better-reply security ⇔ K_r ∩ {v : v≥s} = ∅,

where K_r is the closure of ACTUAL independent prescribed payoff vectors.
Under this exclusion Reny's theorem gives one exact Nash point in law
space, hence an exact terminal Nash profile against all behavioral
deviations. The pair-square-root constraint verifies the exclusion
throughout the explicit radius-1/100 reward ball.

I opened the [original 1999 paper](https://kylewoodward.com/blog-data/pdfs/references/reny-econometrica-1999A.pdf)
and directly read original pp. 1032–1036. Section 2 allows nonempty compact
strategy subsets of topological vector spaces and bounded payoffs.
Quasiconcavity includes convex strategy sets and own-strategy
quasiconcavity. Theorem 3.1 on p. 1033 assumes compactness, quasiconcavity,
and better-reply security. The definitions require one fixed strategy and
one neighborhood of opponents, uniformly over all profiles in that
neighborhood. The theorem has no finite-dimensional restriction and no
joint continuity requirement. These match the proposed application.

The [2022 corrigendum](https://ewerhart.net/files/2022%20Ewerhart%20Reny%20Etrica.pdf)
was read in full; it leaves Theorem 3.1 unaffected. The draft does not use
the corrected quasisymmetric results. Proposition 3.2 and reciprocal USC
are not being silently substituted for the theorem actually invoked.

P(T) is a nonempty compact metrizable convex subset of the locally convex
space of finite signed measures with weak topology. Compactness follows
from compact metrizability of T; the ambient weak topology is the one
generated by integrals of continuous functions. These facts are stronger
than the paper requires. Own payoff is affine in the complete law, so
quasiconcavity is literal, even though joint payoff continuity fails.

## 2. Agency and unrestricted responses: PASS

Before the first Quit there is exactly one public action history of each
length: everyone has continued. A behavioral strategy supplies a private
hazard on that history. Its first-Quit law therefore has an ordinary
complete stopping-law representation, including Never. Conversely any
complete law is realized by the conditional hazards of its finite masses.
Off-path actions after absorption are immaterial. Private randomization
of distinct players remains independent. No public random variable is
introduced by this representation.

The source declarations explicitly omit the incidental Nontrivial-player
assumption, so there is no hidden two-player restriction in the use here.

For fixed finite t, the deterministic response payoff Fᵢ(t) depends only
on finitely many opponents' finite masses and finite-head survival
probabilities. Finite singletons in T are clopen. Thus this payoff is
continuous under mere weak convergence, including all ties at t.
The exact declaration is
`quittingTerminalPayoff_update_compactStoppingLawProfile_finiteTime_tendsto`

They give exactly

    Fᵢ(t) → Vᵢ + sᵢ∏[j≠i]aⱼ.

When sᵢ≥0 this limit is at least the Never payoff Vᵢ. Therefore the
complete cap is sup over finite dates. For α<Bᵢ choose one t with
Fᵢ(t)>α; continuity gives an opponent neighborhood where this SAME date

## 3. Payoff-graph factorization: PASS

The relevant discontinuity is at the joint all-infinite event, not at
finite collisions. For fixed H, early absorption contribution P_H and
joint survival R_H are continuous finite-head functions. On R_H>0,
conditioning each clock on Tᵢ≥H and subtracting H preserves independence:
the joint survival event is the product of those coordinate events.
Thus the suffix used in

    U(μ)=P_H(μ)+R_H(μ)U(μ|H)

really is an admissible independent profile, even when several individual
Never atoms are positive. Its payoff is not selected from a convexified
coalition-moment relaxation.

As H tends to infinity, R_H(μ)→A(μ) and P_H(μ)→U(μ).
For A=0 the error bound by M R_H gives continuity of the prescribed payoff.
No analogous continuity of the full cap is assumed.

For A>0, the diagonal choice of H_k and n_k is legitimate: at each fixed
H_k only finitely many continuous scalar expressions need approximation,
and n_k can also be required to increase and approximate the payoff limit.
For all large k, R_{H_k}(μ^{n_k})>0. The resulting suffix payoff sequence
is bounded in finite-dimensional reward space, and a convergent subsequence
has limit v∈K_r by definition of that closure. Passing to limits gives

    u*=U(μ)+A(μ)v.

The proof needs no uniform tightness, absence of ties, convergence of Never
atoms, or common chronology across the suffixes. The shift-by-n converse
at all-Never also holds when the chosen realizing profiles vary with n:
after shifting, every marginal has zero mass in any fixed finite head for
all sufficiently large n, while terminal rewards are unchanged.

## 4. Better-reply-security equivalence and Nash conclusion: PASS

For A=0 the graph limit equals the prescribed payoff. At a non-Nash point
some cap strictly exceeds that payoff, and Section 2 supplies the fixed
secure response required by the original definition.

For A>0, exclusion of K_r above s gives a coordinate vᵢ<sᵢ. The precise
strict inequality is

    Bᵢ−u*ᵢ=dᵢ−Avᵢ≥A(sᵢ−vᵢ)>0.

This strict margin is exactly what permits a fixed finite response and an
open opponent neighborhood. There is no unjustified claim that a
graph-closure payoff itself is attained by the limit profile.

Conversely, v∈K_r with v≥s is a graph payoff at all-Never. That strategy
profile is non-Nash because one singleton is positive. Its cap is sᵢ for
every player under the nonnegative-singleton assumption. Since the original
opponents belong to any neighborhood, no strategy secures above vᵢ.
This refutes better-reply security at that graph point.

All hypotheses of original Theorem 3.1 have therefore been constructed.
A pure point of this law game consists of independent possibly mixed laws,
not deterministic quitting dates. The terminal Nash conclusion is exact
and unrestricted. The checked
`quittingGame_exists_uniformEquilibriumPayoff_iff_terminalNash_all_errors`
in `UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Terminal/TargetTail/TerminalUniformPayoffSelection.lean`
then gives the advertised UE consequence. It need not assert uniform
finite-horizon convergence of deviations under that same exact profile.

## 5. Pair exclusion, perturbation, and pure-clock test: PASS

I inspected `twoDisjointFirstStoppingPairMasses_sqrt_sum_le_one` and its
defining summands in `MathUE/Probability/IndependentFirstStoppingPair.lean`.
They are exactly the masses of T₀=T₁=t<T₂,T₃ and T₂=T₃=t<T₀,T₁,
with arbitrary complete independent laws. Their inequality is the one
used here: √a+√b≤1. Singleton masses satisfy ∑xᵢ≤1−a−b because all
other finite coalitions and joint Never have nonnegative mass.

For base payoff coordinates ≥c=49/50, each pair yields
4a−b≥24/25 and 4b−a≥24/25. Substitution gives
a,b≥8/25>1/4, contrary to the square-root inequality. These constraints
are closed and persist along any convergent subsequence of terminal law
vectors. Thus their use for K_{r⁰}, not only the actual payoff image,
is justified.

For a fixed perturbed table the maximum coordinate difference Δ is strictly
below 1/100. Every actual payoff differs from its base payoff by at most
Δ. Taking a realizing sequence and a convergent subsequence of base payoffs
therefore gives v⁰ᵢ≥vᵢ−1/100≥sᵢ(r)−1/100≥49/50. This contradicts the
previous exclusion and proves the full open-ball claim. No uniform
selection of equilibria over the reward ball is needed or asserted.

The weighted-social separation is also correct: the average of the two
pure-pair payoff vectors dominates the singleton vector, with coordinate
margin at least 1/4−2/100. The correlated lottery is not replaced by a
legal independent profile. Reciprocal USC fails at the base graph point
obtained by delaying pure pair A; the original definition on p. 1034
indeed requires equality in every coordinate in that case.

I checked every join/leave case for the proposed e=1/200 perturbation.
An exact Fraction regression enumerated all sixteen first-coalition cases;
each has a strict same-date join/leave improvement, with minimum best gain
1/200. A separate exact check of all 81 deterministic profiles with dates
{0,1,Never} found none Nash. The ordinary first-coalition argument, not
that bounded enumeration, excludes ALL deterministic timing profiles:
joiners may switch to the actual earliest date, and leavers switch to Never
while at least one other first quitter remains. All-Never is separately
defeated by a singleton Quit. The perturbation is strictly within the ball.

## 6. Explicit adversarial boundaries

- One player, s>0: K_r=[0,s] contains s, and BRS fails at the all-Never
  graph point with limiting payoff s. Immediate Quit is nevertheless exact
  Nash. Thus the theorem correctly characterizes BRS, not Nash existence.
- All singleton rewards zero and every reward zero: every profile is Nash,
  so BRS is vacuous, while K_r contains s=0. This verifies that the
  positive-singleton hypothesis in necessity cannot simply be dropped.
- One player, singleton −1: Never gives zero while every finite date gives
  −1. This directly falsifies the finite-response representation of the cap
  without the nonnegative-singleton hypothesis. The draft retains it.
- Zero joint reach but positive owner-deleted reach: let one player quit
  surely at date zero and all others Never. The graph-payoff argument
  correctly uses joint survival, while response security is checked from
  finite responses; no false deleted-survival or cap-continuity conclusion
  is taken from the zero joint reach.
- Delayed ties: delaying an entire pair retains its tie and its payoff.
  The graph factorization handles precisely this escape rather than
  replacing it by a singleton or an independent lottery over pairs.
- Correlation: half mass on each target pair would violate √a+√b≤1.
  Its convex payoff vector is not an admissible suffix witness v.

None of these attempts falsifies the stated mathematical theorem.

    Mᵢⱼ=rᵢ({j})−rᵢ({i}).
