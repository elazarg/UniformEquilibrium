# Review of CODEX_CEDAR paid-row re-entry, Section 56

Reviewer: `CODEX_RAMSEY`

Verdict: **PASS in the stated necessary-condition scope.**

I checked the following claim.  For two behavioral profiles `sigma,tau` and
one player `m`, let `E_m` be the supremum, over every behavioral strategy of
`m`, of the absolute payoff difference generated by the two opponent
environments.  If

```text
U_m(tau) <= U_m(sigma)-a,
```

then

```text
E_m(sigma,tau) >= a+d_m(sigma)-d_m(tau).
```

This is exact.  The unrestricted cap is the supremum of the same payoff
family in each environment, so the standard supremum inequality gives
`|B_m(sigma)-B_m(tau)|<=E_m`.  The displayed prescribed inequality gives

```text
B_m(sigma)-B_m(tau)
  >= a+d_m(sigma)-d_m(tau).
```

Since an absolute value dominates the signed difference, no missing sign
hypothesis is needed even when the right side is negative.  Using
`d_m(sigma)>=0` and `d_m(tau)<=eta` gives `E_m>=a-eta` with no extra constant.

The coupling corollary is also exact under its explicitly uniform
quantifier.  For **every** mover strategy, a coupling with mismatch at most
the same `epsilon` makes the two bounded payoff variables differ in
expectation by at most `2*M*epsilon`; taking the supremum over strategies
therefore gives `E_m<=2*M*epsilon`.  Thus `(a-eta)/(2M)` is the correct lower
bound when `M>0` and `a>eta`.

The full-replacement specialization has the right orientation.  With
`sigma=F_n`, `tau=P_n`, and `a=G_n=U_m(F_n)-U_m(P_n)`, opponents are identical,
so `E_m=0`, while

```text
d_m(P_n)=d_m(F_n)+G_n.
```

The right side of the general lower bound is therefore exactly zero.  For a
low-debt target `T_n`, the stated `o(1)` payoff and debt errors give
`liminf E_m(F_n,T_n)>=lim G_n` whenever the previously established limit of
`G_n` is used.  The two-player sharpness examples realize respectively the
target-debt and opponent-movement alternatives exactly.

The scope is correctly narrow.  This proves a necessary, uniform
cross-opponent movement cost.  It constructs neither that movement nor an
exact Bellman edge, charged path, or payoff return.
