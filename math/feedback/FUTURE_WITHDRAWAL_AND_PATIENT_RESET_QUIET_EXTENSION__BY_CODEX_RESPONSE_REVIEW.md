## Complete-clock proof

The clock transformations are legal. Each child response uses only its
original private clock, an independent replica of the outsider law, and
the fixed table. The common outsider clock is a coupling among separate
unilateral experiments; it is not a correlated prescribed child profile.
Before absorption there is one live history per date, so these independent
laws implement unrestricted behavioral replacements.

For a fixed outsider deadline t, patient replacement on T_i>=t preserves
every earlier child outcome. At or after t, withdrawing a member of a
nonsingleton first coalition leaves the other members at the original
date. With a singleton first owner, the eventual outcome is the first
later opponent coalition if one exists; otherwise it has payoff
a_i=max(0,s_i). In the nonnegative-singleton case that last payoff is
obtained by an actual finite quit whose date tends to infinity, not by
Never itself. The minimum c_i includes exactly these alternatives.

A child outside the original first coalition eventually makes no change
to that coalition. Thus the patient term is ell_i(A), or a larger limit,
and appears in both the future F row and the join J row. Joint Never gives
the extra a_i term in N. The four-case proof is exhaustive, including ties
and t=Never. Hidden later coalitions are retained in the singleton case.

Every finite patient replacement is a genuine child deviation whose
expected gain is at most that child's complete regret. Its gains are
uniformly bounded by twice a reward bound and have a pointwise limit.
Bounded convergence gives the same bound on the limiting expected gain.
The outsider supremum is taken only after proving the comparison for each
complete outsider law. No interchange of a supremum and a limit occurs.

Necessity for this particular limiting pointwise inequality is valid:
take joint Never for N, first A at date 1 with outsider date 0 for F,
and first A at date 0 for J. When A is a singleton, select the hidden
later coalition attaining c_i, or all opponents Never if a_i attains it.
Other patient terms are zero in the limit, so each row is recovered exactly.

## Cancellation and the terminal-only boundary

The plain-cancellation variant is valid for every nonincreasing evaluation.
Its floor is nonpositive and includes zero. If a singleton withdrawer
reveals a later reward, discounting that reward cannot lower it below
the current evaluation weight times the nonpositive floor. For an early
outsider response, the residual splits into two nonnegative coefficients
multiplying the old Never residual and the modified F residual. This
requires the old Never row, exactly as stated.

The stronger patient-reset criterion cannot simply be used evaluation by
evaluation. Here is a complete exact falsifier to that omitted implication.
Take child S={0,1}, outsider 2, with reward table

| A | r_0 | r_1 | r_2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| 1 | 2 | 0 | 1 |
| 2 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| 01 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 02 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| 12 | 0 | 0 | 1 |

Never pays zero. Patient weights lambda=(0,0), mu=(1,0) satisfy every
row: a_0=c_0=1, singleton ell_0 is zero, and ell_0({0,1})=1.
Let both children quit surely at date 1. At horizon H=3 their prescribed
child-0 payoff is 1/3 and its complete cap is 2/3, hence d_0=1/3.
The quiet outsider has payoff zero and complete cap 2/3, so d_2=2/3.
The terminal numerical coefficient one therefore fails at this horizon.
For discount factor d in (0,1), the corresponding regrets are
max(d-d²,d²) and d, also violating the proposed evaluated bound.
At terminal evaluation both relevant regrets equal one, as the theorem
correctly predicts.

The two raw cones for a fixed child are incomparable. This concerns their
feasible reward tables, not merely their different regret constants.

For one child 0, take r({0})=(-1,-1), r({1})=(0,0),
r({0,1})=(0,0), with outsider 1. Patient weights lambda=0, mu=1 pass,
whereas the bidirectional F row is the impossible inequality 1<=0,

One consolidated theorem package is appropriate, sharing the independent
