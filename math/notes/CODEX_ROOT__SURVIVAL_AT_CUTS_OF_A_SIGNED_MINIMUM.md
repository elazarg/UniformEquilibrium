# Minimum survival at reached continuations of a signed four-player source

The [signed-price reduction](../exports/PRESCRIBED_SIGNS_AND_NATIVE_LIMITS_OF_FOUR_PLAYER_ESCAPE.md) starts from a native table with positive infimum of regret on absorbing profiles. An unrestricted minimizing source exists for each nonempty proper partition into winning and losing recipients. In the compact closure of the actual independent stopping laws, write $n_i$ for the marginal probability of Never, $U_i$ for the payoff, $B_i$ for the cap over all replies, and $e_i$ for the literal Never offer. Put $d_i=B_i-U_i$. At the smallest feasible price ρ, the winners have zero regret and at least one winning clock stops surely. For each losing recipient with weight $c_j>0$ one has $n_j>0$, $d_j=n_j(B_j-e_j)≤c_jρ$, with equality in the last inequality when $n_j<1$. Equivalently, paying a private bonus $c_jρ/n_j$ for choosing Never makes the prescribed law optimal for every owner. The following arguments use the actual conditional payoffs of these sources. The [three-winning-clock selection](CODEX_SOURCE_GATE__GIFTED_CAP_POSITIVE_OR_ZERO.md#which-winning-clocks-can-be-required-to-absorb) supplies the specified additional zero marginals when invoked.

## One small losing weight and the first-exit law in class 6

Let the native rewards satisfy $|Z_i(T)|\le M$, with $M\ge1$, and let $A>0$ be the infimum of total native regret on absorbing profiles. Fix the single winner $p$. Write $c_{(2)}$ for the middle of the three positive losing weights. Then the unrestricted minimum in the signed-price reduction satisfies

$$
  \frac{A}{\sum_{i\ne p}c_i}\le\rho_*\le R:=\frac{2M}{c_{(2)}} .
$$

The lower bound follows by summing the native debts at the eligible absorbing point. For the upper bound choose a least-weight loser $k$ and put
$a=Z_p(\{k\})$, $b=Z_p(\{p,k\})$, $g=Z_k(\{p\})$, $d=Z_k(\{p,k\})$, and $w=c_kR$. The other two losers always choose Never. Their native debts equal their finite-cap advantage over Never and are at most $2M\le c_iR$. The following clocks make the pivot surely finite with zero native regret and meet the remaining constraint at price $R$. A diffuse finite clock below means uniform on $L$ consecutive dates, with $L\to\infty$ in the actual-law carrier.

- If $g\ge0$, let $k$ choose Never and let $p$ use a diffuse finite clock. A finite reply by $k$ at date $t$ pays $g\Pr(T_p<t)+d\Pr(T_p=t)\le g+|d|/L$. Its native regret tends to zero.
- If $g<0$ and $d\le g$, let $p$ quit surely at the first date and $k$ choose Never. The opening and all later replies by $k$ pay $d$ and $g$, respectively.
- If $g<0$, $d>g$ and $b\ge a$, let $p$ quit surely at the first date and let $k$ quit there with probability $1-n$, where $n=\min(1,w/(d-g))$ is its Never probability. The pivot's first-date payoff is $(1-n)b$ and every later offer is $(1-n)a$. For $k$, the cap and Never offer are $d$ and $g$. Its regret is $n(d-g)\le w$, with equality when $n<1$.
- If $g<0$, $d>g$ and $b<a$, let $k$ quit at the first date with probability $1-n$, with $n=\min(1,w/(-g))$. Let $p$ use a diffuse finite clock strictly later. Waiting gives the pivot $(1-n)a$, and quitting first gives $(1-n)b$. The first-date offer of $k$ is zero. Its offer at a later date is at most $\max(d,0)/L$, and Never pays $g$. Its limiting regret is $n(-g)\le w$, with equality when $n<1$.

In all four cases the losing regret is $n_k(B_k-e_k)$, including at $n_k=1$. This proves feasibility. At **every** minimizing point the bonus-adjusted cap $b_i=U_i+c_i\rho_*$ of a losing recipient is nonnegative by the finite-block graft theorem. Consequently its actual native prescribed payoff satisfies $U_i\ge-c_iR$, and its native regret is at most $c_iR$. These bounds apply to the same one-winner source as its first-exit law. [Price feasibility and graft theorem](../exports/PRESCRIBED_SIGNS_AND_NATIVE_LIMITS_OF_FOUR_PLAYER_ESCAPE.md)

In strict class 6 use the census labeling. The unique mutually negative pair is $\{0,1\}$, and $Z_3(\{0\}),Z_3(\{1\})<0$. Choose either member $p$ as winner, write $j$ for its mate, and take $c_j=c_2=1$, $c_3=\varepsilon\in(0,1]$. Put $\delta=\min(-Z_3(\{0\}),-Z_3(\{1\}))>0$, $h=\max(0,Z_3(\{0,1\}))$, and $l=\min(1,A/(6M))>0$. Let $x$ be the probability in the limiting first-exit law that player 2 or 3 belongs to the quitting coalition. Every one-winner minimizer satisfies

$$
  x\ \ge\ \frac{(h+\delta)l-h-2M\varepsilon}{M+\delta}.
$$

Indeed $n_j\ge l$ since either $n_j=1$ or $2Mn_j\ge\rho_*\ge A/(2+\varepsilon)$. Couple the first exit with the one obtained by deleting the clocks of players 2 and 3 on the same trials. The two first coalitions agree except on an event of probability at most $x$. In the reduced pair, whenever $j$ chooses Never the surely finite winner $p$ exits alone. Hence the original law gives the two singleton columns of the pair combined mass at least $l-x$. The argument passes to actual-law limits by first moving any vanishing pivot Never mass to a finite date. Player 3 receives at most $h$ on the pair and at most $-\delta$ on either singleton. Thus $-2M\varepsilon\le U_3\le Mx+h(1-x)-(h+\delta)(l-x)$, as required. If $Z_3(\{0,1\})<0$, one can also take $u=\min(\delta,-Z_3(\{0,1\}))$ to get $x\ge(u-2M\varepsilon)/(M+u)$ directly. [Singleton census and labeling](../experiments/FIN4_SINGLETON_SIGN_CLASSES.py)

In particular, if $Z_3(\{0,1\})\le0$, choose $2M\varepsilon<\delta l/2$. Every minimizing law then has $x\ge\eta:=\delta l/[2(M+\delta)]$. This includes every choice of the other higher rewards. At the intermediate signed prices of the escape theorem, first take the inner near-minimizing limit at each price and then let its scale grow. Every joint accumulation law is a native minimizer, so the unedited reference laws eventually have outside mass at least $\eta/2$. This follows also by taking a diagonal sequence with inner debt errors $o(a/K)$ in the notation of that theorem.

At the first substantial date of each such inner sequence the probability of any earlier exit tends to zero at fixed prices. Consequently, on sufficiently accurate profiles, either at that date an outside player belongs to the first exit with unconditional probability at least $\eta/8$, or a first exit involving an outside player occurs **strictly later** with unconditional probability at least $\eta/4$. In the latter case the literal all-Continue continuation has conditional outside mass at least $\eta/4$ as well. This gives timing information at the first substantial date. The separately retained mark of a causalized packet can have earlier absorption. The funded coalition may still be $\{0,1\}$. Closing the case requires controlling sources that admit the stated outside participation. **Two different losing players must participate on every remaining six-entry assignment in class 6.** More generally write $J_{ik}=Z_i(\{i,k\})$ and $\Gamma_{ik}=Z_i(\{k\})$ for distinct players. Assume only

$$
  Z_3(\{0,1\})\le0,\qquad J_{2p}\le\Gamma_{2p},\qquad J_{p3}<\Gamma_{p3}.
$$

This includes all 16 surviving six-entry position sets at the labeled sign pattern, in either assignment of the outsiders. Every participating pair reward is zero on those templates and their only possible passive pair entry is paid to a mate on a different pair. The argument works at every magnitude in the sign class, including nongeneric tables. [Six-entry templates](../exports/FIVE_NONSINGLETON_REWARDS_AND_THE_SIX_ENTRY_FRONTIER.md)

The outside mass just proved rules out having no finite loser or having the mate $j$ as the sole finite loser. If 2 alone has finite mass among the losers, its Never offer is $e_2=\Gamma_{2p}>0$. Its reward is $\Gamma_{2p}$ when the pivot exits alone, zero when 2 exits alone, and at most $\Gamma_{2p}$ when they exit together. Thus $U_2\le e_2$, contradicting $U_2-e_2=(1-n_2)c_2\rho_*/n_2>0$.

If 3 alone has finite mass among the losers, player $p$ can wait forever for $(1-n_3)\Gamma_{p3}>0$. On a trial where 3 chooses a finite date the pivot earns $\Gamma_{p3}$ when 3 quits strictly first, $J_{p3}<\Gamma_{p3}$ if they tie, and zero when the pivot quits first. On the other trials it receives zero. Equality with the best response therefore forces the entire finite mass of 3 to exit strictly before $p$ in the limiting law. The only first exits are $\{3\}$ with probability $1-n_3$ and $\{p\}$ with probability $n_3$. In the native data $U_3=n_3\Gamma_{3p}$, $e_3=\Gamma_{3p}$, and $B_3-U_3=n_3(B_3-e_3)$, hence $B_3=0$. Since $n_3<1$,

$$
 n_3=\varepsilon\rho_*/(-\Gamma_{3p}),\qquad b_3=0.
$$

The uniform price bound makes $n_3$ arbitrarily small with $\varepsilon$. Both $b_p=(1-n_3)\Gamma_{p3}$ and $b_j=(1-n_3)\Gamma_{j3}+n_3\Gamma_{jp}+\rho_*$ are then positive, since $\Gamma_{p3},\Gamma_{j3}>0$. This would give precisely one or two zero coordinates at the minimum. The one- and two-zero cases of the [finite-block graft argument](CODEX_SOURCE_GATE__GIFTED_CAP_POSITIVE_OR_ZERO.md) exclude both for arbitrary payoffs. All margins used to make these choices are uniform over minimizers.

There is a complementary test under the generic joining-increment and reward-column conditions of the same lemma. Choose $\varepsilon$ so $c$ lies outside every span of at most three native reward columns (here $c_p=0$). The generic three-row projection minors prevent those spans from containing the coordinate axis of player 3. Each span therefore meets the line $c_j=c_2=1$, $c_3=\varepsilon$ at most once.

For each $q\in\{2,3\}$ suppose at least one of the necessary inequalities below fails.

$$
   J_{pq}>0,\qquad J_{pq}\ge\Gamma_{pq},\qquad
   J_{qp}>\max(0,\Gamma_{qp}).
$$

For example, $Z_p(\{p,2\})<0$ and $Z_p(\{p,3\})<0$ suffice. Keep $Z_3(\{0,1\})\le0$. The positive outside mass already excludes both the pivot alone and its mate as its sole participating loser. If instead there were a sole loser $q\in\{2,3\}$ with finite mass, the prescribed first exit would lie on the three columns $\{p\},\{q\},\{p,q\}$. An all-zero effective cap would put $-\rho_*c$ in their span. The signed continuation lemma therefore gives a strictly positive effective cap in every coordinate. At the first date of nonvanishing activity, a lone active owner would have immediate Quit offer zero. Thus $p$ and $q$ both act, at hazards $u_p,u_q$, and their effective caps equal their immediate Quit offers $b_p=u_qJ_{pq}$ and $b_q=u_pJ_{qp}$. The pivot can wait forever for $(1-n_q)\Gamma_{pq}$, and $u_q\le1-n_q$ since all earlier marginal mass vanishes. The loser's augmented Never offer is $\Gamma_{qp}+c_q\rho_*/n_q$, since its deletion leaves only the surely finite pivot. These comparisons give all three displayed inequalities. For each loser with $n_i<1$, the difference $U_i-e_i=(1-n_i)c_i\rho_*/n_i$ is strictly positive. Deleting its clock on the same trials bounds this difference by $2M$ times its first-exit participation probability. At least two losers therefore actually participate. Compactness of the joint minimizing tuples and their first-exit laws bounds the second largest such probability away from zero for this fixed table and these weights. They can participate on different dates. Existence for the unrestricted 44 higher rewards in class 6 remains open.

## A grand first-exit mark on the six-entry frontier

Take the [six-entry template](../exports/FIVE_NONSINGLETON_REWARDS_AND_THE_SIX_ENTRY_FRONTIER.md) with either outsider assignment, either withdrawing mate $u$, and arbitrary positive prices. Write $a$ for the owner paid on the triple $\{u,v,a\}$ and $b$ for the other outsider. Assume strict foreign singleton comparisons and nonzero determinants on all four principal singleton triples. Suppose either the withdrawal at $u$ is the negative participating entry $-N$ at $\{u,v,b\}$, or there exists $h>0$ with $h^\top\Gamma\ge0$. Under $A>0$, fix **any** one-winner partition and positive loss weights. Every minimizing signed-price source has grand first-exit probability bounded below by a common positive constant at that table and those weights.

All joining-pair rewards vanish, so the joining-increment matrix equals $-\Gamma$. The [signed continuation lemma](CODEX_SOURCE_GATE__GIFTED_CAP_POSITIVE_OR_ZERO.md) gives either $b_i>0$ for all four effective caps or $b_i=0$ for all four (here $b_i$ denotes a cap). In the positive case, at least two players act at the first date of nonvanishing activity, since a lone quitter's immediate offer is zero. Each acting player's immediate Quit offer equals its positive cap. An active owner other than $a$ receives a positive participating reward only at the grand exit, so all its rivals must have positive hazards there. This gives a positive grand first-exit probability. [First positive opening](CODEX_ROOT__INDEPENDENT_TAILS_AT_THE_FIRST_POSITIVE_OPENING.md)

In the negative participating implementation the forced offer at $u$ is $q_vq_b[-N(1-q_a)+R_uq_a]$, where $R_u>0$ is its grand reward. At all sufficiently small nonzero hazard vectors an active $u$ therefore has nonpositive offer. When $u$ is inactive each remaining active owner has offer zero, since every possible nonzero participating payoff requires $u$ to quit. The [local Quit-offer theorem](../exports/LOCAL_PRODUCT_LOW_EXCLUDES_ALLNEVER.md) gives a nonblank native zero-regret law, which excludes an all-zero effective cap by the signed continuation lemma.

For the positive passive implementation of $N$, every proper nonsingleton reward is nonnegative. Thus $h^\top Z(T)\ge0$ at every proper exit. With an all-zero effective cap the absorbing first-exit law pays $h^\top U=-\rho_*h^\top c<0$, taking $c_p=0$ for the winner. This requires positive grand first-exit probability. Compactness of the minimizing tuples with their first-exit laws gives the common mass floor in both cases.

The conclusion also specifies a coalition in an **actual uniform-escape packet**. Along the intermediate signed prices selected in the [signed escape theorem](../exports/PRESCRIBED_SIGNS_AND_NATIVE_LIMITS_OF_FOUR_PLAYER_ESCAPE.md), every joint accumulation law of sufficiently accurate references is a native minimizer at the stated weights. A diagonal sequence consequently bounds the grand mass of the original joint minimizing laws away from zero at every sufficiently large selected scale. For actual laws with grand mass $P$ there is a date with unconditional grand mass at least $P^{4/3}$. Indeed if $f_i(t)$ is the private probability of a first quit at $t$, the grand mass at $t$ is $A_t=\prod_i f_i(t)$, and Hölder gives $\sum_t A_t^{1/4}\le1$. ## A terminal cutoff for a single winning clock

Fix a native table Z, |Z|≤M, with positive absorbing regret infimum. Take any minimizing point in the one-winner price problem, with winner p, losers L, minimum ρ>0, n_p=0 and n_j>0. Give each losing player's complete choice of Never its private bonus λ_j=c_jρ/n_j, independent of what the other players do. Let λ_p=0. The augmented prescribed payoff equals its full augmented cap b_i, with all finite deadlines and literal Never tested. Augment the compact data also by the original first-exit coalition law μ. The following modification preserves μ, all prescribed and Never payoffs, all genuine marginal Never limits, all augmented caps, and the optimal price. Native full caps of losers that play only Never in the limit may change.

Start with actual independent approximants to these data. Move p's vanishing Never mass to a finite date, at vanishing total-variation cost, including in all complete unilateral comparisons. Fix

    τ=min(1/4,min_{j∈L} λ_j/(8M))>0.

Let t be the first physical date at which p's unconditional remaining mass strictly after the date is at most τ. Such a date exists on each approximant. Immediately before t its surviving mass exceeds τ. For any j∈L and finite deadline k>t, write F_j(k) for its native offer and e_j for its native Never offer on the approximant. Coupling the two responses on the same rival clocks gives

    F_j(k)−e_j ≤ 2M Pr(all rivals have survived strictly before k)
               ≤ 2M Pr(T_p>t) ≤ λ_j/4.

If a rival quit earlier, the two realized payoffs agree. The augmented cap is at least e_j+λ_j, so every such late finite deadline loses at least 3λ_j/4. The actual augmented regrets tend to zero. Therefore all losing mass strictly after t but before Never is o(1), in total across the three individual laws. Move it to Never. The change costs o(1) in all native and augmented data and original coalition probabilities.

Keep every action through t, and move all p's finite mass after t to the one date t+1. In the modified suffix no rival quits. The two p-laws therefore induce exactly the same first-exit coalitions, prescribed payoffs and literal Never offers as each other after the foregoing o(1) edit. All early deadline offers are unchanged; p's full cap is unchanged since its rivals are unchanged. For each loser, both before and after the p edit the entire late finite menu is strictly worse than its augmented Never offer by the displayed bound. Hence its full augmented cap is exactly unchanged. Writing h=Pr(T_p>t), its new native cap is the maximum of its deadline offers through t and the two values

    e_j,     e_j+h (∏_{k∈L−{j}} N_k)[Z_j({p,j})−Z_j({p})].

The second is its response on t+1; every even later response equals e_j. Whenever 0<n_j<1, its nonvanishing finite mass through t and vanishing assigned defect force the unchanged early supremum to tend to b_j. Its native cap therefore still tends to its old value. The modified native limit is feasible at the very same minimum for every j: the exact augmented regret identity gives d_j=n_jΔ_j≤c_jρ, with equality if n_j<1, and d_p=0. In particular recovery at this minimum gives actual signed-game near-minimizers on the previously proved intermediate scales. A specific preselected marked source stream need not realize the compressed native limit.

The physical prefix strictly before t has a uniform total-hazard bound. The sum of its conditional hazards over all owners and dates is at most −log τ−∑_{j∈L}log N_j, because a player's cumulative survival is the product of its Continue probabilities and −log(1−q)≥q. For all sufficiently close approximants N_j≥n_j/2. Every date through t is actually reached with probability at least τ∏_{j∈L}N_j. The number of earlier physical dates has no bound in this argument.

Extract a subsequence with h→h_*∈[0,τ]. If h_*>0, t+1 is reached with limiting probability h_*∏_L n_j>0 and p alone quits surely there. Its response at t+1 equals its native Never offer, and so Δ_p=0 by optimality of its positive assigned mass. If h_*=0, the o(1) remaining p mass can be moved to t without changing any limit. The last p atom at t then has limiting mass at least τ, and the all-Continue suffix beyond t has zero limiting reach. Here p's native cap equals its offer at t. When Δ_p>0, some nonempty losing subset A must first quit at t with positive limiting probability and Z_p(A∪{p})>Z_p(A). Indeed the finite-minus-Never difference at that deadline is exactly the sum of these joining increments weighted by the rival first-exit probabilities there, since no rival has any later finite mass. Independence and p's macroscopic atom give positive actual first-exit mass at A∪{p}. The argument permits a cutoff whose date tends to infinity and supplies no finite bound on earlier timing complexity.

## How long all four continuation caps can stay positive

Fix 0<δ≤M. Starting from the chosen minimizing point, along the same actual approximants one can take consecutive first significant rows as long as the current effective caps satisfy b_i≥δ for every i. If at least one such row is taken, their number is at most

    1 + log(2M/(ρ min_{j∈L}c_j)) / [−log(1−δ/(32M))].

The alternatives at the end are a sure winning quit at the selected row or a genuine continuation with at least one effective cap below δ. A continuation produced after the first row can have a larger price and need not be another global minimizer.

Here are the conditional statements underlying the bound. At an actual cut of reach s>0, write a_i for i's individual survival to the cut, H_i=s/a_i, and use the conditional bonus λ_i/H_i. In a single step denote its rival survival by h_i, its passive head payoff by P_i and its current Quit offer by Q_i. Successive effective unrestricted caps satisfy the exact recursion

    b_i(t)=max(Q_i(t),P_i(t)+h_i(t)b_i(t+1)),

since the bonus on the complete action Never is divided by h_i in the child. At cuts of positive limiting reach all required divisors are bounded away from zero on that subsequence. Augmented prescribed values obey the corresponding relation with assigned Quit and Wait probabilities. Consequently the initial regret is at least s times conditional augmented regret, by telescoping the nonnegative assigned Quit/Wait losses. At a cut of limiting reach s>0 the conditional numbers n'_j=n_j/a_j, λ'_j=λ_j/H_j give λ'_j n'_j=c_jρ/s. The conditional p still has zero Never mass and every losing n'_j>0. Vanishing conditional augmented regret gives the same native feasibility equations at the new price ρ/s by the three-term augmented regret identity.

At min b_i≥δ, in these normalized conditional clocks choose the first physical date on which the sum of individual finite masses through it reaches δ/(8M). It exists because p is surely finite in the limit. A preceding finite deadline earns at most δ/8, since only an earlier or simultaneous rival could contribute a nonzero payoff. Vanishing finite-action shortfall implies that the entire skipped prefix has marginal mass o(1). The limits of the hazards on the selected date satisfy ∑q_i≥δ/(8M) and Q_i=b_i for each active owner. A sole active owner would earn its own singleton zero: there are at least two, including a loser j. Since n_j>0 and q_j>0, its current marginal lies strictly between zero and one. Its saturation gives c_jρ_current=d_j≤2M. Thus every counted row starts below the same price ceiling. A sure row can only be sure at p. Otherwise its survival is at most 1−max q_i≤1−δ/(32M), and the next price is the current price divided by this actual survival. Induct along a subsequence for each successive cut; the skipped mass o(1) has no effect on the survival product. The geometric increase of the price at the counted rows proves the bound.

On the finite prefix strictly before the terminal cutoff, the same recursion also gives bounded total variation of the effective caps, uniformly in the prefix length. All reaches there (and at its closing date t) are bounded below by a fixed s_*>0, all conditional bonuses and caps are bounded, and all individual continuation probabilities on the prefix are bounded below by a fixed a_*>0. Indeed each actual marginal survival through such a row is at least τ for p and at least N_j for a loser. Write E_i(t)=b_i(t)−P_i(t)−h_i(t)b_i(t+1)≥0. The sum of s(t)(1−q_i(t))E_i(t) over this entire prefix is at most the initial augmented regret, so ∑E_i(t)=o(1), independently of the number of dates. Increments of b_i are therefore bounded in total by ∑_t (M+sup|b_i|)(1−h_i(t))+o(1), which is finite uniformly by the cumulative hazard bound above. This estimates actual paths; it does not bound the number of small calendar steps when an effective cap approaches zero.

## Exact minimum survival at an actual cut

For *any* continuation v∈ℝ⁴, let σ(v) be the least limit of joint survival on finite private timing blocks with native absorbing rewards, terminal payoff v on joint continuation, and total normal-form regret tending to zero. Lengths may vary and each complete unilateral choice of a block date or waiting through is allowed. Such limits exist by finite-game Nash existence and compactness of [0,1], with attainment of the least limit by a diagonal sequence. This definition also applies when v has negative coordinates: an extra finite date beyond the terminal boundary is a different option. Each block's payoffs and its complete cap are 1-Lipschitz in v with the sup norm, independently of length. In particular σ is lower semicontinuous by a diagonal selection. The previously used whole-block bonus transport applies to this definition, including normal-form equilibria that need not be subgame-perfect.

**Cut identity.** Along any realizing sequence of globally ρ-optimal native points in this price problem, take arbitrary physical cuts of cumulative reach tending to s>0. Extract joint limits of the conditional native data, individual survivals and conditional augmented caps v. Then

    σ(v)=s.

The same proof holds for a restricted minimum whenever its extra conditions, such as specified zero winning marginals, persist both under positive-reach conditioning and under prefixing. To prove the lower bound, the conditional limiting native point is feasible at price ρ/s as shown above. Prefix any sequence of finite blocks of vanishing regret at v and limiting survival C, multiplying each conditional private Never bonus by the probability all its opponents survive the block. This makes the prefixed augmented regret equal to the block regret in the limit. If C=0 all realized bonuses vanish on the absorbing prefixed point, contradicting positive native absorbing gap. If C>0, the prefixed native point is feasible at Cρ/s, so C≥s by original minimality. This also proves the more general inequality σ(b_X)≥ρ/r for every feasible point X at any price r in this same problem, where b_X is its canonical augmented cap.

Conversely take the historical finite blocks strictly before the chosen cuts. At each approximant use as block terminal value its *actual conditional augmented cap*. Across the whole block the maximum between all in-block finite replies and the right to wait through to that terminal value equals the actual augmented cap at the initial point. This is the exact bonus transport, read forward using conditional bonus λ_i/H_i. Giving the block the conditional *prescribed* augmented value instead reproduces precisely its actual prescribed augmented payoff from the beginning. Replacing prescribed by cap at this terminal node increases the prescribed block payoff coordinate by cumulative joint survival times the conditional augmented regret, a nonnegative quantity. Thus block regret at that terminal cap is at most the original augmented regret, which vanishes. Replacing the terminal cap by its limit v changes every block regret by o(1), uniformly for these variable lengths. The blocks' actual survival tends to s, proving the opposite inequality. The empty historical block at the initial point gives s=1; the lower bound already forces σ=1 there. In particular two actual limiting cuts with different positive cumulative survivals cannot have the same effective cap vector, even when their stopping profiles and dates differ.

**One-stage finite test.** Consider the first significant opening row q from a strictly positive minimizing cap b and suppose S=∏(1−q_i)>0. Its common child continuation is v=u⁺+(ρ/S)c, with u⁺ in the native absorbing payoff hull and c_p=0. ## The outsider's upper envelope on three-player equilibrium payoff fibers

Let j be the only losing recipient, W the other three, and c_j its price weight.     F_W(u)=max {∑_{∅≠A⊆W} μ(A) Z_j(A) : μ from such a point over u}.

    (T−F_W(u)) σ(u,T) ≥ c_jρ    for every u∈D_W and T≥F_W(u)+2M.      (1)

## Which first-exit coalitions can fill a three-winner suffix?

**Complete vertex test.** For a nonempty S⊆W let δ_S be the terminal law charging S alone.

1. If |S|≥2, δ_S∈E_fin exactly when
   Z_i(S)≥Z_i(S−i) for i∈S and Z_i(S)≥Z_i(S+i) for i∈W−S.
2. If S={p}, δ_S∈E_fin exactly when both of the following conditions hold. There is h∈[0,1] with h J_kp≤Γ_kp for both k∈W−p. In addition, the two-rival number

       c_p = inf_{q∈[0,1]², H(q)<1} max(Q_p(q), P_p(q)/(1−H(q)))

   is at most zero. Here H is rival survival in a row, P the unconditional passive reward on nonempty rival exits and Q the forced Quit reward. The formula involves only two real variables, including boundary limits. Thus both conditions are finite real algebraic tests, allowing arbitrary signs and zero entries.

For |S|≥2 the sufficiency construction makes precisely S quit surely at date zero and every remaining clock quit at date one. Every deviator faces another sure first quitter. For necessity and explicit gaps write ε=1−μ(S) and let M≥1 bound the three winners' native rewards. For an outsider k gaining g>0 by joining S, choose two members i,l of S. Except on a set of probability ≤2ε, the actual first coalition is S at that very date. The outsider's deadline t gains g on that event and loses at most 2M otherwise. Hence ε≥g/(2g+4M). These conclusions concern the realized first exit and work even when t moves to infinity.

The two-rival number c_p is the exact infimum of p's complete cap over all pairs of surely finite independent rival clocks. To see the lower bound take a trial level c<c_p. If only joint finite absorption is required in E and individual off-path rivals may both choose Never, this extra condition for the singleton vertex is omitted.

For δ_{p}, an opening hazard h of p has a subsequential limit in [0,1].     ε ≥ max(0, c_p/M, min_{0≤h≤1} max_{k≠p}(h J_kp−Γ_kp)/(4M)).

This last Never-deletion bound uses the earlier calendar polytope construction.

## Choosing the physical coalition before selecting the counterexample

Suppose a four-player table has a positive infimum of total terminal regret against unrestricted private deviations. Choose any nonsingleton coalition T in advance. There exists another positive-gap table with the same original own levels, the same literal singleton matrix and the same rewards outside T, with the following property. Three players have regret tending to zero, and **every** sequence minimizing the remaining player's regret subject to that requirement puts a uniformly positive probability on first exit exactly at T on a single physical date. The remaining player is fixed on the selected table. The date may vary from profile to profile along a minimizing sequence.

For each player i set aᵢ(T)=[rᵢ(T△{i})−rᵢ(T)]₊. Raise the entries rᵢ(T) by these amounts one player at a time. At the final vertex pure T is an equilibrium, since every player faces a sure quitting rival. Stop at the first vertex admitting a uniform-equilibrium payoff. The preceding vertex has positive terminal gap. Only the row of one player h changes along the joining segment; write x∈[0,1] for its fractional increase and a>0 for its size. The set of actual sequences with the other three regrets tending to zero stays fixed. It is nonempty, since h can play Never while the other three use their restricted-game approximate equilibria. Let m(x) be the infimum of limiting h-regrets on this set, taking subsequences to obtain limits. Then m(0)>0 and m(1)=0 by the terminal criterion, and m is Lipschitz. For any actual profile p, lowering x by t>0 lowers its full h-cap weakly and subtracts ta μₚ(T) from its prescription. Thus dₕ(x−t,p)≤dₕ(x,p)+ta μₚ(T). Choose an interior differentiability point with −m′(x)>m(0)/2. There m(x)>0, so the original game still has positive gap. The comparison at fixed t on any minimizing sequence gives liminf μₚ(T)≥−m′(x)/a as t↓0. For k=|T|, the k-th roots of the date masses sum to at most one by Hölder's inequality on the k independent marginal atom sequences. The largest date mass is consequently at least μₚ(T) raised to k/(k−1).

There is also a **single positive-gap table for all eleven nonsingleton coalitions and Never**. Start from a native table Z with positive absorbing gap A. Add a uniform positive row shift s/4 on every nonempty outcome. For each nonsingleton T introduce u_T∈[0,1] and raise Zᵢ(T) by u_T aᵢ(T). Independently let u₀ reduce the common shift to s(1−u₀)/4. These changes preserve the literal singleton matrix. Write m(u) for the unrestricted original total-regret infimum. For a given profile this original objective equals its modified native total regret plus s(1−u₀)ν, where ν is its probability of Never. A zero at the origin would give both vanishing native regret and ν→0, and moving the smallest marginal Never mass to a finite date would contradict A>0. Thus m(0)>0. Every upper face has m=0. At u₀=1 all-Never is native Nash. At u_T=1 each recipient's payoff at T reaches the higher of its original two own-action endpoint payoffs. The other endpoint on that same edge never exceeds this maximum, even after all the other interventions.

Choose 0<12λ<m(0) and maximize m(u)+λΣu on the cube. Lipschitz continuity gives a maximizer with positive m and every u below one. In particular, the table also retains all 28 original direct joining-incentive signs, including ties. $$

This follows from monotonicity of every native reply payoff and the exact change to the prescription. ## When a prescribed coalition and Never occur on the same minimizing law

Suppose a native table has positive absorbing regret infimum. Fix any nonsingleton T, including the grand coalition. One can select a native table with the same singleton matrix, changing only rewards at T, for which one of the following holds.

* After finite signed row shifts, the unrestricted original game has positive gap, and **every** minimizing sequence puts probabilities bounded away from zero on both first exit exactly at T and on Never. In particular the maximum-Never minimum itself carries the specified mark.
* At the selected native table, with absorbing infimum A>0, some γ>0 satisfies the following inequality for **every** absorbing independent profile P, including profiles of unbounded support:
  $$D(P)\ \ge\ A+\gamma\sum_i n_i(P)\big(B_i(P)-e_i(P)\big).$$

To select the table, interpolate the rewards at T from their initial values to the values making pure T weakly stable, using the increments aᵢ(T) above. The absorbing gap is positive at the initial endpoint and zero at the final endpoint. At an interior point of strictly negative derivative the same backwards difference estimate gives A>0 and μ(T)≥β>0 at every compact absorbing minimum. Keep this table fixed. Write Lᵢ=nᵢ(Bᵢ−eᵢ), so 0≤Lᵢ≤dᵢ.

Choose one owner h. Give h shift −K and distribute a total positive shift λK among the other three, with 0<λ<1. Let Cₕ be the compact native carrier, retaining the actual first-exit law, with at least one zero Never marginal outside h. It is the closure of profiles with such a clock surely finite, by redirecting a vanishing Never marginal. **The unrestricted original infimum tends to the minimum of D−(1−λ)Lₕ on Cₕ as K tends to infinity.** On every sequence within o(1) of the infimum, each converging native subsequence minimizes this expression and Kν tends to its Lₕ.

Indeed, put z=K∏ⱼ≠ₕ nⱼ, n=nₕ and Δ=Bₕ−eₕ. The exact signed regret is
$$
D-(1-\lambda)L_h+(1-\lambda)n(\Delta-z)_+
 +[1-(1-\lambda)n](z-\Delta)_+ .
$$
Bounded scores keep z bounded, so a winning marginal tends to zero along any convergent subsequence. For recovery take actual approximants with a winning clock surely finite. Independently replace the same fraction u of each winning law by Never. The product of their new Never marginals is continuous in u, starts at zero and is at least u³. Hence some u≤(Δ/K)¹ᐟ³ sets z equal to the target Δ of the carrier point. Coupling controls all payoffs, caps and first-exit probabilities uniformly as u tends to zero. This gives the asserted minimum limit. The two nonnegative terms must vanish on minimizing sequences, and their sum bounds min(λ,1−λ)|Kν−Lₕ|.

If the second alternative fails, some fixed h admits absorbing profiles with D−tLₕ<A for arbitrarily small t>0. They have nₕ>0 and hence belong to Cₕ. Fix such a t with λ=1−t. Every minimizer on Cₕ has D≤A+2Mt, where M bounds the absolute native rewards. By compactness all carry μ(T)≥β/2 when t is small enough. All have Lₕ≥(A−min₍Cₕ₎(D−tLₕ))/t>0, and the limiting score is at least λA. Consequently for every sufficiently large fixed finite K, every unrestricted minimum charges T and Never by positive amounts and the original gap is positive. This gives the first alternative.

The inequality in the second alternative forces every absorbing minimum to assign its entire regret to finite deadlines. It bounds the cost of adding Never mass even off the minimizing set. For example, at an actual absorbing minimum, suppose h has unbounded finite support and two other owners are surely finite. Send h's remaining finite mass δ after a cut to Never, keeping its rivals fixed. Its term Lₕ rises by δ(Bₕ−eₕ). For each other owner k the uniform change to any deadline payoff is at most 2Mδ times the probability that both clocks outside {h,k} survive the cut. These probabilities tend to zero, and the same coupling bounds the change in prescriptions. The edited absorbing profile therefore has D≤A+o(δ), forcing Bₕ=eₕ. The unrestricted same-law conclusion for arbitrary tables satisfying the inequality remains open.
