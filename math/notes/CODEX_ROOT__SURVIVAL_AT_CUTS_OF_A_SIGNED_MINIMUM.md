# Minimum survival at reached continuations of a signed four-player source

The [signed-price reduction](../exports/PRESCRIBED_SIGNS_AND_NATIVE_LIMITS_OF_FOUR_PLAYER_ESCAPE.md) starts from a native table with positive infimum of regret on absorbing profiles. An unrestricted minimizing source exists for each nonempty proper partition into winning and losing recipients. In the compact closure of the actual independent stopping laws, write $n_i$ for the marginal probability of Never, $U_i$ for the payoff, $B_i$ for the cap over all replies, and $e_i$ for the literal Never offer. Put $d_i=B_i-U_i$. At the smallest feasible price ρ, the winners have zero regret and at least one winning clock stops surely. For each losing recipient with weight $c_j>0$ one has $n_j>0$, $d_j=n_j(B_j-e_j)≤c_jρ$, with equality in the last inequality when $n_j<1$. Equivalently, paying a private bonus $c_jρ/n_j$ for choosing Never makes the prescribed law optimal for every owner. The following arguments use the actual conditional payoffs of these sources. The [three-winning-clock selection](CODEX_SOURCE_GATE__GIFTED_CAP_POSITIVE_OR_ZERO.md#which-winning-clocks-can-be-required-to-absorb) supplies the specified additional zero marginals when invoked.

## A terminal cutoff for a single winning clock

Fix a native table Z, |Z|≤M, with positive absorbing regret infimum. Take any minimizing point in the one-winner price problem, with winner p, losers L, minimum ρ>0, n_p=0 and n_j>0. Give each losing player's complete choice of Never its private bonus λ_j=c_jρ/n_j, independent of what the other players do. Let λ_p=0. The augmented prescribed payoff equals its full augmented cap b_i, with all finite deadlines and literal Never tested. Augment the compact data also by the original first-exit coalition law μ. The following modification preserves μ, all prescribed and Never payoffs, all genuine marginal Never limits, all augmented caps, and the optimal price. Native full caps of losers that play only Never in the limit may change.

Start with actual independent approximants to these data. Move p's vanishing Never mass to a finite date, at vanishing total-variation cost, including in all complete unilateral comparisons. Fix

    τ=min(1/4,min_{j∈L} λ_j/(8M))>0.

Let t be the first physical date at which p's unconditional remaining mass strictly after the date is at most τ. Such a date exists on each approximant. Immediately before t its surviving mass exceeds τ. For any j∈L and finite deadline k>t, write F_j(k) for its native offer and e_j for its native Never offer on the approximant. Coupling the two responses on the same rival clocks gives

    F_j(k)−e_j ≤ 2M Pr(all rivals have survived strictly before k)
               ≤ 2M Pr(T_p>t) ≤ λ_j/4.

If a rival quit earlier, the two realized payoffs agree. The augmented cap is at least e_j+λ_j, so every such late finite deadline loses at least 3λ_j/4. The actual augmented regrets tend to zero. Therefore all losing mass strictly after t but before Never is o(1), in total across the three individual laws. Move it to Never. The change costs o(1) in all native and augmented data and original coalition probabilities.

Keep every action through t, and move all p's finite mass after t to the one date t+1. In the modified suffix no rival quits. The two p-laws therefore induce exactly the same first-exit coalitions, prescribed payoffs and literal Never offers as each other after the foregoing o(1) edit. All early deadline offers are unchanged; p's full cap is unchanged since its rivals are unchanged. For each loser, both before and after the p edit the entire late finite menu is strictly worse than its augmented Never offer by the displayed bound. Hence its full augmented cap is exactly unchanged. Writing h=Pr(T_p>t), its new native cap is the maximum of its deadline offers through t and the two values

    e_j,     e_j+h (∏_{k∈L−{j}} N_k)[Z_j({p,j})−Z_j({p})].

The second is its response on t+1; every even later response equals e_j. Whenever 0<n_j<1, its nonvanishing finite mass through t and vanishing assigned defect force the unchanged early supremum to tend to b_j. Its native cap therefore still tends to its old value. The modified native limit is feasible at the very same minimum for every j: the exact augmented regret identity gives d_j=n_jΔ_j≤c_jρ, with equality if n_j<1, and d_p=0. In particular recovery at this minimum gives actual signed-game near-minimizers on the previously proved intermediate scales. A specific preselected marked source stream need not realize the compressed native limit.

The physical prefix strictly before t has a uniform total-hazard bound. The sum of its conditional hazards over all owners and dates is at most −log τ−∑_{j∈L}log N_j, because a player's cumulative survival is the product of its Continue probabilities and −log(1−q)≥q. For all sufficiently close approximants N_j≥n_j/2. Every date through t is actually reached with probability at least τ∏_{j∈L}N_j. The number of earlier physical dates has no bound in this argument.

Extract a subsequence with h→h_*∈[0,τ]. If h_*>0, t+1 is reached with limiting probability h_*∏_L n_j>0 and p alone quits surely there. Its response at t+1 equals its native Never offer, and so Δ_p=0 by optimality of its positive assigned mass. If h_*=0, the o(1) remaining p mass can be moved to t without changing any limit. The last p atom at t then has limiting mass at least τ, and the all-Continue suffix beyond t has zero limiting reach. Here p's native cap equals its offer at t. When Δ_p>0, some nonempty losing subset A must first quit at t with positive limiting probability and Z_p(A∪{p})>Z_p(A). Indeed the finite-minus-Never difference at that deadline is exactly the sum of these joining increments weighted by the rival first-exit probabilities there, since no rival has any later finite mass. Independence and p's macroscopic atom give positive actual first-exit mass at A∪{p}. The argument permits a cutoff whose date tends to infinity and supplies no finite bound on earlier timing complexity.

## How long all four continuation caps can stay positive

Fix 0<δ≤M. Starting from the chosen minimizing point, along the same actual approximants one can take consecutive first significant rows as long as the current effective caps satisfy b_i≥δ for every i. If at least one such row is taken, their number is at most

    1 + log(2M/(ρ min_{j∈L}c_j)) / [−log(1−δ/(32M))].

The alternatives at the end are a sure winning quit at the selected row or a genuine continuation with at least one effective cap below δ. A continuation produced after the first row can have a larger price and need not be another global minimizer.

Here are the conditional statements underlying the bound. At an actual cut of reach s>0, write a_i for i's individual survival to the cut, H_i=s/a_i, and use the conditional bonus λ_i/H_i. In a single step denote its rival survival by h_i, its passive head payoff by P_i and its current Quit offer by Q_i. Successive effective unrestricted caps satisfy the exact recursion

    b_i(t)=max(Q_i(t),P_i(t)+h_i(t)b_i(t+1)),

since the bonus on the complete action Never is divided by h_i in the child. At cuts of positive limiting reach all required divisors are bounded away from zero on that subsequence. Augmented prescribed values obey the corresponding relation with assigned Quit and Wait probabilities. Consequently the initial regret is at least s times conditional augmented regret, by telescoping the nonnegative assigned Quit/Wait losses. At a cut of limiting reach s>0 the conditional numbers n'_j=n_j/a_j, λ'_j=λ_j/H_j give λ'_j n'_j=c_jρ/s. The conditional p still has zero Never mass and every losing n'_j>0. Vanishing conditional augmented regret gives the same native feasibility equations at the new price ρ/s by the three-term augmented regret identity.

At min b_i≥δ, in these normalized conditional clocks choose the first physical date on which the sum of individual finite masses through it reaches δ/(8M). It exists because p is surely finite in the limit. A preceding finite deadline earns at most δ/8, since only an earlier or simultaneous rival could contribute a nonzero payoff. Vanishing finite-action shortfall implies that the entire skipped prefix has marginal mass o(1). The limits of the hazards on the selected date satisfy ∑q_i≥δ/(8M) and Q_i=b_i for each active owner. A sole active owner would earn its own singleton zero: there are at least two, including a loser j. Since n_j>0 and q_j>0, its current marginal lies strictly between zero and one. Its saturation gives c_jρ_current=d_j≤2M. Thus every counted row starts below the same price ceiling. A sure row can only be sure at p. Otherwise its survival is at most 1−max q_i≤1−δ/(32M), and the next price is the current price divided by this actual survival. Induct along a subsequence for each successive cut; the skipped mass o(1) has no effect on the survival product. The geometric increase of the price at the counted rows proves the bound.

On the finite prefix strictly before the terminal cutoff, the same recursion also gives bounded total variation of the effective caps, uniformly in the prefix length. All reaches there (and at its closing date t) are bounded below by a fixed s_*>0, all conditional bonuses and caps are bounded, and all individual continuation probabilities on the prefix are bounded below by a fixed a_*>0. Indeed each actual marginal survival through such a row is at least τ for p and at least N_j for a loser. Write E_i(t)=b_i(t)−P_i(t)−h_i(t)b_i(t+1)≥0. The sum of s(t)(1−q_i(t))E_i(t) over this entire prefix is at most the initial augmented regret, so ∑E_i(t)=o(1), independently of the number of dates. Increments of b_i are therefore bounded in total by ∑_t (M+sup|b_i|)(1−h_i(t))+o(1), which is finite uniformly by the cumulative hazard bound above. This estimates actual paths; it does not bound the number of small calendar steps when an effective cap approaches zero.

## Exact minimum survival at an actual cut

For *any* continuation v∈ℝ⁴, let σ(v) be the least limit of joint survival on finite private timing blocks with native absorbing rewards, terminal payoff v on joint continuation, and total normal-form regret tending to zero. Lengths may vary and each complete unilateral choice of a block date or waiting through is allowed. Such limits exist by finite-game Nash existence and compactness of [0,1], with attainment of the least limit by a diagonal sequence. This definition also applies when v has negative coordinates: an extra finite date beyond the terminal boundary is a different option. Each block's payoffs and its complete cap are 1-Lipschitz in v with the sup norm, independently of length. In particular σ is lower semicontinuous by a diagonal selection. The previously used whole-block bonus transport applies to this definition, including normal-form equilibria that need not be subgame-perfect.

**Cut identity.** Along any realizing sequence of globally ρ-optimal native points in this price problem, take arbitrary physical cuts of cumulative reach tending to s>0. Extract joint limits of the conditional native data, individual survivals and conditional augmented caps v. Then

    σ(v)=s.

The same proof holds for a restricted minimum whenever its extra conditions, such as specified zero winning marginals, persist both under positive-reach conditioning and under prefixing. To prove the lower bound, the conditional limiting native point is feasible at price ρ/s as shown above. Prefix any sequence of finite blocks of vanishing regret at v and limiting survival C, multiplying each conditional private Never bonus by the probability all its opponents survive the block. This makes the prefixed augmented regret equal to the block regret in the limit. If C=0 all realized bonuses vanish on the absorbing prefixed point, contradicting positive native absorbing gap. If C>0, the prefixed native point is feasible at Cρ/s, so C≥s by original minimality. This also proves the more general inequality σ(b_X)≥ρ/r for every feasible point X at any price r in this same problem, where b_X is its canonical augmented cap.

Conversely take the historical finite blocks strictly before the chosen cuts. At each approximant use as block terminal value its *actual conditional augmented cap*. Across the whole block the maximum between all in-block finite replies and the right to wait through to that terminal value equals the actual augmented cap at the initial point. This is the exact bonus transport, read forward using conditional bonus λ_i/H_i. Giving the block the conditional *prescribed* augmented value instead reproduces precisely its actual prescribed augmented payoff from the beginning. Replacing prescribed by cap at this terminal node increases the prescribed block payoff coordinate by cumulative joint survival times the conditional augmented regret, a nonnegative quantity. Thus block regret at that terminal cap is at most the original augmented regret, which vanishes. Replacing the terminal cap by its limit v changes every block regret by o(1), uniformly for these variable lengths. The blocks' actual survival tends to s, proving the opposite inequality. The empty historical block at the initial point gives s=1; the lower bound already forces σ=1 there. In particular two actual limiting cuts with different positive cumulative survivals cannot have the same effective cap vector, even when their stopping profiles and dates differ.

**One-stage finite test.** Consider the first significant opening row q from a strictly positive minimizing cap b and suppose S=∏(1−q_i)>0. Its common child continuation is v=u⁺+(ρ/S)c, with u⁺ in the native absorbing payoff hull and c_p=0. The negligible skipped mass and conditional vanishing regret show that q is a Nash row at v, has parent payoff b, and satisfies σ(v)=S. In particular, *every* Nash row q' at that same v has survival S'≥S. This is an additional finite real-algebraic condition on the first-row criterion, quantifying over a single four-vector q'. More generally, if b' is that alternative row's Nash payoff then

    S' σ(b') ≥ S.

Indeed any vanishing-regret timing block at b' can be followed by q', which is Nash at v. The concatenated block has vanishing regret at v and survival equal to the product. The complete-menu argument follows by splitting a unilateral replacement into an in-block date or waiting for that last Nash row. If S'=S this forces σ(b')=1, hence every finite timing game with child b' has only the all-Continue equilibrium and b'≥0. At a later cut the right-hand side is the entire cumulative reach s at v. For the actual preceding row with survival h and preceding cap w the cut identities give h σ(w)=σ(v) whenever both cuts have positive limiting reach. Testing only h would discard the earlier survival factors. Earlier least-Never selection on native zero-debt carriers and finite-source whole-block bonus competition motivate this calculation; here the actual historical prefixes attain the least block survival at each varying conditional cap of the same optimal source.

## Several winning clocks and an explicit single-winner ray

For a general nonempty proper winner set W, start at a minimum for its eligible carrier, with ∏_{i∈W}n_i=0, every losing n_j>0 and λ_j=c_jρ/n_j. This can be the unrestricted minimum. It can also be a minimum with specified winning marginals n_i=0 whenever the required seed exists, as those constraints survive both conditioning at positive reach and prefixing. Repair precisely the designated zero winning marginals to be surely finite on actual approximants. A general unrestricted minimizer need only have at least one such winner. By the absorbing-clock selection in the companion note, some choice of a single losing recipient permits all three winning marginals to be set to zero at an unrestricted minimum. For any separately prescribed losing recipient the same is possible at a possibly larger restricted minimum.

With τ=min(1/4,min_{j∈L}λ_j/(8M)), let t be the first date after which the product of the winning clocks' individual survivals is at most τ. For a losing player's every finite deadline strictly after t, the probability all rivals still survive is at most this product. The finite-minus-Never comparison and vanishing-regret bound therefore still remove all losing finite tails at o(1) total-variation cost. This edit preserves every native payoff, cap and first-exit law in the limit. The cumulative hazard of all winners strictly before t is at most −log τ; adding the analogous losing bounds gives the same bounded four-player prefix as above. Every date through t is reached with probability bounded below by τ∏_{j∈L}N_j. Extract a limit of the cumulative joint survival s after t. If s=0, some winning owner's hazard at t converges to one, because the previous winning survivals and the current losing survivals are all bounded away from zero. No conditional post-date game is needed in that case.

If s>0, after the losing-tail edit the remaining physical clocks give a genuine restricted W-only game. Its conditional native debts on W tend to zero by the conditional regret inequality: winners receive no Never bonus, and the losing clocks all choose Never on the suffix. Its prescribed play absorbs, and every originally designated surely finite winning marginal remains zero conditionally. For j∈L let e_j⁺ be its expected passive payoff under this common restricted exit law. Its conditional Never probability is one, and its conditional effective cap is exactly e_j⁺+c_jρ/s in the limit; its private conditional credit is c_jρ/s since its individual survival to the cut tends to n_j. For each i∈W the effective cap is its own prescribed W-only equilibrium payoff. The resulting vector v has σ(v)=s. Thus for a selected three-finite-winner source the suffix is an actual approximating sequence to an absorbing equilibrium payoff of the known three-player subgame, with all three clocks surely finite. Arbitrarily replacing this suffix by a different three-player equilibrium could change the opening incentives and the losing recipient's passive payoff.

For one winner p the positive post-cut case determines the block survival function on an entire unbounded ray. In the previous notation let h_*>0 and s₀=h_*∏_{j∈L} n_j. For every R≥ρ/s₀ define v(R)_p=0 and v(R)_j=Γ_jp+c_jR. Then

    σ(v(R))=ρ/R.

Indeed fix s∈(0,s₀]. In the already compressed approximants replace p's post-t mass h by h(1−s/s₀) at t+1 and h s/s₀ at t+2. All other remaining clocks choose Never. This changes no first-exit coalition, prescribed or Never payoff, or deadline through t. Every later losing deadline still fails against its augmented Never by the same strict margin (p's entire post-t mass is at most τ). The pivot's offers are unchanged since its opponents are unchanged, and its prescribed payoff on the modified tail is always its zero singleton. Consequently the same optimal effective source and price are retained, even if an inactive loser's late native cap changes. The cut just before t+2 has limiting cumulative survival s. Given the cut, p quits surely and all others choose Never, so the conditional cap is zero for p and Γ_jp+c_jρ/s for each loser. Applying the exact cut identity and putting R=ρ/s proves the formula. Here c_jρ/s≥λ_j/τ≥8M, so all entries of v(R) are nonnegative. On this ray σ also equals the least limiting joint Never probability of actual near-equilibria in the original infinite game with nonempty rewards Z−v(R). To see the equality, truncate any approximate infinite profile at a sufficiently late physical date, changing all opponent and prescribed payoffs by o(1). In the reverse direction any reply beyond a finite block against the truncated clocks receives the native own singleton zero on rival survival, whereas waiting through the block gives the nonnegative coordinate v_i(R). Thus no omitted late reply increases its regret. The unbounded-ray conclusion requires a positive surviving p tail; a final indispensable joint p atom at zero post-cut reach supplies no such ray.

## The outsider's upper envelope on three-player equilibrium payoff fibers

Let j be the only losing recipient, W the other three, and c_j its price weight. In the native three-player W game take the compact closure of joint data consisting of prescribed payoffs, all response caps, actual Never marginals and the first-exit law. Keep only the zero-regret points with product of the three Never marginals zero; any *required* individual zero winning marginals in the original minimization must also be kept. For the unrestricted global minimum the comparison below uses **every** absorbing three-player equilibrium limit, even if the selected minimizing point happens to have all three winning marginals zero. Write D_W for the compact projection on the three prescribed payoffs. A point over u∈D_W has an absorbing first-exit law μ on the seven nonempty subsets of W. Define

    F_W(u)=max {∑_{∅≠A⊆W} μ(A) Z_j(A) : μ from such a point over u}.

This maximum is attained; compactness of the joint data also makes F_W upper semicontinuous on D_W. Only actual independent-law equilibrium limits in the three-player game enter this fiber, which need not be convex. For T∈ℝ let (u,T) denote the four-vector with W-coordinates u and j-coordinate T. The exact bound is

    (T−F_W(u)) σ(u,T) ≥ c_jρ    for every u∈D_W and T≥F_W(u)+2M.      (1)

To prove it take a maximizing point over u and make j play only Never. Its native payoff for j is now F_W(u). Give j the private Never bonus T−F_W(u)≥2M. No native offer exceeds M, and its augmented Never offer T≥M is optimal. Each winner still has native zero regret. Thus this extended point is feasible at the scalar price (T−F_W(u))/c_j with effective caps (u,T). The general lower bound on σ established by grafting arbitrary finite comparison blocks proves (1).

At a positive-reach suffix of the multiwinner cutoff the passive recipient receives some e over the actual three-player target u. Let T=e+c_jρ/s denote its effective cap. Since the probability that all three rivals of j survive through this cut is at most τ, its conditional bonus c_jρ/s=λ_j/H_j is at least 8M. In particular T≥7M and T−F_W(u)≥6M. The cut identity gives σ(u,T)=s. Substitution into (1) yields

    c_jρ ≤ (T−F_W(u))s = c_jρ + s(e−F_W(u)) ≤ c_jρ.

Consequently the selected suffix attains the outsider's maximum F_W(u) on this exact three-player equilibrium payoff fiber. If this positive-reach branch occurs, (1) is sharp across the entire three-player domain:

    c_jρ = min_{u∈D_W, T≥3M} (T−F_W(u)) σ(u,T).

The fixed lower cutoff 3M works since F_W≤M everywhere and the selected T≥7M attains the bound. With a terminating sure winning row one has the inequality throughout the domain, while attainment by an actual reached suffix would require a separate argument.

There is additional freedom in comparing winner targets if some winners made no earlier finite moves. Write a_i for i's limiting marginal survival through the actual historical prefix to this suffix. It is impossible to find another eligible three-player point with greater passive payoff e'>e that preserves u_i for i∈W with a_i<1 and weakly raises u_i for every other winner. Indeed give its j the smaller private bonus T−e'≥6M. In the historical finite comparison blocks at continuation (u,T), raise winner i's terminal coordinate by δ_i=u'_i−u_i≥0 while keeping T fixed. Its best payoff can rise by at most H_iδ_i, where H_i is the probability all rivals survive the historical block; the prescribed payoff rises by exactly a_iH_iδ_i, using the actual marginal survivals on each approximant. The limiting change in regret is at most H_i(1−a_i)δ_i=0. Thus the same historical blocks are nearly Nash at (u',T) with survival tending to s. Applying the graft inequality to the new three-player point would give c_jρ≤(T−e')s<c_jρ. The argument does not require a common public mixture of different three-player equilibrium laws.

## Which first-exit coalitions can fill a three-winner suffix?

Let W have three owners, with native own singleton, live and Never rewards zero. Let j be a fourth owner held at Never throughout the suffix. Use only the seven W-coalition reward columns, including j's passive entries. Let E_fin consist of limits of first-exit laws and payoffs of actual independent W-profiles in which all three marginal clocks are surely finite and every winning regret against all deadlines and literal Never tends to zero. Such laws exist at a positive-reach ending of the signed price source whenever all three winning marginals are zero and the original debtor has no remaining finite mass. Marginals tending to zero Never may be made exactly finite by coupling. This note applies to any supplied E_fin ending. It asserts no unconditional positive reach after a particular cutoff.

**Complete vertex test.** For a nonempty S⊆W let δ_S be the terminal law charging S alone.

1. If |S|≥2, δ_S∈E_fin exactly when
   Z_i(S)≥Z_i(S−i) for i∈S and Z_i(S)≥Z_i(S+i) for i∈W−S.
2. If S={p}, δ_S∈E_fin exactly when both of the following conditions hold. There is h∈[0,1] with h J_kp≤Γ_kp for both k∈W−p. In addition, the two-rival number

       c_p = inf_{q∈[0,1]², H(q)<1} max(Q_p(q), P_p(q)/(1−H(q)))

   is at most zero. Here H is rival survival in a row, P the unconditional passive reward on nonempty rival exits and Q the forced Quit reward. The formula involves only two real variables, including boundary limits. Thus both conditions are finite real algebraic tests, allowing arbitrary signs and zero entries.

For |S|≥2 the sufficiency construction makes precisely S quit surely at date zero and every remaining clock quit at date one. Every deviator faces another sure first quitter. For necessity and explicit gaps write ε=1−μ(S) and let M≥1 bound the three winners' native rewards. If a member i gains g>0 by withdrawing from S while others remain, its Never deviation gains at least (1−ε)g−2Mε. Thus every limiting law satisfies ε≥g/(g+2M). For an outsider k gaining g>0 by joining S, choose two members i,l of S. Their independent clocks coincide with probability at least 1−ε, so i has an atom on some date t of mass at least 1−ε, by averaging its atom probabilities against l. Except on a set of probability ≤2ε, the actual first coalition is S at that very date. The outsider's deadline t gains g on that event and loses at most 2M otherwise. Hence ε≥g/(2g+4M). These conclusions concern the realized first exit and work even when t moves to infinity.

The two-rival number c_p is the exact infimum of p's complete cap over all pairs of surely finite independent rival clocks. To see the lower bound take a trial level c<c_p. Quit on the first date with Q_p≥c against the known conditional hazards, or Never if none occurs. On every preceding nonblank date the passive contribution satisfies P_p≥c(1−H); on blank dates the same inequality holds with zero on both sides. Summing with actual rival survival shows the complete deadline yields at least c, also if the first such date is never reached since rival absorption is sure. For the upper bound take a constant row with H<1; geometric summation gives cap max(Q_p,P_p/(1−H)). An absent rival can be given arbitrarily small constant positive hazard by continuity at that nonblank row, making both rivals surely finite. Taking the infimum proves equality. The standard unrestricted punishment calculation also allows the fully blank row, whereas here it may give an unattainable lower value because all rival clocks are required to be surely finite. If only joint finite absorption is required in E and individual off-path rivals may both choose Never, this extra condition for the singleton vertex is omitted.

For δ_{p}, an opening hazard h of p has a subsequential limit in [0,1]. If ε=1−μ({p}) then each other winner's opening forced offer is ≥hJ_kp−2Mε: any rival besides p stopping at the opening already excludes singleton p. The prescribed value of k is within 2Mε of Γ_kp. Also U_p≤Mε while B_p≥c_p. Thus every limiting law has

    ε ≥ max(0, c_p/M, min_{0≤h≤1} max_{k≠p}(h J_kp−Γ_kp)/(4M)).

Conversely let p act with constant h through a long head, moving its remaining mass to the last head date if necessary, leaving the rival clocks quiet until the head ends. If h=0 use positive h_n→0. Choose the head length such that p's residual geometric mass tends to zero. For each rival k the native offers along the unmodified geometric head interpolate between h J_kp at the beginning and Γ_kp. The final tiny mass redirection affects all these comparisons and later offers by o(1); k's prescribed payoff tends to Γ_kp. After the head let the two rivals use surely finite laws giving p cap at most c_p+o(1). Player p's full cap is then at most max(0,c_p)+o(1) because its rivals were quiet throughout the head. This gives vanishing W regrets and first-exit probability tending to one at singleton p, as required.

**Joint payoff bound.** Intersect the seven-outcome probability simplex with all the preceding coordinate bounds; also impose the three winning payoff identities at the specified suffix vector u_W. The max and min of ∑_S μ(S)Z_j(S) over this finite polytope give valid conditional passive debtor bounds. As a further tightening, use thirteen weight variables for all weak orders of three finite clocks, with μ its projected first block. For each owner append the linear inequality that its prescribed mean exceeds or equals its mean when removed from the sampled weak order. This last Never-deletion bound uses the earlier calendar polytope construction.

Here is a strict example compatible with the degree-one singleton pattern. Take W={0,1,2}; Γ has rows (0,−2,3,−1),(−1,0,−1,3),(3,−1,0,−1),(−1,3,−1,0). Put J_01=−5. If |Z_i(T)|≤12 for i∈W,T⊆W, withdrawal from the pair 01 gives owner 0 a gain 3. Hence every three-winner ending puts μ(01)≤8/9. Take spectator rewards Z_3(01)=5 and Z_3(02),Z_3(12),Z_3(012)≤3. Its singleton passive maximum is 3. Then every conditional passive value is at most 43/9<5, even when the unfiltered hull has upper endpoint 5. All these inequalities have open strict variants. There is no premise about positive social welfare or individual higher-coalition payoff signs. The selected Γ is the asymmetric degree-one matrix already audited in the quadratic-cost example; its corresponding J_01 there is exactly −5.

**A simple exact use of the maximizing fiber.** Suppose a linear functional on winning payoff triples exposes a set of coalition columns F, all with the identical winning payoff v. If a coalition S in that face satisfies the vertex test and maximizes Z_j on F, then at an ending with winning payoff v which maximizes j's passive payoff among E_fin laws on that fiber, only first coalitions in F attaining that maximum can be observed. Indeed the supporting payoff functional confines every realization of v to F, and δ_S is an admissible same-winning-payoff comparator. In particular if the maximum is unique, the entire suffix first-exit law is δ_S. This can select an outcome even when another physically attainable losing-row payoff elsewhere in the raw hull is larger.

For instance, with the same Γ on W use restricted coalition columns 01 and 02 both equal to (10,10,10) in the winning rows.
