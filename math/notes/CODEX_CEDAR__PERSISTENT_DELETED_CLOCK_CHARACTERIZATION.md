## 1. Self-contained question

Let `I` be a finite player set.  At time `t`, player `j` quits with probability

\[
 p_{t,j}\in[0,1]
\]

in a product root.  For player `i`, put

\[
 c_{t,-i}:=1-\prod_{j\ne i}(1-p_{t,j}),                       \tag{1.1}
\]

the one-stage probability that at least one opponent quits.  The deleted
survival from suffix `m` for `n` stages is

\[
 J_{-i}(m,n)=\prod_{t=m}^{m+n-1}(1-c_{t,-i}).                 \tag{1.2}
\]

The joint survival `J(m,n)` is the analogous product over every player.
Characterize exactly when, for every `m` and `i`,

\[
 J_{-i}(m,n)\to0,
 \qquad J(m,n)\to0.                                         \tag{1.3}
\]

No uniform rate in `m` and no pair-deleted survival are requested.

## 3. Exact characterization

Call player `j` **persistent** if

\[
 \sum_{t=0}^{\infty}p_{t,j}=+\infty,                         \tag{3.1}
\]

equivalently the nonnegative sequence `t ↦ p_{t,j}` is not summable.

### Proposition 1 (two persistent players iff all required clocks die)

Assume `I` is finite and has at least two players.  The following are
equivalent.

1. At least two distinct players are persistent.
2. For every player `i`, the opponent charge sequence `t ↦ c_{t,-i}` is not
   summable.
3. For every player `i` and every suffix `m`,
   `J_{-i}(m,n)→0` as `n→∞`.

Any of these conditions also implies `J(m,n)→0` on every suffix.  Conversely,
joint survival alone does not imply the full family of deleted-player
conclusions.

#### Proof

For every `i`, the elementary union bounds give

\[
 p_{t,j}\le c_{t,-i}\quad(j\ne i),
 \qquad
 c_{t,-i}\le\sum_{j\ne i}p_{t,j}.                           \tag{3.2}
\]

The first inequality holds because the union of opponent Quit events contains
player `j`'s Quit event; the second is the finite union bound.  Since all terms
are nonnegative and `I` is finite, (3.2) shows

\[
 (c_{t,-i})_t\text{ is summable}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (p_{t,j})_t\text{ is summable for every }j\ne i.             \tag{3.3}
\]

Thus all opponent charges are nonsummable exactly when deleting any one player
leaves some persistent player.  For a finite set this is exactly the existence
of at least two distinct persistent players, proving `1⇔2`.

If the opponent charge is nonsummable, removing a finite prefix preserves
nonsummability, and
`tendsto_zero_quittingOpponentSurvivalWeight_of_not_summable_charge`
gives every suffix limit in item 3.  Conversely, if the charge is summable,
`exists_suffix_half_le_quittingOpponentSurvivalWeight_of_summable` supplies a
suffix whose every finite survival product is at least `1/2`, contradicting
item 3.  Hence `2⇔3`, including the case of finitely many isolated sure-Quit
factors.

Finally joint Continue mass is bounded above by every deleted Continue mass.
Choose either persistent player and any other player as the deleted label;
the corresponding deleted survival tends to zero, so joint survival does too.
This proves the final assertion.

### Boundary cases

- With one player, the only deleted clock is the empty product and equals one;
  the required opponent-survival field is impossible.
- A single sure-Quit date kills the date-zero joint product but does not help
  later suffixes.  It contributes only finite hazard mass and is correctly
  rejected by (3.1).
- Exactly one persistent player kills joint survival, but deleting that player
  leaves survival identically one if everyone else always Continues.

## 4. Robust source-changing concatenation

Partition time into consecutive finite blocks `B_k`.  Fix distinct anchors
`a,b`.  Let `bar p_{t,j}` be nominal packet hazards and `p_{t,j}` the hazards
of the actual source-reprojected chronology.  Define

\[
 H^j_k:=\sum_{t\in B_k}\bar p_{t,j},\qquad
 E^j_k:=\sum_{t\in B_k}|p_{t,j}-\bar p_{t,j}|.                \tag{4.1}
\]

### Proposition 2 (summable-error two-anchor adapter)

If

\[
 \sum_k H^a_k=\sum_k H^b_k=+\infty,
 \qquad
 \sum_k E^a_k<\infty,
 \qquad
 \sum_k E^b_k<\infty,                                      \tag{4.2}
\]

then the actual chronology satisfies both survival fields (1.3) on every
suffix.

The same conclusion holds under the scale-free alternative

\[
 p_{t,a}\ge\theta\bar p_{t,a},\qquad
 p_{t,b}\ge\theta\bar p_{t,b}                              \tag{4.3}
\]

for one fixed `theta>0`, without a summable error assumption.

#### Proof

For either anchor `j`, blockwise triangle inequality gives

\[
 \sum_{t\in B_k}p_{t,j}\ge H^j_k-E^j_k.
\]

Summing over blocks, a divergent nonnegative nominal series minus a finite
error budget is still divergent.  Hence both actual anchor hazard sequences
are nonsummable, and Proposition 1 applies.  Under (4.3), the actual cumulative
hazard is at least `theta` times the divergent nominal hazard, with the same
conclusion.

### Sharpness and adapter deficit

1. **Joint charge is insufficient.**  Take one owner `a` with
   `p_{t,a}=1/(t+2)` and all other hazards zero.  Joint survival dies on every
   suffix, but `J_{-a}(m,n)=1` identically.
2. **Pointwise reprojection error is insufficient.**  For two distinct
   anchors `a,b`, let both nominal hazards be
   `bar p_{t,a}=bar p_{t,b}=1/(t+2)` and set both actual hazards to zero.  Both
   errors tend to zero pointwise, yet they erase both divergent clocks because
   their sums diverge.
3. **Two anchors, not two active players per row.**  The anchors may alternate
   and need never be active simultaneously.  What matters is divergent
   cumulative incidence for both labels.  Thus this criterion does not impose
   pair-deleted survival or a two-active product row.

For the actual atom/reset frontier, the remaining raw-clock producer statement is now
precise: choose changing-source packets and tolerances so that two distinct
labels receive divergent nominal marginal hazard while their total marginal
reprojection errors are summable (or a fixed fraction is retained).  Existing
frozen periodic survival proves this only before source changes.  Proposition
2 does not supply the packet labels, the reprojection modulus, or small actual
initial debt.

## 5. Actual atom access forces one labelled hazard quota

Let

\[
 K=|\operatorname{QuittingTerminalOutcome}(I)|,
 \qquad
 M=\max_{S,i}|r(S)_i|.
\]

Consider two profiles `P,Q` which differ only in mover `m`'s complete behavior
strategy, say `sigma,tau`.  Other players may use arbitrary but identical
strategies in the two profiles.  Write `e(sigma),e(tau)` for the probabilities
that the corresponding mover stopping laws ever Quit.

### Proposition 3 (terminal atom to a finite mover block)

Suppose `alpha>0` and, for a nonempty terminal coalition `S` and observer `o`,

\[
 \alpha\le K\,
   \bigl(\Pr_P(S)-\Pr_Q(S)\bigr)r(S)_o.                    \tag{5.1}
\]

Then `M>0` and

\[
 \max\{e(\sigma),e(\tau)\}\ge {\alpha\over4KM}.          \tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently a
`HasQuittingStoppingLawVanishingDebtAtomAlternative` of charge `q>0`
forces, on either the source mover law or the replacement mover law,

\[
 e\ge {q\over16KM}.                                       \tag{5.3}
\]

On that same selected law there is a finite cutoff `N` such that the sum of
the mover's raw marginal Quit probabilities before `N` is at least

\[
 \gamma={q\over32KM}>0.                                   \tag{5.4}
\]

#### Proof

Condition on the mover's pure Quit-time/Never stopping choice.  With all other
strategies fixed, the probability of terminal outcome `S` is a test function
`f : Option Nat -> [0,1]`.  Hence the two outcome masses are the expectations
of the same bounded `f` under the mover stopping laws `mu_sigma,mu_tau`.
The general bounded-expectation total-variation estimate gives

\[
 |\Pr_P(S)-\Pr_Q(S)|
 \le 2\,\operatorname{TV}(\mu_\sigma,\mu_\tau).            \tag{5.5}
\]

This deliberately uses the repository's factor-two absolute-bound estimate;
the sharper `[0,1]` estimate is unnecessary.  From (5.1),
`|r(S)_o|<=M`, and positivity,

\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\mu_\sigma,\mu_\tau)
 \ge {\alpha\over2KM}.                                    \tag{5.6}
\]

The distinguished-`Never` estimate
`stoppingLawTV_le_everQuitMass_add` gives

\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\mu_\sigma,\mu_\tau)
 \le e(\sigma)+e(\tau),                                   \tag{5.7}
\]

so one of the two masses is at least `alpha/(4KM)`.  In the prescribed atom
arm, `alpha=q/2`; in the rectangle arm, `alpha=q/4`.  The profiles in the
rectangle still differ only in the mover law because the same pure-time
observer response is used on both sides.  The uniform weaker conclusion is
(5.3).

Finally `hasSum_quittingHazardStopMass` says the finite stopping masses sum to
`e`.  Choose a finite prefix carrying at least `e/2`.  At each date, stopping
mass is survival times the raw Quit marginal and is no larger than that raw
marginal.  This proves (5.4).

### Corollary 3A (what actual atom access already supplies)

For any `QuittingVanishingDebtAtomAccess`, at every sufficiently large rank
one may choose either the source or replacement side and a finite local prefix
whose mover hazard sum is at least the fixed positive constant (5.4), with
`q=access.charge` and `M=quittingRewardBound reward`.

More strongly, if the positive-debt support contains two distinct movers,
`exists_quantitativeStrongVanishingDebtAtomAlternative_of_mover` can be
applied separately to them.  Alternating their selected raw finite prefixes
then gives two persistent labelled hazard streams, so Propositions 1--2 give
all survival limits for that root schedule.

Concatenation of finite lists of product roots is already an executable root
sequence in a one-state quitting game.  Applying
`QuittingChronologicalDebtData.exactOfRoots` gives its literal reached-tail
semantic pairs and exact prefix recursion.  Thus this construction genuinely
produces the two survival fields.  What it does **not** preserve is the atom
inequality's original whole-profile semantic provenance: after concatenation,
the continuation following a donated row is generally not the suffix against
which that atom was proved.  Consequently the construction does not by itself
make the exact concatenated profile's initial terminal exploitability small.
Its exact chronological datum already supplies zero discrepancy and zero
adverse direct forcing.

### Sharp interface falsifier: one atom need not yield two clocks

Take two players `a,o`.  Let the source have both players Never and let the
replacement make only `a` Quit at time zero.  Set `r({a})_o=-1` and every
other reward coordinate to zero.  For the terminal outcome `{a}`, the signed
atom from source to replacement in observer coordinate `o` equals

\[
 (0-1)(-1)=1.
\]

Thus the prescribed arm of the vanishing-debt atom interface holds for a
positive charge, while only label `a` carries any hazard.  Repeating the
high-mass side kills joint survival but deletion of `a` leaves survival one.
This is an exact counterexample to extracting two clocks from the atom
interface alone, not a claim that the table is an extracted positive-minimum-
debt frontier.

The surviving actual-data alternatives are therefore exact:

1. prove that the extracted positive-debt support has at least two usable
   movers and source-match their selected blocks;
2. in a singleton-support frontier, obtain a second persistent label from
   extra rectangle/coalition incidence not present in the bare atom interface;
   or
3. bypass deleted-clock shadowing with a different semantic endpoint.

### Proposition 4 (one labelled mover plus its deleted clock)

Fix one player `a` and finite consecutive blocks `B_k`.  Put

\[
 H^a_k=\sum_{t\in B_k}p_{t,a},\qquad
 C^{-a}_k=\sum_{t\in B_k}c_{t,-a}.                         \tag{5.8}
\]

If

\[
 \sum_k H^a_k=+\infty,
 \qquad
 \sum_k C^{-a}_k=+\infty,                                 \tag{5.9}
\]

then every required joint and one-player-deleted survival dies on every
suffix.  In particular, it is enough that occurrences of two alternating
packet types give a fixed positive mover quota and a fixed positive
owner-deleted charge quota; the two quotas need not occur in the same row.

#### Proof

The first series makes `a` persistent.  The finite union bound gives

\[
 c_{t,-a}\le\sum_{j\ne a}p_{t,j}.
\]

If every `j ne a` had summable marginal hazard, the second series in (5.9)
would be summable.  Hence some `b ne a` is persistent.  Proposition 1 applies
to the two distinct labels `a,b`.

This form matches the shape of one checked source interface only when its
owner label is aligned correctly.  In a diffuse reprojection window with
displayed owner `a`, the finite-window deleted mass is

\[
 \sum_t \operatorname{live}(t)c_{t,-a}.
\]

Because `0<=live(t)<=1`, any fixed positive lower bound on that
survival-weighted mass is also a lower bound on the raw block charge
`C^{-a}_k`.  The theorem
`QuittingReprojectionDiffuseWindowPacket.exists_concentrated_or_diffuseDeleted`
(`UniformEquilibrium/Diagnostics/Quitting/TerminalSemanticResetReprojectionDiffuseClockBridge.lean`)
keeps this clock on the original executable profile windows and either finds
a recurrent exact opponent coalition or a complete diffuse deleted clock.
Thus it supplies precisely the second kind of quota in Proposition 4 **for
its displayed owner** when such windows are concatenated.

There are two exact compatibility deficits.  First, Proposition 3 puts the
mover hazard on one of two counterfactual sides of the atom chord, while the
diffuse window uses its own actual profile.  The present declarations do not
say that the high-mass mover side is that profile, nor do they reproject a
fixed fraction of its hazard onto it.  Second, in the natural rectangle route
the atom mover and the vanishing-debt observer are distinct, and the diffuse
deleted-clock owner is the observer.  Divergence of `c_{-observer}` may be
caused entirely by the already-known mover hazard.  It therefore does not
supply the mover-deleted charge `c_{-mover}` required by Proposition 4 and
does not by itself expose a second persistent label.

The remaining persistent-clock producer is therefore the following aligned
compatibility statement:

> On the same reached source-matched windows, retain nonsummable raw hazard
> for one label and divergent deleted charge for that **same** label.

This is weaker than preselecting a second opponent label: finite pigeonhole
and Proposition 4 recover that label automatically.  It is stronger than the
currently crossed mover/observer data.

### Proposition 5 (one atom co-realizes two raw clocks off the singleton)

Retain the hypotheses and notation of Proposition 3.  Assume additionally
that `S != {m}`.  Then there is a fixed `b in S`, `b ne m`, such that the
literal profile obtained by giving mover `m` the half-and-half stopping-law
mixture of `sigma,tau` and leaving every other strategy unchanged has a finite
prefix `N` with

\[
 \sum_{t<N}p_{t,m}\ge {\alpha\over8KM},
 \qquad
 \sum_{t<N}p_{t,b}\ge {\alpha\over2KM}.                    \tag{5.10}
\]

For either arm of a vanishing-debt atom of charge `q`, the uniform weaker
conclusion is that both prefix sums are at least `q/(32KM)`.

#### Proof

Write `x=Pr_P(S)` and `y=Pr_Q(S)`.  Positivity of (5.1) and the reward bound
give

\[
 |x-y|\ge {\alpha\over KM}.                                \tag{5.11}
\]

The larger of two nonnegative numbers is at least their absolute difference.
On terminal event `S`, every member of `S` Quits at the finite first-absorption
date.  Since `P,Q` differ only in mover `m`, the complete stopping law of each
`b ne m` is identical on the two sides.  Choose `b in S`, `b ne m`; it exists
because `S` is nonempty and not the mover singleton.  Its common ever-Quit
mass therefore obeys

\[
 e(b)\ge\max\{x,y\}\ge {\alpha\over KM}.                  \tag{5.12}
\]

For the mover, Proposition 3 gives
`TV(mu_sigma,mu_tau)>=alpha/(2KM)` and
`TV<=e(sigma)+e(tau)`.  Ever-Quit mass is affine under complete stopping-law
mixing, so the half mixture has mover mass

\[
 {e(\sigma)+e(\tau)\over2}\ge {\alpha\over4KM}.            \tag{5.13}
\]

Choose finite prefixes retaining half of the masses in (5.12)--(5.13), take
their maximum cutoff, and bound stopping mass by raw marginal hazard.  This is
(5.10).  The rectangle arm has `alpha=q/4`, which gives the displayed uniform
constant.

There is a second exact raw-clock escape in the rectangle arm.  If its common
observer response is `Quit` at a finite time, then the observer—distinct from
the mover—has ever-Quit mass one on both sides.  Combining that response with
the half-mixed mover law gives two clocks even when `S={m}`.  Therefore, after
passing to the fixed-label sequence supplied by
`exists_prescribedAtomSequence_or_vanishingDebtRectangleSequence`, bare atom
access can fail to supply two raw labels only in these cases:

1. the prescribed arm has fixed terminal `{m}`; or
2. the rectangle arm has fixed terminal `{m}` and its common response is
   eventually `Never` (if finite responses occur infinitely often, restrict
   to that subsequence).

This classification agrees with the exact strategic orientation already in
the source.  In the rectangle arm every observer-containing terminal is good
because `observer ne mover`; the surviving mover-singleton lies in the
observer-absent orientation.  The existing forced-owner wall for that
orientation still uses the mover as its only coalition owner and does not
manufacture a second clock.

The half mixture is one literal complete behavioral profile, so Proposition
5 removes the need to choose the mover and opponent quotas on different sides
of an atom chord.  Its finite root prefix can be concatenated with the other
selected blocks to form an executable clock chronology.  The missing datum is
that the original atom/debt estimates are not automatically valid against the
new continuation tail of that concatenation.

## 6. Clock-only actual-data adapter

### Proposition 6 (literal atom blocks produce exact reached clocks)

Let `L_k` be finite lists of literal product roots, and concatenate them into
one root sequence `roots`.  Suppose there are distinct labels `a,b` such that

\[
 \sum_k\sum_{t\in L_k}p_{t,a}=+\infty,
 \qquad
 \sum_k\sum_{t\in L_k}p_{t,b}=+\infty.                    \tag{6.1}
\]

Then `QuittingChronologicalDebtData.exactOfRoots reward roots` is executable,
has exact reached-tail semantic recursion, has prescribed and direct-debt
defects identically zero, and its joint and every one-player-deleted survival
field vanish on every suffix.

#### Proof

The root concatenation is a literal sequence of product mixed actions.  The
definition `exactOfRoots` reads its actual infinite continuation profile and
therefore satisfies the checked identities
`exactOfRoots_prescribedDefect` and `exactOfRoots_directDebtDefect`
(`UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Debt/Dynamic/ExactChronologicalData.lean`).
The two series in (6.1) make `a,b` persistent, so Proposition 1 gives the
survival conclusions.

This applies directly to actual atom access in either of two cases.

1. If the positive-debt support contains two distinct movers, alternate the
   finite prefixes from Corollary 3A for the two supplied-mover accesses.
   Each occurrence contributes a fixed positive quota to its mover.
2. For one fixed-label atom sequence covered by Proposition 5, use at every
   selected rank the finite prefix of the half-mixed literal profile (or the
   finite observer response).  Both fixed labels receive a positive quota.

The conclusion is an actual producer for the named clock fields, not merely a
frozen-source estimate.  It does not claim that the candidate values/debts in
`exactOfRoots` stay close to the original atom frontier.  In fact
`PositiveSlopeCausalRegression.exact_source_has_zero_forcing_but_unit_initialDebt`
(`UniformEquilibrium/Diagnostics/Quitting/Regression/SourceMatchedExposureNoGo.lean`)
shows sharply that exact reached-tail data can have zero local defects while
retaining large initial debt.  Nor does a donated atom row keep its signed
terminal-payoff comparison when the following continuation is replaced.

Thus the persistent-clock obligation itself is reduced to the exact
mover-singleton prescribed-like atom branch.  Outside that branch, the live
universal obligation has moved to `CONDITIONED_PACKET_REPROJECTION`: transport
the atom's low-debt estimate onto the exact initial tail already generated by
the clock chronology.  No separate forcing datum is needed for `exactOfRoots`.
