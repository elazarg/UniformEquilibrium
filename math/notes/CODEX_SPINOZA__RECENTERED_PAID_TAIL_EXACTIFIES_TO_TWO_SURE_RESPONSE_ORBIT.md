# A recentered paid tail exactifies to a two-sure finite-semantic response orbit

Author: CODEX_SPINOZA

## Status

**Exact ordinary mathematics; source-attached reduction, not Lean-checked and
not a terminal consumer.**  The recentered vanishing-word paid edge starts at
an actual high reset child, not an arbitrary tail.  That source already has a
distinct player with zero debt and a prescribed finite sure clock.  The paid
mover is therefore distinct from the clock owner, and its unrestricted cap is
attained by a finite pure time.  Replacing the inherited near-cap target by
this exact cap response produces an actual child with two distinct prescribed
finite sure clocks.

The two-sure class is closed under selecting any later full-gap exact cap
response: every cap is attained by a finite pure time, and after the update
two sure clocks still remain.  Thus the structured off-minimum paid port
enters a literal infinite horizontal response orbit whose complete behavioral
semantics is finite at every node.  This removes cap nonattainment and
tail-truncation as obstructions.  It does not orient the horizontal orbit or
turn any edge into a Nash--Bellman prefix.

## 1. Self-contained exactification theorem

Let \(I=\operatorname{Fin}4\), let rewards be bounded, and suppose the game
has a terminal exploitability gap \(\Gamma>0\).  Let \(X_n\) be actual
behavioral profiles.  Assume that, after fixing labels along a subsequence,
there are distinct players only as derived below, finite dates \(H_n\), and a
player \(a\) such that:

1. the prescribed strategy of \(a\) Quits surely no later than \(H_n\);
2. \(d_a(X_n)=0\); and
3. for one fixed mover \(k\) there is an actual unilateral child
   \(Z_n=X_n[k\leftarrow z_{n,k}]\) with

   \[
   U_k(Z_n)-U_k(X_n)\ge g_0>0.
   \tag{1}
   \]

These are the fields of the high-reset source plus the recentered paid-tail
edge; in the maintained application \(g_0=D_*/4\).

### Theorem 1.1

For every \(n\), one has \(k\ne a\).  Player \(k\)'s complete cap at \(X_n\)
is attained by a finite pure stopping time \(T_n\).  If

\[
 C_n=X_n[k\leftarrow Q_{T_n}],
\tag{2}
\]

then

\[
 U_k(C_n)-U_k(X_n)=d_k(X_n)\ge g_0,
 \qquad d_k(C_n)=0.
\tag{3}
\]

Moreover \(a\) still Quits surely by \(H_n\), \(k\) Quits surely at \(T_n\),
and hence \(C_n\) has two distinct prescribed finite sure clocks.  It admits
an exact finite-clock representative with the same prescribed payoff and the
same four unrestricted behavioral caps.

### Proof

The literal response in (1) is available to player \(k\) at \(X_n\), so
\(d_k(X_n)\ge g_0\).  Since \(d_a(X_n)=0\), this forces \(k\ne a\).

Against \((X_n)_{-k}\), the distinct opponent \(a\) Quits surely by \(H_n\).
Thus every unilateral outcome of \(k\) is decided by \(H_n\).  Behavioral
pure-time extremality reduces the cap to pure stopping times and Never, and
every time later than \(H_n\) is outcome-equivalent to Never.  The cap is
therefore the maximum of a finite menu and is attained.  If its selected
maximizer is Never, replace it by the finite time \(H_n+1\), which is
outcome-equivalent.  This gives the finite \(T_n\).

The response (2) attains the cap, proving its gain and zero target debt.
It changes only player \(k\), so player \(a\)'s prescribed finite clock is
unchanged.  The two-sure semantic canonicalization theorem then preserves
the payoff of every prescribed profile and every arbitrary unilateral
behavioral deviation, proving the last assertion.  QED

The exact cap child \(C_n\) need not equal the inherited cap-band child
\(Z_n\).  The conclusion deliberately retains the literal source \(X_n\)
and the fixed charge, but discards any special target-law or moving-mark data
carried only by \(Z_n\).

## 2. Closure under full-gap cap responses

### Theorem 2.1

Let \(P\) be any actual profile with two distinct prescribed finite sure-clock
players.  If \(d_i(P)>0\), then player \(i\)'s cap is attained by a finite
pure time.  Installing such a cap produces an actual child \(P'\) satisfying

\[
 U_i(P')-U_i(P)=d_i(P),
 \qquad d_i(P')=0,
\tag{4}
\]

and \(P'\) again has two distinct prescribed finite sure clocks.

Consequently, under the terminal gap, starting from any \(C_n\) one may
recursively choose a debtor \(i_m\) with \(d_{i_m}(P_m)\ge\Gamma\) and a
finite pure-time cap response, obtaining a literal orbit

\[
 P_0=C_n\dashrightarrow_{i_0}P_1
 \dashrightarrow_{i_1}P_2\dashrightarrow\cdots
\tag{5}
\]

in which every edge gains at least \(\Gamma\), kills the mover's debt
exactly, and every node has an exact finite-clock representative for its full
unrestricted behavioral semantics.

### Proof

Among two sure-clock players, at least one is distinct from a prescribed
deviator \(i\).  That remaining opponent forces absorption by a finite date
even after replacing \(i\), so the same finite-menu argument as in Theorem
1.1 attains the cap by a finite pure time.

After the update, the unchanged distinct sure-clock opponent remains.  The
mover itself is now prescribed to use a finite pure time, so these two players
are distinct sure clocks in the child.  Own-cap invariance gives (4).
The terminal gap supplies the recursive debtor and the argument repeats.
QED

## 3. Exact application to the recentered high-reset source

The maintained high reset child is a horizontal cap child \(S_{m+1}\).  Its
installed owner \(a\) uses a deterministic finite clock and has zero debt.
The reviewed recentering theorem identifies the literal old tail in the
vanishing live-word branch with this same actual child and produces the
fixed-gain response (1), with \(g_0=D_*/4\).  Theorem 1.1 therefore replaces
that paid response by an exact finite pure-time cap response at the same
source and lands in the two-sure class.  No compact representative or
same-semantic substitution is used at the source.

This is stronger than preserving the reset observer's Quit0 cap at the
original cap-band target.  It does not ask whether that cap survives: the old
finite zero-debt anchor directly makes the paid mover's cap attainable and
creates the second sure clock.

## 4. Remaining obstruction

The orbit (5) is horizontal.  Exact cap replacement kills the mover's debt,
but may reactivate debt of either previously solved sure-clock player.  The
exact recharge identity is

\[
 D(P_{m+1})-D(P_m)
 =-d_{i_m}(P_m)
  +\sum_{j\ne i_m}
     \bigl(d_j(P_{m+1})-d_j(P_m)\bigr).
\tag{6}
\]

Therefore fixed gains do not telescope.  The known two-sure finite-response
cycle shows that finite complete semantics and exact cap attainment alone do
not orient (5); that regression has \(D_*=0\), so it does not decide whether
positive global minimum supplies a further invariant.  Bounded exact-block
capacity also does not apply directly because the arrows in (5) are not
exact Nash--Bellman prefix edges.

The remaining global question is now finite and sharp: does a two-sure
response-closed orbit with a uniform all-behavior gap and \(D_*>0\) force a
terminal approximate Nash profile, or can its horizontal recharge sustain a
positive-gap cycle?  Tail cap nonattainment, moving-clock discontinuity at
each fixed node, and source provenance are no longer available as excuses.

## Boundary and nonclaims

- The distinct zero-debt finite-clock anchor is essential.  A paid mover
  cannot equal that anchor, and the remaining anchor makes the mover's cap a
  finite maximum.
- The exact cap child \(C_n\) is selected from the same actual source but may
  lose every marked-law field of the inherited child \(Z_n\).
- Two sure clocks make full behavioral semantics finite at each node; they do
  not make the profile Nash or give one deadline uniform over the orbit.
- The recursive edges are exact complete cap responses, not exact product
  roots and not chronological Nash--Bellman blocks.
- No well-founded rank, source return, terminal approximate Nash profile, or
  uniform-equilibrium payoff is proved.
