# Separating every Nash move above a fixed rate can require unbounded degree

On a particular rational four-player quitting game, for each $\eta>0$ there is a polynomial strictly decreasing along **every** feasible, individually rational one-stage Nash move with total quit rate at least $\eta$. If its degree is $d$, then $\eta\ge c\,4^{-d}$ for a constant $c>0$ independent of the polynomial. All simultaneous supports are included. This game has an original uniform-equilibrium payoff and no exact terminal equilibrium. Consequently, feasibility with successively smaller rate cutoffs differs from finding one certificate for the unrestricted conjecture. ## Invariance in physical dates

Let $H$ be the hull of the fifteen original exit rewards and $\mu$ the vector of punishment lower bounds. Write $Z$ for the compact set of exact one-stage Nash rows $(w,q)$ at continuations $w\in H$, $w\ge\mu$, with $a=\sum_iq_i$ and one-step payoff $F=F(w,q)$. Assume a positive own singleton level, no sure opening at $\mu$, and the positive sole-mover margin $\Delta$ defined in the linked criterion. All rows in $Z$ are uniformly proper by compactness and lowering a sure player's continuation to $\mu$. [Residual conditions and row definitions](../exports/FORBIDDEN_COALITIONS_AND_FIXED_TARGET_MOMENTS.md)

An exact terminal equilibrium with positive upper asymptotic mean hazard per date exists if and only if some probability $\sigma$ on $Z$ has $\int a\,d\sigma>0$ and
\[
 \int [P(F)-P(w)]\,d\sigma=0\qquad\text{for every polynomial }P .
\]
Equivalently, a probability balancing all the normalized increments charges only positive-rate rows and has finite reciprocal moment $\int a^{-1}\,d\rho$. Multiplication by $a$ or by $1/a$ followed by normalization interchanges the measures, after omitting identity loops of $\sigma$.

Equal polynomial moments give equal $w$ and $F$ marginals. Disintegrating edges by $F$ and starting with this common law yields a stationary Markov path following $F\to w$, in physical order. Choose an ergodic component of positive mean hazard. At least two identities have positive mean on it. Otherwise almost every path uses only one owner $i$. The ergodic theorem gives eventual absorption by $i$ on every finite restart, so the exact bounded payoff recursion gives $w=r(\{i\})$ throughout. At any positive hazard $h$ on this path the quiet rivals' comparisons contradict $\Delta>0$. With two or more identities of positive mean hazard, every calendar obtained by deleting one player absorbs exponentially fast along almost every path. Fix one such path. Bounded Bellman iteration proves exact terminal Nash for every replacement, including Never.

Conversely, in an exact terminal equilibrium with positive upper mean hazard, every finite restart is reached. Indeed a reached sure row would be exact at its successor best-response caps above $\mu$, and hence still sure at $\mu$. On every finite restart the original terminal continuation is optimal against replacements; the divergent total hazard ensures absorption, putting the payoff in $H$. Physical-date empirical laws on these exact edges have a subsequential limit with positive mean hazard. The unnormalized increments telescope.

In particular, if there is no exact terminal equilibrium, for each $\delta>0$ some polynomial satisfies $a+P(F)-P(w)<\delta$ throughout $Z$. Otherwise the finite-dimensional convex hull of its degree-$d$ increment vectors and mean rate intersects the ray of zero increment and rate at least $\delta$ for every $d$. Indeed a strict separator for disjoint sets has nonnegative rate coefficient by unboundedness of the ray. If positive, divide through by that coefficient to obtain the desired polynomial. If zero, multiply its uniformly negative increment by a sufficiently large constant since $a\le4$. Nested weak compactness would now give a physical invariant probability of mean rate at least $\delta$, contrary to the equivalence. Taking $\delta=\eta/2$ gives strict descent whenever $a\ge\eta$.

## A lower bound from the explicit rational table

Use the rational center in the linked construction, in that construction's pair labels. Its two factors are $u=v=1$. Recall its exact row recursions in the order $w\to F$, opposite to physical play. Starting at the native value $Te_2$, use the infinite solo-3 arm with successive player-2 values $T(2/3)^n$, omitting the finite approximation's last cutoff. All its rows are exact. Its endpoint is $(Ap,Bp,0,0)$. Follow it by the fixed finite solo-2 block and then infinitely many pair rows converging back to $Te_2$. Restoring the own singleton levels puts these annotations in $H$ above punishment. Translation preserves polynomial degree and all increments. The initial point is an attained equilibrium payoff, the convex recursions preserve $H$, and the displayed native annotations are nonnegative. The two infinite arms have finite positive total charge by geometric decay. Weighting each exact row by its rate and normalizing telescopes the normalized increments around this loop, with zero mass at the two limiting states. Every normalized balance supported on positive rows has infinite reciprocal moment by the physical-date result. [Exact recursions and all unilateral comparisons](../exports/ALL_FOUR_FIRST_QUITTERS_AND_UNBOUNDED_JOINT_DATES.md)

Write $t=(2/3)^n$ on the first arm. The vector $w(t)$ is affine in $t$ by common interpolation between its endpoints, and the next step has rate $(1-2/3)Tt/(k+Tt)\ge c_0t$. On the pair arm write $t=2^{-n}$. The formulas $1+1/X_{n+1}=2(1+1/X_n)$ and the same relation for $Y_n$ give $X_n=b_Xt/(1-b_Xt)$, $Y_n=b_Yt/(1-b_Yt)$, with $b_X=X_0/(1+X_0)$ and similarly for $b_Y$. The rate is at least $c_1t$ by the pair hazards. Each component of $w(t)$ has a numerator of degree at most two and common denominator $D(t)=(1-b_Xt)(1-b_Yt)$. This follows for the two observers from their bilinear invariant. Both $c_0,c_1$ are positive constants.

Normalize a proposed degree-$d$ separator to have minimum zero and maximum one on the compact set of loop nodes and endpoints. Its oscillation is positive because it strictly decreases at some retained row for all sufficiently small $\eta$. On either arm let $q=2/3,l=1,D=1$ or $q=1/2,l=2,D$ as just given. Then
\[
 |P(w(t))-P(w(0))|\le C4^d t\qquad(0\le t\le q^{ld}).
\]
To prove this, $D(t)^d[P(w(t))-P(w(0))]/t$ is a polynomial of degree at most $m=ld-1$. It is bounded in absolute value by $q^{-m}$ on $1,q,\ldots,q^m$. For $0\le t\le q^m$ every Lagrange basis coefficient is bounded by its absolute value at zero. At node $q^j$ that value is at most
\[
 \prod_{k\ge1}(1-q^k)^{-2}\,q^{(m-j)(m-j+1)/2}.
\]
Their sum is bounded independently of $m$. This bounds the polynomial by $C_q q^{-m}$. On the pair arm and $t\le4^{-d}$ one has $D(t)^{-d}\le(1-t)^{-2d}\le e^{2/3}$ since $d4^{-d}\le1/4$. Multiplication by $t/D(t)^d$ proves the estimate.

On each infinite arm stop immediately before the first rate below $\eta$. The parameter of that step and of all later nodes is at most $\eta/c_j$. For $\eta\le c'4^{-d}$ with $c'>0$ sufficiently small, both interpolation estimates apply and the finite solo block is retained. Jumping from the last retained endpoint to each arm's limiting vector closes the path. Its only possible increases of the normalized polynomial are these two jumps, each at most $C'4^d\eta$. Its oscillation on the retained nodes is therefore at most $2C'4^d\eta$. All omitted nodes lie within $C'4^d\eta$ in polynomial value of a retained limiting endpoint. Thus $1\le4C'4^d\eta$. When $\eta>c'4^{-d}$ the claimed lower bound already holds, after adjusting the game-dependent constant.

### Uniform absorption after every deviation in the quadratic-invariant example

On the [priority subfamily of the quadratic-invariant theorem](../exports/UNIFORM_EQUILIBRIUM_FROM_A_QUADRATIC_INVARIANT_ON_CONTINUATION_PAYOFFS.md), the limiting Nash calendar has a uniform conditional first-exit probability at least $d>0$ on each date. The centered rewards have absolute value at most $M$. For each owner $p$ there is an owner $j$ with a uniform margin $g>0$ satisfying $\min(0,J_{jp})-\Gamma_{jp}\ge g$. In any $L$-date window of the calendar beginning at a continuation, let $u$ be the probability that a rival of $p$ stops in that window. With probability at least $1-u-(1-d)^L$, player $p$ is the sole first quitter in the window. Thus $j$'s prescribed payoff is at most $\Gamma_{jp}+2M(u+(1-d)^L)$. Its immediate Quit offer is at least $\min(0,J_{jp})-2Mu$, since all current hazards except $p$'s can affect that comparison on an event of probability at most $u$. Conditional Nash gives $4Mu\ge g-2M(1-d)^L$.

Fix $L$ so that $2M(1-d)^L\le g/2$. Against any private replacement by $p$, the probability that all rivals survive $k$ consecutive such windows is at most $(1-g/(8M))^k$. Independence lets the rival clocks be restarted after their own survival to each window. The expected absorption time is uniformly bounded over all replacements. Consequently the same calendar on every signed row raise has regret $O(1/N)$ in the $N$-date average payoff and has the same rate of convergence to its terminal target. The constants depend on the table and the chosen row levels. PRICE derived the estimate; SOURCE checked the argument at every restart of the limiting calendar.

## A prescribed singleton matrix and a neighborhood of priority completions

Every hollow symmetric four-by-four R₀ matrix $G$ admits the following completion. R₀ means that no simplex vector $z$ satisfies $Gz\ge0$ and $z_i(Gz)_i=0$ for all $i$. Choose any priority order. In a nonempty coalition $S$ count owner $j\in S$ unless some earlier $i\in S$ has $G_{ij}>0$. Let $b^S_j$ be the indicator of being counted and pay $Z^0(S)=G b^S$. A full-dimensional neighborhood of these 56 centered entries admits exact absorbing native Nash play and uniform-equilibrium payoffs at every signed own level. Nonsingularity is unnecessary. Positive diagonal row scaling extends the result to symmetrizable R₀ matrices.

Here is a finite asymmetric version and a proof. Write $G$ for any hollow singleton matrix with a negative entry in each row and column. Choose a positive diagonal $D$, let $H_{ij}=\min((DG)_{ij},(DG)_{ji})$, and require $z^\top Hz>0$ whenever $z$ is a simplex vector with $Gz\ge0$. In a symmetric R₀ game take $D=I$: a zero sum of the nonnegative $z_i(Gz)_i$ would contradict R₀, and every column, hence every row, has a negative entry. For each unordered pair with a positive weighted entry, direct an arc from $i$ to $j$ when $(DG)_{ij}$ is the larger entry, breaking positive ties arbitrarily. Put no arc on pairs with both entries nonpositive. A player is counted when it is present and no other participant directs an arc to it. The promised symmetric rule breaks ties by the chosen order. In the asymmetric version directed cycles are allowed.

At a product row $q$ write $C=\prod(1-q_i)$, $r=1-C$ and $x=\mathbb E_q b^S$ with $b^\varnothing=0$. The forced Quit offer $Q_i^0$ sums $G_{ij}x_j$ over $j\ne i$ for which $i$ does not suppress $j$. Thus $2x^\top DQ^0\le x^\top Hx$: an oriented pair contributes only the smaller of its two weighted entries to the coefficient after dividing by $2x_ix_j$, and a pair of nonpositive entries contributes their sum.

On bounded genuine Nash rows at nearby tables, $\sum_i x_i\ge h r$ with uniform $h>0$. At blank the ratio tends to one. A nonblank row with $x=0$ must have a nonempty set $T$ of sure owners: otherwise some singleton has positive probability. Every member of $T$ is suppressed by another member on the positive-probability event where all optional owners wait. The directed graph on $T$ then has minimum indegree one. With at most four vertices and at most one arc per unordered pair it has a vertex $j$ of indegree exactly one. Its unique blocker $i$ strictly benefits by waiting when all optional owners wait, as this restores the positive reward $G_{ij}$. On all other events every additional restored owner is also positively valued by $i$; the disappearance of $i$'s own counted indicator is immaterial. Some rival in $T$ quits surely throughout. Thus such a row is never Nash. Compactness proves the uniform bound. For the priority order alone every nonempty coalition has a counted member already.

For clarity the perturbation estimate does not invert $G$. Choose $y_0=t(e_a+e_b)$ with $H_{ab}<0$ and $t>0$ small. Then $y_0^\top Hy_0<0$. For each $i$ a rival $j$ with $G_{ij}<0$ gives $i$ the same negative reward at $j$ and at $ij$: $i$ never suppresses $j$. Hence choosing $j$ surely is a punishment below $(Gy_0)_i$ for sufficiently small $t$, also under small perturbations. Start backward Nash iteration at $v_0=Gy_0$. Write $v=Gy+e$ and propagate $y'=Cy+x$ exactly. At sup-norm reward distance $\epsilon$ propagate $\|e'\|\le C\|e\|+\epsilon r$, so $\|e\|\le\epsilon$. The nonnegative total of $y$ stays between $\min(2t,h)$ and $\max(2t,4)$. Since $x_i>0$ implies true active indifference or a sure Quit with parent payoff $v'_i=Q_i$, expansion gives

$$y'^\top H y'-C^2y^\top Hy=2x^\top H y'-x^\top Hx\le 2x^\top Dv'-x^\top Hx+O(\epsilon r)\le O(\epsilon r).$$

Thus $y^\top Hy\le O(\epsilon)$ uniformly. The simplex test and compactness keep $v$ a uniform distance from the nonnegative orthant at all these cuts. Blank is therefore uniformly separated from every selected Nash row. The finite-word diagonalization gives exact native absorption; terminating each long block at the displayed protection for its least-surviving player and placing all others surely on the next date gives the signed uniform target. [Quadratic invariant theorem](../exports/UNIFORM_EQUILIBRIUM_FROM_A_QUADRATIC_INVARIANT_ON_CONTINUATION_PAYOFFS.md)

At the symmetric reference any pair with $G_{ab}<0$ can also quit surely together at date one. They are indifferent, and an outsider's joining can only remove positively valued contributors. This particular calendar absorbs immediately against any one deviation, at all signed levels. In the full perturbation neighborhood only joint geometric absorption is needed in the preceding proof. There is also an open subfamily at each prescribed $G$ on which a single exact all-signed calendar has **geometrically decaying opponent survival**: for each column $k$ increase a negative $J_{ik}=G_{ik}$ by an arbitrarily small positive amount, so $\min(0,J_{ik})>G_{ik}$. The diagonal backward calendar is Nash from every indexed date, including a suffix after a sure row, because the finite blocks are constructed at those dates too. If some arbitrarily long window kept all rivals of $k$ almost surely quiet, its joint geometric bound would force a first singleton $k$ with probability tending to one inside the window. At its start the chosen hostile $i$ would receive almost $G_{ik}$ but could quit for at least $\min(0,J_{ik})-o(1)$. Hence on some fixed window every player faces a uniform positive probability of an opponent exit. Iteration gives the geometric bound under all deviations. This is a window guarantee; the argument need not produce two hazards on each date.

## The cost in simultaneous exits just above the threshold

Fix the singleton matrix with mate rewards 3 and crossed rewards −1, the directed crossed joining rewards with $0<d<1$, and the three matched entries other than $x=J_{20}$ strictly between zero and $x_*=(3+\sqrt5)/2$. Fix the own levels, at least one of them positive. For every finite reward bound $M$ there are $a,b>0$ such that, for $x_*<x<x_*+a$ and every completion bounded by $M$, every original private profile satisfies

$$e+u\ \ge\ b(x-x_*),$$

where $e$ is maximum signed terminal regret and $u$ is the supremum over physical dates of their unconditional simultaneous first-exit probabilities. The infimum of $e+u$ has order exactly $x-x_*$ on every such completion. Indeed keep the critical four-solo calendar and start it with owner 0. Its prescribed payoffs stay unchanged and $u=0$. Only owner 2 can gain from the change, by quitting on an owner-0 date, and the relevant tie has probability at most $h=(\sqrt5-1)/2$ under any deadline. At the first date the gain is exactly $h(x-x_*)$ because that offer was binding. For games satisfying the strictly positive slope test there is also an exact equilibrium attaining the linear upper bound on collisions with zero regret. Its entrant hazard is $O(x-x_*)$, and even total collision probability is $O(x-x_*)$ because per-period survival is bounded below one. Constants in the lower bound may depend on the fixed inputs and $M$.

Write $r=e+u$. Signed centering bounds native total regret and joint Never mass by $O(e)$. At a date of joint reach $S>0$ their conditional values are $O(e/S)$. Each subsequent conditional one-date collision probability is at most $u/S$, so the total conditional collision probability is at most $7(u/S)^{1/3}$ by the reach splitting estimate. The finite graph argument implies that there are $K<\infty$ and $\eta>0$ such that every row with $S>Kr$ has stopping probability at least $\eta$, uniformly for the bounded tables near $x_*$. Indeed if conditional native regret, Never and total collision all tend to zero on such rows, the initial value approaches the energy-zero image of the simplex. This compact set avoids the nonnegative orthant. A convergent subsequence of the hazards has at most one active owner; if its limit were blank each immediate native Quit offer would be zero, contradicting the negative coordinate. Taking $K$ sufficiently large applies this compactness implication. Up to the first cut $T$ with $S_T\le Kr$ one therefore has $\sum_{t<T}S_t\le1/\eta$.

For clarity this gives a sharper bound on the quadratic expression $E(w)=w^\top G^{-1}w$ than applying total collision directly. At a date let $c$ be row survival, $\ell$ conditional collision probability and $z_i$ the probability of exactly singleton $i$ in the row. Its expected head reward differs from $Gz$ by $O(\ell)$, and $|z^\top Gz|=O(\ell)$ since $G$ is hollow and $z_iz_j\le q_iq_j\le\ell$. Expanding the payoff recursion gives $E(w)-c^2E(w^+)=2z^\top w+O(\ell)$. For the native forced offer $Q_i$, conditional cap $B_i$, and conditional debt $d_i$, one has $|w_i|\le |Q_i|+(B_i-Q_i)+d_i$ and $\sum_i z_i|Q_i|=O(\ell)$ since the own singleton pays zero. Along the truncated prefix, $\sum_t S_t q_i(B_i-Q_i)$ and each $S_td_i$ are bounded by initial native debt for that owner, while $\sum_t S_t z_i\le1$. The total absolute contribution to the telescoped energy from noncollision terms is therefore $O(e)$. Also $\sum_{t<T} S_t^2\ell_t\le u\sum_{t<T}S_t=O(u)$ and $S_T^2|E(w_T)|=O(r^2)$. This proves $|E(w)|=O(r)$ initially and at every cut of reach bounded below, applying the same proof to the conditional law. If $r=0$, use the infinite telescope instead.

At $x=x_*$ every limiting calendar with $r\to0$ follows the forward orientation, has one proper owner on every date and has uniformly bounded run lengths, say $L$. Here total collision tends to zero by the preceding reach splitting estimate. Take $N=10(L+1)$. For all sufficiently small $r$ and $|x-x_*|$, the first $N$ dates have joint reach bounded below, each largest hazard lies in a fixed compact subinterval of $(0,1)$, and their owner word contains a complete run of 0 flanked by the preceding and following owners. Otherwise take violating sequences with $r\to0$, $x\to x_*$ and extract on these dates. Bounded tables are compact, and their limiting profile has the asserted properties. Positive solo hazards stabilize the labels. The same argument makes the sign and ratio of the two nonzero coordinates at these switches arbitrarily close to their values in the golden cycle. Taking $a,b$ smaller covers profiles outside the small-$r$ neighborhood.

On these dates all other hazards are $O(u)$, since their simultaneous quitting with the dominant owner has unconditional probability at most $u$. The dominant owner's two actions each have probability bounded below, so their losses relative to its conditional native cap are $O(e)$ by its one-date cap recursion. Forced Quit is $O(u)$, and optimal Wait is its successor cap plus $O(u)$ since its rivals almost surely wait on that date. Thus at a change of dominant owner the previous and current coordinates of the prescribed native value are $O(r)$. At the end of the 0 run write $v$ for the next value, $p=3$ for the previous owner of the run and $m=2$ for the mate of 0. Here $v_p$ stays strictly positive. From $|E(v)|=O(r)$ and the inverse entries $2/15$ on the diagonal and $1/5$ on crossed pairs, $t=v_m/v_p$ satisfies $t^2+3t+1=O(r)$. The qualitative golden signs and ratio select the simple root $-\rho$, where $\rho=(3-\sqrt5)/2$. Hence $v_m=-\rho v_p+O(r)$, without estimating the sum of the coordinates at this rate.

Write $S'$ for the product of the dominant owner's survival factors through the complete 0 run and $H=1-S'$. Its other hazards change the payoff recursion by $O(Nu)$. The previous owner's starting value is $O(r)$, its singleton payoff is −1 and its ending value is $v_p$. Thus $S'v_p=H+O(r)$. The last dominant hazard $q$ in the run is bounded below and satisfies $q\le H$. The mate's prescribed payoff there is $3q+(1-q)v_m+O(u)$ and its forced Quit payoff is $qx+O(u)$. Its conditional debt is $O(e)$. Using $v_m<0$, these give

$$x\le 3+\frac{1-q}{q}v_m+O(r)\le 3+\frac{S'}H v_m+O(r)=3-\rho+O(r)=x_*+O(r).$$

This proves the bound for one physical date.
