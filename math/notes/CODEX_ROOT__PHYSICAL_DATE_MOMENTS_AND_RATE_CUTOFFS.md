# Separating every Nash move above a fixed rate can require unbounded degree

On a particular rational four-player quitting game, for each $\eta>0$ there is a polynomial strictly decreasing along **every** feasible, individually rational one-stage Nash move with total quit rate at least $\eta$. If its degree is $d$, then $\eta\ge c\,4^{-d}$ for a constant $c>0$ independent of the polynomial. All simultaneous supports are included. This game has an original uniform-equilibrium payoff and no exact terminal equilibrium. Consequently, feasibility with successively smaller rate cutoffs differs from finding one certificate for the unrestricted conjecture. ## Invariance in physical dates

Let $H$ be the hull of the fifteen original exit rewards and $\mu$ the vector of punishment lower bounds. Write $Z$ for the compact set of exact one-stage Nash rows $(w,q)$ at continuations $w\in H$, $w\ge\mu$, with $a=\sum_iq_i$ and one-step payoff $F=F(w,q)$. Assume a positive own singleton level, no sure opening at $\mu$, and the positive sole-mover margin $\Delta$ defined in the linked criterion. All rows in $Z$ are uniformly proper by compactness and lowering a sure player's continuation to $\mu$. [Residual conditions and row definitions](../exports/FORBIDDEN_COALITIONS_AND_FIXED_TARGET_MOMENTS.md)

An exact terminal equilibrium with positive upper asymptotic mean hazard per date exists if and only if some probability $\sigma$ on $Z$ has $\int a\,d\sigma>0$ and
\[
 \int [P(F)-P(w)]\,d\sigma=0\qquad\text{for every polynomial }P .
\]
Equivalently, a probability balancing all the normalized increments charges only positive-rate rows and has finite reciprocal moment $\int a^{-1}\,d\rho$. Multiplication by $a$ or by $1/a$ followed by normalization interchanges the measures, after omitting identity loops of $\sigma$.

Equal polynomial moments give equal $w$ and $F$ marginals. Disintegrating edges by $F$ and starting with this common law yields a stationary Markov path following $F\to w$, in physical order. Choose an ergodic component of positive mean hazard. At least two identities have positive mean on it. Otherwise almost every path uses only one owner $i$. The ergodic theorem gives eventual absorption by $i$ on every finite restart, so the exact bounded payoff recursion gives $w=r(\{i\})$ throughout. At any positive hazard $h$ on this path the quiet rivals' comparisons contradict $\Delta>0$. With two or more identities of positive mean hazard, every calendar obtained by deleting one player absorbs exponentially fast along almost every path. Fix one such path. Bounded Bellman iteration proves exact terminal Nash for every replacement, including Never.

Conversely, in an exact terminal equilibrium with positive upper mean hazard, every finite restart is reached. Indeed a reached sure row would be exact at its successor best-response caps above $\mu$, and hence still sure at $\mu$. On every finite restart the original terminal continuation is optimal against replacements; the divergent total hazard ensures absorption, putting the payoff in $H$. Physical-date empirical laws on these exact edges have a subsequential limit with positive mean hazard. The unnormalized increments telescope.

In particular, if there is no exact terminal equilibrium, for each $\delta>0$ some polynomial satisfies $a+P(F)-P(w)<\delta$ throughout $Z$. Otherwise the finite-dimensional convex hull of its degree-$d$ increment vectors and mean rate intersects the ray of zero increment and rate at least $\delta$ for every $d$. Indeed a strict separator for disjoint sets has nonnegative rate coefficient by unboundedness of the ray. If positive, divide through by that coefficient to obtain the desired polynomial. If zero, multiply its uniformly negative increment by a sufficiently large constant since $a\le4$. Nested weak compactness would now give a physical invariant probability of mean rate at least $\delta$, contrary to the equivalence. Taking $\delta=\eta/2$ gives strict descent whenever $a\ge\eta$.

## A lower bound from the explicit rational table

Use the rational center in the linked construction, in that construction's pair labels. Its two factors are $u=v=1$. Recall its exact row recursions in the order $w\to F$, opposite to physical play. Starting at the native value $Te_2$, use the infinite solo-3 arm with successive player-2 values $T(2/3)^n$, omitting the finite approximation's last cutoff. All its rows are exact. Its endpoint is $(Ap,Bp,0,0)$. Follow it by the fixed finite solo-2 block and then infinitely many pair rows converging back to $Te_2$. Restoring the own singleton levels puts these annotations in $H$ above punishment. Translation preserves polynomial degree and all increments. The initial point is an attained equilibrium payoff, the convex recursions preserve $H$, and the displayed native annotations are nonnegative. The two infinite arms have finite positive total charge by geometric decay. Weighting each exact row by its rate and normalizing telescopes the normalized increments around this loop, with zero mass at the two limiting states. Every normalized balance supported on positive rows has infinite reciprocal moment by the physical-date result. [Exact recursions and all unilateral comparisons](../exports/ALL_FOUR_FIRST_QUITTERS_AND_UNBOUNDED_JOINT_DATES.md)

Write $t=(2/3)^n$ on the first arm. The vector $w(t)$ is affine in $t$ by common interpolation between its endpoints, and the next step has rate $(1-2/3)Tt/(k+Tt)\ge c_0t$. On the pair arm write $t=2^{-n}$. The formulas $1+1/X_{n+1}=2(1+1/X_n)$ and the same relation for $Y_n$ give $X_n=b_Xt/(1-b_Xt)$, $Y_n=b_Yt/(1-b_Yt)$, with $b_X=X_0/(1+X_0)$ and similarly for $b_Y$. The rate is at least $c_1t$ by the pair hazards. Each component of $w(t)$ has a numerator of degree at most two and common denominator $D(t)=(1-b_Xt)(1-b_Yt)$. This follows for the two observers from their bilinear invariant. Both $c_0,c_1$ are positive constants.

Normalize a proposed degree-$d$ separator to have minimum zero and maximum one on the compact set of loop nodes and endpoints. Its oscillation is positive because it strictly decreases at some retained row for all sufficiently small $\eta$. On either arm let $q=2/3,l=1,D=1$ or $q=1/2,l=2,D$ as just given. Then
\[
 |P(w(t))-P(w(0))|\le C4^d t\qquad(0\le t\le q^{ld}).
\]
To prove this, $D(t)^d[P(w(t))-P(w(0))]/t$ is a polynomial of degree at most $m=ld-1$. It is bounded in absolute value by $q^{-m}$ on $1,q,\ldots,q^m$. For $0\le t\le q^m$ every Lagrange basis coefficient is bounded by its absolute value at zero. At node $q^j$ that value is at most
\[
 \prod_{k\ge1}(1-q^k)^{-2}\,q^{(m-j)(m-j+1)/2}.
\]
Their sum is bounded independently of $m$. This bounds the polynomial by $C_q q^{-m}$. On the pair arm and $t\le4^{-d}$ one has $D(t)^{-d}\le(1-t)^{-2d}\le e^{2/3}$ since $d4^{-d}\le1/4$. Multiplication by $t/D(t)^d$ proves the estimate.

On each infinite arm stop immediately before the first rate below $\eta$. The parameter of that step and of all later nodes is at most $\eta/c_j$. For $\eta\le c'4^{-d}$ with $c'>0$ sufficiently small, both interpolation estimates apply and the finite solo block is retained. Jumping from the last retained endpoint to each arm's limiting vector closes the path. Its only possible increases of the normalized polynomial are these two jumps, each at most $C'4^d\eta$. Its oscillation on the retained nodes is therefore at most $2C'4^d\eta$. All omitted nodes lie within $C'4^d\eta$ in polynomial value of a retained limiting endpoint. Thus $1\le4C'4^d\eta$. When $\eta>c'4^{-d}$ the claimed lower bound already holds, after adjusting the game-dependent constant.
