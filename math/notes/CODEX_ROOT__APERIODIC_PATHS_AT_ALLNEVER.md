# Aperiodic linear trajectories at the AllNever boundary

Work in a four-player native table with zero own singleton, perpetual and live rewards, and write Γ,J for its 12 passive singleton and 12 joining pair entries. For any support S of size at least two assume Γ is invertible and the principal blocks J_SS, (J−Γ)_SS are invertible. One only needs these block assumptions on supports used in the criterion. The remaining 32 entries are arbitrary finite reals.

A projective linear step consists of y∈Δ, a vector 0≤x≤y, and S=supp x. Write σ=1−∑x, y'=(y−x)/σ if σ>0, and g=Γy−Jx. For δ>0 a step is regular at margin δ if

    |S|≥2,       δ≤σ≤1−δ,
    xᵢ≥δ, gᵢ=0 for i∈S,       gᵢ≥δ for i∉S.

It takes y to y'. Every condition is on the 24 pair entries and the normalized finite masses. There is no assumption of periodicity, a fixed ordering of the supports, any simple or dominant eigenvalue, contraction in all directions, or recurrence around a given word.

**Theorem.** If arbitrarily long finite paths of regular steps at some fixed δ exist, every completion of the 32 remaining entries admits a genuine nonblank native Nash profile with positive probability that nobody ever quits. Its finite masses and payoffs can be taken arbitrarily small with first-exit probability comparable to its starting amplitude. In particular any native AllNever-only table in the generic domain has a finite upper bound, depending only on Γ,J,δ, on all paths at that margin. This statement concerns the AllNever-only source, and does not imply an absorbing equilibrium or no unrestricted counterexamples.

Here is a general version which permits vanishing step and guard margins. Suppose an invariant ergodic probability on bi-infinite marked paths of the preceding *exact* linear steps is supplied. Require |S|≥2, 0<σ<1, and all active and all inactive comparisons strictly separated almost surely, with

    ∫|log min({1,σ,1−σ}∪{xᵢ:i∈S}∪{gᵢ:i∉S})| < ∞.

Then the same conclusion holds. Uniformly positive margins are the special case for which the probability is produced automatically. Indeed the set of labeled regular transitions is closed and bounded (the finite support label is retained). Compactness and diagonal extraction of increasingly long paths around their center gives a nonempty compact bi-infinite shift space. The empirical occupation of a bi-infinite trajectory has an invariant probability accumulation point; an ergodic component supplies the required law.

**The exact local maps.** Let w be a physical child payoff. For support S set the other hazards to zero and, for owners in S, solve forced Quit = Wait at w, allowing signed hazards in a neighborhood of zero temporarily. These are polynomial equalities with linear part (J−Γ)_SS q_S = w_S. The implicit function theorem gives a real-analytic q_S(w) with derivative (J−Γ)_SS⁻¹w_S. Prescribing Wait for everyone’s value defines the smooth parent map Φ_S(w). Its linearization A_S acts by w↦w+Γ_{*,S}(J−Γ)_SS⁻¹w_S. Its determinant is det J_SS / det(J−Γ)_SS by the determinant lemma, so Φ_S has a smooth inverse Ψ_S fixing zero and with derivative M_S=A_S⁻¹. All these are functions on an ordinary neighborhood of zero. There are finitely many supports, so uniform bounds on the second derivatives are available there for any fixed completion.

Let (y_n,x_n,S_n,σ_n) be a typical bi-infinite trajectory, abbreviate R_0=1 and R_n=∏_{j<n, j≥0}σ_j on the forward half. Define l_n=R_n Γy_n. The exact linear conditions give l_{n+1}=M_{S_n}l_n, and the hazards given by the linear part of q_{S_n} at l_{n+1} are R_n x_n. By Birkhoff,

    n⁻¹ log R_n → λ=∫log σ<0.

Since Γ is invertible and y stays in the probability simplex, ∥Γy∥ is uniformly bounded above and away from zero. Thus l_n has exponent λ. By integrability of the logarithmic margins, for every ε>0 their values along almost every forward trajectory are bounded below by c(ε)exp(−εn). This follows by the summable tail bound and Borel–Cantelli, which does not require independence.

**Tracking the nonlinear trajectory.** We give the details since merely following the linear support word in the nonlinear game need not respect its incentive comparisons. Apply the invertible multiplicative ergodic theorem to the matrix cocycle M_{S_n} over the shift with the ergodic law above. The matrices and their inverses form a finite bounded family. At a regular point the usual two-sided Lyapunov norms, for any tolerance ε, have the following properties. The four-dimensional space splits into invariant exponent spaces of exponents χ; in norms |·|_n at time n, the one-step bounds on such a space are exp(χ±ε). Use the sum norm over the spaces, so the corresponding projections have norm at most one. One may arrange ∥u∥≤|u|_n≤C exp(εn)∥u∥ on the forward half. These are the tempered-norm form of Oseledets' theorem; the subexponentially tempered factor can always be bounded by C exp(εn). The theorem requires log⁺ integrability of the cocycle and its inverse and an invertible measure-preserving base, all ensured here. One construction sums the two-sided norms of iterates within each exponent subspace with exponential weights exp(−χk−ε|k|); tempered angles and limits of products give the stated equivalence.

Choose 2λ<γ<λ avoiding the finite list of exponents and choose ε>0 small relative to all separations and to |λ|. On the space of sequences with norm sup_n exp(−γn)|v_n|_n, the difference operator v_{n+1}−M_{S_n}v_n has a bounded right inverse for forcing f_n at time n+1, measured in sup_n exp(−γ(n+1))|f_n|_{n+1}. Indeed for χ<γ take the forward sum with zero initial component, and for χ>γ take the negative sum backwards from infinity. The geometric bounds are furnished by |χ−γ|>ε and the one-step estimates. This claim includes possible positive, zero and multiple exponents.

Write H_n(u)=Ψ_{S_n}(u)−M_{S_n}u. Uniformly within a fixed Euclidean ball, |H_n(u)|≤C∥u∥² in Euclidean norm and ∥H_n(u)−H_n(v)∥≤C(∥u∥+∥v∥)∥u−v∥. For amplitude T>0 seek u_n=T l_n+d_n with d the right inverse applied to H_n(Tl_n+d_n). Since ∥l_n∥≤C exp((λ+ε)n), on a sequence ball ∥d∥_γ≤C_0 T² the forcing norm is O(T²), using 2λ+3ε<γ. Its Lipschitz constant on that ball is O(T), since multiplication by the norm-equivalence factor exp(εn) still leaves exp((λ+2ε)n) bounded. Choose C_0 large and T small (the quadratic and higher-in-T terms in the ball estimate may depend on C_0); Banach's contraction theorem produces d and a genuine forward trajectory u_{n+1}=Ψ_{S_n}(u_n), with ∥d_n∥≤C T² exp(γn). All maps are evaluated inside their common Euclidean domains. There is no solution of an uncontrolled boundary-value matching problem at a finite terminal date.

**Implementation.** The exact hazards q_n=q_{S_n}(u_{n+1}) on S_n, zero elsewhere, equal T R_n x_n up to O(T² exp(γn)) coordinatewise by Taylor expansion and 2λ+2ε<γ. An inactive player's true Wait-minus-Quit margin equals T R_n gᵢ(n) with the same error, since ΓR−Jx is precisely its first-order margin. The forward bounds R_n≥c exp((λ−ε)n) and the subexponential margin estimate ensure both positive hazards and all inactive inequalities, uniformly over n for sufficiently small T, using γ<λ−2ε. Each hazard is <1 and ∑_{n,i}q_{ni}=O(T). At least one first-date hazard has a positive lower multiple of T. Consequently original joint Never is positive and the total exit probability lies between cT and CT for trajectory-dependent constants. The constructed u_n tend to zero. Because everyone waits with positive probability, the Wait recursions are exact at u_n and the active Quit actions tie. Iterating shows the actual prescribed payoff and the payoff of waiting forever equal u_n, since any terminal remainder is bounded by ∥u_m∥→0. Every finite or Never replacement is bounded by the same value; mixtures of those deadlines are all behavioral responses. Thus the result gives actual native Nash, not just formal annotations or selected local roots.

**Relevance at a real minimizing source.** On the small-price N0 branch with total finite mass β≫t, all near-minimizing native profiles have D_Z=o(β²). For the normalized unconditional finite masses p/β from any such beginning, the complete-menus estimate eᵢ−Fᵢ(n)=β(ΓR(n)−Jx(n))ᵢ+O(β²) is uniform, the maximal violation of g≥0 is o(1), and ∑ₙ xᵢ(n)gᵢ(n)→0 for each i. Here R, x are in units of initial β. Therefore arbitrarily long strings starting from a fixed positive fraction of initial finite mass, with all regular-margin requirements (inactive g positive, active relative hazards bounded below, both survival bounds) and their active equalities accurate in the limit at each fixed normalized step, would give an infinite regular-margin linear path by compactness. In particular the active equalities are forced on such strings by the preceding energy estimate: at each fixed step the retained fraction is bounded below, all active masses are bounded below relative to β, and all negative g's are o(1). A surviving N0 source must encounter arbitrarily thin supports, a quiet indifference, an endpoint of the radial step interval, or a solo/continuous phase rather than remain in this strictly regular region on arbitrarily long anchored strings. The finite linear tests for each proposed path length use only Γ,J. Periodic expanding clocks were already usable from their specified finite words; this criterion does not assume or conclude that a periodic linear itinerary exists.

The external input is V. I. Oseledets, "A multiplicative ergodic theorem. Characteristic Ljapunov exponents of dynamical systems", Transactions of the Moscow Mathematical Society 19 (1968), 197–231, in its standard finite-dimensional invertible splitting and tempered-norm formulation.
