# Cantor sets of uniform-equilibrium payoffs on an open class of four-player quitting games

The original uniform-equilibrium payoff set of the game below is exactly
\[
 \{(0,0,0,v):v\in K\},\qquad
 K=(K/3+1/3)\ \cup\ (K/4+3/4)\ \cup\ (3K/8+47/96),
\]
where $K$ is the unique nonempty compact solution. Every infinite sequence of three specified action rows gives an exact terminal equilibrium. Conversely every uniform-equilibrium payoff comes from such a sequence. This description persists topologically on an open neighborhood in all sixty rewards. Even for the fixed rational center on the usual no-sure-opening residual branch, no finite degree of a polynomial target-moment hierarchy with semialgebraic finite-degree tests characterizes every attainable target.

## Game and static equations

Three players have labels $0,1,2$, with their predecessor and successor taken modulo three. The fourth player has label $3$. Each independently chooses Quit or Continue by private behavioral randomization at the unique live history. The action profile is publicly observed. The first nonempty quitter set receives its four-vector of rewards. Live play and perpetual continuation pay zero. A unilateral replacement can change the entire private stopping law, including choosing Never.

Each $i<3$ gets zero whenever absent from the first quitting coalition. When present, its reward depends on which of its predecessor and successor are also present, regardless of player 3. The four columns list neither, predecessor only, successor only, and both.

| Player | Neither | Predecessor | Successor | Both |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 1 | −1 | −3 |
| 1 | −1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 2 | 1 | −1 | 1 | −4 |

Player 3 gets zero when present. When absent it gets $-1$ at $\{1\}$, $11/8$ at $\{0,1,2\}$, and two at each other nonempty coalition. The own singleton vector is $(1,-1,1,0)$.

Write $(x,y,z)$ for the hazards of players $0,1,2$. Their respective immediate Quit offers are
\[
 d_0=1-2y-2yz,\qquad d_1=-1+2x+3z-2xz,\qquad d_2=1-2y-3xy.
\]
A domestic row at zero continuation is Nash precisely if each positive hazard has $d_i\ge0$ and each hazard below one has $d_i\le0$. The complete list is
\[
 A=(0,1/2,1/3),\qquad B=(1/2,1/2,0),\qquad C=(1/6,2/5,1/4).
\]

Here is the exact enumeration. No binary profile passes the three signs: at the eight corners $000,001,010,011,100,101,110,111$ respectively, profitable flips are available to players $0,0,1,2,1,1,0,0$. An equilibrium with just one interior player would make one of its four nonzero table entries vanish. With two interior players, fix the remaining hazard at zero or one and solve the two required ties. Fixing $x=0$ gives $(y,z)=(1/2,1/3)$ and $d_0=-1/3$. Fixing $x=1$ gives $(1/5,-1)$, outside the cube. Neither $y=0$ nor $y=1$ yields feasible ties for the other two. Fixing $z=0$ gives $(x,y)=(1/2,1/2)$ and $d_2=-3/4$. At $z=1$ player 1's offer is two and cannot vanish. At a fully interior solution $d_0=d_2=0$ gives $y=1/(2+2z)$ and $3x=2z$. The equation $d_1=0$ becomes $4z^2-13z+3=0$, with roots $1/4$ and $3$. Only $C$ lies in the cube.

Write $c(q)$ for the probability all three domestic players continue. Their three stationary rows have $c(A)=1/3,c(B)=1/4,c(C)=3/8$. Player 3's unconditional first-date contribution from passive exits at those rows is respectively $1/3,3/4,47/96$. Indeed it is $2(1-c)-3\Pr(\{1\})-(5/8)\Pr(\{0,1,2\})$.

## Every vanishing-regret limit follows these rows

For a prescribed calendar let $b_i(t)$ be its full unilateral best-response cap on survival to date $t$, $W_i(t)$ its prescribed continuation, $S_t$ its incoming joint survival, and $e=\max_i(b_i(1)-W_i(1))$. All caps are nonnegative: domestic players can wait forever and player 3 can quit immediately. At every nonnegative continuation vector there is no exact one-stage Nash row with a sure quitter. If a domestic player is sure, every other domestic player faces a sure rival, while the sure player's normalized Wait offer is nonnegative. Consequently the proposed domestic hazards must form a zero-continuation static domestic Nash row with a sure player, absent from the enumeration. If only player 3 is sure, every domestic player again faces a sure rival, so the domestic row must be $A,B$ or $C$. Player 3 can wait and obtain its strictly positive passive first-date contribution plus a nonnegative continuation. Quitting gives zero. This also excludes a sure opening at the vector of punishment levels, since each player can secure zero.

There is a fixed one-unit margin against a sole mover at every hazard. For mover 0 choose challenger 2, for mover 2 choose challenger 0, for mover 1 choose player 3, and for mover 3 choose player 0. In each case the challenger's immediate Quit offer against the sole mover at any hazard exceeds its payoff when the mover alone absorbs by exactly one. This verifies the raw sole-owner exclusion on the whole interval $[0,1]$.

We give the short argument which makes the domestic states collapse even for arbitrary time-dependent approximations. At a date let $F_i(b(t+1),q(t))$ be the expected prescribed one-step payoff with the cap on joint survival and let
\[
 \ell_i(t)=b_i(t)-F_i(b(t+1),q(t))\ge0.
\]
The cap is the maximum of its two first-action offers. Subtraction of the prescribed recursion gives $\sum_t S_t\ell_i(t)\le e$ and $0\le b_i(t)-W_i(t)\le e/S_t$ at positive reach. Compactness and the absence of a sure row at a bounded nonnegative continuation imply fixed $e_0,\gamma>0$ such that every first date of a calendar with $e\le e_0$ has every hazard at most $1-\gamma$. Otherwise the first-row regrets tend to zero along a subsequence with a sure coordinate.

For completeness let $M=4$, which bounds all absolute rewards, and choose $0<\theta<1/(16M)$. When initial regret is sufficiently small, stop after joint survival first falls below $\theta$. Such a date exists. If perpetual survival is positive, conditional future exit probabilities tend to zero, prescribed tail payoffs tend to zero, and immediate Quit asymptotically guarantees one to player 0. The restart bound then gives $S_\infty\le e$. Through the chosen date incoming survival is at least $\theta$, so restarts have regret at most $e/\theta$ and the last row and all earlier rows obey the uniform nonsure bound. Full survival to the block end is therefore at least $b=\theta\gamma^4>0$.

For any domestic or outside owner $i$, survival of its three *rivals* through this block is at most $1-\theta$. Otherwise with probability exceeding $1-\theta$ none of those rivals attempts a quit on the block, whereas with probability exceeding $1-\theta$ prescribed play exits within the block. Thus singleton $i$ absorbs with probability at least $1-2\theta$. The challenger's prescribed initial payoff differs from its reward at singleton $i$ by at most $4M\theta$. Its initial absolute Quit offer differs by at most $2M\theta$ from that against $i$'s sole initial hazard, by coupling away any rival other than $i$. The one-unit margin would make the initial regret at least $1-6M\theta$, a contradiction for sufficiently small $e$.

Given calendars with $e\to0$, concatenate $k\to\infty$ such actual blocks, taking $k$ so slowly that $e/b^k\to0$. All restarts needed exist by the same argument. Through their end the sum of all one-stage $\ell_i(t)$ for each fixed $i$ is at most $e/b^k$. If $i<3$, waiting has zero exit contribution at every stage. Its loss against the first-action optimum is nonnegative and at most $\ell_i(t)/(1-q_i(t))\le\ell_i(t)/\gamma$, because the prescribed mixture puts weight $1-q_i(t)$ on Wait. Iterating the Wait comparison through the blocks yields
\[
 0\le b_i(1)\le e/(\gamma b^k)+M(1-\theta)^k.
\]
Hence all initial domestic caps and prescribed payoffs tend to zero. This is a property of every vanishing-regret sequence and applies equally to sequences of restarted calendars whose regret tends to zero.

Now pass to any subsequential limits of the hazards and continuation values at all fixed dates, extracting diagonally by boundedness. Incoming reach is positive in the limit at date one. Whenever it is positive, the limiting row is exact at the limiting cap vector and therefore has no sure quitter; the next date also has positive limiting reach. Every such restart has vanishing regret, so all of its domestic prescribed and optimal continuations tend to zero. Thus at each finite date the domestic hazards are one of $A,B,C$. At any of these three rows the fourth player's Wait offer has strictly positive passive exit contribution and a nonnegative continuation. That player is strictly quiet. Finite-prefix joint survival for the limiting word is at most $(3/8)^N$ after $N$ dates. The original expected terminal rewards converge along this subsequence to those of this word by bounding the tails using finite-prefix survival.

## The unrestricted payoff set at the rational center

Conversely choose any infinite word over $A,B,C$ and prescribe it with player 3 always quiet. All three domestic continuation payoffs are zero by the stage equations, boundedness and geometric survival. Their two offers therefore satisfy exactly the static signs on each date. The fourth player's continuation is obtained by composing $v\mapsto v/3+1/3$, $v\mapsto v/4+3/4$, and $v\mapsto3v/8+47/96$ respectively; it stays in $[1/2,1]$. Its alternative Quit payoff is zero. Every deleted-player survival is geometrically bounded, uniformly in the word, because each of the three rows has at least two positive hazards and there are only three rows. Backward iteration of the pure-action bounds proves optimality against every unilateral behavioral replacement, including Never. The same geometric tail bound makes finite-average errors vanish uniformly over deviations; each word gives its fixed original uniform payoff.

Conversely every original uniform payoff is the limit of terminal equilibria with errors tending to zero. For each uniform witnessing profile at fixed accuracy, let the averaging horizon tend to infinity. Bounded convergence gives the terminal payoff for prescribed play and for every fixed unilateral replacement, and the uniform incentive bound passes to these limits. Apply the preceding necessity argument. Equivalently the terminal directions are the declarations `quittingGame_isUniformEquilibriumPayoff_of_terminalNash_tendsto` and `exists_terminalNash_terminalPayoff_close_of_isUniformEquilibriumPayoff` in `UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Terminal/TargetTail/TerminalUniformPayoffSelection.lean`.

The set $K$ consists of the values of all infinite words. It is compact by uniform contraction, and nontrivial since the constant words $A$ and $B$ have values $1/2$ and $1$. It has no isolated point. After any prescribed prefix, the all-$A$ and all-$B$ continuations give two distinct values arbitrarily close to the originally coded value as the prefix grows. At depth $N$, $K$ is covered by $3^N$ intervals of total length at most $(1/2)(23/24)^N$. Hence it has Lebesgue measure zero and no nondegenerate interval, so as a compact subset of the line it is totally disconnected. It is a Cantor set. In increasing order the images of $[1/2,1]$ under $A,C,B$ are $[48/96,64/96]$, $[65/96,83/96]$ and $[84/96,96/96]$.

## Persistence under perturbations of all sixty rewards

The Cantor payoff set persists on an open neighborhood in the space of all sixty original absorbing rewards. Its points need not stay on the same line. The complete set there is the image of the space of all infinite words on $A,B,C$ under a continuous one-to-one payoff map.

Write $(w,v)\in\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$ for trial continuations of the domestic players and the spectator. On each of the three supports keep the spectator and inactive domestic hazards zero. Solve the domestic active indifferences for the remaining hazards, now as functions $q_j^r(w)$, where $r$ denotes the reward table and $j$ labels one of the three rows. These functions are independent of $v$. At the center their equations at $w=0$ are exactly the corresponding equations $d_i=0$. Their Jacobian determinants in the active hazards are $6,4,11$ for $A,B,C$, respectively. The implicit function theorem gives unique smooth solutions near the three rows for all nearby $r,w$. All active probabilities remain interior. The inactive domestic player at $A$ or $B$ has a strict Wait inequality at the center. The spectator's Wait inequality is strict on all three rows for every $v\in[1/2,1]$. These guards persist on a product neighborhood of the center states.

Let $G_j^r$ be the prescribed one-stage payoff map $(w,v)\mapsto F_r((w,v),q_j^r(w))$. Its first three coordinates depend only on $w$. Since Continue is optimal at the indicated rows, these coordinates at the base table are $H_i(q_j^r(w))w_i$, where $H_i$ is the probability no rival quits at that date. Thus the derivative of its domestic part at zero is the diagonal matrix with entries $H_i(q_j)$, all strictly between zero and one. Its last coordinate is $P_3^r(q_j^r(w))+c(q_j^r(w))v$, where $P_3^r$ is the spectator's passive exit contribution.

Here are details ensuring that all words still give equilibria. Choose a small padding $\delta>0$ about the interval $[1/2,1]$. At the base table and $w=0$, all three maps send $[1/2-\delta,1+\delta]$ strictly inside itself since their slopes are less than one. Their images lie in three mutually disjoint closed strips of spectator payoffs for sufficiently small $\delta$, by the gaps $1/96$. Take a sufficiently small closed convex cube $B$ about $w=0$, then a small reward neighborhood. All the chart and guard conclusions hold on the product $D=B\times[1/2-\delta,1+\delta]$, and the last-coordinate images remain in those disjoint strips inside the interval. For the domestic maps the derivative norm is uniformly bounded by some $L<1$ there, using the sup norm. At the base their value at zero is zero, so they map $B$ strictly inside itself; after choosing $B$ this strict inclusion persists for sufficiently small reward perturbations. Thus $G_j^r(D)\subset D$ for all three maps. The last coordinates have derivative in $v$ bounded by some $c_0<1$ and bounded derivative in $w$. In the norm $\max(\|w\|_\infty,\varepsilon |v|)$ with sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, all three maps are strict contractions.

Each $G_j^r$ is also injective on $D$. The domestic derivative may be kept uniformly within half the smallest diagonal entry of its base diagonal matrix, in the induced sup norm. Integrating along the segment between two domestic states then bounds their image distance below by a positive multiple of their original distance. At a fixed $w$, the spectator coordinate has strictly positive slope in $v$. These two facts give the claim. It follows that infinite words have well-defined payoff vectors by repeatedly composing the maps on $D$, and their code is continuous. Two words first differing at one letter enter disjoint strips there; injectivity of their common prefix separates their initial payoffs. Every coded word is an exact terminal equilibrium by the same original Bellman comparisons, since active mixing remains uniformly proper and each deleted survival is uniformly below one. It delivers its original uniform payoff.

It remains to exclude behavior missing all three charts. For each table $r$, consider all limits of triples consisting of the prescribed payoff at dates one and two and the intervening hazard row in calendars of exploitability tending to zero. At the base, the preceding arbitrary-calendar argument puts both payoffs on $\{0\}^3\times K$ and the row in $\{A,B,C\}$ with the spectator quiet. All such triples of nearby games must lie in any preassigned neighborhood of this compact set of possible triples once the reward neighborhood is sufficiently small. Otherwise choose games $r_n$ tending to the base with a bad limiting triple and approximate it within $1/n$ by an actual calendar of regret at most $1/n$ in $r_n$. On this same calendar the base game's prescribed payoff at each date differs by at most $\|r_n-r_0\|_\infty$, and its initial exploitability increases by at most twice that amount. A convergent subsequence would yield a bad triple at the base.

Use this fact to put the two payoffs in the interior of $D$, the row near one of $A,B,C$, and its survival uniformly above zero. For any limiting sequence in a fixed nearby table, the next restart again has vanishing regret, and the statement applies at every fixed date by induction. The limiting rows are exact for the next prescribed continuation since the next cap differs from that continuation by at most regret divided by reach. The strict inactive comparisons force the spectator and the indicated inactive domestic player to wait, while the active probabilities stay interior. Local uniqueness puts the row exactly on the designated indifference chart. Hence the initial limiting payoff is the code of that word. This proves equality of the full uniform-payoff set with the coded compact set. A continuous injection from the compact space of ternary infinite words into Euclidean space is a homeomorphism onto its image, which is a Cantor set. The hypotheses of the residual fixed-target moment test persist on the same smaller neighborhood: positivity and the sole-mover margin are strict, and the punishment vector is Lipschitz in the rewards by the infimum-of-best-responses definition. Absence of sure openings at that vector then persists by compactness.

## Consequence for testing individual targets

In the fixed-target polynomial criterion, a positive-rate row uses its exact one-stage Nash conditions at a bounded trial continuation $w$, with $a=\sum_i q_i>0$, and tests $T_P=P(F)+(P(F)-P(w))/a$. At zero rate the conditions are $w_i\ge s_i$ and $z_i(w_i-s_i)=0$ for a probability vector $z$, and $T_P=P(w)+\nabla P(w)\cdot\sum_i z_i(r(\{i\})-w)$. At degree $d$ the vector of monomials evaluated at the target must belong to the convex hull of the vectors of these tests on all rows. Passing every degree is necessary and sufficient on the residual branch under discussion. Each fixed-degree accepted set is semialgebraic in the target by Carathéodory's finite-atom bound and real quantifier elimination. Such a set has only finitely many connected components. It cannot equal the full Cantor payoff set at any table of this open family. Thus even a cutoff allowed to depend on the table can fail for membership of arbitrarily specified target payoffs.

At the rational center one can exhibit the difficulty on rational input without varying the rewards. The open interval $(64/96,65/96)$ is a complementary gap of $K$, with both endpoints attained. Its images under $f_A^n$ are mutually disjoint complementary gaps: the leftmost branch is disjoint from the two others and all three maps are increasing. Their midpoints are
\[
  v_n=\frac12+\frac{11}{64\,3^n},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots .
\]
Every $(0,0,0,v_n)$ is unattainable. Its first rejecting degree is at least $2n+2$. To prove the bound, suppose degree $d$ rejects this target. Strict separation from the compact convex test hull gives a polynomial $P$ of degree at most $d$ with $P(0,0,0,v_n)>0$ and $T_P\le0$ on every tested row, after subtracting the supremum of the separating linear functional on the hull. This centering works since $T_1=1$. Write $p(v)=P(0,0,0,v)$. Every $v\in K$ passes the test, hence $p(v)\le0$ there.

For every $v\in[1/2,1]$ the $A$ row is exact at $(0,0,0,v)$ and its payoff is $(0,0,0,f_A(v))$. These states remain eligible even if the test restricts continuations to the feasible reward hull above the punishment vector: both endpoints are attainable under almost-sure absorption, and that restricted domain is convex. Since the total hazard of row $A$ is $a=5/6$, the explicit positive-rate test gives
\[
 (1+a)p(f_A(v))\le p(v).
\]
As $v_{k+1}=f_A(v_k)$, positivity at $v_n$ propagates to every $v_0,\ldots,v_n$. Each of these $n+1$ gap midpoints lies strictly between two attained endpoints, where $p\le0$. The intermediate value theorem gives one distinct root on either side of each midpoint, counting a zero endpoint if present. These closed gaps are pairwise disjoint, since each is obtained from the first gap by a different power of the increasing leftmost branch. The nonzero polynomial $p$ therefore has at least $2n+2$ distinct roots. Each individual nonmember is rejected at a finite degree by the all-degree characterization. This lower bound concerns membership of a specified target. The existence conjecture for arbitrary four-player tables remains open.
