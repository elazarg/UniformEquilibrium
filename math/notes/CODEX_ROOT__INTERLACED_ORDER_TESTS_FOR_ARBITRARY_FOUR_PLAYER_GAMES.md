# A finite order LP for actual laws and caps with inverse-linear error

Fix any four-player quitting table with positive integer dates, independent private clocks, and zero ordinary payoff on perpetual play. Permit fixed private bonuses for choosing the complete action Never. Let M≥1 bound the absolute ordinary payoff entries, bonus sizes and augmented payoffs. Specify any of the sixteen outcome probabilities, prescribed payoffs, marginal Never probabilities, and first-coalition payoffs after deleting designated clocks. Such scores may include a prescribed owner's private Never bonus. Specify upper allowances b_i on the four unrestricted caps. Optionally specify any players who must be individually surely finite. The closed carrier means limits of target coordinates with limsup caps at most the allowances, along genuine independent profiles satisfying that individual finiteness requirement.

The following finite tests are linear programs over an explicit finite alphabet with rational coefficients at rational input. Their intersection is exactly this closed carrier. A passing order cell at level N gives a private calendar on at most 4N finite dates with error at most 24M/N simultaneously in every stated target coordinate and positive cap excess. For the graph of the caps, add one marker per desired cap to the calendar size. This preserves arbitrarily positive regrets as well as vanishing regrets.

**Orders and cell equations.** Introduce N training labels and N validation labels for each player. Their dates form a weak order with finite ties retained and a distinguished final Never class, possibly empty. The labels within each owner's two lists can be required to be nondecreasing in list index. Retain only those orders in which, for every owner and at every cut of the order, the two cumulative counts in that owner's lists differ by at most one. Include any requested named marker dates, finite or Never, for cap lower witnesses. A designated surely finite owner has no labels at Never. Variables are nonnegative masses on these orders, summing to one. Markers, when used, are included in both the full order and the training order. Their values need no training/validation approximation.

For a fixed order compute f_val for each chosen target as its expectation when each owner's validation label is chosen uniformly and independently. The same convention computes Y_i(t;val), the augmented payoff of the reply t against three independent validation labels. In addition to a training rank, marker or Never the reply may be Late. Its score is that of the first finite rival coalition, or the own singleton without a private bonus when all rivals are Never. This is the bounded-convergence limit of genuine deterministic deadlines in the original game, including arbitrary signed own levels.

For each specific marked training order E and every specified target value y_f impose

    Expectation[1_E f_val] = y_f Pr(E).

For each such E, each i and every training label of any owner, marker, Never and Late as t, impose

    Expectation[1_E Y_i(t;val)] ≤ b_i Pr(E).

All scores in these constraints are explicit finite sums on a labeled order. A training label or marker at Never represents the reply Never. To require actual cap convergence to b_i, include a marker for a nearly highest pure reply of i and target its augmented validation payoff at b_i. To require different cap graphs on the same clocks, use the corresponding bonuses and markers. Prescribed augmented payoffs and caps need not coincide.

**Necessity.** Given actual marginal distribution function F_i on the ordered dates with Never last, take independently for each player and side a uniform shift U in (0,1). On that side use the N dates

    F_i^(-1)((k+U)/N),     0≤k≤N−1.

Here the inverse is the first date with cumulative probability at least the given quantile, and Never is used when it is not reached at any finite date. At any date t the count through t is either floor(N F_i(t)) or ceil(N F_i(t)), and at integer N F_i(t) the two agree. The left-cut version uses the cumulative mass strictly before t; the same dichotomy holds, including before Never, outside a null set. Thus the allowed per-owner discrepancy is at most one everywhere. The identity remains valid at random pooled order cutpoints because it holds at all dates at once. The conditional average of the empirical validation distribution over the random shift equals the original marginal: a uniform choice of k produces an exactly uniform quantile (k+U)/N after averaging over U. The four validation shifts and the four label indices are independent across players and independent of the entire training side. Hence conditional on a marked training cell the expected validation targets are exactly the true targets. A training label viewed at its actual date gives a legal reply independent of the validation sample, and its true offer is bounded by the cap. Markers are deterministic for this profile. This yields each cap inequality, also for Late by its limit interpretation. Near a cap supremum choose a deterministic date or Never within an arbitrarily small error. Taking convergent finite order probabilities along a sequence of profiles gives all LP constraints exactly at any claimed closure point.

**Recovery.** Consider any positive-mass training cell E in a feasible LP. Build the four independent empirical laws on its training labels. Put all distinct finite training and marker dates on consecutive natural ranks starting at one and keep Never distinct. The calendar's targets f_tr and pure offers Y_i(t;tr) at its retained labels, markers, Never and Late depend only on E. Every genuine finite replacement date is one of those finite ranks or lies after them all, when its offer is exactly Late. There are at most 4N plus the number of markers finite ranks.

For each order extending E and every owner the sup difference of its two empirical distribution functions, including their left limits, is at most 1/N by construction. Changing one marginal from training to validation in a first-exit score changes expectation by at most 4M/N. Indeed after fixing the other effective clocks, the score as a function of this clock is constant strictly before, tied with, and strictly after the earliest of them. The two jumps each have size at most 2M and involve cumulative probabilities at that rank and directly before it. When the other clocks are all Never, the only split needed is finite versus Never. A marginal Never probability uses the single cut before Never. For a prescribed private bonus add at most M/N for its owner's Never indicator. The same proof applies when clocks are deleted or replaced by a fixed marker, training rank, Never or Late. Under Late the scored outcome is the rivals' earliest finite coalition or the given owner's singleton if none is finite. Telescoping the four independent marginal replacements bounds every target and every offered action difference by 24M/N, uniformly over all ranks in E. The argument compares the two sides in the full order; compressing the training ranks leaves all the indicated training scores unchanged.

Conditional expectation given E now gives |f_tr−y_f|≤24M/N for every target. For the training best reply choose a maximizing retained rank, marker, Never or Late, and use its conditional cap inequality to obtain B_i(tr)≤b_i+24M/N. The marker targets give the matching lower bounds whenever required. Individual finiteness holds on the constructed calendar. These simultaneous conclusions hold for every positive training cell without a union bound or a correlation device in the realized game. As N tends to infinity they prove the asserted closed-carrier equivalence. The LP tests do not need a joint choice of solutions across their levels; intersection over all levels suffices.

**Global minimum.** To minimize the sum of unrestricted regrets, omit the target equations and substitute unnormalized variables h_i(E) for b_i Pr(E) on the right of the reply inequalities. Minimize the linear objective

    sum over i,E of h_i(E) minus the sum of the validation prescribed payoffs in expectation.

The original global infimum A (with or without individually designated finite players) is at least this LP optimum L_N by necessity at actual profiles, taking h_i(E)=B_i Pr(E). Conversely at any feasible point, empirical best offers in a positive cell are at most h_i(E)/Pr(E)+24M/N, and its own prescribed payoffs differ from their mean validation scores conditional on E by at most 24M/N. Averaging gives an actual cell of total true regret at most the LP objective plus 192M/N. At a zero-mass cell the inequalities force h_i(E)≥0, which can only increase that objective. In particular

    L_N ≤ A ≤ L_N + 192M/N.

The LP may also be constrained to give total lower bound zero by taking max(0,L_N). Every actual regret is nonnegative. A positive dual value at any N is a counterexample certificate for the unconstrained original terminal criterion; existence of some such finite value whenever A>0 follows from the inverse-linear bound. An individually finite restriction has the endpoint appropriate to that restriction and need not by itself witness failure of original UE.

Earlier charged polynomial tests already characterized the whole first-exit-law and upper-cap carrier. The verified quantile hierarchy already gives complete initial-regret finite-clock search and inverse-linear approximation. This alternative uses two independently shifted grids to represent the whole prescribed law exactly conditional on each training order in expectation. It gives a finite rational outer LP, covers exact cap graphs and joint deleted/ordinary scores, and reconstructs an actual private calendar from any positive training cell with uniform simultaneous coordinatewise error. Its weak-order alphabet is very large. Neither the existence of a positive dual value nor universal vanishing of the global infima is proved here. See math/notes/CODEX_ROOT__TRUE_CAPS_AND_FIRST_EXIT_LAWS.md and Research/Quitting/EscapeAwareQuantileClockHierarchy.lean.

## A finite combinatorial alternative for arbitrary four-player tables

Keep the two interlaced lists per player at level N as above. Omit the cap markers, fixed targets and private bonuses for this statement. Let Ω be the finite set of allowed pooled orders. An observation E is the weak order of the training labels including their Never marks. A test a=(i,E,t) names an owner, an observation and an action available at that observation. For each ω define a vector d_a(ω) in four blocks of fifteen coordinates, one per recipient and nonempty coalition. It is zero outside block i and zero unless ω has observation E. In block i it is the distribution of the first coalition after i uses t against the validation rivals minus the prescribed validation first-coalition distribution, restricted to nonempty coalitions. Both distributions are computed by independent uniform validation draws. For Late use the first finite rival exit if one exists, and otherwise the singleton i. All coefficients are rational and depend only on the finite orders.

For a signed reward vector R with sixty entries, the following statements are equivalent.

1. R admits a uniform-equilibrium payoff against all private unilateral behavioral deviations.
2. For every integer N≥1 there is a probability distribution π on Ω with
```
  Σ_ω π(ω) R·d_a(ω) ≤ 0   for every test a.
```
3. For every N and all nonnegative test weights λ_a there is a pooled order ω with
```
  R·Σ_a λ_a d_a(ω) ≤ 0.
```

For (1)⇒(2), the original terminal-regret infimum is zero. Take actual independent profiles with total regret tending to zero and sample the two quantile grids. Their validation law in expectation conditional on the training observation is exactly the original prescription. Every observed-rank deviation earns at most its unrestricted cap in expectation conditional on the observation. The violation of an individual inequality is bounded by the original total regret. Take a limit on the fixed finite alphabet. Conversely a feasible distribution's positive training cells have true empirical regret at most 48M/N for each owner: conditional validation rewards for the best observed rank and prescription differ from their two training counterparts by at most 24M/N each. In the compressed calendar the best observed rank, Late or Never attains the true cap. Taking N arbitrarily large gives original terminal regret tending to zero. The bounded payoff vectors have a convergent subsequence, and the fixed-target conclusion is the checked terminal-debt criterion. Conditions (2) and (3) are the finite minimax or separating-hyperplane alternative for the simplex of pooled-order probabilities and the negative orthant. A strict dual improvement at any finite N gives an original counterexample on that same input. The weights can be rational whenever the strict separation occurs since the list of pooled orders is finite, even for real R.

In particular the universal Fin 4 conjecture itself is equivalent to the following statement containing no reward entries:
```
  For every N and every λ≥0,
      0 ∈ conv{ Σ_a λ_a d_a(ω) : ω∈Ω }.
```
Indeed all R are allowed, so we can interchange the two universal quantifiers over R and λ in (3). If the indicated finite convex hull omits zero, a strict separating vector R supplies a counterexample. Conversely any counterexample separates one such finite hull. An obstruction can have rational weights and rational rewards, by strictness and the finiteness at that level. The convex hull lives in at most sixty dimensions, so whenever zero belongs, at most 61 pooled orders suffice for that fixed vector of test weights. The supporting mixture may depend on λ. It need not be a public randomization available in the game; the quantile reconstruction in the proof of (2) produces independent private clocks. This alternative tests a jointly specified collection of contingent changes of date without choosing the game in advance.

Level N=1 always passes on the original unrestricted problem. If all own singleton rewards are nonpositive, put all clocks at Never. Otherwise choose an owner p with nonnegative own level, make validation p finite and training p strictly later, and put all other labels at Never. The unit discrepancy allows this order. Every available reply of another player leaves the prescribed singleton p undisturbed; p can achieve at most its own singleton, which it already receives. This proves feasibility for every signed table, hence the reward-independent convex-hull condition at this level. The same construction applies to a prescribed surely finite pivot in native units, where all own singleton rewards vanish. Higher levels allow a training date before a validation rival and the argument stops. The conjecture remains open.

The earlier quantile hierarchy already gives two-sided approximations to terminal regret and a complete counterexample semidecision. The distinction here is that true zero regret passes every finite unrelaxed obedience test in expectation conditional on the observed ranks. Separation is by a nonnegative collection of rank-based changes of stopping date, and for the universal problem these yield an explicit finite convex hull in the original sixty reward coordinates. No universal finite cutoff in N is claimed. [Original terminal-debt criterion](../../UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Terminal/TerminalDebtSumInf.lean) · [Earlier quantile hierarchy](../../Research/Quitting/EscapeAwareQuantileClockHierarchy.lean)

For four players the first three levels now pass unconditionally on the unrestricted signed and prescribed-owner native domains. With a weak blocker anywhere, all three also pass on the all-four-finite native domain. The rank-three argument appends a physical continuation to the hidden tail using the full singleton sign classification. ## Every signed game passes the first two levels

At N=2 fix the same training observation in every game: each player has one label at date 1 and one at Never. For each player its two validation labels may both lie at date 1, both at any later finite date, or both at Never. All cuts satisfy the unit count condition, including the split immediately before Never. The finite training menu contains only date 1.

If all own singleton rewards are nonpositive, choose validation Never for everyone. A unilateral date 1 or Late then pays at most the prescribed zero. Otherwise choose a player p with nonnegative own singleton reward s_p. Solve the finite one-date game that pays r(S) on each nonempty cohort S and pays r({p}) when everyone waits. Take an ordinary mixed Nash equilibrium of this game. Draw those four stage actions independently. For an owner assigned Quit, put both validation labels at 1. For any owner assigned Wait, put both at Never, except for p whose Wait labels go to the unobserved date 2. The distribution of these validation extensions is a feasible order prior at the *fixed* training observation. It prescribes the one-date equilibrium payoff, including a sole quit by p if all wait.

Against these draws, date 1 gives each player its one-date Quit offer. For each nonpivot, both Never and Late give its one-date Wait offer, since rival p is surely finite. For the pivot, Late gives its one-date Wait offer. Literal Never instead drops the payoff by s_p on the event that all its rivals wait. Every test therefore earns at most the prescribed value. This proves level-two feasibility for all signed tables and, by the universal alternative, the convex-hull inclusion for every set of weights at N=2. The proof works for any finite number of players. An unrestricted deviation to date 2 in the original game may profit by joining p; the finite-level claim tests date 1, Never and Late. This elementary argument covers rank two in any finite number of players.

For a further raw check, fix N≥3 and a Nash row q of that same one-date game. Use N−1 training labels of p at date 1 and one at Never. For each i≠p put floor(N q_i) at 1 and the rest at Never; only date 1 is observed. If q_p≥1−2/N, independently round each owner's validation count at 1 to floor(N q_i) or ceil(N q_i) with the correct mean. Put the rest at Never for the nonpivots and at hidden date 2 for p. The cumulative pivot count is at least N−2 at the observed date and N at hidden date 2. The interlacing constraints hold. By independent rounding the validation payoff and every tested offer have exactly the stage expectations just computed.

There is a second sufficient check using the same Nash row with payoff u. If u_p≤s_p and q_p≥(N−3)/(N−1), move the observed date to 2. Give p one validation label at an earlier unobserved date 1, an independently rounded number with mean (N−1)q_p at date 2, and the rest at unobserved date 3. The other owners still perform the preceding rounding on the observed date, with all other labels at Never. Through date 1 the pivot count discrepancy is one. Through date 2 its validation count is at least N−2. At date 3 the final discrepancy is one. Each nonpivot has the same payoff on an early pivot exit under all its tested replies, and otherwise faces the one-date experiment above. The pivot's prescribed payoff is s_p/N+(1−1/N)u_p≥u_p, while its best tested reply earns at most u_p. Hence this order prior passes as well. At N=3 any failure requires, at every pivot with s_p≥0 and every Nash row at child r({p}), both q_p<1/3 and u_p>s_p. Since all-nonpositive own levels pass at every N by AllNever, the condition has content on any failing table. Whether arbitrary games pass at every higher level is open.

# A small flow LP for each proposed training order

Fix a training order E at level N of the interlaced order test, including optional fixed cap markers and the distinguished class Never. Within-owner training and validation labels are in nondecreasing order. All constraints and targets in the conditional test on E depend only on a distribution over its compatible validation extensions. At least one E of positive mass exists; concentrating the full probability on any such E preserves feasibility of every homogeneous cell equation and inequality. For an objective with free cell cap allowances h_i(E), normalization on a positive cell divides its contribution by its mass. Hence the global optimum is the minimum of the normalized fixed-cell objectives. There is a polynomial-size flow LP for each fixed E, including all complete deadline inequalities.

List the m distinct finite training or marker dates in order (m≤4N plus the number of markers). Give each training date its vector of four nonnegative training multiplicities and record any finite or Never marker locations. Include gaps before the first and after the last listed date. Given the current four cumulative finite training counts T, each gap has at most 3⁴ vertices d, where d_i∈{−1,0,1} and 0≤T_i+d_i≤N. A path starts with T=d=0. Within a gap put an edge from d to every componentwise greater d′, with at least one strict increase, in that gap. This edge inserts one finite validation-only date with vector of multiplicities d′−d. At the next training date with vector a, use an edge from a gap state d to a state d′ in the following gap precisely when a+d′−d is componentwise nonnegative. That edge is this physical training date and a+d′−d is the validation multiplicity vector tied there. It may vanish. Across this edge T advances to T+a. After the last gap, the terminal edge puts all remaining validation labels, N−T−d, in Never. For a designated surely finite player this number and its training analog must both be zero. The graph is acyclic because every gap edge increases the sum of d and all training edges advance the gap index.

Every path gives a compatible labeled weak order: the sorted validation lists have exactly the specified counts at each successive date, finite ties are retained, and d gives the discrepancy at every cut. Reading off counts in a compatible order gives such a path. With sorted labels the correspondence is exact. A nonnegative unit source-to-sink flow is a convex combination of paths by ordinary acyclic flow decomposition.

To assign rewards to edges, at a finite date write V_i=T_i+d_i before the date and V′_i for the count after it. For each nonempty coalition C the unconditional probability that C first quits at this date under the validation product law is

    ∏_{i∈C}[(V′_i−V_i)/N] · ∏_{i∉C}[(N−V′_i)/N].

At the terminal edge the perpetual probability is ∏_i[(N−V_i)/N]. The native or signed prescribed score and first-exit probabilities are sums of these contributions; an owner's Never marginal or bonus is a terminal-edge contribution. A literal deletion uses the same product with the deleted players removed. A fixed probe at a finite training/marker date receives the rival-only passive contribution on earlier edges. On the edge at its date add the products over all rival subsets (including the empty one) for which those rivals stop at the date and all others survive it, with the tested player joining. Later edges contribute zero. A Never probe accumulates passive contributions at all finite dates and its private bonus at the terminal edge; Late instead adds its own singleton reward times the probability that all rivals choose Never. The multiplicative factors for every indicated coordinate are constants attached to the edge, including for probes that are themselves Never labels. These formulas are evaluated on full validation counts even when some training dates have not yet occurred; the ordering relative to any fixed test date is known from the gap index.

Consequently the conditional target equalities and best-response inequalities are linear in the edge flows. For m fixed finite ranks there are at most (m+1)·81 gap vertices, O((m+1)·81²) edges and O(m) incentive rows per fixed finite list of bonuses. With rational rewards their coefficients have polynomial bit size in the input bits and log N. A proposed training skeleton uses at most 4N+marker ranks and polynomially many bits; an ordinary feasible rational flow LP has a rational witness of polynomial bit length in its explicit system. In fixed player dimension, for level N supplied in unary, feasibility of the full interlaced test at rational targets therefore has nondeterministic polynomial-size certificates. No convex mixing between different E is needed. This supplies a polynomial-size exact oracle for one candidate training order, not a deterministic polynomial search over all training orders.

For the global zero endpoint one may simply replace the conditional target/cap rows by E[Y_i(t;val)−U_i(val)|E]≤0 for every owner and test deadline. If total unrestricted regret has infimum zero, a limiting actual tuple has equal cap and prescription for each owner, hence the exact interlaced tests admit one skeleton at every N. Conversely every passing skeleton constructs an actual calendar of total regret ≤192M/N, so feasibility for arbitrarily large N suffices. For any fixed skeleton the zero condition is equivalently that the convex hull of validation-extension gain vectors meets the nonpositive orthant. If it fails, a nonnegative unit-mass mix of owner/response comparisons has strictly positive total gain on every compatible validation path. Its minimum path cost is found by shortest path on the same acyclic graph. Finite-level failure requires such a separator for each possible training skeleton.

# The native absorbing order hull in 56 reward coordinates

For each centered table Z with zero own singletons, zero live payoffs and zero on perpetual play, write A_Z for the infimum of total unrestricted terminal regret over independent profiles that absorb almost surely. Arbitrary signed Fin 4 is equivalent to A_Z=0 for every such Z. A counterexample supplies such a kernel with positive A_Z by the checked same-table single-pivot normalization and the equality of regrets on absorbing profiles after that pivot raise. Conversely if A_Z>0, raising any one own level by s>0 on every nonempty exit of that row, and keeping the others at zero, gives a counterexample. The total original debt equals native debt plus s times joint Never because the native Late and Never offers agree. If both terms tended to zero, sending the smallest marginal Never mass, at most the fourth root of the joint Never probability, to a finite date would give absorbing native profiles of vanishing regret. The checked terminal criterion then gives the asserted equivalence.

Use the interlaced rank arrays of the previous theorem, with N sorted training and N sorted validation labels per owner and at most unit discrepancy in their counts at each cut. Define Ω_A to consist only of those pooled orders in which at least one owner has **no Never label in either of its two lists**. This can be a different owner on different pooled orders. Use training observation E including Never and test a=(i,E,t), where t ranges over its finite training ranks and Never. The offer from any finite date after all ranks has the same monetary value as Never for every native table, since the only affected event has all rivals Never and i's own singleton pays zero.

In player i's block omit the coordinate for i's own singleton; retain all 14 other nonempty coalition coordinates. In the resulting 56-dimensional space define d_a(ω) to vanish outside block i or off E, and otherwise use the validation first-coalition distribution under replacement by t minus its prescribed first-coalition distribution, projected to those 14 coordinates. As before all coefficients are rational and reward-independent. Then arbitrary Fin 4 is equivalent to the single assertion

    For every N≥1 and every collection λ_a≥0,
    0 belongs to conv{ sum_a λ_a d_a(ω) : ω∈Ω_A }.

Indeed, at fixed Z, A_Z=0 gives a sequence of absorbing independent profiles with total regret tending to zero. Because the clocks are independent, absorption with probability one means some owner has zero marginal Never; by finiteness one can fix that owner p on a subsequence. Independent random quantile shifts for the two sides give allowed orders with p finite on both lists. Conditional on the training order, validation scores average to the physical scores and every deviation at a selected training rank is priced by the true cap. Taking limits gives an order distribution π with Expectation[Z·d_a]≤0 for every test. This works at each N with that same p.

Conversely suppose such distributions exist at arbitrarily large N, allowing the whole union Ω_A. At a positive training cell every allowed validation extension has at least one owner with finite training list; in particular the cell itself exhibits at least one such owner. The independent uniform empirical training clocks consequently absorb almost surely. Their finite menu includes every training rank and Never, and every later finite offer equals Never in native units. The interlacing comparison bounds the difference between the validation and training gain of each test by 48M/N for a uniform reward bound M. Thus every positive cell yields an actual absorbing profile with sum of complete native regrets at most 192M/N. This proves A_Z=0. Finite minimax and interchanging the two universal quantifiers over Z and λ give the displayed hull criterion; an absent zero is strictly separated by a native table and supplies original signed counterexamples by the positive raise. Strict finite separation allows both the weights and separating table to be rational. For any fixed λ whose hull contains zero, at most 57 pooled orders are needed by Carathéodory.

One can require a prior supported on a separately specified p-finite face to test the infimum A_{Z,p} over that face. A_Z=min_p A_{Z,p}. In fact $A_{Z,p}=A_Z$ for every native table and every owner. The [zero-column translation and normal-core argument](../revisit/ZERO_SINGLETON_COLUMN_EQUIVALENCE.md) establishes this pointwise equality. One may prescribe the same owner in every test. A failed test on that face therefore supplies a positive native absorbing gap. At one finite level an order prior on Ω_A need not be a mixture of separately obedient p-face priors; the closed-carrier equivalence above only uses the absorbing empirical calendar revealed by its training observation. If only the validation lists are required to contain a surely finite owner, the same all-level equivalence follows: in a positive cell one training owner has Never mass at most 1/N by unit discrepancy and can be repaired at O(M/N) cost. Requiring a common owner finite on both sides avoids repair altogether.

The first two levels pass even when one prescribes the finite owner p. At level one give p its sole validation clock finite, place its training label strictly later, and put everyone else at Never. Every deviation keeps the same scored coalition or changes only p's unscored own singleton/perpetual outcome. At level two let p have one training label at date 1 and one at date 3. Everyone else trains once at date 1 and once at Never. Take a mixed one-date Nash row at continuation Z({p}). Draw the four actions independently. An owner scheduled to quit has both validation labels at date 1. A scheduled waiter uses both labels at Never except p, who uses hidden date 2. Each realization satisfies unit discrepancy, including p at cuts after 1, 2 and 3. On this one fixed observation the test to quit at 1 gives its one-date Quit offer. Never and date 3 give the one-date Wait offer, even for p, whose own singleton is zero. This proves all finite obedience inequalities on the p-face for every native table.

For N≥3 this formulation leaves the finite-owner existence condition as the boundary obstruction. The simple cube fixed point that treats Late as Never on unconditioned profiles may place positive Never mass on every owner, in which case its sampling need not lie in Ω_A. Any claimed invariant update proof needs to preserve enough absorption in the pooled orders or prove that the resulting defect can be removed in the limit.

# A two-owner hidden tail and unbounded first failure levels on a hypothetical counterexample kernel

Consider the unconditioned interlaced level-N obedience test for the original signed table. Its integer discrepancy bound is one on each owner's training and validation cumulative counts at every order cut. Tests are training ranks, literal Never and Late, which waits past all finite opposing clocks and receives its own singleton when all rivals Never. We use no designated surely finite owner in this result.

Suppose only p has nonzero own reward s_p=s>0 and every other owner has own reward zero. Choose j≠p and denote r=r_p({j}). For any N≥3 put h=1/(N−1). At a first hidden tail date let p alone stop with probability h. At a second hidden date j alone stops with probability h, independently. All remaining clocks choose Never. Write v for the tail's actual prescription. Player p's prescription and two tested continuation offers are

    v_p = h s + (1−h)h r,       Never_p = h r,       Late_p = h r + (1−h)s.

For j the three values all equal h r_j({p}), since j has zero own level. For either other player its tested offers both equal its prescription, because its own singleton and perpetual payoff are zero. Since s>0, p's Late dominates its Never and

    v_p − Late_p = (2h−1)s−h²r.

Thus the tail passes all its Never and Late comparisons whenever

    r ≤ −(N−1)(N−3)s.

Play an ordinary one-stage mixed Nash row q with continuation v at an observed date preceding these two hidden dates. Its prescribed head value dominates its test to quit at that date. For every owner either test Never or Late earns the passive contribution on an immediate rival exit, and otherwise receives its tested tail offer, at most v_i. The ordinary stage Wait payoff consequently bounds both, proving the three observed comparisons for this experiment. Deviations to the hidden tail dates are not included in this conclusion.

For each i choose K_i=min(N,floor(N q_i)+1), and put K_i training labels at the observed date and the rest at Never. Use independent random-shift order-N quantile grids on the experiment's four unconditional clock laws for validation. Through the observed date the validation count is floor or ceil of N q_i, so differs from K_i by at most one. At any hidden cut, the finite mass for an owner is at most q_i+(1−q_i)h. When k=floor(Nq_i)≤N−2 this implies

    N[q_i+(1−q_i)/(N−1)] ≤ k+2.

Indeed the left side is increasing in q_i; substituting (k+1)/N yields k+1+(N−k−1)/(N−1)≤k+2. For k≥N−1 all validation counts are at most N. Counts do not decrease after the observed date, so unit discrepancy holds at every finite cut and before Never. Independent uniform choices of validation labels, averaged over the four independent shifts, yield exactly the experiment's product law. All orders have the same training observation and the validation mixture therefore passes the exact zero-excess test. This proves a raw family passing each specified level, independently of the equilibrium hazards or the values of entries left unrestricted by these hypotheses. In particular N=3 passes whenever r≤0.

# Balancing rank tests that copy another owner's private clock

At level N of the native absorbing order hull use four blocks of 14 payoff coordinates, omitting each owner's own singleton. A test in owner i's block replaces its validation clock by a training rank or Never. A separate Late may be combined with Never after projection because all native own singletons vanish. In each pooled order a uniformly and independently chosen validation label per owner determines its private outcome distribution. A nonnegative weighted sum of test displacements uses the weights of the observed training order. Here is a reward-independent class whose hull contains zero at every level N.

Consider first a collection which, apart from a common nonnegative multiplier depending on the observation, gives owner i a constant total test weight W_i≥0. At a positive row weight normalize it to one: amounts a_{ijr}≥0 choose the r-th ordered training label of owner j, 1≤r≤N, and the remainder chooses Never. Thus Σ_{j,r}a_{ijr}≤1. A selected label at Never gives the action Never; weights choosing coincident ranks may be added. Put a_{ij}=Σ_r a_{ijr} and

    x_{ij}=Σ_r a_{ijr}(N−r)/N.

Define b_{ij}=x_{ij}/(1−a_{ii}−x_{ij}) for j≠i, and b_{ii}=0. Take the row b=0 when W_i=0 or all of i's tests copy itself. Otherwise the denominator is positive because x_{ij}≤(1−1/N)a_{ij}≤(1−1/N)(1−a_{ii}). If there is a nonzero vector t≥0 with Bt≤t, the 56-coordinate hull contains zero. In particular this holds whenever x_{ij}≤a_{ij}/2 for each i≠j. It therefore allows arbitrary asymmetry and cycles in donor choice, arbitrary self or Never weights, and uniform copying of the N labels of any selected donor. More generally a donor's rank distribution may favor later labels or pass the displayed mean test separately for each recipient and donor.

To prove this, use only orders indexed by a first owner f and, optionally, a different second owner g. Each selected owner has all its training and validation labels on a finite interval, before the second owner's whole interval when there is a second owner. Within each interval use distinct dates in order V₁,T₁,V₂,T₂,…,V_N,T_N. Other owners' labels are all Never. These are allowed interlaced pooled orders with the same surely finite first owner on both lists. Every first exit under both the prescription and a tested reply has at most one participant. When testing i≠f, the prescribed outcome is f. The only way to displace f is to select an f training label ahead of its sampled validation date. The r-th f label succeeds with probability (N−r)/N; its success gives i its own unscored singleton. Thus the only scored displacement in row i is −x_{if} on the singleton f. If i=f and there is no second owner, its whole row vanishes. If i=f and the second owner is g, its only scored displacement is positive on singleton g. It occurs unless its test copies a label of f or copies a g rank early enough to precede g. Its size is 1−a_{ii}−x_{ig}.

Normalize t to sum one. Give total probability t_i to first owner i, including probability b_{ij} t_j to first i with second j. This leaves t_i−Σ_{j≠i}b_{ij}t_j≥0 on the order with just i finite. The expected singleton-j displacement in each tested row i≠j is

    b_{ij}t_j(1−a_{ii}−x_{ij})−t_j x_{ij}=0.

The other scored coordinates already vanish. Restoring the constant W_i multiplies an entire zero row. If a positive factor depending on the observation multiplies all four rows, divide the order probabilities by that factor and renormalize. An allowed order with shared factor zero already supplies the desired zero. Finally if x_{ij}≤a_{ij}/2, one has b_{ij}≤a_{ij}/(1−a_{ii}) in every nontrivial row. The row sums are at most one and t_i=1/4 works. This proves the claim with at most 16 explicit pooled orders, uniformly in N. A common prior here certifies a fixed dual collection; it is not a public signal in an original game.

For arbitrary weights that vary with the entire training observation there is a finite extension of this certificate. Treat the 16 above orders separately, with second owner indicated by k or ∅. At the observation for first f and second k let X_i^{fk} be the total unnormalized weight of tests of i≠f choosing an f label, weighted individually by (N−r)/N. For first i and second j define Y_i^{ij} to be the total weight of i’s tests there minus the weight copying i's labels minus the weighted-early mass of tests selecting j's labels. Always Y_i^{ij}≥0.     π_{ij}Y_i^{ij} = Σ_{k∈(I∖{j})∪{∅}} π_{jk} X_i^{jk}.

These twelve equalities and normalization are a directly checkable linear system in sixteen masses, even for completely adaptive tests. If all Y are positive, the equation for a pair variable is a nonnegative linear combination of incoming pair and singleton variables after division by its Y. Spectral radius less than one for the induced 12×12 incoming-pair matrix is sufficient: choose nonzero singleton masses, sum the nonnegative geometric series for all pair masses, and normalize. Radius one is also sufficient through a nonnegative eigenvector on pair orders alone. This conditional test allows genuinely observation-dependent copying rates and does not impose the rank-blind assumptions of the first theorem.

# A fixed solvable table requires linearly many ranks in the native absorbing hull

Index four players cyclically, set all own singleton and all passive payoffs to zero, and give player i the reward +1 if the set of rivals quitting with i consists only of i+1. Give i the reward −1 whenever any other nonempty rival set quits with it. These rules specify one bounded native table. For every K≥1 let e_K be the infimum of total unrestricted terminal regret on independent absorbing calendars with at most K distinct finite support dates in the union of the four clocks. Then

    1/(4096K) ≤ e_K ≤ 1/K.

For the upper bound choose any player p, uniform on K distinct dates, and put all other players at Never. Every prescribed value is zero. Only p's predecessor can gain. Its fixed date hits p with probability at most 1/K, with equality for a supported date. Arbitrary unilateral mixtures cannot improve on the best pure reply. Thus these absorbing calendars have total regret exactly 1/K and A_Z=0.

For the lower bound write q for any reached date's four conditional hazards, h=max_i q_i, and g(q) for the expected loss of total welfare on the independent head exits, with zero on survival. Singletons and adjacent pairs have total welfare zero, opposite pairs have −2, triples have −3, and the grand coalition has −4. Therefore

    g(q) ≥ 2(q₀q₂+q₁q₃).

Indeed the loss on each realized coalition dominates twice the number of opposite pairs it contains. The forced-Quit offer for i against successor hazard a and other rival hazards b,c is

    Q_i(q)=(1+a)(1−b)(1−c)−1 ≥ a−2(b+c).

For every head with h>0, it follows that either g(q)≥h²/64 or some Q_i(q)>h/8. To check this take q_k=h and assume g<h²/64. Then q_{k+2}<h/128 and q_{k−1}q_{k+1}<h²/128. If q_{k+1}≥h/4, its opposite q_{k−1}<h/32, and the bound for Q_k exceeds h/8. If q_{k+1}<h/4 apply the bound to Q_{k−1} with successor hazard h.

For a calendar of total regret e denote its four full caps by B_i. Never guarantees zero to each player. Writing R_t for the joint survival to date t and summing welfare losses gives

    e=Σ_i B_i+Σ_t R_t g(q_t).

At any date, the pure deadline t earns its forced-Quit offer multiplied by the probability all *rivals* survived up to it. Earlier rival exits pay this player zero. Its rival-survival probability is at least R_t, so R_t(Q_i(q_t))_+≤B_i. On dates with g(q_t)<h_t²/64 the preceding dichotomy therefore gives R_t h_t≤8e. On the remaining dates the welfare identity gives Σ_t R_t h_t²≤64e. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the sum of R_t h_t on these latter dates by 8√(Ke). Since absorption is certain,

    1 = Σ_t R_t[1−∏_i(1−q_{i,t})] ≤ 4Σ_t R_t h_t ≤ 32(Ke+√(Ke)).

At Ke<1/4096 the right side is strictly below one. This proves the lower bound for any calendar of the stated kind, allowing arbitrary mixture, ties, private Never mass and arbitrary gaps between dates.

In the interlaced native absorbing rank test, a positive-mass obedient training cell at level N constructs an absorbing calendar on its own finite ranks with e≤192/N at this normalized table. Consequently it must contain at least N/(192·4096) distinct finite ranks. For every N there is a passing cell with N ranks: choose a single owner p, give it one validation label at each odd date through 2N−1 and one training label at each even date through 2N, and give all other labels Never. Both lists for p are surely finite and differ in cumulative counts by at most one. No training date coincides with p's validation date, so every native training-rank or Never reply has value zero just like every prescription. The number of distinct finite training ranks needed on this fixed table has order N.

This is a bound on absolute support dates for arbitrary absorbing approximants as their requested regret tends to zero, and on the timing witnesses in the 56-dimensional native absorbing order hull. The earlier all-four-first-quitters result gives a logarithmic bound in inverse error on the number of *joint* dates within certain intervals of a different strict family. Earlier cyclic welfare and target-moment examples have support lower bounds growing with changed reward magnitudes. Here the table is fixed with entries in {−1,0,1}, the absorbing target is free, and a solo calendar attains the stated rate. The conclusion concerns finite realized ranks and leaves open symbolic descriptions of arbitrarily long calendars by a bounded number of parameters.

## Detailed balance on 15 pure coalitions

Fix arbitrary nonnegative, observation-dependent weights λ at one level N of the native absorbing order hull. For each nonempty S⊆I take a deterministic pooled order with every label of members of S at date 1 and all remaining labels at Never, in both grids. Let E_S be its training observation. Write F_i(S) for the sum of i's weights selecting the finite rank at this observation and W_i(S) for the sum selecting Never (including Late). A mixture on these fifteen orders has exactly zero in the native 56-coordinate displacement space if and only if some probability vector π on the nonempty sets satisfies
```
π(R) F_i(R) = π(R∪{i}) W_i(R∪{i})    (i∉R, ∅≠R⊆I).
```
Indeed in i's block, when the prescribed first set is R not containing i, a finite test changes the first set from R to R∪{i}. When its prescribed set is R∪{i}, a Never test makes precisely the opposite change. The case R=∅ makes no scored change because i's own singleton is omitted. For fixed i the seven vectors e_{R∪{i}}−e_R have pairwise disjoint supports, and different i use distinct blocks. Their coefficients must therefore vanish separately.

Equivalently, assign the birth rate F_i(R) and reverse death rate W_i(R∪{i}) to each of the 28 edges of the four-dimensional cube after removing its empty vertex. Treat λ as jump rates. A zero mixture on these orders exists exactly when one closed communicating class admits detailed balance. On its positive support, every used edge has a reverse edge, and the products of the directed rates around every cycle agree in the two directions. Conversely, on any closed class with these properties take π positive at one state and propagate across each edge using the indicated rate ratio. The cycle equalities make the result independent of the path; normalize it and set it to zero elsewhere. A singleton closed class is included. Therefore a true separating collection of weights must violate this reversibility condition in every closed class. This is an all-level reward-independent necessary condition on the weights at only fifteen observations. Failure of this test on pure orders leaves the rest of the absorbing hull available.

## What changing the orders accomplishes

For an explicit irreversible example, each owner selects a uniformly sampled training label of its cyclic successor with probability α∈(0,1), otherwise Never, with total row weight one. The pure-coalition chain on the nonempty sets is irreducible: from a singleton successively add its predecessors around the cycle, then delete any unwanted owners while keeping the target nonempty. Deaths always have positive rate. The edge {0,1}→{0} has rate one because 1's successor 2 is absent, whereas at {0} owner 1 cannot appear by copying 2. Thus no mixture of the 15 simultaneous schedules works. The two-interval construction nevertheless gives a zero mixture at all N, since its early-copy coefficient is α(N−1)/(2N)≤α/2. Conversely, if every owner always selects its successor's first label, the all-four-sure simultaneous profile yields zero. For N>2 the disjoint two-interval balance would require t_i≥(N−1)t_(i+1) around the cycle and has no nonzero solution. With probability α<1 of selecting that earliest label and α(N−1)/N>1/2, both restricted tests fail, yet the endpoint theorem still passes.

There is also an explicit limitation on *every* fixed deterministic validation schedule, whatever its strict ordering, simultaneous dates, and interlaced training labels. On the cyclic table of LINEAR_TRAINING_RANKS, each native row pays zero at all passive exits and its own singleton. It pays one upon joining only its successor, and minus one at all its other coalitions with rivals. Fix any N≥3. On every absorbing pooled order with all four validation laws deterministic, these second labels decode their actual first coalition. Thus the same λ and the same fixed signed table give weighted gain exactly one on ALL such orders, uniformly over every choice of interlaced training and every weak order of the validation clocks. Thus allowing more deterministic order types does not repair this obstruction; actual distribution over private finite dates can be essential even on a proved yes table.

# Any common distribution of rank choices can be balanced

Consider the 56-dimensional native absorbing hull at level N. The cross-owner tests in row i have normalized weights a_{ijr} on choosing donor j's r-th training label. Assume i never copies itself and the *total weight at each ordinal rank*, u_r=Σ_{j≠i}a_{ijr}, is the same in all four rows. The remaining weight is assigned to Never. The row normalizations may differ, and all weights can have a common multiplier depending on the observed training order. Donor choice at any rank may depend freely on both the recipient and the rank. There is no restriction on how early or late the common rank distribution is concentrated. For every N and all such tests, zero belongs to the native absorbing rank hull.

Write F(k)=Σ_{r≤k}u_r and take the greatest integer K in [0,N] with K≤NF(K)+1. At a common observed date give each owner K training labels and independently randomize its number of validation labels between the adjacent integers with mean Ny, where y=F(K). Each owner's actual ex ante probability of acting on this date is exactly y. Each tested response also chooses the common date with probability y, independently of the actual rival labels. Consequently the weighted assigned and tested first-exit distributions at this head agree in all rows, including exit before a later test. The tail distribution chosen below is independent of the four count roundings. Conditional on all continuing, the same probability factor (1−y)⁴ multiplies the assigned and tested tail displacements in every normalized row. The common multiplier on a training observation can be removed by inverse reweighting as in the two-owner construction.

If K=N then Ny≥N−1. There are no training labels after the head and the only remaining tested action is Never. Give one owner all its remaining validation labels at a hidden later date; every other owner puts its remaining labels at Never. This respects unit discrepancy, makes that owner surely finite on both lists, and changes only its own omitted singleton compared with the remaining Never response.

Now suppose K<N, write n=N−K, z=1−y and d=K−Ny. Maximality gives NF(ℓ)<ℓ−1 for every integer ℓ>K, hence 0<d≤1 and Nz=n+d. The validation head counts therefore equal K−1 or K, allowing one side of zero-probability rounding. Choose a first and optionally second tail owner. Place all training labels of each chosen owner not used at the head in successive disjoint finite intervals, and place every remaining label of each unchosen owner at Never. All remaining validation labels of the chosen owners are finite in their respective intervals. Within each interval, immediately before the training label at original rank r=K+1,…,N, ensure that precisely r validation labels of this owner have already occurred *including the head*. For the first such training label insert one or two validation events according as its head count was K or K−1, then one before each subsequent training label. Keep all these tail dates distinct. Counts differ by at most one at every cut; at least the chosen first owner has both lists surely finite.

For a chosen donor j and a tail training label r>K, the conditional probability its date is strictly before j's conditional validation date is (N−r)/(Nz). Indeed exactly N−r validation labels remain past that training rank regardless of its head rounding, and the ex ante probability to reach the conditional donor draw is z. The independent roundings also retain independence of the two conditional private draws. In recipient i's tail weights, each a_{ijr} is divided by z. Thus replace the early-copy coefficient in the two-owner balance theorem by

    x_{ij}=Σ_{r>K} a_{ijr}(N−r)/(Nz²).

These coefficients have row sums at most 1/2. To see this, sum the cumulative inequalities at each integer between K and N−1:

    Σ_{r>K}u_r(N−r)=Σ_{ℓ=K}^{N−1}[F(ℓ)−F(K)]
                     ≤ (n−1)(n/2+d−1)/N
                     < (n+d)²/(2N)=Nz²/2.

For n=1 the sum is zero; otherwise the intermediate estimate follows by adding m+d−1 for m=1,…,n−1. In particular Σ_{j≠i} x_{ij}/(1−x_{ij})≤2Σ_j x_{ij}≤1. Give first i and second j probability x_{ij}/[4(1−x_{ij})], filling its first-owner total to 1/4 by sole-i orders. As in the two-owner calculation, passive singleton-j displacement in row i is positive of size 1−x_{ij} with first i and second j, and negative of size x_{ij} whenever j is first. All these coordinates cancel. Other scored tail coordinates vanish, while the head was already exactly balanced. Thus every permitted ordinal bias with common rank frequencies has an absorbing hull witness. These tests may use the earliest interior ranks only, making the separate half-early-weight criterion inapplicable.

For heterogeneous rows the same situated calculation remains a sufficient test: choose a vector of training head counts K_j, set y_i=Σ_{j,r≤K_j}a_{ijr}, and ensure the rounded validation head counts differ from K_i by at most one. With all y_i<1, the tail early-copy coefficients for r>K_j become Σ a_{ijr}(N−r)/[N(1−y_i)(1−y_j)]. The proof permits a passing nonnegative subinvariant vector for the resulting balance matrix. The scalar maximality inequality used for common rank laws does not automatically extend to heterogeneous choices.

# A single flow for all training and validation orders of a typed tester

Fix level N of the 56-coordinate native absorbing rank hull. A static tester has weights a_ijr≥0 on row i choosing owner j's r-th training label, and a_i∞≥0 on Never. Let W_i be their total in row i. Coincident labels coalesce and a training label physically at Never selects Never. The states are the cumulative training and validation counts (T,V)∈{0,…,N}⁸ with |T_i−V_i|≤1 for all i. Start at zero. A finite edge advances weakly in each coordinate and strictly in at least one.     H_iC = m_i g_iC − W_i P_C,  if i∈C,

# Cancelling every finite-rank response with prescribed fallback laws

Fix N and any nonnegative tester on the all-finite interlaced 56-coordinate hull H_N(λ), allowing arbitrary dependence of the row weights and selected training rank on the complete training observation. Let D be any nonempty finite ordered set of physical dates. For each owner i, freely choose a continuous map K_i from the product of the four date simplexes to probability laws on D. Write P_i(t;q_−i) for the fourteen scored first-coalition probabilities under reply t against the three independent rival clocks of q. The same notation for a mixed reply denotes its linear extension. Then H_N(λ) contains a vector with i-th block

    b_i(q) [P_i(Never;q_−i) − P_i(K_i(q);q_−i)],  i=0,1,2,3,

at one and the same independent, individually finite physical profile q. Here b_i(q) is the expected total weight of row i's literal Never actions on the systematic training grid of q, after folding equivalent Late deadlines into Never. More precisely, the order prior realizing that vector consists of two independent systematic quantile grids for each physical clock at this q. Thus the finite-rank contributions cancel even when all four rows are using the entire observation differently.

For a proposed profile q, sample its independent systematic training shifts. Let m_i(t,q) be the expected weight of row i tests choosing the actual finite date t in D, and L_i(q) the expected total weight in row i. These functions and b_i are continuous. Indeed, under one fixed uniform shift for each owner, all sorted quantile labels are eventually unchanged along qⁿ→q almost surely: any boundary equality at a physical date and label pins a shift to one of finitely many points. The finite observation space and bounded weights then give convergence of the expectations. We have Σ_t m_i(t,q)+b_i(q)=L_i(q).

For ε>0 the simplex-valued map with i-th component

    [m_i(·,q)+(b_i(q)+ε)K_i(q)] / [L_i(q)+ε]

is continuous and maps the compact convex product to itself. Take a Brouwer fixed point, then a convergent subsequence as ε↓0. At its limit q, L_i(q)q_i=m_i(·,q)+b_i(q)K_i(q), also when L_i(q)=0. Conditional on the full training draws, the independent validation shifts and the uniformly selected validation labels give precisely the physical product law. Thus the tested output is Σ_t m_i(t,q)P_i(t;q_−i)+b_i(q)P_i(Never;q_−i), while the assigned output is L_i(q)P_i(q_i;q_−i). Substituting the fixed-point identity proves the display. The sampled grids are interlaced on every date and all labels are finite, as required.

In particular if every K_i is the point mass at the last physical date T, its i-th residual is exactly b_i(q)∏_{j≠i}q_j(T) times the vector e_{I∖{i}}−e_I in block i. Mere intersection with the cone spanned by those four differences also follows by putting every label at T. The freedom to specify distinct, profile-dependent fallback laws and retain the full independent profile identities is the stronger assertion.

For any fallback choice a coupled realization of the residual has an elementary sign restriction. When the fallback ties a nonempty first rival set R, literal Never changes first coalition R∪{i} to R. When it strictly precedes that set, literal Never changes the omitted own singleton to R. Otherwise nothing changes. Thus passive coordinates are nonnegative and nonsingleton participant coordinates are nonpositive in every residual. A mixture of such fixed-point vectors can have zero nonsingleton coordinates only if, on its charged fallbacks, ties with the earliest rival disappear and any newly exposed rival exit is a singleton. In a prescribed singleton sign chamber, those exposed singletons must moreover be negative or have zero total mass whenever the associated sign is positive. The theorem imposes no sign hypothesis and does not establish that a choice of fallbacks with these properties exists.

The fixed-point construction alone cannot prove even one of the 21 sign-cone assertions for all weights by varying its fallback laws. To see the limitation at any level $N\ge2$, have owner $p$ test only Never and every other owner copy the $r$th training label of $p$, with $2\le r\le N$. At every one of these fixed points the three rivals have the same distribution, that of $p$'s quantile at $(r-1+U)/N$ for uniform $U$. The probability that $p$'s independent date is at or before all three rivals is at least $(r-1)/N$, by taking the $(r-1)/N$ quantile of $p$'s law. Choose the magnitudes on its singleton row to give their three off-diagonal entries a strictly positive mean $a$. This is possible in every listed class. Pay $p$ zero when it participates, and $a$ on every nonsingleton exit where it is absent. The three rival labels are exchangeable, so the conditional expected payoff on an exposed rival singleton is $a$ irrespective of the exit time. On an exposed nonsingleton it is also $a$. Only row $p$ carries a deletion residual, and its gain is at least $a(r-1)/N$ at every such fixed point and mixture of fixed points.

For those same tests a single all-finite interlaced order meets the target cone. Choose $f$ with $G_{fp}<0$. Put all labels in four separate owner intervals, first $f$, then $p$, then the other two. In each interval alternate validation and training, beginning with validation. The only scored change is in row $f$ on singleton $p$, with probability $r/N$; all higher-coalition changes vanish. Thus restricting the order prior to those obtained by the proposed physical fixed points would discard a valid sign certificate. For a solved game realizing the displayed prices one can set the other three rows' nonsingleton entries to zero. Have the three rivals quit together on the first date and $p$ wait. The three quitters are indifferent and $p$ earns $a>0$ by waiting.

# Worst-case regret on an entire singleton class

Fix one of the 21 strict singleton sign patterns \(C\) in the census, up to simultaneous relabeling. Center the four own singleton payoffs at zero and write \(G_{ij}=Z_i(\{j\})\). For a table \(Z\) let \(a(Z)\) be the infimum over absorbing independent profiles of the maximum of the four unrestricted terminal deviation gains. Define
\[
 \Delta_C=\max\{a(Z):\|Z\|_\infty\le1,\ G\in\overline C\}.
\]
The cube bounds all 56 entries, and the sign closure allows ties. The infimum is 2-Lipschitz in the table, since a change of at most \(h\) in rewards changes any prescription or deviation payoff by at most \(h\). Compactness and density make this maximum equal to the supremum over strict tables. By homogeneity and the native translation theorem, \(\Delta_C=0\) is equivalent to UE for all original own levels, all strict matrices in \(C\), and all higher rewards. Here the maximum and sum of four nonnegative gains vanish together in the limit. In particular a lower bound from a restricted strategy family cannot certify \(\Delta_C>0\). [Native absorbing-profile equivalence](../revisit/ZERO_SINGLETON_COLUMN_EQUIVALENCE.md) · [Census and labels](../revisit/TWENTY_ONE_SINGLETON_SIGN_CLASSES_FOR_FOUR_PLAYER_QUITTING_GAMES.md)

For all 21 classes, \(\Delta_C\le1/8\). For the matched class 9, \(\Delta_9\le1/16\). More generally, on a strict table measure player \(i\)'s regret in units of \(g_i=\max_{j\ne i}(G_{ij})_+>0\). The same bounds hold in those units for arbitrary finite higher rewards and arbitrarily large negative singleton comparisons. The full list of certified bounds is in the coverage map. On the cube each \(g_i\le1\), giving the displayed bounds by continuity. The following is an ordinary mathematical proof with an exact integer subcalculation. **Equalizing four deviation gains.** Divide each row by its \(g_i\) for the rest of the proof. If \(a(Z)>0\), the zero-column translation gives a signed game without UE on this normalized matrix. The checked degree criterion therefore makes the homogeneous map \(y\mapsto\min(y,Gy)\) have only the zero root and local Brouwer degree one there. We claim that some \(x>0\) with \(\sum x_i=1\) and some \(k>0\) satisfy
\[
 (Gx)_i=k(1-x_i)/x_i\quad\text{for every }i .
\]
For integers \(N\ge1\) put \(f_{N,i}(z)=\min(N,(1-z_i)/z_i)\) on the closed simplex, with value \(N\) at \(z_i=0\). For \(s=\sum_i(y_i)_+>0\), form \(h_N(y)=(1-\psi(s))\mathbf1+\psi(s)f_N(y_+/s)\), where \(\psi(s)=\min(1,2s)\). Set \(h_N=\mathbf1\) at \(s=0\). This bounded continuous map gives a homotopy \(\min(y,Gy-\lambda h_N(y))\), \(0\le\lambda\le1\). For fixed \(N\) all its roots lie in a common ball, since normalizing any unbounded sequence would give a nonzero homogeneous root. Degree invariance gives a root \(y_N\) at \(\lambda=1\). It is nonnegative, nonzero, and \(Gy_N\ge h_N(y_N)\). Some coordinate of \(z_N=y_N/\sum y_N\) is at most \(1/4\), so its component of \(h_N\) is at least one. Since \(G_{ij}\le1\), \(s_N=\sum y_N\ge1\) and the cutoff is inactive. [Singleton degree theorem](../../UniformEquilibrium/Diagnostics/Quitting/FinFourSingletonDegreeCriterion.lean)

The \(s_N\) remain bounded as \(N\to\infty\). Otherwise pass to \(s_N\to\infty\) and \(z_N\to z\). Then \(Gz\ge0\), and every \(z_i>0\) forces \((Gz)_i=0\) because the corresponding unscaled right side stays bounded and complementarity is an equality in that row. This gives a nonzero homogeneous root. With \(s_N\) bounded, no coordinate of a subsequential limit of \(z_N\) can vanish, since the corresponding \(f_{N,i}\) would diverge while \(Gy_N\) stays bounded. Take limits in the four equalities with \(k=\lim1/s_N\) to obtain the claim.

For fixed shares \(x\) with at least two positive entries, give each player a constant independent quitting probability \(\epsilon x_i\) per date. As \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) its prescribed payoff tends to \(w_i=(Gx)_i\), immediate Quit to zero, and Never to \(w_i/(1-x_i)\). Stationarity puts every finite deadline offer between immediate Quit and Never by conditioning on earlier rival exits. Multiple-exit probabilities conditional on absorption go to zero, even for arbitrarily large fixed higher rewards. Thus the limiting debt is \(\max(0,w_i/(1-x_i))-w_i\), equal to \(k\) for every player at the equalizer. Every player faces a rival who eventually quits almost surely, even after its own deviation. This also proves the bound on the original signed table in the stated row units when this construction is used.

A second construction bounds \(a(Z)\) by \(c=\min_j\max_i(-G_{ij})_+\). For each candidate \(j\) put shares \(1-\eta,\eta\) on \(j\) and one rival \(\ell\) with \(G_{j\ell}\le0\). Hence \(a(Z)\le\min(k,c)\).

**An integer certificate for every chamber.** To prove a given \(1/n\) bound, suppose \(k,c>1/n\). From each column \(j\) pick \(b(j)\) with \(G_{b(j),j}<-1/n\). The equalizer and the upper bound of one on the positive entries imply, in each row,
\[
 x_i\left(1+n\sum_{j:G_{ij}>0} x_j-\sum_{j:b(j)=i}x_j\right)>1 .
\]
The checker excludes this system on the whole simplex for every allowed choice of \(b\) in each claimed class.  u_i\Big(U+n\sum_{G_{ij}>0}u_j-\sum_{b(j)=i}\ell_j\Big)\le U^2.

Any feasible strict violation would have a positive factor, justifying this upper test. Otherwise the checker bisects the widest interval, retaining the common boundary, and requires all branches to close. The \(1/8\) check across all 21 representatives visits 21,693 boxes. **The two mate pairs in class 9.** Every player has its positive comparison \(1\) on its mate. For an ordered pair from different mate groups put \(c_{ij}=-G_{ij}>0\). At the equalizer let \(B=\sum_{i,j\ {\rm crossed}}c_{ij}x_j\). Summing the four equations gives

 1=B+k\left(\sum_i 1/x_i-4\right).
\]
Set \(t=1/16\). For a crossed pair \(i,j\) give them shares \(q,1-q\). Their debts are \(c_{ij}(1-q),c_{ji}q\).  c_{ij}c_{ji}>t(c_{ij}+c_{ji}),\qquad
 c_{ij}c_{j'i}>t(1+c_{ij}+c_{j'i}),\qquad

\]

 B/t\ \ge\ \sum_e(x_i+x_j+2s_e\sqrt{x_i x_j}).

                -(\max_i L_i-\min_i L_i)^2/(16H).
\]
Four weak conflicts give \(L_i=4\) throughout, hence the lower bound 20. Two strong ones add 5 to 16, with a deduction at most \(100/(16\cdot8)<1\) since \(d\le8\). One strong and two weak ones add \(9/2\), with deduction at most \(49/(16\cdot11)<1/2\) since \(d\le5\). In all three cases the summed equalizer equation gives \(k\le1/16\). If a crossed pair passed its test earlier, its two-player schedule already gave the bound.

**A positive floor for this construction.** Set every mate comparison to 1 and every crossed comparison to \(-5/16\). Among all fixed shares in the vanishing-hazard construction, the least limiting maximum debt is exactly \(1/32\). Equal shares attain it. For the converse a group of total share \(a\) gives an owner of share \(u\) the payoff \(s-u\), \(s=(21a-5)/16\). A pure donor leaves some outsider debt \(5/16\). At other shares debt strictly below \(t=1/32\) implies both \(u<s+t\) and \(F(u)=u^2-(s+t)u+t>0\). Let \(a\ge1/2\) be the larger mate-group mass and \(b=1-a\). The larger owner of the smaller group has negative-payoff debt bound \(-w_i\ge(5-13b)/16\), so \(b>9/26\) and \(a<17/26\). In the larger group its smaller owner has \(L=(9-10a)/32\le u\le a/2\) by its mate's first inequality. But \(F(a/2)=-(2a-1)(13a+2)/64\) and \(F(L)=(2a-1)(130a-97)/512\) are both nonpositive on this interval of \(a\). Convexity contradicts \(F(u)>0\). This floor concerns one family of profiles. The unrestricted \(\Delta_9\) still has known lower bound zero.

**Resolution of the measure.** On the cube the scalar \(\Delta_C\) also admits two-sided finite enclosures. Replace each clock by its \(N\) equally weighted midpoint quantiles. Each cumulative cut changes by at most \(1/(2N)\). Conditional on the other clocks, the payoff as a function of one moved date has at most three consecutive values (before, tied, after), of total variation at most four. If all other clocks are Never there are just two values. The one-clock expectation changes by at most \(2/N\). Telescoping bounds the four-clock prescribed error by \(8/N\) and the three-rival error of every pure reply by \(6/N\), hence the maximum-debt error by \(14/N\). A surely finite clock stays surely finite. Compress occupied finite dates to consecutive ranks, retaining ties and Never. Every new pure reply has an old counterpart, including the late and Never options, so caps can only decrease. Let \(a_N\) minimize over the resulting finite menu of absorbing empirical profiles with at most \(4N\) dates. Then \(a(Z)\le a_N(Z)\le a(Z)+14/N\). On a rectangular grid of spacing \(h\) in the signed cube including its endpoints, let \(M\) be the largest \(a_N\) on the grid. Choosing grid coordinates within \(h/2\) of a maximizer gives
\[
 \max(0,M-14/N)\ \le\ \Delta_C\ \le\ M+h .
\]
These inequalities specify what a strict improvement in either direction would certify. The bounds above use particular profiles and integer simplex inequalities, avoiding enumeration of this much larger finite menu.
