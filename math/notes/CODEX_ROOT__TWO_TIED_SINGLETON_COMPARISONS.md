# Two tied singleton comparisons admit a two-date cutoff

Use the original four-player game, independent per-date hazards, zero Never and live payoffs, own singleton s, punishment floors mu, singleton comparisons Gamma_kl=r_k({l})-s_k and joining premiums J_kl=r_k({k,l})-s_k. Assume max s>0, no sure Nash row at continuation mu, and Delta=min_(l,h in [0,1]) max_(k different from l) (h J_kl-Gamma_kl)>0. Fix i,j such that Gamma_ij=Gamma_ji=0 and at least one of J_ij,J_ji differs from zero.

**Theorem.** Terminal exploitability and the probability that either outsider participates in first exit can tend jointly to zero if and only if one of the following two finite tests succeeds.

1. Some point w in the closed segment between singleton vectors r^i,r^j dominates s coordinatewise.
2. Both J_ij and J_ji are nonpositive, and there exist two proper hazards h_i,h_j strictly between zero and one for which the repeated two-date calendar with sole i at h_i and sole j at h_j passes the one-stage best-response inequalities at both of its actual continuation vectors.

In the first case dilute stationary hazards realize an original uniform payoff w. In the second the two-date calendar is an exact terminal equilibrium and yields its initial target as an original uniform payoff. The second test amounts to two unknowns in rational inequalities with positive denominators, since its two prescribed continuations are geometric sums. If the first test fails, an actual pair-confined approximate sequence always yields a two-date calendar as in the second test, even when that sequence occasionally uses either outsider.

**The compact relation from actual profiles.** We record the required necessity in detail. For each near-Nash calendar let e be exploitability, C outside first-exit probability, S_t live survival, b(t) each player's best response from t and W(t) prescribed payoffs. All are original payoffs. Define the row map F(w,q) as the one-stage expected payoffs with continuation w on all-Continue, let c be its all-Continue probability, and put a=sum q. The following elementary bounds hold:

    b(t)>=mu,       0<=b_k(t)-W_k(t)<=e/S_t,
    l_k(t)=b_k(t)-F_k(b(t+1),q(t))>=0,       sum_t S_t l_k(t)<=e.

The first two follow from the definition of the punishment floor and the option to deviate after t is reached; the last two follow from the first-action best-response recursion and telescoping the corresponding prescribed recursion. Further S_infty max s<=e: if S_infty>0 then the conditional probability of any future exit tends to zero, so W(t) tends to zero and the immediate Quit offer tends to s.

For e+C tending to zero choose positive eta>=e+C tending to zero and cut each calendar just after survival first falls below delta=sqrt(eta). At all included dates q_k<=1-gamma for some gamma>0 uniformly. Otherwise, since l_k<=e/delta there, bounded caps b(t+1)>=mu and compactness give a sure limit row that is Nash at its limit cap. One sure quitter can only prefer Quit more when its continuation is lowered to mu; all other comparisons are independent of the continuation. With several sure quitters every comparison is independent of the continuation. This contradicts the residual assumption. Survival just after the cut is consequently at least gamma^4 delta, and prefix charge H=sum a diverges by -log c<=a/gamma.

Give each nonzero date mass a/H, with source w=b(t+1), and record (w,q,z) where z=q/a. On this compact marked space pass to a weak limit. The limiting sources lie above mu in the triangle conv{r^i,r^j,r({i,j})}: conditional outsider and Never probabilities are at most (C+e/max s)/(gamma^4 delta), and b-W vanishes uniformly there. The limit uses only the two named labels since sum_t q_k(t)<=C/delta for each outsider. Mean normalized first-action loss l_k/a is at most e/(delta H); thus at positive charge limit rows are Nash, and at zero charge limit rows have w>=s and z_k(w_k-s_k)=0. Indeed negative w_k-s_k makes the quit advantage bounded below even as q vanishes (and Wait is played with probability approaching one); positive w_k-s_k makes the normalized regret equal to z_k times that strict Wait advantage.

For each polynomial P the marked measure balances (P(F)-P(w))/a, with continuous zero-charge extension grad P(w) dot (sum z_l r^l-w). To see this, telescope P(b(t))-P(b(t+1)) over the prefix, replacing b(t) by F(b(t+1),q(t)) with total error O(sum l_k)<=O(e/delta), and divide by H. Omitted zero-charge dates have the same endpoint error bound. The normalized extension follows by the first-order product expansion.

For the domestic i the Nash row is proper, so Continue is optimal and F_i-s_i=(1-q_j)(w_i-s_i), since the sole foreign singleton has level s_i. The square normalized increment is -z_j(2-q_j)(w_i-s_i)^2, also on tangents by the active-coordinate equality there. Its integral is zero, hence w_i=s_i when z_j>0. At solo-i positive rows w_i=s_i by its own indifference and on a solo-i tangent by the tangent equality. Thus both domestic coordinates equal their s throughout the measure's support. Since at least one J differs from zero, the source triangle on these domestic levels reduces to the singleton segment: its barycentric mass on the joint reward has to vanish. At a positive joint row domestic mixing would require q_j J_ij=q_i J_ji=0. Thus none exists. If the segment misses {w>=s}, tangents cannot occur anywhere in the support; more precisely the positive rates are bounded below there. Indeed any Nash rows tending to zero have vanishing unnormalized regret and w>=s by comparison with immediate Quit. Properness uniformly bounds the rates away from one as above. Every support edge is now a solo move with source w, owner l, charge h in a compact subinterval of (0,1), and target F=(1-h)w+h r^l.

After reweighting the measure by 1/h and normalizing, its source and target marginals agree: this follows for polynomials by balance, and then for all continuous functions by polynomial approximation. Each target in the compact support is therefore the source of a further edge, and each source has a preceding edge. This gives a two-sided path of permitted edges within the same support. Both owners occur in the support. Were all rows solo l, first moments before reweighting would give E w=r^l; at each row a waiting rival needs w_k-s_k>=(h/(1-h))(J_kl-Gamma_kl). Averaging contradicts Delta at hbar=E[h/(1-h)]/(1+E[h/(1-h)]). Any l appearing on a row forces the other domestic player's joining premium against l to be nonpositive, since that player waits, has unchanged domestic level on the entire singleton segment, and could join for native payoff h J. Both domestic premiums are consequently nonpositive.

**The geometry of the two quiet players.** Write g_l=(Gamma_kl) over the two outsiders, D_l=(J_kl-Gamma_kl) over the same two, and g(x)=x g_i+(1-x)g_j for 0<=x<=1. Then a solo-l edge with source x needs exactly g(x)>=b D_l for the outside inequalities, where b=h/(1-h)>0. The target is x'=(x+b)/(1+b) for l=i and x'=x/(1+b) for l=j. These conditions follow directly by comparing Wait with immediate Quit.

The outsider segment is disjoint from the nonnegative quadrant, so strict separation gives a vector p>0 with p dot g(x)<0 throughout (perturb a nonnegative separator slightly if necessary). Any feasible l has p dot D_l<0. Delta at h=1, with the domestic joining premiums nonpositive, implies that D_l has a strictly positive entry. Its other entry is strictly negative. Let k be its positive component. The source permits an odds b>0 if and only if

    g_k(x)/(-p dot g(x)) >= (D_l)_k/(-p dot D_l).

Indeed this is equivalent to the interval between the lower bound from the negative component and the upper bound from the positive component being nonempty. Its positive right-hand side forces g_k(x)>0 and the other outsider payoff is negative. The function on the left is fractional linear with a strictly positive denominator; it is monotone or constant on the entire segment. The permitted source set for each l is closed and nonempty, and excludes l's own endpoint by Delta: at g_l>=b D_l the two outsider blockers and the domestic blocker would all have h J_kl-Gamma_kl<=0, with h=b/(1+b). Thus the source set for i contains the endpoint j and lies away from i, and conversely for j. The positive components of D_i and D_j must be different, since two upper level sets of the same monotone function are nested, whereas neither source set contains the other's excluded endpoint. Their source sets are disjoint and have strictly opposite outsider signs.

On the two-sided support path, a run of one owner has bounded length: each move changes x toward that owner's endpoint by at least the minimum rate times the distance of its whole source set from that endpoint. Both owners must therefore switch infinitely often in both directions. At a switch from i to j, let k be the positive component of D_i. The target on that final i move has negative outsider coordinate g_k (it is a source for j). The optimal Wait payoff at the move dominates immediate Quit, so g_k(x')>=h J_ki, forcing J_ki<0. The symmetric conclusion holds at the other switch.

**Compressing all runs to two dates.** On an i move use y=x/(1-x), the odds of the eventual i singleton. The two outside inequalities become

    (D_i)_k y' <= J_ki y + Gamma_kj

for each outsider k, with y'>y. In fact y'-y=b(1+y). The positive component of D_i yields the affine upper bound U_i(y) on y' and its slope J_ki/(D_i)_k is strictly negative as just proved. The negative component yields an affine lower bound L_i(y) on y'. For j moves the identical statement holds in reciprocal odds v=1/y. No domestic constraint remains since both domestic J are nonpositive. All path states have finite odds bounded above and away from zero. Indeed every i target satisfies y'<=U_i(0)<infinity when its source y is finite; an i source lies away from x=1. Every j target similarly satisfies v'<=U_j(0) from sources away from x=0. A j move decreases x and an i move increases x; bounded run lengths and the presence of both switches propagate the two bounds to all path states.

Compress each run of consecutive i to one move from its first y to its final y'. The latter meets the starting lower bound because the first move did and subsequent moves increase y. It meets the starting upper bound since the final move meets its own upper bound and U_i is decreasing. It thus corresponds to one proper hazard with exactly those endpoints. Do the same for j in reciprocal odds. The resulting path alternates L_n to R_n to L_(n+1) in y odds. Every L_n is in i's source interval and every R_n in j's, separated from each other. Take L=inf_n L_n and R=sup_n R_n. Then R<=U_i(L), whereas R>=L_i(L) follows by taking a subsequence L_n tending to L and using continuity of the lower bound. In reciprocal odds, the infimum of the starting j odds is 1/R and the supremum of their target odds is 1/L, so the same argument proves the j move R to L feasible. We have 0<L<R<infinity and these edges have proper, strictly positive hazards. Both domestic rows satisfy their prescribed support comparisons, and the two outside inequalities hold by construction. Reversing the payoff-map edge order gives the two-date calendar. Its actual values equal the two states because the survival product contracts, and unrestricted deviations obey the same Bellman inequalities since every player faces recurrent rival absorption. This proves necessity of the tests and their second sufficiency statement.

For the first test write w=x r^i+(1-x)r^j. Both coefficients are positive, since Delta at h=0 excludes either singleton point dominating s. Use stationary hazards q_i=epsilon x,q_j=epsilon(1-x). All players face a recurrent rival for every fixed epsilon>0. The prescribed payoff tends to w. Against stationary opponents the best response is the better of immediate Quit and perpetual Wait, by the one-state Bellman recursion. Immediate Quit tends to s_k for each player. The domestic Wait values are s_i,s_j and the outsiders' Wait values equal their prescribed payoffs. Therefore exploitability tends to zero and the terminal targets tend to w. The original fixed uniform payoff follows from the terminal-approximation characterization, or by choosing each finite averaging threshold after the fixed profile's and every unilateral replacement's geometric rival survival have decayed. This proves the first sufficiency statement.

## Both reciprocal singleton comparisons can vanish with strict joining penalties

Take original own levels s=(1,0,1,1). For each player k, the following are its native rewards after subtracting s_k at every nonempty exit. P_k(T) is the payoff when a nonempty rival set T quits and player k waits. Q_k(T) is the payoff when it joins T. Its native own singleton is zero. Write D_k=Q_k-P_k.

* P_0=P_1=0 at every nonempty rival coalition. D_0(T)=-1 if 2 belongs to T, -1/64 if T={1}, and +1 otherwise. D_1(T)=+1 if 3 belongs to T and -1 otherwise.
* P_2({0})=-1, P_2({1})=1/2, P_2({3})=-1/2, with all other P_2(T)=0. D_2=1/20 when 1 is absent and -1 otherwise.
* P_3({0})=1/2, P_3({1})=-1, P_3({2})=-1/2, with all other P_3(T)=0. D_3=1/20 when 0 is absent and -1 otherwise.

Thus both domestic singleton comparisons vanish, and J_01=-1/64, J_10=-1 are strictly negative. For sole movers 0,1,2,3 the outsider blockers 2,3,3,2 respectively give h J_kl-Gamma_kl at least 1/20 for every h in [0,1]. Player 1's original punishment value is zero: Never guarantees it zero since its passive rewards always vanish, and a sure 0 at the first date bounds the attainable payoff above by zero.

**No sure Nash row at punishment.** When some rival is sure, an agent's gain from Quit is its expected D on the rival coalition, irrespective of continuation.

If 0 is sure, then 3 is silent; 1 is silent; 2 is sure; then 0 strictly prefers Wait. If 3 is sure then 1 is sure, 2 is silent, and 0 is sure (the sure 3 excludes the exceptional rival singleton {1}), which is impossible by the preceding chain. If 2 is sure then 0 is silent and 3 is sure, again impossible. Finally suppose 1 is sure. Then 2 is silent. If 0 is silent then 3 is sure; thus 0 mixes. If 3 is silent then 0's gain is -1/64, so 0 is silent, a contradiction. Thus 3 mixes also. The two indifferences imply q_0=1/21 and q_3=1/65. The sure player 1 itself gains q_3-(1-q_3)q_0=-43/1365 from quitting at punishment zero. This excludes the remaining possibility.

**Exact alternating solo calendar.** Alternate a sole 0 at hazard 3/4 and a sole 1 at hazard 3/4. Both domestic original continuation values equal their own s. At their own turns they are indifferent; at the rival's turn joining is strictly worse because J_01,J_10<0. An outsider with native passive rewards (a,b) at singleton 0,1 obtains (4a+b)/5 on 0's turn and (a+4b)/5 on 1's. The native values on its losing and winning mover's turns are -7/10 and 1/5, respectively. Its forced Quit on those turns pays (3/4)(-1+1/20)=-57/80 and (3/4)(1/2-1)=-3/8. Both quiet inequalities are strict. Every unilateral opponent set includes a recurrent rival clock, so Bellman iteration verifies all replacements. This is an exact original terminal equilibrium and is uniformly feasible at its initial target.

**Stationary impossibility on the same pair.** AllContinue fails because s_0>0; a sole positive mover fails the displayed blocker test. To see the latter directly, perpetual waiting earns r_k({l}) against a sole mover l with hazard h>0, whereas an immediate Quit earns s_k+h J_kl. The margin is their difference. If both move with positive probability, player 1 could wait always for a singleton-0 reward of zero. The prescribed strategy absorbs almost surely and puts positive mass on the joint coalition giving it a negative reward. It therefore loses strictly. This direct argument handles sure hazards as well.

**Even stationary approximation fails on this pair.** Let e be terminal exploitability and C the probability that 2 or 3 belongs to the first stopping coalition. Then the infimum of e+C over all original stationary profiles is strictly positive. Otherwise take a stationary sequence q with e+C tending to zero, and a subsequence converging in the closed cube. AllContinue is excluded by the fixed positive original singleton payoff. Each original profile with positive charge absorbs almost surely, and its exit law is one-row product law conditional on exit. In particular both outsider hazards tend to zero since even their first-date quitting probabilities are at most C.

If the limiting row has both domestic hazards positive, the native payoff of 1 converges to the strictly negative value -Pr({0,1} at first exit) from its negative joining payoff. Never secures zero for 1 on every profile, contradicting e tending to zero. If the limit has exactly one positive mover l with probability h>0, the limiting law is concentrated on {l}. The designated outsider blocker's difference between an immediate quit and its prescribed payoff converges to h J_kl-Gamma_kl >= 1/20. It remains that all four limiting hazards are zero. The conditional probability of any collision tends to zero since the sum of pairwise hazard products divided by the exit probability is at most six times the largest hazard. Together with C tending to zero, only the two singleton exits 0 and 1 can carry limiting mass. At each, the sum of the two outsiders' native payoffs is -1/2. Both outsiders could immediately quit for a native payoff tending to zero. This gives liminf e >= 1/4, a contradiction.

The result concerns stationary profiles whose physical exit laws approach this specified pair. It allows stationary equilibria using other player identities.
