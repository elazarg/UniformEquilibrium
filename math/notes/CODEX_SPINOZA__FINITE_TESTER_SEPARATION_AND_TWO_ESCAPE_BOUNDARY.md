## 1. Self-contained question

Let (I) be finite and nonempty, let (r) be a bounded quitting reward
table, and let

\[
 U_i(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i})
\]

denote player (i)'s expected terminal payoff after replacing its complete
behavioral strategy by \(\tau_i\).  Never and arbitrary calendar-dependent
hazards are allowed.  Suppose that for some \(\Gamma>0\),

\[
 \forall\sigma\ \exists i,\tau_i,\qquad
 U_i(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i})-U_i(\sigma)\ge \Gamma .       \tag{1.1}
\]

Can compactness replace the profile-dependent witnesses in (1.1) by one
finite player-indexed menu?  If not, what finite object can a positive Fin4
gap actually produce?

## 2. A finite actual tester cover is impossible

For each player (i), let \(D_i\) be any finite nonempty set of complete
behavioral strategies.  The checked finite-watchdog theorem gives an actual
behavioral profile \(\sigma_D\) such that

\[
 U_i(\tau_i,(\sigma_D)_{-i})\le U_i(\sigma_D)
 \qquad(i\in I,\ \tau_i\in D_i).                    \tag{2.1}
\]

The proof forms the finite normal-form game with pure strategy sets \(D_i\),
takes a mixed Nash equilibrium, and realizes every marginal finite mixture of
complete stopping laws by one ordinary behavioral hazard.  Thus (2.1) is an
inequality between literal quitting-game profiles, not a relaxed or
correlated statement.

### Proposition 2.1 (positive-gap witnesses escape every finite menu)

Assume (1.1).  For every player-indexed finite nonempty menu (D), the
profile \(\sigma_D\) from (2.1) has a gap witness \((i,\tau_i)\) with

\[
 \tau_i\notin D_i.                                  \tag{2.2}
\]

In particular, no finite set of actual player/strategy testers covers all
actual controller profiles at any positive margin.

**Proof.** Apply (1.1) to \(\sigma_D\).  If its witness belonged to \(D_i\),
(2.1) would bound its gain by zero, contradicting \(\Gamma>0\).  \(\square\)

This also identifies the precise failure of the naive compactness argument.
If a compact space of *actual* controller states carried continuous gains for
actual testers, and the positive-gain open sets of those testers covered all
states, compactness would give a finite subcover.  Proposition 2.1 rules that
out.  In the split stopping-law compactification the missing hypothesis is
actuality: mass may converge to the Late point \(\omega\), which is not an
actual stopping law.  Replacing \(\omega\) by one fixed finite date is not
uniform over controller states.

## 3. One arbitrary range is harmless; two identities must escape

For a profile-dependent selector

\[
 s(\sigma)=(i(\sigma),\tau(\sigma))
\]

let (D_i(s)) be the range of selected strategies whose selected player is
\(i\).  Strategic distance is uniform payoff distance against every actual
opponent profile, exactly as in `QuittingStrategicallyWithin`.

The checked one-exception theorem says more than Proposition 2.1.  If all
nonempty ranges except possibly one are strategically totally bounded, then
for every \(e>0\) there is an actual profile safe within \(e\) against every
strategy in every range, including the unrestricted exceptional range.  It
follows that a selector satisfying

\[
 U_{i(\sigma)}(\tau(\sigma),\sigma_{-i(\sigma)})
 -U_{i(\sigma)}(\sigma)\ge\Gamma>0                 \tag{3.1}
\]

has two distinct identities \(i\ne j\) whose nonempty ranges are not
strategically totally bounded.

The checked stopping-law boundary then supplies \(\kappa_i,\kappa_j>0\) such
that, for every horizon \(H\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\exists\tau\in D_i(s),\qquad
   \Pr_\tau(H<T<\infty)\ge\kappa_i,\\
 &\exists\rho\in D_j(s),\qquad
   \Pr_\rho(H<T<\infty)\ge\kappa_j.                \tag{3.2}
 \end{aligned}
\]

The two strategies in (3.2) may be selected at different source profiles.
Neither the checked theorem nor compactness makes them coexist at one
profile.  This is the exact simultaneous-source field still missing from a
two-clock consumer.

Under (1.1), classical choice gives a selector satisfying (3.1).  No
measurability or online policy is needed: the controller--tester value is an
offline terminal value.

If the resolver emits a lower tree at a positive scale \(\varepsilon\), then

**Proof.** The checked lower-branch decoder gives (4.3).  Choose one displayed

## 5. Global branch statement and its limit

The checked theorem `finFourExactScale_infimum_zero_or_lower_event` gives the
exact normalized-rational disjunction:

* if the global exploitability infimum is zero, the scale resolver supplies
  actual finite-clock profiles at all errors and the terminal-selection
  consumer returns one uniform-equilibrium payoff;
* if the infimum is positive, some positive scale emits a finite lower tree.

Combining its second arm with Proposition 4.1 adds the two-identity escape
conclusion.  This is a real finite separation theorem, but it does not decide
the sign of the infimum.  Searching dyadic scales semidecides the positive
branch; absence of a lower event at finitely many scales says nothing about
exact zero.

The tempting stronger step is now formally excluded:

\[
 \text{positive gap}
 \quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{one finite global menu of actual testers}.   \tag{5.1}
\]

The smallest remaining direct question is instead:

> Can the two separately late selector ranges forced by (3.2) be coupled to
> one actual Fin4 source profile, or can failure of such coupling be encoded
> as an explicit accepted lower tree for a concrete rational table?

Merely compactifying both ranges does not answer this: their Late limits may
be nonactual, and the two witnesses need not share a source.

## 6. A finite-clock co-source theorem

There is nevertheless a stronger source conclusion which does not attempt to
align the two selector ranges themselves.

### Theorem 6.1 (finite-clock double full-gap source)

Let (I) be finite and let (r) satisfy (1.1) for some \(\Gamma>0\).  Then
there are a finite clock bound (H), an actual profile \(\sigma\) whose
stopping laws are supported on

\[
 \{0,\ldots,H-1\}\cup\{\infty\},
\]

and distinct players (i,j) such that

\[
 B_i(\sigma)-U_i(\sigma)\ge\Gamma,
 \qquad
 B_j(\sigma)-U_j(\sigma)\ge\Gamma.                 \tag{6.1}
\]

Moreover both caps in (6.1) are attained by literal pure stopping times from
the finite list

\[
 \{0,\ldots,H-1,H,\infty\}.                        \tag{6.2}
\]

Thus the two responses are co-sourced and executable.  Each is either an
internal finite deadline, the auxiliary Late deadline (H), or Never.

**Proof.** Let \(C_H\) be the product of the finite stopping-law simplices
supported on the displayed set.  It is compact and connected.  On \(C_H\),
every prescribed payoff is a polynomial in the finitely many marginal
weights.  Against finite-clock opponents the unrestricted cap is the maximum
of the finitely many candidate payoffs in (6.2), by
`exists_finiteClockCandidate_payoff_eq_continuationBestResponseValue`.
Consequently every debt coordinate (d_i=B_i-U_i) is continuous on \(C_H\).

Put

\[
 A_i^H=\{\sigma\in C_H:d_i(\sigma)\ge\Gamma\}.
\]

These are closed and cover \(C_H\) by (1.1).  Suppose, toward a
contradiction, that no (C_H) contains a point belonging to two distinct
sets.  The finite closed cover \(\{A_i^H\}_i\) is then pairwise disjoint.
Connectedness forces one identity (i_H) to satisfy

\[
 A_{i_H}^H=C_H.                                    \tag{6.3}
\]

The clock simplices are nested.  By finiteness of (I), there are one fixed
identity (i) and unboundedly many (H) for which (i_H=i).  Every
finite-clock profile belongs to one of these later simplices, so (6.3) gives

\[
 d_i(\sigma)\ge\Gamma
 \quad\hbox{for every finite-clock profile }\sigma. \tag{6.4}
\]

Finite-clock stopping laws are total-variation dense among all stopping laws:
retain the Never atom and move the finite tail after (H) to one finite
date.  Terminal payoffs and unrestricted caps are continuous under this
total-variation approximation.  Hence (6.4) holds for every actual behavioral
profile.

At each profile choose a deviation of player (i) with gain at least
\(\Gamma/2\).  This gives a positive-gain selector whose player identity is
always (i).  It contradicts the checked two-range theorem, which forces two
distinct nonempty strategically nonprecompact player ranges.  Therefore some
\(C_H\) contains a point of (A_i^H\cap A_j^H), proving (6.1).  Finite-clock
cap attainment gives (6.2). \(\square\)

### Corollary 6.2 (finite rational co-source below the certified margin)

If the reward table is rational, then for every (0<\Gamma'<\Gamma) the

Indeed, first fix cap-attaining candidates for the real profile.  Their two

### Corollary 6.3 (decoder for a Fin4 lower tree)

If a normalized rational Fin4 exact-scale resolver emits a lower tree at

### Corollary 6.4 (effective rational source extraction)

From a verified lower tree and its rational reward code, a rational

This does not turn the lower tree into a positive solution: the tree already

## 7. Joint Fable dispatch from the finite co-source

The cap-attaining candidates allow the checked common-prefix construction to
be sharpened without an approximate-maximizer loss.  This section is ordinary
mathematics obtained by rerunning the displayed proof of
`positiveDebt_exists_commonPrefix_profitableStoppingLawFork` with the supplied
finite-clock maximizer.  It is not claimed as a named checked declaration.

Let (M) be the reward bound and let one player (k) have debt at least
\(\Delta>0\) at a finite-clock profile \(\sigma\).  Necessarily (M>0).  Let
\(q_k\in\{0,\ldots,H-1,H,\infty\}\) attain the cap, put

\[
 v_k(s)=U_k(s,\sigma_{-k}),\qquad C_k=v_k(q_k),
\]

and let \(\nu_k\) be (k)'s prescribed stopping law.  Define

\[
 \mathrm{Bad}_k=
 \{s:C_k-v_k(s)\ge\Delta/2\},
 \qquad \alpha_k=\nu_k(\mathrm{Bad}_k).             \tag{7.1}
\]

Since (U_k(\sigma)=\mathbb E_{\nu_k}v_k),

\[
 \Delta\le C_k-U_k(\sigma)
 \le \Delta/2+2M\alpha_k,
\]

and therefore

\[
 \alpha_k\ge \frac{\Delta}{4M}.                   \tag{7.2}
\]

Choose the earliest finite supported bad time, if one exists, and otherwise
choose the supported Never atom.  Call it (s_k).  Redirect every bad atom
of \(\nu_k\) to (q_k), producing a stopping law (f_k).  Since (q_k) is
an exact maximizer, it is not bad.  The transported law has gain

\[
 U_k(f_k,\sigma_{-k})-U_k(\sigma)
 =\mathbb E_{\nu_k}
   [\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{Bad}_k}(C_k-v_k)]
 \ge \frac{\Delta^2}{8M}.                          \tag{7.3}
\]

Let (a_k) be the first-disagreement cut between the supported source
(s_k) and receiver (q_k).  It is finite and (a_k\le H).  No supported
bad mass lies below (a_k), and the receiver is not below (a_k).  Hence

\[
 f_k(t)=\nu_k(t)\quad(t<a_k),
 \qquad
 \Pr_{f_k}(T_k\ge a_k)=\Pr_{\nu_k}(T_k\ge a_k).
                                                               \tag{7.4}
\]

The pure-time edge has gain at least \(\Delta/2\).  The Fable
first-disagreement factorization and the (2M) payoff-width bound therefore
give

\[
 \Pr_{\sigma,-k}(\hbox{opponents reach }a_k)
 \ge\frac{\Delta}{4M}.                              \tag{7.5}
\]

Equations (7.2), (7.4), and (7.5) give the source joint-reach floor

\[
 \Pr_\sigma(T_\ell\ge a_k\ \forall\ell)
 \ge \kappa,
 \qquad
 \kappa:=\frac{\Delta^2}{16M^2}.                   \tag{7.6}
\]

The candidate type is retained literally:

* (q_k<H) is an internal finite cap response;
* (q_k=H) is the auxiliary Late response; and
* (q_k=\infty) is Never.

The supported bad source (s_k) is either one of the source profile's actual
finite atoms below (H), or its actual Never atom.  Thus no annotation or
unattained cap is used.

### Theorem 7.1 (same-source two-fork reach/absorption dichotomy)

Apply the preceding construction to the two debtors (i\ne j) from Theorem
6.1 with the same \(\Delta\).  Put

\[
 \beta:=\frac{\Delta^3}{128M^3}.                   \tag{7.7}
\]

After exchanging (i,j), exactly one of the following holds.

1. **Same cut.** (a_i=a_j).  The two paid pure-time edges and two fork laws
   have one literal common source prefix and source joint reach at least
   \(\kappa\) at that cut.
2. **Reached two-cut fork.** (a_i<a_j), and the profile obtained by applying
   both fork laws has joint survival to the later cut (a_j) at least
   \(\beta\).  It agrees with the source at every date before (a_i), and
   the (i)- and (j)-laws have their respective first disagreements at the
   displayed two cuts.
3. **Absorbing two-cut block.** (a_i<a_j), and under the profile obtained by
   applying only (f_i),

   \[
   \Pr(a_i\le T_i<a_j,\ T_\ell\ge a_j\ 
       \text{for every }\ell\ne i)\ge\beta.        \tag{7.8}
   \]

   Thus the actual block between the two source-attached cuts has a uniformly
   positive singleton-(i) terminal atom.

**Proof.** The same-cut arm is immediate from (7.4)--(7.6).  Suppose
\(a_i<a_j\), and abbreviate \(\alpha=\Delta/(4M)\).  By (7.4),
\(f_i\)'s survival at (a_i) is at least \(\alpha\).  The later (j)-row
gives source joint survival at (a_j) at least \(\kappa\).

If \(f_i\)'s survival at (a_j) is at least \(\alpha/2\), then (f_j) has
the same survival as its source law at (a_j).  All other coordinates are
unchanged, so the joint two-fork survival at (a_j) is at least

\[
 (\alpha/2)\kappa=\beta.
\]

This is arm 2.  Otherwise (f_i) assigns mass at least \(\alpha/2\) to
\([a_i,a_j)\).  The product of the source opponents' survivals to (a_j) is
at least the source *joint* survival there, hence at least \(\kappa\).
Independence gives (7.8) with the same product
\((\alpha/2)\kappa=\beta\). \(\square\)

The global positive gap may be reapplied at each actual output profile, but
that alone does not regenerate the same common source or preserve either
original paid edge.  The next missing adapter is therefore precise: renew the
off-minimum or paid-splice output of (7.16)--(7.17), or make the two labelled
hazard budgets in (7.13) persist after the checked consumer acts.

## 8. Sharp linear obstruction to Nash--Bellman conversion

The labelled hazards in Theorem 7.2 cannot in general be retained with
support-Nash error sublinear in their charge.  The following rational Fin4
regression realizes both surviving temporal patterns.

Let players 0 and 1 be active and players 2 and 3 be payoff-zero dummies.  For
every nonempty coalition (S), set

\[
 r_0(S)=
 \begin{cases}
 -1,&0\in S,\\
  1,&0\notin S,\ 1\in S,\\
  0,&S\cap\{0,1\}=\varnothing,
 \end{cases}
\qquad
 r_1(S)=
 \begin{cases}
 -1,&1\in S,\\
  1,&1\notin S,\ 0\in S,\\
  0,&S\cap\{0,1\}=\varnothing,
 \end{cases}                                      \tag{8.1}
\]

and \(r_2(S)=r_3(S)=0\).  Dummy membership therefore does not change either
active payoff.  Fix rational \(0<p<1\), and let the dummies use Never.

### Proposition 8.1 (common-cut linear screen)

Let both active players stop at date zero with probability (p) and otherwise
use Never.  Both prescribed payoffs are \(-p^2\), both unrestricted caps are
attained uniquely by Never with value (p), and both debts are

\[
 p+p^2>0.                                          \tag{8.2}
\]

Thus both paid pure edges move from the same supported source time zero to
Never, and each pure edge gain is \(1+p\).

Now hold the post-zero tail at all-Never and put active Quit probabilities
\(x,y\) in one product root.  For player 0 the Quit and Continue endpoints
are \(-1\) and (y), respectively; for player 1 they are \(-1\) and (x).
The root Nash defects are exactly

\[
 \rho_0=x(1+y),
 \qquad
 \rho_1=y(1+x).                                    \tag{8.3}
\]

Hence

\[
 \rho_0+\rho_1=x+y+2xy\ge x+y,
 \qquad
 \max\{\rho_0,\rho_1\}\ge{x+y\over2}.             \tag{8.4}
\]

More strongly, whenever an active Quit action is used with positive
probability, that support action is at least one below the best endpoint.
Thus no positive two-label charge in this common-tail block has vanishing
support-Nash error.  The only exact root on the two active coordinates is
all Continue.

At the source \(x=y=p\), the active charge is \(2p\).  Re-solving to the exact
all-Continue root changes each active cap from (p) to zero.  Therefore the
semantic seam is at least

\[
 p={1\over2}(2p),                                  \tag{8.5}
\]

exactly linear in the erased charge.

### Proposition 8.2 (distinct-cut linear screen)

Instead let player 0 stop at date zero with probability (p), player 1 stop at
date one with probability (p), and otherwise let both use Never.  Their
prescribed payoffs, caps, and debts are

\[
 U_0=-p^2,\quad B_0=p,\quad d_0=p+p^2,
\qquad
 U_1=p^2,\quad B_1=p,\quad d_1=p-p^2.               \tag{8.6}
\]

Again Never is the unique cap response.  The two paid source times are now
zero and one, their fork laws are jointly all-Never, and the later cut is
reached with probability \(1-p\).

In the literal two-row source chronology, player 0's date-zero root defect is
\(p(1+p)\), while player 1's date-one root defect is (p).  The two labelled
hazards have total charge \(2p\), and hence

\[
 \rho_{0,0}+\rho_{1,1}=2p+p^2\ge2p.                \tag{8.7}
\]

Each used active Quit action is again at least one below its Continue
endpoint at its own row.  Exact backward re-solving selects active
all-Continue at both rows and changes both caps by (p), again one half of the
two-label charge.

All formulas follow by direct enumeration.  For example, in the distinct-cut
source, player 0 receives \(-1\) on its own date-zero stop and receives (1)
only when it uses Never and player 1 stops at date one, giving
\(-p+(1-p)p=-p^2\).  Player 1 receives (1) if player 0 stops first and
\(-1\) on its own later stop, giving \(p-(1-p)p=p^2\).  Its Never payoff is
(p), so its debt is \(p-p^2\).  The endpoint computations then give
(8.3) and (8.7).

This is not a positive-global-gap counterexample: all-Never is terminal Nash,
so \(D_*=0\).  Its role is exact and narrower.  The co-source debts, attained
Never responses, actual supported source atoms, common-prefix identities,
two labels, common or distinct cuts, and positive reach do **not** imply an
\(o(\text{charge})\) Nash--Bellman conversion.  Any proof under the
hypothetical \(D_*>0\) branch must use the global minimum floor to forbid or
pay the linear seam (8.5), rather than relying only on the Fable packet.

## 9. Finite two-block flow formulation

There is an exact finite-dimensional formulation of the proposed long
deterministic schedule.  It also shows precisely why a separation certificate
for the two fork blocks is not yet a certificate for the full quitting game.

Fix two finite blocks \(b=0,1\), each consisting of a displayed finite word of
product roots.  Let (K) be a coordinate reward box.  Bellman evaluation through
block (b) has the scalar-affine form

\[
 T_b(w)=a_b+c_bw,
 \qquad 0\le c_b<1,                                \tag{9.1}
\]

where \(c_b\) is its joint survival and positive block absorption gives the
strict inequality.  Put

\[
 R_b={a_b\over1-c_b}.
\]

This is the block's conditional terminal reward vector and belongs to (K).

For an exit continuation (w), evaluate all internal continuation vectors
backward by exact Bellman recursion.  Define \(E_b(w)\) to be the largest
support-Nash error at any internal row: if a player's Quit action has positive
probability include \((C-Q)_+\), and if Continue has positive probability
include \((Q-C)_+\).  With the roots fixed, every displayed endpoint
difference is affine in the appropriate continuation coordinate.  Therefore

\[
 P_b(\delta)=\{w\in K:E_b(w)\le\delta\}             \tag{9.2}
\]

is a compact polytope, rational when the reward and roots are rational.

### Theorem 9.1 (guarded two-block occupation dichotomy)

Assume \(R_0\ne R_1\), and parametrize their segment by

\[
 v(z)=(1-z)R_0+zR_1,qquad 0\le z\le1.
\]

Every value in a positively absorbing cyclic concatenation of the two blocks
lies on this segment.  In the (z)-coordinate, the two Bellman maps are

\[
 \phi_0(z)=c_0z,
 \qquad
 \phi_1(z)=c_1z+(1-c_1).                           \tag{9.3}
\]

The exit guards

\[
 I_b(\delta)=\{z\in[0,1]:v(z)\in P_b(\delta)\}     \tag{9.4}
\]

are closed intervals, possibly empty.  A cyclic word
\(b_0\ldots b_{m-1}\) is a \(\delta\)-support-Nash--Bellman packet exactly
when there are cyclic scalars \(z_m=z_0\) satisfying

\[
 z_t=\phi_{b_t}(z_{t+1}),
 \qquad z_{t+1}\in I_{b_t}(\delta)                 \tag{9.5}
\]

for every (t).

For every fixed \(\delta\ge0\), exactly one of the following robust
alternatives holds.

1. There is an infinite guarded schedule satisfying (9.5).  Then for every
   \(\epsilon>0\) there is a finite cyclic concatenation satisfying the same
   equations with support-Nash error at most \(\delta+\epsilon\).
2. There is no infinite guarded schedule.  Then a finite binary prefix tree
   proves this: every leaf word has an empty rational interval feasibility
   problem obtained from (9.4)--(9.5).  For rational data and rational
   \(\delta\), each empty leaf has an exact rational endpoint, or equivalently
   Farkas, certificate.

If \(R_0=R_1\), the same statement holds on the one-point segment, with both
maps constant there.

**Proof.** A composition of the homotheties (9.1) is another strict
homothety.  Its unique fixed point is a convex combination of \(R_0,R_1\),
and the same is true after every cyclic rotation.  This proves the segment
reduction and (9.3).  The support conditions are finitely many affine
inequalities, proving the interval assertion and the equivalence (9.5).

Suppose an infinite guarded schedule exists.  Its scalar states lie in the
compact interval and its labels lie in a two-letter alphabet.  Choose two
arbitrarily distant phase boundaries with close scalar states.  Close the
intervening word at the unique fixed point of its composition.  If (C) is
that word's survival product, the closing perturbation is bounded by

\[
 {C\over1-C}|z_m-z_n|,
\]

and all internal perturbations are no larger.  Since
\(C\le\max(c_0,c_1)^{m-n}\), the perturbation can be made arbitrarily small.
The finitely many affine support gaps are uniformly Lipschitz on the segment,
giving the \(\delta+\epsilon\) cyclic packet.

Conversely, form the binary tree of finite words admitting compatible states
for (9.5).  It is prefix closed and finitely branching.  If it had arbitrary
depth, König's lemma followed by compactness of the closed interval
constraints would give an infinite guarded schedule.  Hence in the second
case it has finite depth.  Substitution of (9.3) turns each leaf feasibility
set into finitely many one-variable rational linear inequalities, so
emptiness has the claimed exact certificate. \(\square\)

### Exact limit of the flow route

For a fixed pair of rational fork blocks, Theorem 9.1 gives exactly the
requested finite primal/dual alternative: an approximate periodic
Nash--Bellman packet inside their deterministic occupation alphabet, or a
finite exact tree excluding every infinite schedule in that alphabet.

The second output is **not** an all-behavior positive-gap certificate.  The
common-cut and distinct-cut tables in Section 8 have an immediate terminal
Nash profile outside the charged fork alphabet, even though their literal
charged blocks have the linear support screens above.  Thus a finite flow
tree can feed the global Fin4 lower-certificate machinery only after an
additional response-completeness theorem says that every controller state
has an accepted continuation block in this two-letter alphabet.  The
co-source/Fable construction supplies two blocks at one source, not that
global completeness field.

## 10. Exact lower-tree to guarded-block audit

The finite exact-scale lower tree and Theorem 9.1 are both finite, but their
leaf data live on different sides of the controller--tester interface.  The
strongest direct bridge supplied by the checked shell semantics is the
following pointwise statement.

### Proposition 10.1 (quantitative tester at the reconstructed center)

Fix a normalized rational Fin4 table, a positive shell level \(N\), and a
checked lower tree with objective threshold \(\gamma>0\).  Let \(x\) be any
feasible assignment followed through the tree, let

\[
 p=(u^p,b^p),\qquad c=(u^c,b^c)
\]

be its point and center semantic pairs, and put \(r=12/N\).  If
\(\gamma>2r\), then the center decodes to an actual finite-clock product
profile \(\widehat\sigma_x\), and there are a player \(i\) and a finite-clock
candidate

\[
 q\in\{0,\ldots,H-1,H,\mathsf{Never}\}
\]

such that

\[
 U_i(q,(\widehat\sigma_x)_{-i})-U_i(\widehat\sigma_x)
 \ge \gamma-2r>0.                              \tag{10.1}
\]

Here \(H\) is the auxiliary after-support date: the decoded center itself has
zero mass at \(H\), while a cap candidate is allowed to use it.

At the exact scale

\[
 N=\left\lfloor{96\over\varepsilon}\right\rfloor+1,
 \qquad \gamma={\varepsilon\over4},
\]

the margin in (10.1) is

\[
 {\varepsilon\over4}-{24\over N}>0.             \tag{10.2}
\]

**Proof.**  A feasible assignment cannot end at an equality-separated or
required-nonnegative-separated leaf.  Tree soundness therefore gives

\[
 \max(0,b^p_0-u^p_0,\ldots,b^p_3-u^p_3)\ge\gamma.
\]

Because \(\gamma>0\), some \(i\) has \(b^p_i-u^p_i\ge\gamma\).  The sixteen
shell inequalities give

\[
 |u^p_i-u^c_i|\le r,\qquad |b^p_i-b^c_i|\le r,
\]

and hence \(b^c_i-u^c_i\ge\gamma-2r\).  Feasibility decodes the center to the
literal finite-clock profile \(\widehat\sigma_x\), identifies \(u^c_i\) with
its terminal payoff and \(b^c_i\) with its unrestricted continuation cap,
and the cap-tight product supplies a candidate \(q\) attaining that cap.
This proves (10.1).  Finally the checked exact-scale bracket says
\(24/N<\varepsilon/4\), proving (10.2).  \(\square\)

The finitely many closed semialgebraic sets

\[
 C_{i,q}=\{x\text{ feasible}:
 U_i(q,(\widehat\sigma_x)_{-i})-U_i(\widehat\sigma_x)
 \ge\gamma-2r\}                                  \tag{10.3}
\]

cover the feasible set when \(\gamma>2r\).  Thus one may augment the lower
certificate by a finite *center-tester* cover.  This conclusion is stronger
than what an individual tree leaf records: a leaf stores only an equality
index, a nonnegative-row index, or the undifferentiated max-objective lower
bound.  It stores neither \(i\) nor \(q\).

### Proposition 10.2 (the center-tester cover is not a guard cover)

The cover (10.3), together with the existing checked declarations, does not
supply any of the following fields required by Theorem 9.1:

1. a block whose initial law is the actual profile represented by the point
   \(p\), when that point pair comes from an actual source;
2. positive absorption of the selected replacement;
3. support-Nash inequalities for all players at every row of the block; or
4. a closed source-to-exit transition relation on which the construction can
   be renewed.

More precisely, the loss in each field is literal.

* The reconstructed center is only semantically within radius \(r\) of the
  point.  The shell theorem does not identify their stopping laws, prefixes,
  or the payoff of the *same* candidate against both opponent profiles.
  Consequently (10.1) is paid at \(\widehat\sigma_x\), not at an original
  source having semantic pair \(p\).
* The attaining candidate may be Never, which has no absorption.  If it is
  the finite candidate \(H\), then it is exactly the first date excluded from
  the center support.  Enlarging the source class to include that date moves
  the auxiliary boundary to \(H+1\).
* A strict best-response inequality for player \(i\) says that the old center
  is not Nash.  It gives no optimality inequality for the supported actions of
  the other three players after \(i\)'s replacement.  Simultaneously choosing
  four cap-attaining candidates is not justified because each candidate is a
  best response to the old center opponents, not to the jointly replaced
  profile.
* If one nevertheless interprets finitely many labels as fixed actual paid
  replacements applicable at every actual profile, Proposition 2.1 and
  `exists_quittingBehaviorProfile_forall_mem_finiteMenu_payoff_le` give an
  actual profile safe against all of them.  Hence no such finite fixed paid
  alphabet can be source complete.

The first three bullets are not mere absent annotations.  The exact local
tables in Section 8 have paid cap candidates and positive block charge while
their support-Nash guards are empty over the whole continuation reward box at
error below a linear multiple of that charge.  Those tables have zero global
minimum, so they do not exclude a new theorem that uses the positive global
gap; they do exclude a decoder using only the lower-tree leaf payload and the
local cap-attainment equations.

### Exact disposition of the proposed synthesis

The lower tree supplies the profitable-tester half of the proposed program
only after moving to a source-dependent reconstructed center.  It supplies
neither the absorbing affine block nor its Nash guard.  Finitely subdividing
the same tree can select a debtor and cap candidate, but cannot restore the
lost source law or create the missing joint support inequalities.

A sufficient new adapter would have to be genuinely parametric.  On every
relevant actual controller state \(z\), it would construct one of finitely
many block types \(B_\ell(z)\) such that:

\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{(source)} & B_\ell(z)\text{ starts from, or has a checked common
 prefix with, the actual source }z,\\
 \text{(charge)} & 1-c_\ell(z)\ge\chi>0,\\
 \text{(guard)} & z\text{ (or its prescribed exit state) lies in the full
 support-Nash guard of }B_\ell(z),\\
 \text{(renewal)} & T_{B_\ell(z)}(z)\text{ lies again in the covered compact
 state and the graph of this transition is closed.}
 \end{array}                                      \tag{10.4}
\]

The fixed-test watchdog forces the actual replacement inside \(B_\ell(z)\)
to vary with \(z\) or to lose a uniform paid-first-edge interpretation.  No
current exact-scale tree declaration constructs (10.4).  Therefore the
suggested dependent-choice/compact-recurrence conclusion is conditional on
\(10.4\); it is not an additional consequence of the existing finite lower
certificate tree.

## Boundary checks

- All deviations are complete behavioral replacements.
- A finite mixture of complete strategies is realized by one ordinary
  stopping-law hazard; no public correlation is introduced.
- The finite-menu safe profile depends on the menu.
- The selected late laws and their source profiles may depend on the horizon.
- Late finite mass excludes the Never atom.
- The lower tree proves a non-strict infimum bound; the literal deviation gap
  is deliberately reduced from \(\varepsilon/4\) to
  \(\varepsilon/8\).
- The upper branch evaluates unrestricted caps, not only deviations inside
  the finite clock.
- The zero branch fixes one payoff target through the checked terminal
  selection theorem.
- The joint Fable dispatch uses an exact finite-clock cap maximizer; the
  checked general theorem uses an approximate maximizer, so the sharpened
  constants in Section 7 are ordinary mathematics.
- The two source-paid edges are not asserted paid at the combined profile.
- The charged orientation uses the checked positive-minimum two-cut consumer
  only after building a literal behavioral root sequence and adding one
  deterministic marked row.  It does not identify a response fork with a
  Nash--Bellman root.
- The linear screens in Section 8 deliberately have \(D_*=0\).  They falsify
  a local packet-to-Nash--Bellman implication, not the positive-minimum Fin4
  conjecture-facing statement.
- The finite flow tree in Section 9 certifies infeasibility only inside its
  two supplied block types.  It is not a global exploitability tree unless a
  separate response-completeness adapter is proved.
- A lower-tree objective leaf certifies a max debt at the shell point, not a
  selected deviation or a Nash guard.  Proposition 10.1 transfers that debt
  to a profitable pure candidate only at the reconstructed center and loses
  twice the shell radius.
