## 3. The rational two-player table

Let the players be `p,q`.  Never quitting pays `(0,0)`.  The three nonempty
terminal rows are

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
S&r_p(S)&r_q(S)\\ \hline
\{p\}&-1&1\\
\{q\}&0&2\\
\{p,q\}&1&0.
\end{array}
\]

Let `r*` be the product root at which both players Quit with probability
`1/2`, and put

\[
                         U=(0,1).
\]

### 3.1 Exact Bellman and root-Nash calculation

Against `q`'s half--half marginal, player `p` receives

\[
 Q_p={-1+1\over2}=0,
 \qquad
 C_p={U_p+0\over2}=0.
\]

Against `p`'s half--half marginal, player `q` receives

\[
 Q_q={2+0\over2}=1,
 \qquad
 C_q={U_q+1\over2}=1.
\]

The product expectation is therefore again `(0,1)`.  Equivalently, the
one-row terminal charges are `0` for `p` and `3/4` for `q`, while row survival
is `1/4`; division by the absorption mass `3/4` gives `(0,1)`.

The stationary profile generated by `r*` is exact Nash against arbitrary
behavioral deviations, not just stationary ones.  Conditional on every live
history, both pure actions give player `p` value `0` and both give player `q`
value `1`.  The opponent's independent half hazard makes absorption almost
sure, so iterating this equality prices every history-dependent hazard at the
same value.  This proves S.1 with an exact full-support root.

## 4. Exact punishment floors

### 4.1 Player `p`: `P_p=0`

Against any behavior of `q`, player `p` can Continue forever.  If `q` Quits,
the terminal row is `{q}` and pays `p` zero; if nobody ever Quits, Never also
pays zero.  Hence every best-response envelope for `p` is at least zero.

Conversely, if `q` Continues forever, every `p` strategy pays either `-1`
when `p` eventually Quits alone or `0` when `p` never Quits.  The envelope is
zero.  Therefore

\[
                              P_p=0.
\]

### 4.2 Player `q`: `P_q=1`

Fix an arbitrary behavior of `p`, and let `A` be the probability that `p`
ever Quits when `q` Continues.  Continuing forever gives `q` payoff `A`, since
an eventual singleton `p` exit pays `q` one.

For a deterministic time `T`, let `q` Continue before `T` and Quit at `T`.
Its payoff is

\[
 \Pr(T_p<T)+2\Pr(T_p>T),
\]

because an earlier `p` exit pays one, a strict `q` exit pays two, and a
simultaneous exit pays zero.  As `T` tends to infinity, the point mass
`Pr(T_p=T)` tends to zero and this expression tends to

\[
                         A+2(1-A)=2-A.
\]

The unrestricted best-response supremum is therefore at least
`max(A,2-A)>=1`.  If `p` Quits surely at date zero, `q` obtains at most one:
Continue gives the singleton-`p` payoff one and Quit gives the collision
payoff zero.  Thus

\[
                              P_q=1.
\]

This proof explicitly prices collisions and uses the supremum over actual
behavioral deviations.

## 6. Every endpoint is positive and none has a sure root

Let `(U,B)` be any
`QuittingPositiveJointPrefixReachPunishmentEndpoint` for this reward table;
its punished label may be either player.  The checked carrier-floor theorem
and Section 4 give

\[
                         B_p\ge P_p=0,
 \qquad                   B_q\ge P_q=1.
\]

The endpoint has zero debt, so `U=B`.  Consequently

\[
                         U_p\ge0,
 \qquad                   U_q\ge1>0.
\]

Thus **every** eligible endpoint lies in the positive-coordinate complement
asked about in the question.

Now write `x` for `p`'s Quit probability and `y` for `q`'s Quit probability
in a proposed exact one-stage Nash root over `U`.

* If `x=1`, player `q` strictly prefers Continue: it receives `1` from the
  singleton `{p}` and `0` from the collision.  Hence `y=0`.  But then `p` gets
  `-1` from Quit and at least `U_p>=0` from Continue, contradiction.
* If `y=1`, player `p` strictly prefers Quit: it receives `1` from the
  collision and `0` from Continue.  Hence `x=1`.  Player `q` then strictly
  prefers Continue, contradiction.

There is no exact root with a sure quitter over **any** eligible endpoint.
The source of Section 5 therefore defines a literal
`QuittingPositiveJointPrefixReachNoSureExitResidual`.

## 7. The global S.2 branch has a uniform gap

The preceding endpoint argument is exact.  The following estimates rule out
even arbitrarily accurate executable instant-punishment profiles.

Suppose first that `p` is the sure quitter and let `y` be `q`'s root Quit
probability.  The prescribed root payoffs are

\[
                         u_p=-1+2y,
 \qquad                   u_q=1-y.
\]

Player `q` can Continue and obtain `1`, so epsilon-Nash gives `y<=epsilon`.
Player `p` can Continue at the root and, after root survival, approximate its
punishment best-response envelope, which is at least `P_p=0`.  Its deviation
payoff has supremum at least zero, so epsilon-Nash gives

\[
                         y\ge(1-\epsilon)/2.
\]

These inequalities are incompatible when `epsilon<1/3`.

Suppose instead that `q` is the sure quitter and let `x` be `p`'s root Quit
probability.  The prescribed root payoffs are

\[
                         u_p=x,
 \qquad                   u_q=2(1-x).
\]

Player `p` can Quit and obtain the collision payoff `1`, hence
`x>=1-epsilon`.  Player `q` can Continue: if `p` Quits it receives `1`, and if
`p` Continues it can approximate a punishment best response whose value is at
least `P_q=1`.  Thus its deviation supremum is at least `1`, and epsilon-Nash
requires

\[
                         x\le(1+\epsilon)/2.
\]

Again the inequalities are incompatible for `epsilon<1/3`.

Since every instant-punishment witness names a sure quitter, these two cases
prove failure of `QuittingInstantPunishmentεEquilibriumExistence` and of the
paper S.2 branch.  The use of a supremum does not assume attainment: take a
suffix deviation within an arbitrary positive `delta` of the envelope and
let `delta` tend to zero.

Player `q`'s Quit and Continue endpoints are both `2`.  Player `p` uses only

\[
