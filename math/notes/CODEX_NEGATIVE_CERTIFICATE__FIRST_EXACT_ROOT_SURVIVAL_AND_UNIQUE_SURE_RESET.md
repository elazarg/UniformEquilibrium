# First exact-root survival leaves a delayed paid fork or a unique-sure Never reset

Author: CODEX_NEGATIVE_CERTIFICATE

## Status

**Exact ordinary mathematics; not Lean-checked and not proposed for export.**
This note classifies the first debt-dropping exact payoff-root prefix at the
structured tropical source. After passage to a subsequence, positive joint
survival preserves an actual shifted paid response with uniformly positive
reach and gain. Vanishing joint survival cannot have two limiting sure
quitters under the positive global debt floor. It has exactly one sure owner;
that owner carries asymptotically all debt of the prefixed source and has an
exact Continue-then-tail cap response. Repeating the limiting root produces
an actual stationary unique-debtor source whose exact unrestricted cap is
literal Never with gain at least the terminal gap.

The last object is a genuine paid/reset source, not a terminal equilibrium.
Deleting its sure owner can reactivate an outsider, so it is not a renewable
rank or a solution of the Fin4 conjecture. The exact regression in Section 7
shows that this nonrenewal is real.

## Exact question

Let \(I=\operatorname{Fin}4\) and suppose the quitting game has no uniform
equilibrium payoff. Let \(\tau_n\) be the actual stationary final sources from
FIN4_TROPICAL_TWO_NEVER_TO_OFFMINIMUM_PAID_PORT. Write

\[
 X_n=(u_n,B_n)=\operatorname{Sem}(\tau_n),\qquad
 d_{n,i}=B_{n,i}-u_{n,i}.                                      \tag{1}
\]

There are fixed \(b\in I\), \(\gamma>0\), \(a>0\), and \(D_*>0\) such that

\[
 d_{n,b}\ge\gamma,\qquad B_{n,b}\to s_b:=r_b(\{b\}),            \tag{2}
\]

\[
 D_*\le D(X)\quad(X\in\mathcal C(r)),                           \tag{3}
\]

and every exact product Nash root \(q\) against \(u_n\) has

\[
 \operatorname{Abs}(q)\ge a.                                   \tag{4}
\]

Choose \(M>0\) which bounds every reward coordinate and every source-payoff
coordinate in absolute value, and put

\[
 \delta_b=\min\{\gamma/2,\gamma^2/(16M)\}>0.                    \tag{5}
\]

For every sufficiently large \(n\), the twice-reviewed fixed-cap-pin theorem
gives, for every exact root \(q\) at \(u_n\),

\[
 d_{n,b}-d_b(\operatorname{Prefix}(q,X_n))\ge\delta_b.          \tag{6}
\]

Exact prefixing weakly decreases every other debt coordinate, and hence

\[
 \operatorname{Drop}(X_n,q)\ge\delta_b.                        \tag{7}
\]

Neither Fin4, the global floor \(D_*\), nor the absorption floor \(a\) is used
to prove (6)--(7). Those hypotheses reenter only in the survival
classification below.

Choose an arbitrary exact root \(q_n\) at each late \(u_n\), and set

\[
 \rho_n=q_n\mathbin{::}\tau_n,\qquad
 Y_n=\operatorname{Sem}(\rho_n)
     =\operatorname{Prefix}(q_n,X_n),                           \tag{8}
\]

\[
 c_n=\prod_i(1-q_{n,i}).                                       \tag{9}
\]

What structure survives after this first exact prefix?

## Exact survivor theorem

After passage to a subsequence, exactly one of the following two alternatives
holds.

### A. Positive-survival delayed paid fork

There is \(\eta>0\) such that

\[
 \eta\le c_n\le1-a,                                           \tag{10}
\]

\[
 D_*\le D(Y_n)\le D(X_n)-\delta_b,                             \tag{11}
\]

and the original player \(b\) has an actual behavioral response from
\(\rho_n\) with the following properties:

1. it agrees with \(q_{n,b}\) at the new date zero;
2. conditional on joint Continue in that root, it uses the original
   cap-attaining Quit0 response in the stationary tail \(\tau_n\);
3. its first changed row is the shifted source row at date one, reached under
   \(\rho_n\) with probability \(c_n\ge\eta\); and
4. its whole-profile gain is exactly

   \[
   c_n d_{n,b}\ge\eta\gamma.                                  \tag{12}
   \]

Consequently

\[
 d_b(Y_n)\ge c_nd_{n,b}\ge\eta\gamma.                          \tag{13}
\]

This is a source-matched delayed paid fork at a strictly lower-debt actual
carrier point. It is not asserted to attain the whole cap of \(Y_n\), and
\(Y_n\) is not stationary.

### B. Vanishing-survival unique-sure reset

One has \(c_n\to0\). After a further root-simplex subsequence there are a
limit root \(q\) and a unique player \(k\) such that

\[
 q_{n}\to q,\qquad q_k=1,\qquad q_j<1\quad(j\ne k).            \tag{14}
\]

Put

\[
 O_k=\prod_{j\ne k}(1-q_j)>0.                                 \tag{15}
\]

Then:

1. every nonowner debt at the finite prefixed source vanishes,

   \[
   d_i(Y_n)\to0\quad(i\ne k),                                 \tag{16}
   \]

   while

   \[
   d_k(Y_n)\ge D_*/2                                          \tag{17}
   \]

   for all sufficiently large \(n\);
2. the exact cap of player \(k\) at \(\rho_n\) is the response which forces
   Continue in the new first root and, if all opponents Continue, uses an
   exact cap response in the stationary tail \(\tau_n\);
3. the response in item 2 has gain \(d_k(Y_n)\ge D_*/2\) and enters that
   tail against unchanged opponents with probability tending to \(O_k\), so
   its deleted-player tail-entry probability is eventually at least
   \(O_k/2\);
4. after a further two-valued subsequence, the tail cap used in item 2 is
   always one of the two literal stationary endpoints: Quit0 or Never; and
5. the stationary profile \(\widehat q\) which repeats \(q\) at every date
   has exactly one debtor, namely \(k\), and literal Never is \(k\)'s exact
   cap with gain at least one fixed terminal exploitability gap
   \(\Gamma>0\).

Thus the zero-survival arm does not leave a mixed no-sure endpoint or a
two-sure terminal endpoint. It lands on an actual stationary
unique-sure-owner Never reset. The Never child
\(\widehat q[k\leftarrow\operatorname{Never}]\) is literal, but need not be
Nash, floor-safe, or another structured tropical source.

## Definitions and behavioral semantics

For an exact root \(q\) at a semantic pair \(X=(u,B)\), write

\[
 s_i(q)=\prod_{j\ne i}(1-q_j),\qquad
 e_i(q;u)=[Q_i(q_{-i})-C_i(q_{-i};u_i)]_+.                    \tag{18}
\]

The exact complete-semantic debt action is

\[
 d_i(\operatorname{Prefix}(q,X))
   =[s_i(q)d_i(X)-e_i(q;u)]_+.                                \tag{19}
\]

In particular,

\[
 0\le d_i(\operatorname{Prefix}(q,X))\le s_i(q)d_i(X).         \tag{20}
\]

The cap in (19) is over every unilateral behavioral strategy. A deviator may
change its current root action and, after joint Continue, use any randomized,
late, or Never strategy in the old tail. Exact one-stage Nash is used only
against the prescribed tail \(u\); formula (19) is what retains the complete
tail cap \(B\).

For a stationary opponent root, behavioral pure-time extremality says that
the unrestricted cap is the better of Quit0 and Never. If the opponents'
one-row Continue probability is \(O<1\), every finite quitting time lies on
the affine segment between those endpoints. If \(O=1\), every finite Quit
time has the Quit0 value and Never has the nonabsorption value zero, so the
same endpoint conclusion holds directly.

## Proof

### 1. Compact survival split

The root simplexes and semantic carrier are compact. Pass to a subsequence on
which

\[
 X_n\to X,\qquad q_n\to q,\qquad c_n\to c_\infty.             \tag{21}
\]

Since root absorption is \(1-c_n\), (4) gives
\(c_\infty\le1-a\). Either \(c_\infty>0\), which is alternative A after
taking \(\eta=c_\infty/2\), or \(c_\infty=0\).

Every \(Y_n\) is an actual semantic pair and hence a carrier point. Exact
prefixing weakly decreases every coordinate debt, while (6) spends at least
\(\delta_b\) in coordinate \(b\). This proves (11).

### 2. Positive survival preserves the shifted row exactly

Assume \(c_\infty>0\). Then every marginal of \(q_n\), including \(b\)'s,
has positive Continue probability for all large \(n\). Exact root
complementarity makes \(b\)'s two prescribed-payoff endpoints equal whenever
it mixes, and makes Continue optimal if it is pure Continue. In either case
(19) gives

\[
 d_b(Y_n)=s_b(q_n)d_{n,b}\ge c_nd_{n,b},                      \tag{22}
\]

because \(s_b(q_n)=c_n/(1-q_{n,b})\ge c_n\). This proves (13).

There is also a direct executable witness for the smaller right side in
(22). Player \(b\) copies its prescribed root randomization at date zero.
If the root absorbs, source and response laws are identical. If every player
Continues, the response replaces the stationary tail action by the original
Quit0 cap response. All earlier terminal contributions cancel, and the
tail payoff improvement \(d_{n,b}\) is multiplied by the exact joint-entry
probability \(c_n\). This proves (12), including its complete agency and row
reach.

The source after joint Continue is literally \(\tau_n\), so the original
stationary paid row and its self-successor are still present at the shifted
date. But the full profile \(\rho_n\) starts with \(q_n\). Its cap coordinate
need not converge to \(s_b\), and the displayed response need not dominate a
different profitable root-date deviation. Thus compactness supplies the
uniform paid fork (12), not a regenerated instance of the cap-pin theorem.

### 3. Vanishing survival produces at least one sure quitter

Now suppose \(c_n\to0\). From (21),

\[
 \prod_i(1-q_i)=0,
\]

so at least one coordinate of \(q\) equals one.

The exact roots pass to an exact root at the limiting prescribed payoff
\(u=X.1\). Suppose two distinct coordinates of \(q\) were sure Quit. Then
every unilateral deviation would leave another sure quitter. The semantic
tail, including every complete cap coordinate, would be screened for every
player. Exact root Nash would therefore make
\(\operatorname{Prefix}(q,X)\) diagonal.

Equivalently, (19) has \(s_i(q)=0\) for every \(i\), hence every prefixed
debt is zero. Joint prefix continuity would give

\[
 D(Y_n)\to0,
\]

contradicting \(D(Y_n)\ge D_*>0\). Therefore the sure quitter is unique;
call it \(k\). Uniqueness gives (15).

### 4. The finite prefixed source has one macroscopic cap-tail defect

For every \(i\ne k\), the opponent-survival factor \(s_i(q_n)\) contains
\(1-q_{n,k}\to0\). The source debts are uniformly bounded by carrier
compactness, so (20) gives (16). Since \(D(Y_n)\ge D_*\), equation (16)
implies (17).

Let \(U'_{n,k}\) be player \(k\)'s prescribed payoff in \(Y_n\). Its complete
cap is

\[
 B'_{n,k}=
 \max\{Q_k(q_{n,-k}),C_k(q_{n,-k};B_{n,k})\}.                 \tag{23}
\]

For large \(n\), \(q_{n,k}>0\). Exact root Nash then makes forced Quit a
maximizing prescribed-payoff endpoint. Since \(B_{n,k}\ge u_{n,k}\), the
Continue endpoint is monotone:
\(C_k(q_{n,-k};B_{n,k})\ge C_k(q_{n,-k};u_{n,k})\).
If the maximum in (23) were the Quit endpoint, it would equal
\(U'_{n,k}\), giving \(d_k(Y_n)=0\), contrary to (17). Hence the maximum in
(23) is strictly the Continue-with-cap endpoint.

Thus an exact cap response forces \(k\) to Continue at the new root and, on
the event that all opponents Continue, uses a cap-attaining response in
\(\tau_n\). The event has probability

\[
 O_{n,k}=\prod_{j\ne k}(1-q_{n,j})\to O_k>0.                  \tag{24}
\]

The response gain is exactly \(d_k(Y_n)\), proving items 2--3 of alternative
B. Against the stationary source opponents, unrestricted pure-time
extremality selects Quit0 or Never. A subsequence fixes which of the two
endpoints is used, proving item 4.

This is a literal source-attached reset response, but conditional entry
produces

\[
 \tau_n[k\leftarrow\beta_{n,k}],
\]

not \(\tau_n\): the same owner whose forced Continue exposes the old tail
must change its tail strategy to realize the gain. The opponents' source tail
is preserved, but the full source is not regenerated.

### 5. The limiting root itself gives a stationary Never reset

Let \(\widehat q\) repeat \(q\) stationarily. Since \(q_k=1\), this profile
absorbs in its first row. Let \(W\) be its prescribed payoff. Every outsider
\(i\ne k\) is screened by the sure quitter \(k\), even after an arbitrary
behavioral deviation. The limiting exact-root inequalities therefore give

\[
 B_i(\widehat q)=W_i\quad(i\ne k).                             \tag{25}
\]

For \(k\), Quit0 yields \(W_k=Q_k(q_{-k})\). Against the stationary opponent
root \(q_{-k}\), let \(N_k\) be the payoff from literal Never. Pure-time
extremality gives

\[
 B_k(\widehat q)=\max\{W_k,N_k\}.                              \tag{26}
\]

Under no uniform equilibrium there are \(\Gamma>0\) and a terminal
exploitability gap of size \(\Gamma\) against every actual profile. Apply it
to \(\widehat q\). By (25), no outsider can pay the gap. Equations (25)--(26)
therefore force

\[
 N_k-W_k\ge\Gamma.                                             \tag{27}
\]

Thus \(k\) is the unique debtor of \(\widehat q\), literal Never attains its
complete behavioral cap, and the update

\[
 \widehat q\longrightarrow
 \widehat q[k\leftarrow\operatorname{Never}]                  \tag{28}
\]

is an exact stationary paid/reset edge of gain at least \(\Gamma\). This
proves item 5 and completes the dichotomy.

## Boundary and falsification tests

### Positive survival does not regenerate the cap pin

Equation (12) controls a particular response copied through the root. The
whole cap at \(Y_n\) may instead be attained by changing the date-zero root
action. Consequently \(B_b(Y_n)\) need not approach \(s_b\), even though the
old paid row is reached with fixed probability. The cap-pin theorem cannot be
iterated from (12) alone.

### Two sure quitters are genuinely closed

With two sure quitters, every unilateral deviation leaves one sure quitter,
so the tail is unreachable for every deviator. There is no hidden owner
Never branch. This is why the direct zero-debt contradiction in Section 3 is
complete rather than a root-only screening argument.

### Exact reactivation regression after the Never reset

Pad the following three-player table by a fourth player with identically zero
payoffs who always Continues. Name the active players \(k,a,b\). Every
unspecified payoff below is zero. Set

\[
 r_a(\{a\})=\tfrac12,
\]

\[
 r_k(\{k\})=0,\qquad
 r_k(S)=\tfrac43\ \text{if }k\in S,\ S\ne\{k\},\qquad
 r_k(S)=\tfrac65\ \text{if }k\notin S,
\]

and, on coalitions containing \(k\), set

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
S&\{k\}&\{k,a\}&\{k,b\}&\{k,a,b\}\\ \hline
r_a(S)&1&0&0&1\\
r_b(S)&0&1&1&0.
\end{array}                                                    \tag{29}
\]

Let \(q_k=1\), \(q_a=q_b=1/2\). The outsiders play exact matching pennies:
each forced endpoint pays \(1/2\). Player \(k\)'s Quit0 payoff is

\[
 Q_k=\tfrac34\cdot\tfrac43=1.
\]

If \(k\) Never quits, the outsiders eventually absorb and pay \(k\)
\(6/5\). Hence the stationary profile repeating \(q\) is exactly a
unique-debtor source and Never is \(k\)'s exact cap with gain \(1/5\).

After the literal Never update, the stationary child has only \(a,b\)
mixing \(1/2\). Its eventual terminal coalitions
\(\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\) each have probability \(1/3\), so \(a\)'s prescribed
payoff is \(1/6\). Quitting immediately yields

\[
 \tfrac12 r_a(\{a\})+\tfrac12r_a(\{a,b\})=\tfrac14,
\]

a strict reactivation gain \(1/12\). Thus (28) need not be terminal Nash or
regenerate the reset hypotheses.

This exact table is an interface regression, not a no-uniform-equilibrium
counterexample; it may have another equilibrium. It proves that unique
debtor, exact Never cap, literal stationary update, and positive reset gain
do not by themselves give renewal.

## Lean handoff

The narrow formal result should be split into three declarations.

1. A positive-survival transport lemma showing that copying a root action and
   changing only the attached tail response multiplies its payoff difference
   by the root joint-Continue mass. Existing root-then-continuation payoff
   identities should make this a direct calculation.
2. A compact Fin4 classification: under a positive carrier debt floor,
   vanishing joint survival and exact-root prefixes cannot have two limiting
   sure quitters; a unique sure \(k\) makes every other prefix debt vanish and
   leaves \(d_k\ge D_*\).
3. A stationary unique-sure theorem: if \(q\) is exact for all screened
   outsiders and has unique sure owner \(k\), a terminal gap forces literal
   Never to be \(k\)'s stationary unrestricted cap with the full gap.

The finite owner response uses the exact formula

\[
 B'_{n,k}=\max\{Q_k,C_k(B_{n,k})\}.
\]

Once \(d_k(Y_n)>0\), exact root Nash rules out the Quit branch, so no
selection theorem beyond stationary Quit0/Never cap attainment is needed.
The noncontracting opponent boundary \(O_k=1\) should be handled separately:
all finite Quit times have the Quit0 value and Never pays zero.

## Scope and nonclaims

- The dichotomy is subsequential. It does not assert that one arm holds
  eventually without passing to a root/carrier cluster.
- Alternative A gives a fixed-reach delayed paid fork, not an exact whole-cap
  response or a stationary successor.
- Alternative B gives an exact whole-cap response at the finite prefixed
  source and an exact stationary Never reset at the limit. These are related
  by the root cluster but are not identified as the same behavioral profile.
- The finite cap response preserves the old tail opponents but changes the
  owner's tail strategy. It does not literally reenter the complete source
  \(\tau_n\).
- The stationary Never child may reactivate an outsider, violate punishment
  floors, lose the original paid label, or return to a previously seen
  support.
- No source return, well-founded rank, repeated debt descent, or uniform
  equilibrium is proved.
