# Pairwise social duality gives source-supported atoms, not a pair-base consumer

Identity: `CODEX_SOCIAL_PAIR`  
Date: 2026-08-31  
Status: **ordinary mathematics proved below.**  The two-dimensional dual
certificate has an exact crossed-pair normal form.  In the positive-minimum
punishment-normal branch its outcomes may be chosen from the support of one
fixed minimum law.  This retains genuine atom provenance, but not the law's
weights, its semantic cap, or a product/Nash realization.  Exact regressions
show that even simultaneous certificates for all six player pairs, numerical
singleton separation, punishment normality, and product realizability do not
by themselves activate the known pair-base consumers.

## 1. Fixed-pair alternative

Let \(I\) be finite, let

\[
 \Omega=\{\infty\}\cup\{S\subseteq I:S\ne\varnothing\},
 \qquad \bar r(\infty)=0,
\]

and put \(s_i=r_i(\{i\})\).  Fix a two-player set \(J=\{i,j\}\).  For an
outcome \(\omega\) define its projected singleton-surplus vector

\[
 v_\omega=
 \bigl(\bar r_i(\omega)-s_i,\bar r_j(\omega)-s_j\bigr)
 \in\mathbb R^2.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The fixed-support cone alternative in
`CODEX_WEIGHT_GLOBAL__NONNEGATIVE_WEIGHT_SOCIAL_CHAMBER.md` specializes as
follows.

### Theorem 1.1 (two-outcome projected coordinatewise-improving law)

Exactly one of the following holds.

1. There is a vector \(\theta\) in the strictly positive quadrant such that

   \[
    \theta\cdot v_\omega\leq0\qquad(\omega\in\Omega).
   \tag{1.2}
   \]

2. There is a probability law \(\nu\) on \(\Omega\), supported on at most two outcomes,
   such that

   \[
    \mathbb E_\nu v_\omega\in
      \mathbb R^2_{\geq0}\setminus\{0\}.
   \tag{1.3}
   \]

This is Gordan--Stiemke separation followed by conic Caratheodory in
\(\mathbb R^2\).  Never is an ordinary outcome in this statement: its
generator is

\[
 v_\infty=(-s_i,-s_j).
\]

Thus a certificate may say only that Never weakly improves these two
singleton coordinates.

### Exact two-atom normal form

If no single selected outcome already has

\[
 v_\omega\in\mathbb R^2_{\geq0}\setminus\{0\},
\]

then a minimal certificate has two outcomes \(\omega\) and \(\tau\).  After exchanging the
players and outcomes if necessary, there are positive numbers \(a,b,c,d\)
such that

\[
 v_\omega=(a,-b),\qquad v_\tau=(-c,d),
\tag{1.4}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{ad>bc.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

Indeed, for \(\lambda\) mass on \(\omega\) the two inequalities are

\[
 \lambda\geq\frac{c}{a+c},
 \qquad
 \lambda\leq\frac{d}{b+d}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

The interval is nonempty exactly when \(ad\geq bc\).  Equality makes both
coordinates zero at the unique feasible weight, so the nonzero requirement
in (1.3) is exactly the strict determinant inequality (1.5).

This determinant is not a quitting LCP minor.  Its columns compare two
arbitrary terminal reward vectors with the synthetic singleton vector.  An
LCP or root-Nash matrix instead consists of adjacent own-action differences
such as \(r_k(S\cup\{k\})-r_k(S)\).

## 2. Strengthening at one fixed minimum law

There is a source law before any conic duality is used.  At an ordinary
positive global minimum \(z_*=(U,B)\), the checked coordinate singleton
margin and the identity \(d_i=B_i-U_i\) give

\[
 U_i-s_i\geq D_*-d_i=\sum_{k\ne i}d_k\geq0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Summing over \(i\) gives

\[
 \sum_i(U_i-s_i)\geq(|I|-1)D_*>0
\tag{2.2}
\]

when there are at least two players.  Thus \(U\geq s\) and \(U\ne s\)
without punishment normality.  A joint-carrier lift supplies one limiting
terminal law \(\mu\) with reward moment \(U\), together with the usual
source-realizing sequence.  This law is a genuine source-attached law
cluster; it need not be attained by one behavioral profile.

Punishment normality strengthens every inequality in (2.1) to strict
inequality through the checked minimum-fibre isolation theorem.  In that
branch the abstract alternative can be made support-source-attached, but only
in the following precise limited sense.

Let \(\mu\) be a terminal reward-moment law and write

\[
 U=\mathbb E_\mu\bar r.
\]

Assume

\[
 U_k>s_k\qquad(k\in I).
\tag{2.3}
\]

This is available for every point of the positive global minimum-debt fibre
in the Fin4 punishment-normal branch: the checked minimum-fibre isolation
theorem even supplies one uniform positive gap.

### Proposition 2.1 (same-law-support compression)

For every pair \(J=\{i,j\}\), there is a law \(\nu_J\) supported on at most
two members of \(\mu\)'s positive support such that

\[
 \mathbb E_{\nu_J}\bar r_i>s_i,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\nu_J}\bar r_j>s_j.
\tag{2.4}
\]

### Proof

The strictly positive vector

\[
 (U_i-s_i,U_j-s_j)
 =\sum_{\omega\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}
    \mu(\omega)v_\omega
\]

lies in the cone generated by the projected surplus vectors belonging to the
positive support of \(\mu\).  Conic Caratheodory in \(\mathbb R^2\)
represents this same vector as
a nonnegative combination of at most two such generators.  Normalize the new
coefficients by their positive sum.  The resulting expectation is a positive
scalar multiple of \((U_i-s_i,U_j-s_j)\), hence both coordinates remain
strictly positive.  QED.

This is stronger than selecting two arbitrary reward-table outcomes.  Every
selected outcome is a positive atom of one fixed minimum law and therefore
has the project's usual causal-source provenance.

It is also much weaker than retaining the minimum source:

* the new coefficients need not equal the two atoms' relative \(\mu\)-masses;
* \(\nu_J\) need not be a sublaw or a conditional law produced by the source;
* its reward moment is generally not \(U\);
* no best-response cap is transported from the minimum point to \(\nu_J\); and
* the two atoms may occur at unrelated dates and need not form one product
  root or executable finite block.

The six pair laws \(\nu_J\) are also selected independently.  Their quantifiers
are

\[
 \forall J\in\binom I2\;\exists\nu_J,
\tag{2.5}
\]

not the existence of one law, root, or chronology solving all pairs.

Since the outcome space is finite, the two selected atoms have fixed positive
\(\mu\)-mass.  One may therefore retain one common realizing subsequence on
which both atom masses stay positive.  This is still not a realization of
\(\nu_J\): the atoms may occur at different dates, and conic compression has
replaced their relative probabilities.  The existing minimum-law atlas can
launch from either atom separately.  No current consumer uses only their
pairwise reward relation plus common limiting-law provenance.

## 3. What the hard residual adds, and what it does not

Punishment normality gives

\[
 \chi_k\leq s_k.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Hence (2.4) makes each selected pair law individually rational for its two
displayed coordinates.  It does not give obedience.  A player can alter the
terminal coalition immediately, before a punishment suffix can act.

The minimum-fibre singleton gap gives (2.3) for the original minimum law.  It
does not identify the reweighted two-atom moment with a point of the terminal
semantic carrier, nor does it attach the minimum cap to that moment.

This matters because the checked pair-base constructions require different
data.

* `nonempty_finFourPairBaseStationaryDebtLocalization` already constructs an
  actual induced-Nash stationary source for **every prescribed pair base**
  from a terminal exploitability witness.  The dual certificate is therefore
  unnecessary merely to select a pair.
* `nonempty_finFourPairBasePaidCapSemanticDispatch` similarly gives the paid
  cap port on every prescribed pair, but explicitly stops before a restart or
  uniform-payoff consumer.
* The stronger two-debtor handoff requires an adjacent full-gap outsider join

  \[
   r_k(P)+\gamma\leq r_k(P\cup\{k\}),
  \tag{3.2}
  \]

  for the same pair base \(P\).  Neither (1.3) nor (2.4) compares adjacent
  coalitions and neither says that either selected terminal outcome equals
  \(P\).
* A stationary or finite-block consumer needs product realization and exact
  Nash--Bellman conditions against an actual continuation.  A projected
  coordinatewise-improvement inequality supplies neither.

Thus the pair duality does not currently strengthen the pair-base semantic
dispatch.  Its genuinely new datum is only that one can choose at most two
positive minimum-law atoms whose **reweighted reward moment** is good for any
prescribed pair of payoff coordinates.

## 4. Regression I: the two outcomes need not be behavioral

Take players \(0,1,2,3\), put \(s_0=s_1=0\), and consider the two disjoint
coalitions

\[
 A=\{0,2\},\qquad B=\{1,3\}.
\]

Set the projected rewards of players \(0,1\) to

\[
 (r_0(A),r_1(A))=(2,-1),
 \qquad
 (r_0(B),r_1(B))=(-1,2).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The half--half law has projected payoff \((1/2,1/2)>(0,0)\), and neither atom
works alone.  Its determinant is \(4>1\), as in (1.5).

This law is not the terminal law of any ordinary behavioral profile having
only \(A,B\) in its support.  Such a law has zero Never mass and zero
singleton mass.  At the first positive-absorption date of any realizing
profile, a singleton-free positive product root must have at least two sure
quitters, so absorption is certain there and every supported coalition
contains the same fixed pair.  The disjoint coalitions \(A,B\) do not.

This obstruction is about ordinary independent behavioral actions.  A public
lottery would realize the law, but the ordinary quitting game supplies no such
device.

## 5. Regression II: product realization, separation, and normality still do not give Nash

Let

\[
 A=\{2,3\},\qquad B=\{0,2,3\},
\]

and set all own singleton rewards to zero.  Prescribe

\[
 r(A)=(2,-1,1,1),
 \qquad
 r(B)=(-1,2,1,1).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Use the one-row product root in which players \(2,3\) Quit surely, player
\(0\) Quits with probability \(1/2\), and player \(1\) Continues surely.  Its
law is exactly half \(A\), half \(B\), and its prescribed payoff is

\[
 U=(1/2,1/2,1,1)>s=(0,0,0,0).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus this is an actual product realization, has strict all-player singleton
separation, and gives the crossed two-outcome certificate for \(\{0,1\}\).
Nevertheless it is not root Nash: player \(0\) gets \(2\) by Continuing and
\(-1\) by Quitting, whereas the prescribed mixture gives \(1/2\).

Punishment normality can be imposed simultaneously without changing (5.1).
For player \(0\), give both coalitions \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{0,1,2\}\) payoff
\(-3\) in coordinate \(0\).  Do the analogous construction using

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{player}&\text{opponents' sure pair}\\ \hline
0&\{1,2\}\\
1&\{0,2\}\\
2&\{0,1\}\\
3&\{0,1\}
\end{array}
\tag{5.3}
\]

and set that player's reward to \(-3\) both before and after they join the
displayed sure pair.  Set every still unspecified reward coordinate to zero.
The coordinate assignments are compatible with one another and with (5.1).
Against the corresponding two sure-quitting
opponents, every behavioral strategy of the punished player receives
\(-3\).  Therefore

\[
 \chi_i\leq-3\leq s_i
\qquad(i=0,1,2,3).
\tag{5.4}
\]

So even an actual product law, strict numerical singleton separation, and
punishment normality do not turn the projected coordinatewise-improvement
certificate into a Nash root or an LCP solution.

This table is not claimed to have positive global minimum debt.  The example
tests exactly the local fields under discussion; imposing the complete hard
residual would amount to adding the still-unresolved global counterexample
hypothesis.

## 6. Regression III: all six pair certificates do not create a pair-base join sign

There is also one exact table carrying all six pair certificates at once.
Again set \(s_i=0\).  Define rewards by coalition cardinality:

* at a singleton \(\{j\}\), player \(j\) gets \(0\) and every outsider gets
  \(2\);
* at a pair \(P\), its two members get \(1\) and its two outsiders get
  \(-2\);
* at every coalition of cardinality at least three, every player gets \(-2\).

For every player pair \(J\), the pure terminal law on \(J\) itself satisfies

\[
 r_i(J)=1>s_i
 \qquad(i\in J),
\tag{6.1}
\]

so the pair certificate even has support one and is an actual product law.
All players are punishment-normal: to punish \(i\), choose any two opponents
to Quit surely.  Player \(i\) then receives \(-2\) whether they Quit or
Continue, hence \(\chi_i\leq-2\leq s_i\).

But no selected pure pair is Nash.  Either member can Continue, leave the
other member as the unique quitter, and increase their payoff from \(1\) to
\(2\).  At the same time, for every pair base \(P\) and outsider \(k\),

\[
 r_k(P\cup\{k\})-r_k(P)=(-2)-(-2)=0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus the full-gap join premise (3.2) fails for every pair and outsider.

One fixed actual source can also carry strict singleton separation and all
the atoms needed for the pair certificates.  At dates \(0,1,2\), let players
\(0,1,2\), respectively, be the only possible quitter with conditional Quit
probability \(1/2\); if all three survive, let player \(3\) Quit surely at
date \(3\).  The singleton law is

\[
 \mu(\{0\})=\tfrac12,
 \quad \mu(\{1\})=\tfrac14,
 \quad \mu(\{2\})=\tfrac18,
 \quad \mu(\{3\})=\tfrac18.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Its payoff is

\[
 U=(1,3/2,7/4,7/4)>s.
\tag{6.4}
\]

For every pair \(J\), choose a singleton \(\{k\}\) with \(k\notin J\).  It is
a positive atom of this same source law and pays both players in \(J\) the
amount \(2>s_i\).  Hence the pair certificates can be source-supported in one
literal chronology and still do not provide a pair-base join sign or make
the pure pair roots Nash.

This game has exact equilibria and therefore \(D_*=0\).  Indeed, let one player
\(j\) Quit surely at date zero, let all outsiders Continue there, and use an
all-Continue tail.  Player \(j\) gets \(0\) from Quit and \(0\) from deviating
to Continue and reaching Never.  Every outsider gets \(2\) from Continue and
only \(1\) from joining \(j\).  Since \(j\)'s prescribed Quit ends the game at
date zero, these two endpoint comparisons cover every behavioral deviation.
It is not a Fin4
counterexample.  Its role is to prove that the conjunction of the local
fields---six source-supported pair certificates, strict prescribed singleton
gaps, and punishment normality---does not imply the missing strategic or
adjacent-toggle fields.  Any successful theorem must use positive global
minimality, the terminal exploitability witness, or additional chronological
structure in a way not encoded by the pair certificates themselves.

## 7. Conclusion

Failure of every supported social chamber gives a sharp finite static normal
form:

\[
 \boxed{
 \forall J\in\binom{\mathrm{Fin}\,4}{2},\quad
 \exists\nu_J,\qquad
 |\operatorname{supp}\nu_J|\leq2,\qquad
 \mathbb E_{\nu_J}\bar r|_J\geq s|_J,
 }
\]

with one strict coordinate.  In the punishment-normal positive-minimum
branch, the two outcomes may be chosen from the positive support of one fixed
minimum law, and both displayed inequalities may be made strict.

That strengthening is mathematically genuine but does not feed a current
stationary, LCP, or finite-block consumer.  It provides positive source atoms,
not a source-preserving reweighting; absolute terminal surplus, not adjacent
own-action complementarity; and pairwise individual rationality, not
obedience.

The first additional hypotheses that would change this conclusion are:

1. the two outcomes and weights are realized by one actual source-matched
   product block;
2. every player's endpoint action in that block is Nash against the retained
   continuation cap; and
3. the block or its exact semantic endpoint returns to the minimum source
   with the cap/seam estimates required by an existing consumer.

Regression II shows that item 1, even with singleton separation and
punishment normality, is not enough.  The pair duality supplies none of items
2--3.
