## The completion atlas is a transition system, not a DAG

The checked six-leaf object is an **entrance atlas**: it gives a finite
source-preserving classification of the first producer.  The downstream
completion problem is not presently a DAG.  It contains actual source-return
edges, and its correct maintained form is a finite directed transition system.

An edge is admitted only when its output literally carries the fields required
by the target node.  Equality of a semantic pair, reuse of the same reward
table, or matching labels does not create an edge.  Each edge is classified as:

- `terminal`: reaches terminal approximants, a compiled return, or a uniform
  payoff;
- `rank`: carries a renewable well-founded rank decrease together with the
  backward compiler;
- `transition`: preserves enough source data to continue but has no proved
  rank decrease; or
- `open`: the desired source adapter or consumer has not been proved.

Only proved typed edges may be used when computing strongly connected
components.  A suggested regeneration that has not retained its input
passport is not a return edge.

## Logical overlap of leaf predicates

The inductive constructors tag the witness selected by the coverage proof;
they do not partition reward tables.

- For one fixed selected atom, Leaf 1 is incompatible with the nonsingleton
  construction used for Leaves 2--6.  A minimum law may nevertheless contain
  both singleton and nonsingleton positive atoms, and a different selection
  can expose another leaf.
- The high-tail proof branch is chosen whenever high rows occur frequently.
  It may still contain low rows.  Thus existence of a tail-escape subsequence
  does not logically exclude existence of low-tail leaf data elsewhere.
- For one fixed deterministic purification/orbit run, stopping during
  purification, reaching a terminal singleton, and closing a cycle are
  alternative outcomes.  Different best-endpoint choices or starting rows
  may yield different outcomes on the same table.
- One exact cycle cannot have both a common host and complementary vertices.
  The checked interfaces do not rule out one game, or even one low-tail
  source with different endpoint choices, having different cycles of both
  kinds.
