## 2. Exact obstruction to reusing or merely retiming the source

Suppose 0<z_i<1. Write the same p as a timing block, replacing its private
bonus game by PUBLIC all-Continue payoff u. Its supported finite actions
still have value u_i. Its pure Continue value is now

    W_i+D_i u_i=u_i+α_i,      α_i=D_i u_i−ξ_i.

Its prescribed block value is u_i+z_i α_i. Consequently its EXACT
regret within the original N-date block menu is

    (1−z_i)[α_i]_+ + z_i[−α_i]_+.                      (1)

In particular, the same source is block Nash only if

    D_i u_i=ξ_i                                     (0<z_i<1). (2)

This is a support equality, not merely a bound on an omitted response.
It follows directly from the finite/Continue endpoints; no division by
a small marginal mass is involved. A common strictly increasing retiming
of all source dates preserves the displayed supported-action values,
Never masses, and Continue response. It therefore cannot repair a failure
of (2). Extra empty dates may add further deviations, not remove this one.
Changing players' relative order or probabilities is not covered by this
retiming observation.

For a mixed pivot at a putative positive near-minimum, D_0≥E stays bounded
below, ξ_0→0, and u_0≥1−o(1). Thus α_0 is bounded strictly away from zero.
If also its finite mass 1−z_0 has a positive lower bound, (1) gives a
positive block-regret floor. Even without that last mass bound, exact
same-source block Nash fails as long as the pivot has positive finite mass.

For a proper pivot z_0=0, its original menu cap is u_0=U_0. Its new
Continue payoff exceeds the prescribed block payoff by

    W_0+D_0u_0−u_0=E+D_0(u_0−1).                       (3)

This is again positive near the above limiting regime. None of these
same-profile obstructions rules out a jointly chosen different block.

## 4. Every whole block is inactive throughout the strict chamber

For this same raw table H, the following stronger statement holds:

    If v_0>1 and v_1,v_2,v_3>0, every exact finite timing-game Nash
    profile with PUBLIC all-Continue payoff v is all Continue.       (5)

The proof explicitly handles off-path commitments, so it is a statement
about all normal-form block Nash laws, not an assumed backward selection.

First, at any reached date, the conditional finite timing laws are Nash.
To justify their use inductively, initially the date is reached surely;
if the current joint Continue probability is positive, the next date is
reached with positive probability. A conditional profitable deviation can
be copied after this prefix and gives its conditional gain multiplied by
the positive joint prefix reach. Conditioning each independent marginal
on its own survival preserves independence. The boundary payoff remains v.

At such a date player 3 cannot Quit with positive conditional probability.
Its Quit-now response pays zero, whereas the response Never through the
entire remaining block pays either one or v_3, hence at least min(1,v_3)>0.
No active player can Quit surely either. For example, q_0=1 makes player
1 strictly prefer Continue: it gets two instead of one. Hence q_1=0.
Then player 2 prefers Quit, getting zero instead of −1, so q_2=1.
But this makes player 0 prefer Continue, getting three instead of two,
contradicting q_0=1. The two other cyclic cases give the same contradiction.
These comparisons do not depend on any suffix, because another named
player quits surely at the current date. Thus all active q_i<1, and the
joint Continue probability is positive. Induction establishes literal
reach and conditional Nash at every block date before any backward step.

It remains to calculate the one-stage Nash roots at continuation v. Set
a_i=1−q_i for i<3; player 3 has already been excluded. The three Quit
minus Continue gaps are

    Δ_0=1−2q_2−a_1a_2 v_0,
    Δ_1=1−2q_0−a_0a_2(1+v_1),
    Δ_2=1−2q_1−a_0a_1(1+v_2).

Sure hazards were excluded above. If q_0=0, then
Δ_2=a_1(1−v_2)−1<0, forcing q_2=0, and then Δ_1=−v_1<0 forces q_1=0.
If q_1=0, Δ_0=a_2(2−v_0)−1<0 forces q_0=0, reducing to the first case.
If q_2=0, Δ_1=a_0(1−v_1)−1<0 forces q_1=0. The strict inequalities
hold also when a parenthesized coefficient is negative.

If every active hazard is positive, every one is strictly below one and
the three gaps vanish. Rearranging and using 2−1/a≤a for 0<a≤1 gives

    v_0 a_1       =2−1/a_2≤a_2,
    (1+v_1)a_2    =2−1/a_0≤a_0,
    (1+v_2)a_0    =2−1/a_1≤a_1.

Multiplication and cancellation of the positive a_0a_1a_2 imply
v_0(1+v_1)(1+v_2)≤1, contradicting the strict chamber assumptions.
The sole root is therefore all Continue. At the final reached block date
its continuation is v, so its root is all Continue. Its payoff stays v;
backward induction now applies to the preceding reached date. This proves
(5) for the entire finite normal-form block, including all private laws.

Applying (5) to the ACTUAL normalized u in Section 3 shows that no exact
whole-block reselection at this continuation activates any nonpivot. It
is stronger than saying that the displayed source fails the same-profile
test (2): arbitrarily long and completely different block laws do not help.
