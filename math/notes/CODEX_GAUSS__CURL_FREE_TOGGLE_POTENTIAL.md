### 1. Exact discrete potential theorem

Write `S+i` for `S union {i}` when `i notin S`.  Call a function
`Phi : 2^I -> R` an exact player-toggle potential if

`Phi(S+i)-Phi(S) = g_i(S) = w_i(S+i)-w_i(S)`

for every `i notin S`.

**Proposition 1 (ordinary mathematics, not checked in Lean).**  The following
are equivalent:

1. the square-curl identities in the exact question hold; and
2. an exact player-toggle potential exists.

When they hold, one may normalize `Phi(empty)=0` and define `Phi(S)` by taking
any ordering `i_1,...,i_k` of `S` and setting

`Phi(S) = sum_(m=1)^k g_(i_m)({i_1,...,i_(m-1)}).`

**Proof, sufficiency.**  Any two orderings of a finite set are connected by
adjacent transpositions.  Suppose two adjacent new elements `i,j` follow the
same prefix `A`.  Their contribution before the swap is

`g_i(A)+g_j(A+i)`,

and after the swap it is

`g_j(A)+g_i(A+j)`.

The square identity says these are equal.  Later prefix sets are unchanged,
so every adjacent swap preserves the displayed sum.  Thus `Phi` is
well-defined.  To compute `Phi(S+i)`, order `S` first and put `i` last.  The
definition gives `Phi(S+i)=Phi(S)+g_i(S)`, as required.

**Proof, necessity.**  If an exact `Phi` exists, compute
`Phi(S+i+j)-Phi(S)` along the two length-two paths which add `i` then `j`, or
`j` then `i`.  Equality of the endpoints is exactly the square-curl identity.
This also proves uniqueness after fixing `Phi(empty)=0`, because every
coalition is reached from the empty coalition by adding its elements.  QED.

The result is a finite discrete Poincare lemma.  It is an exact
characterization of this scalar-potential adapter, not merely a sufficient
calculation.

### 2. Direct semantic consequence

**Proposition 2 (ordinary mathematics plus named checked consumer).**  Every
finite quitting table satisfying the square-curl identities has a pure
stationary uniform-equilibrium payoff against unrestricted behavioral
deviations.

**Proof.**  Let `S*` maximize `Phi` on the finite coalition cube.  If `i` is
outside `S*`, maximality and Proposition 1 give

`w_i(S*+i)-w_i(S*) = Phi(S*+i)-Phi(S*) <= 0`.

If `i` belongs to `S*`, apply the same identity at `S*-i` and reverse it:

`w_i(S*-i)-w_i(S*) = Phi(S*-i)-Phi(S*) <= 0`.

These are precisely the outsider and member inequalities in
`IsQuittingSureExitSet`.  Therefore `S*` is a sure exit set.  The checked
theorem `isUniformEquilibriumPayoff_setReward_of_isQuittingSureExitSet`
consumes it and says that `w(S*)` is a uniform-equilibrium payoff.  That
consumer already covers every unilateral behavioral deviation and all long
finite horizons.  QED.

Equivalently, a real exact potential can be converted to the natural-valued
ordinal potential expected by `HasQuittingToggleOrdinalPotential`: rank the
finitely many values of `Phi`.  A strict reward improvement is an equal strict
increase of `Phi`, hence a strict increase of its rank.  This identifies the
new content here as an actual-table adapter for the checked supplied-potential
consumer.

### 3. Pairwise-interaction actual-data subclass

A convenient concrete class is obtained from coefficients `a_i` and symmetric
pair coefficients `b_ij=b_ji`.  Suppose the actual reward table satisfies

`g_i(S)=a_i + sum_(j in S) b_ij`

for every `i notin S`.  Then the square identity holds because both two-step
sums equal

`a_i+a_j + sum_(k in S)(b_ik+b_jk) + b_ij`.

The exact potential is the finite Ising-type energy

`Phi(S)=sum_(i in S)a_i + sum_({i,j} subset S) b_ij`,

where the second sum is over unordered pairs.  Proposition 2 therefore gives
a pure uniform-equilibrium payoff for every such table, in any finite player
dimension.  This uses actual coefficients extracted from the own-membership
differences; rewards to a player at coalitions on the same side of that
player's membership may otherwise be arbitrary subject to the displayed
differences.

For an exact three-player check, take `a_A=a_B=1`, `a_C=-1` and
`b_AB=-3`, `b_AC=b_BC=2`.  One maximizing coalition is `{A,C}`, with
`Phi({A,C})=2`.  In the representative realization where outsiders receive
zero and a member `i` receives `a_i + sum_(j in S-{i}) b_ij`, its payoff row is
`(3,0,1)`.  Member removal gives each member zero, while outsider `B` receives
`a_B+b_BA+b_BC=0` on joining, equal to its current zero.  Thus the sure-exit
inequalities can also be checked directly.

### 4. Curl-freeness is not necessary for a pure solution

For two players `A,B`, take

- `r({A})=(1,0)`;
- `r({B})=(0,0)`; and
- `r({A,B})=(5,0)`.

The coalition `{A}` is a sure exit set: `A` gets `1` rather than `0` by
remaining a quitter, and `B` gets `0` whether or not it joins.  Hence the
checked sure-exit consumer gives a pure uniform payoff.

But the sole square identity fails.  Its add-`A`-then-`B` side is
`1+(0-0)=1`, while its add-`B`-then-`A` side is `0+(5-0)=5`.  Thus
Proposition 1 is sharp for exact potentials, not for the existence of a pure
sure-exit set.

### 5. No directed square is not enough: an exact three-player boundary

Orient a coalition-cube edge toward the endpoint which gives the toggling
player strictly higher payoff.  It is tempting to weaken exact curl-freeness
to the local ordinal requirement that no square face is a directed cycle.
That implication is false already for three players.

Take players `A,B,C` and the exact table

- `r({A})=(1,0,0)`, `r({B})=(0,1,0)`, `r({C})=(0,0,1)`;
- `r({A,B})=(-1,1,0)`;
- `r({A,C})=(1,0,-1)`;
- `r({B,C})=(0,-1,1)`; and
- `r({A,B,C})=(-1,-1,-1)`.

Every absent player's payoff is zero, while every present player's payoff is
`+1` or `-1`, so all toggle comparisons are strict.  The oriented edges are

`empty -> A`, `empty -> B`, `empty -> C`,

`A -> AB`, `AC -> A`, `AB -> B`, `B -> BC`, `ABC -> AB`,

`C -> AC`, `BC -> C`, `ABC -> AC`, and `ABC -> BC`.

On each of the six square faces these arrows form a transitive diamond:

- on `C=0`: `empty -> A -> AB -> B` and `empty -> B`;
- on `C=1`: `ABC -> BC -> C -> AC` and `ABC -> AC`;
- on `B=0`: `empty -> C -> AC -> A` and `empty -> A`;
- on `B=1`: `ABC -> AB -> B -> BC` and `ABC -> BC`;
- on `A=0`: `empty -> B -> BC -> C` and `empty -> C`; and
- on `A=1`: `ABC -> AC -> A -> AB` and `ABC -> AB`.

Hence no two-coordinate face contains a directed improvement cycle.  Yet the
whole cube has the directed six-cycle

`A -> AB -> B -> BC -> C -> AC -> A`.

More strongly, it has no sink at all: `empty` can add `A`; each singleton has
the outgoing edge shown in the six-cycle; each pair has the outgoing edge
shown there; and `ABC` can remove any player.  Therefore no coalition is a
sure exit set.  Since any orientation of own-membership edges can be realized
by choosing the corresponding present-player payoff to be `+1` or `-1` and
the absent-player payoff to be zero, this is a literal quitting reward table,
not merely an abstract graph.

This example draws a sharp local-to-global boundary for the pure route:
two-dimensional ordinal consistency on every face does not rule out a longer
profitable toggle cycle and does not produce the semantic endpoint.  It does
not refute uniform-equilibrium existence; in fact three-player quitting games
are already known to have uniform-equilibrium payoffs by a different route.

### 6. What remains beyond this checkpoint

The square-curl adapter covers all exact potential tables, and the last example
rules out replacing it by mere facewise absence of directed cycles.  The
checked ordinal-potential theorem indicates the exact global pure-strategy
condition: the strict-improvement digraph being acyclic permits a natural rank
potential.  But stating global acyclicity is essentially a supplied
certificate, not yet a revealing structural condition on reward data.

The next useful step is therefore not to repackage acyclicity.  It is to find
a broad sign, monotonicity, submodularity, or interaction condition on the
actual table which forbids the displayed six-cycle (and every higher cycle),
while not collapsing back to the exact square equalities.

### 7. Strategic complements give a broader monotone producer

The last question has a clean answer for increasing own-action differences.
Call the table **quit-complementary** if, for every player `i` and coalitions
`S subset T subset I-{i}`,

`g_i(S) <= g_i(T)`.

Thus the gain from joining the quitters weakly increases as more opponents
quit.  This is an actual finite family of reward inequalities.

**Proposition 3 (ordinary mathematics, not checked in Lean).**  Every finite
quit-complementary table has a pure sure-exit set and therefore a pure
stationary uniform-equilibrium payoff against unrestricted behavioral
deviations.

**Proof.**  Start at `S_0=empty`.  If some outsider `i` has `g_i(S_k)>0`, set
`S_(k+1)=S_k+i`; otherwise stop.  Each step strictly increases cardinality, so
the procedure stops after at most `|I|` additions at a coalition `S*`.

Every outsider at termination has `g_i(S*)<=0`, exactly its no-join
inequality.  If `i` belongs to `S*`, let `A` be the coalition immediately
before the step which added `i`.  At that step `g_i(A)>0`.  No player is ever
removed, so `A subset S*-i`.  Quit complementarity gives

`g_i(S*-i) >= g_i(A) > 0`.

Equivalently `w_i(S*-i)<=w_i(S*)`, the member's no-leave inequality.  Hence
`S*` is a sure exit set.  Apply
`isUniformEquilibriumPayoff_setReward_of_isQuittingSureExitSet`.  QED.

The proof is constructive and requires neither exact curl nor a supplied
ordinal potential.  The two-player table in Section 4 is quit-complementary:
`A`'s gain rises from `1` to `5` and `B`'s remains zero, although its square
curl is nonzero.  Conversely, the exact-potential table

`r({A})=(1,0)`, `r({B})=(0,1)`, `r({A,B})=(-1,-1)`

has both join gains fall from `1` to `-1`; it is curl-free but not
quit-complementary.  Thus the two direct classes are genuinely incomparable.

### 8. The substitutes analogue fails sharply

If all the inequalities in quit complementarity are reversed, quit actions
are strategic substitutes.  The three-player table in Section 5 satisfies
this condition.  Indeed, index the players cyclically and let `succ(A)=B`,
`succ(B)=C`, `succ(C)=A`.  Its gain has the exact formula

`g_i(S)=1` if `succ(i) notin S`, and `g_i(S)=-1` if `succ(i) in S`.

This is nonincreasing in `S`.  But a sure exit coalition would have to satisfy

`i in S` if and only if `succ(i) notin S`.

The forward implication follows from the member inequality (a present player
cannot have gain `-1`); the reverse implication follows from the outsider
inequality (an absent player cannot have gain `+1`).  Alternating membership
around the odd three-cycle is impossible.  Thus strategic substitutes alone
do not produce even a pure sure-exit set.  This rederives the no-sink audit of
Section 5 conceptually and shows exactly why the monotone-addition proof needs
the complement direction.

### 9. Current conjecture-facing boundary

Proposition 3 gives an actual-data adapter for an arbitrary-player special
class and reaches a checked semantic consumer.  It is broader than the exact
potential adapter but still a pure-strategy class, so it does not touch tables
with cyclic substitutability such as Section 5.  That table itself has only
three players and therefore has a uniform payoff by the separate checked
three-player closure; its role here is to falsify a purported pure producer,
not to suggest nonexistence.

A possible next extension is block monotonicity.  Build the directed influence
graph with an arrow `j -> i` when adding `j` can decrease `g_i`.  Within a
strongly connected negative-influence block the substitutes obstruction may
require a non-pure continuation, while an acyclic condensation might allow
blocks to be solved and frozen successively.  This is conjectural: no theorem
below is claimed until the effect of freezing a block on earlier no-deviation
inequalities is controlled.

### 10. Action-flipped complements and balanced signed interactions

There is one rigorous extension before the conjectural block question.  Fix a
**polarity coalition** `P subset I` and let

`tau_P(T)=T symmetric_difference P`.

Think of membership in `T` as a relabelled binary action: for players in `P`,
transformed action one means original Continue; for players outside `P`, it
means original Quit.  Define the transformed own-action gain, for `i notin T`,
by

`h_i^P(T)=w_i(tau_P(T+i))-w_i(tau_P(T))`.

Call the table **polarity-complementary** if some `P` makes every `h_i^P`
nondecreasing under inclusion of the other transformed actions.

**Proposition 4 (ordinary mathematics, not checked in Lean).**  Every finite
polarity-complementary quitting table has a pure sure-exit set and hence a pure
stationary uniform-equilibrium payoff.

**Proof.**  Apply the monotone-addition algorithm of Proposition 3 in the
`T` coordinates, using `h^P` in place of `g`.  It terminates at `T*` and gives

`w_i(tau_P(T*)) >= w_i(tau_P(T* symmetric_difference {i}))`

for every player `i`: this is exactly what the transformed outsider/member
inequalities say.  Symmetric difference commutes with toggling one coordinate,
so for `S*=tau_P(T*)` the right-hand coalition is `S* symmetric_difference
{i}`.  Hence no player gains by toggling original Quit/Continue membership at
`S*`; unpacking membership gives the two `IsQuittingSureExitSet` inequalities.
The checked sure-exit consumer finishes.  QED.

This argument does not pretend that the transformed all-zero profile is
nonabsorption: it may be the original coalition `P` and have nonzero payoff.
Only the finite binary normal-form comparison is relabelled.  The final
coalition and the semantic consumer are in the original quitting game.

For a transparent coefficient test, suppose

`g_i(S)=a_i + sum_(j in S)b_ij`

with no symmetry assumption on `b`.  Choose signs `epsilon_i in {+1,-1}` and
put `i in P` exactly when `epsilon_i=-1`.  Writing original membership as

`x_j=(1-epsilon_j)/2 + epsilon_j y_j`,

the coefficient of transformed action `y_j` in `h_i^P` is exactly

`epsilon_i epsilon_j b_ij`.

Therefore the finite sign inequalities

`epsilon_i epsilon_j b_ij >= 0` for all distinct `i,j`

are an actual-data adapter to Proposition 4.  They require no symmetry
`b_ij=b_ji`, unlike the exact-potential condition.  In signed-graph language,
all interaction signs can be made positive by switching vertex signs.

The odd substitutes table in Section 5 has negative coefficients on
`A -> B -> C -> A` (in the influence convention, each successor negatively
affects its predecessor).  Making every transformed coefficient nonnegative
would require

`epsilon_A epsilon_B = epsilon_B epsilon_C = epsilon_C epsilon_A = -1`.

Multiplying these three equations gives `1=-1`, impossible.  Thus its directed
six-cycle is also the exact odd-sign obstruction to the action-flipped
complementarity adapter.  By contrast, an all-negative interaction graph which
is bipartite is covered by taking opposite polarities on its two parts.

Proposition 4 is still a special-case pure producer.  It enlarges the positive
class in a structural, actual-data way but does not resolve an unbalanced
negative-influence component; that is the current boundary of this notebook.

### 11. Exact stationary repair of every one-blocker permutation

The unbalanced odd cycle is outside every pure producer above, but its reward
structure permits an explicit mixed stationary solution.  The construction
extends to any finite dimension.

Let `I` be a nonempty finite player set, let `pi : I -> I` be a permutation
with no fixed point, and choose constants `A_i>0`, `B_i>0`.  Define the reward
of every nonempty quitting coalition `S` by

`r(S)_i = 0` if `i notin S`,

`r(S)_i = A_i` if `i in S` and `pi(i) notin S`, and

`r(S)_i = -B_i` if `i in S` and `pi(i) in S`.

Thus a quitting player is rewarded when its designated blocker continues and
is charged when that blocker also quits; a continuing player always receives
zero.  Put

`p_(pi(i)) = A_i/(A_i+B_i)`.

Because `pi` is a permutation, this defines one quit probability `p_j` for
every player.  Positivity of `A_i,B_i` gives `0<p_j<1`.

**Proposition 5 (ordinary mathematics, not checked in Lean).**  The stationary
profile in which players quit independently with probabilities `p_j` is an
exact terminal Nash profile against every behavioral deviation, and zero is a
uniform-equilibrium payoff.

**Proof.**  Use continuation target `v=0`.  If player `i` chooses pure Quit at
one stationary root, its reward depends only on the independent action of
`pi(i)`.  Its endpoint value is exactly

`A_i(1-p_(pi(i))) - B_i p_(pi(i)) = 0`

by the definition of `p_(pi(i))`.  If player `i` chooses pure Continue, every
absorbing coalition omits `i` and pays it zero, while the all-Continue event
returns continuation value zero.  Its Continue endpoint is therefore also
zero.  Both endpoints equal `v_i`, so the product root is exact endpoint Nash.
The prescribed mixed endpoint is a convex combination of these two zeros;
therefore its one-step successor payoff is also zero, giving the required
fixed-point identity `0=quittingRootSuccessorPayoff(r,0,p)`.

The root jointly absorbs with positive probability because every `p_j` lies
strictly between zero and one.  More strongly, for each `i` the distinct
opponent `pi(i)` quits with positive probability, so the probability that all
opponents of `i` Continue is at most `1-p_(pi(i))<1`.  Thus the playerwise
contraction hypothesis holds.  The checked theorem
`isUniformEquilibriumPayoff_of_stationaryEndpointCertificate_contracts`
now yields that zero is a uniform-equilibrium payoff, with all behavioral
deviations covered.  The checked theorem
`isZeroAsymptoticNash_stationary_of_fixedPoint_endpointNash_contracts` in the
same file separately identifies the stationary profile as an exact terminal
Nash profile.  QED.

For `A_i=B_i=1` and `pi(A)=B`, `pi(B)=C`, `pi(C)=A`, this is exactly the
three-player table in Section 5 and gives `p_A=p_B=p_C=1/2`.  All three-player
instances already have existence-level coverage through
`quittingGame_exists_uniformEquilibriumPayoff_of_card_eq_three`
(`UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Classification/PlayerReindex.lean`).  Likewise,
the uniform five-cycle with `A_i=B_i=1` is an instance of `colliderReward` and
already has existence-level coverage through
`exists_uniformEquilibriumPayoff_colliderReward`
(`UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Classification/Circulant/TerminalExploitabilityColliderClosure.lean`).
Proposition 5 gives a simpler explicit stationary zero certificate for those
overlapping examples, not a new existence result there.

The formula also works on odd cycles of length seven and on arbitrary disjoint
cycle decompositions, with player-dependent positive `A_i,B_i`.  A narrow
repository and Literature search, independently repeated in Cedar's review,
found no declaration covering that full arbitrary finite fixed-point-free
permutation family or its coordinatewise stationary formula.  The FTV table in
`Literature/FleschThuijsmanAndVrieze1997.lean` is different: continuing players
can receive nonzero rewards and its cited example has no exact stationary
equilibrium.  Thus current novelty is claimed only for the general family and
explicit adapter, not for the three-player or uniform five-cycle existence
consequences.  The semantic compilation itself is already checked in Lean.

### 12. Toward several blockers

Suppose instead a quitter's payoff is additive in the blockers who also quit,
so its pure-Quit endpoint against stationary probabilities has the form

`c_i + sum_(j != i) b_ij p_j`,

while a continuer still receives zero.  An interior zero-payoff stationary
certificate is then exactly a solution of the linear system

`c+Bp=0`, with `0<p_i<1` for every `i`.

Whenever such a vector exists, the proof of Proposition 5 applies verbatim:
both endpoints equal zero, the root is a fixed point, and strict interiority
gives joint and playerwise opponent contraction (for at least two players).
The stationary endpoint compiler then yields zero as a uniform payoff.

This is an exact supplied-vector verifier, not yet an actual-data producer.
The one-blocker permutation makes `B` a scaled permutation matrix and supplies
`p` coordinatewise.  The next conjecture-facing task is to find a structural
matrix class for which an interior solution is forced, or an exact small
counterexample to a proposed class.  Merely invoking linear feasibility would
repackage the certificate rather than produce it.

### 13. Robust blocker-switch theorem via Poincare--Miranda

The exact one-blocker formula is not necessary.  Retain a nonempty finite
player set and a fixed-point-free permutation `pi`.  Assume the raw reward
table has two properties.

1. **Passive continuers:** `r(S)_i=0` whenever `i notin S`.
2. **Weak uniform blocker switch:** for every `i` and every
   `T subset I-{i,pi(i)}`,

   `r(T union {i})_i >= 0` and
   `r(T union {i,pi(i)})_i <= 0`.

The first condition makes Continue worth zero at target zero.  The second says
that if `i` Quits, its payoff is nonnegative whenever its designated blocker
continues and nonpositive whenever that blocker also Quits, uniformly over
every coalition of the remaining players.  Other quitters may affect the
magnitude arbitrarily; no additivity, symmetry, or monotonicity is assumed.

**Proposition 6 (ordinary mathematics, not checked in Lean).**  Every table
satisfying passive continuers and a weak uniform blocker switch has a
stationary exact terminal Nash profile with payoff zero.  Consequently zero is
a uniform-equilibrium payoff against unrestricted behavioral deviations.  If
all blocker-switch inequalities are strict, the stationary root can be chosen
fully interior.

**Proof.**  For a vector `p in [0,1]^I`, let `Q_i(p)` be player `i`'s expected
one-root payoff from choosing pure Quit when the other players quit
independently with probabilities `p_j`.  This is a continuous multiaffine
function independent of `p_i`.

If `p_(pi(i))=0`, the blocker certainly continues.  The expectation defining
`Q_i` is a convex combination of nonnegative numbers, so `Q_i(p)>=0`.  If
`p_(pi(i))=1`, it is a convex combination of nonpositive numbers, so
`Q_i(p)<=0`.  Under strict blocker-switch inequalities, these two conclusions
are strict.

Reindex these equations by coordinates: for `j in I`, set

`F_j(p)=Q_(pi^{-1}(j))(p)`.

On the face `p_j=0`, `F_j>=0`; on the opposite face `p_j=1`, `F_j<=0`.
The Poincare--Miranda theorem therefore supplies `p in [0,1]^I` with
`F(p)=0`.  For completeness, this use follows directly from Brouwer: apply the
fixed-point theorem to the continuous cube self-map

`H_j(p)=clamp_[0,1](p_j+F_j(p))`.

At any fixed point, `F_j=0`: in the interior the clamp is inactive; at
`p_j=0`, the lower-face inequality and fixedness force `F_j=0`; and at
`p_j=1`, the upper-face inequality and fixedness again force `F_j=0`.  Under
strict blocker-switch inequalities a fixed point cannot lie on either face,
so the resulting root is interior.

At this interior root every player's pure Quit endpoint is `Q_i(p)=0`.
Every pure Continue endpoint is also zero: an absorbing coalition omits `i`
and pays zero by passive continuers, while joint Continue returns target zero.
Hence the root is exact endpoint Nash and zero is its successor fixed point.

If some `p_j>0`, the root jointly absorbs.  It also satisfies the stationary
boundary packet.  Indeed, if all opponents of player `i` Continue surely, then
the pure-Quit endpoint `Q_i(p)=0` is exactly its singleton reward
`r({i})_i`; hence `max(0,r({i})_i)=0`.  Applying
`isUniformEquilibriumPayoff_of_stationaryEndpointCertificate` proves the
uniform-payoff conclusion, and
`isZeroAsymptoticNash_stationary_iff_boundary_of_fixedPoint_endpointNash`
proves exact terminal Nash.

If instead `p` is the all-zero root, `Q_i(p)=0` says
`r({i})_i=0` for every player.  The empty coalition is then a sure exit set by
`isQuittingSureExitSet_empty_iff`, so the all-Continue stationary profile is
exact terminal Nash and zero is uniform by the sure-exit consumer.  This
covers every boundary case.  Under strict blocker-switch inequalities the
root is interior, and the shorter contracting stationary compiler used in
Proposition 5 applies.  QED.

The one-blocker-permutation family of Proposition 5 is a strict special case:
its two relevant row values are the constants `A_i` and `-B_i`.  Proposition 6
is robust under sufficiently small perturbations of every **quitter** entry
which preserve the two strict signs, while all continuer entries remain
exactly zero.  Thus the class is relatively open inside the
passive-continuers affine subspace, not open in the full space of reward
tables.  Within that subspace the perturbations may introduce high-order
coalition interactions which destroy additivity, exact potential, and full
player symmetry.

For the cyclic `+1/-1` table of Section 5, the Brouwer zero is the explicit
`p_i=1/2` root.  For a five- or seven-player directed blocker cycle,
Proposition 6 gives a relatively open set of asymmetric tables inside the
passive-continuers subspace, even though the odd sign product prevents
action-flipped strategic complements and the corresponding pure alternating
coalitions do not exist.  The uniform five-cycle center overlaps the checked
collider family as recorded above; arbitrary background-dependent quitter-row
perturbations do not visibly do so.

This is a genuine actual-data adapter rather than a supplied-root verifier:
the two finite families of weak raw reward inequalities themselves force the
root.  It remains a special class.  Dropping uniformity over the background
coalition destroys the cube-face sign used by Poincare--Miranda, and no
replacement averaged condition is proved here.

The nearest broad checked stationary classification is
`exists_stationaryUniformEquilibriumPayoff_or_standardQMatrixSide`
(`UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Classification/LCP/StationaryExistence.lean`).
It produces off a stated LCP matrix side and does not provide this raw
blocker-sign adapter or the displayed exact root.  The Solan--Solan (2020)
literature transcription uses the standing normalization of zero singleton
self-rewards, whereas the strict blocker-switch hypothesis gives every
singleton self-reward strictly positive.  No theorem or paper statement found
in the narrow search duplicates Proposition 6's finite raw-table criterion.

### 14. Quantitative dominant-blocker test with many interactions

For the additive class from Section 12, Proposition 6 has a transparent
coefficient adapter.  Suppose continuers receive zero and, when `i` Quits,

`r(S)_i=a_i + sum_(j in S-{i}) b_ij`.

Fix a derangement `pi`.  For a coalition omitting `pi(i)`, the minimum of
player `i`'s reward over every background `T` is

`a_i + sum_(j != i,pi(i)) min(0,b_ij)`.

For a coalition containing `pi(i)`, the maximum over every background is

`a_i+b_(i,pi(i)) + sum_(j != i,pi(i)) max(0,b_ij)`.

Therefore the finite coefficient inequalities

`a_i + sum_(j != i,pi(i)) min(0,b_ij) >= 0`,

`a_i+b_(i,pi(i)) + sum_(j != i,pi(i)) max(0,b_ij) <= 0`

for every `i` imply the weak uniform blocker switch and hence zero uniform
payoff.  Strict coefficient inequalities give a fully mixed stationary root.

This is a genuine several-interaction class.  The designated coefficient
`b_(i,pi(i))` must dominate the worst aggregate signed effect of the other
quitters, but those other effects may be positive or negative, asymmetric,
and supported on an arbitrary directed graph.  Proposition 5 is the case in
which all nondesigned coefficients vanish.

### 15. Endpoint signs alone do not force the stationary zero

The background-uniform hypotheses cannot be replaced by checking only the
singleton and grand-coalition signs.  Here is an exact three-player additive
counterexample to that proposed weakening.  Let players be `A,B,C`, continuers
receive zero, and let a quitter's payoff be

`q_A(S)=1-2*1_(B in S)`,

`q_B(S)=1-2*1_(C in S)`,

`q_C(S)=1-(1/5)*1_(A in S)-1_(B in S)`.

Use the blocker cycle `pi(A)=B`, `pi(B)=C`, `pi(C)=A`.  Every singleton
quitter gets `1>0`, while at the grand coalition the three quitter payoffs are
`-1,-1,-1/5`, all strictly negative.  Thus the tempting endpoint-only test
passes.

At an independent stationary root, however, the three pure-Quit endpoint
equations at target zero are

`Q_A=1-2p_B`, `Q_B=1-2p_C`, and
`Q_C=1-(1/5)p_A-p_B`.

The first two equations force `p_B=p_C=1/2`.  Substitution into the third
forces `p_A=5/2`, outside the probability cube.  Hence no stationary root can
make all Quit endpoints zero.  The failure occurs on an intermediate
background row: when `A` (the designated blocker of `C`) Quits but `B`
continues, `C` still receives `4/5>0` rather than a nonpositive payoff.  The
grand-coalition penalty arrives too late to give the required cube-face sign,
and the resulting coefficient balance points out of the cube.

This example refutes only the endpoint-only stationary-zero producer.  It has
three players, so the integrated three-player theorem still gives it some
uniform-equilibrium payoff.  It is not a counterexample to the quitting
conjecture or to a more flexible nonstationary construction.

### 16. Exact pure-versus-mixed boundary for strict blocker switches

Under the **strict** hypotheses of Proposition 6, the blocker permutation's
cycle parity exactly characterizes whether the table has a pure sure-exit set.

**Proposition 7 (ordinary mathematics, not checked in Lean).**  A coalition
`S` is a sure exit set if and only if

`i in S` exactly when `pi(i) notin S`

for every player `i`.  Consequently a sure exit set exists if and only if
every cycle of `pi` has even length.  If any cycle is odd, Proposition 6's
stationary equilibrium is genuinely mixed in the sense that no pure
stationary terminal Nash profile exists.

**Proof.**  Suppose first that `S` is sure.  If both `i` and `pi(i)` belong to
`S`, strict blocker negativity gives `r(S)_i<0`, while leaving makes `i` a
continuer and pays zero; this is a profitable departure, impossible.  Hence
`i in S` implies `pi(i) notin S`.  If neither belongs to `S`, joining gives
`i` a strictly positive blocker-absent reward instead of its current continuer
payoff zero; this is a profitable join, also impossible.  Thus `i notin S`
implies `pi(i) in S`, proving exact alternation.

Conversely, under exact alternation, every member's blocker is absent, so its
current reward is strictly positive and leaving pays zero.  Every outsider's
blocker is present, so joining would pay strictly negative instead of the
current zero.  Both sure-exit inequalities hold.

Alternating binary membership closes consistently around a permutation cycle
exactly when its length is even.  Choices on distinct cycles are independent,
which proves the existence characterization.  Finally, the checked theorem
`isεAsymptoticNash_pureSetRoot_iff_isQuittingSureExitSet` identifies pure
stationary exact terminal Nash profiles with sure exit sets.  QED.

This gives the blocker-switch route a sharp internal boundary.  Even cycles
already have pure zero/nonzero exit equilibria selected by alternation; odd
cycles force the topological mixed root.  It does not assert that every exact
terminal Nash profile of the game is stationary, only that none of the pure
stationary coalition profiles works on an odd component.

### 17. Exact extension from permutations to compatible blocker maps

For the background-independent family of Proposition 5, bijectivity of the
blocker assignment can be weakened sharply.  Let `b : I -> I` have no fixed
point, but do not assume it is injective or surjective.  Give quitter `i`
reward `A_i>0` when `b(i)` continues and `-B_i<0` when `b(i)` Quits, with
continuers receiving zero.  Define the desired blocker probability

`q_i=A_i/(A_i+B_i)`.

**Proposition 8 (ordinary mathematics, not checked in Lean).**  This table has
a fully mixed stationary zero-payoff equilibrium if and only if

`q_i=q_k` whenever `b(i)=b(k)`.

Under this compatibility condition, zero is a uniform-equilibrium payoff
against unrestricted behavioral deviations.  Moreover, a pure sure-exit set
exists if and only if every directed cycle of the functional graph of `b` has
even length.

**Proof.**  At any stationary product root, player `i`'s pure-Quit endpoint is

`A_i-(A_i+B_i)p_(b(i))`.

It vanishes exactly when `p_(b(i))=q_i`.  Hence simultaneous vanishing is
possible only if the `q_i` agree on every fiber of `b`.  Conversely, under
fiber compatibility, assign `p_j` the common `q_i` for `i in b^{-1}(j)` and
assign any number in `(0,1)` (say `1/2`) when that fiber is empty.  Every
coordinate is interior and every Quit endpoint is zero.  Continue endpoints
are zero by passive continuers.  Since `b(i) != i` is a positive-probability
quitting opponent, the stationary contracting compiler proves exact terminal
Nash and uniform payoff zero.  This proves the stationary characterization
and semantic conclusion.

For a pure sure exit coalition, the same strict-sign argument as Proposition 7
gives the Boolean equations

`1_(i in S)=1-1_(b(i) in S)`.

On each directed cycle these equations are consistent exactly for even cycle
length.  If every cycle is even, choose an alternating assignment on each
cycle and extend uniquely backward along every in-tree; this produces a
solution on the whole finite functional graph.  If one cycle is odd, no
solution exists.  QED.

This exact map theorem covers star-like blocker structures and arbitrary
in-trees feeding even or odd cycles.  The fiber compatibility is load-bearing:
if two players share one blocker but have different ratios `q_i`, their two
zero-indifference equations demand incompatible probabilities from the same
coordinate.  That is a failure of this stationary-zero architecture, not a
nonexistence result for the quitting game.

### 18. Removing passive continuers by an endogenous target

The passive-continuer hypothesis is not intrinsic to the topological
construction.  For a product vector `p`, write

`R_i(p)`

for player `i`'s expected terminal reward if `i` chooses Quit while the other
players use `p`.  Write

`W_i(p)`

for the *unconditional* reward contribution from outcomes in which `i`
chooses Continue and at least one opponent Quits, and set

`c_i(p)=product_(j != i) (1-p_j)`.

Whenever `c_i(p)<1`, define the normalized continuer value

`V_i(p)=W_i(p)/(1-c_i(p))`.

Thus `V_i(p)` is exactly the conditional expected terminal reward to `i`,
given that `i` Continues and at least one opponent Quits.  In particular it is
a convex combination of the actual continuer entries `r(S)_i` over nonempty
`S subset I-{i}`.  The equation `R_i(p)=V_i(p)` makes both pure endpoints
equal to `V_i(p)`: the Continue endpoint is

`W_i(p)+c_i(p)V_i(p)=(1-c_i(p))V_i(p)+c_i(p)V_i(p)=V_i(p)`.

This suggests applying the blocker-face argument not to the raw Quit payoff,
but to the endogenous gap `G_i=R_i-V_i`.

Fix a fixed-point-free permutation `pi` of the finite player set and numbers

`0 < alpha < beta <= 1`.

For each `i`, let `K_i=I-{i,pi(i)}` and define the following extrema of the
literal reward table:

`H_i^- = min_(T subset K_i) r(T union {i})_i`,

`H_i^+ = max_(T subset K_i) r(T union {i})_i`,

`L_i^- = min_(T subset K_i) r(T union {i,pi(i)})_i`,

`L_i^+ = max_(T subset K_i) r(T union {i,pi(i)})_i`,

and, over nonempty opponent coalitions,

`C_i^- = min_(empty != S subset I-{i}) r(S)_i`,

`C_i^+ = max_(empty != S subset I-{i}) r(S)_i`.

The last two extrema are well-defined because the derangement supplies at
least one opponent of every player.

**Proposition 9 (ordinary mathematics, not checked in Lean).**  Suppose, for
every player `i`,

`(1-alpha)H_i^- + alpha L_i^- > C_i^+`,

`(1-beta)H_i^+ + beta L_i^+ < C_i^-`.

Then there is a stationary product profile with every Quit probability in
`(alpha,beta)` which is an exact terminal Nash equilibrium.  Its actual
terminal payoff `V(p)` is a uniform-equilibrium payoff against unrestricted
behavioral deviations.

**Proof.**  Work on the probability box `[alpha,beta]^I`.  Its positive lower
bound makes `c_i(p)<1` for every `i`, since the designated opponent `pi(i)`
Quits with probability at least `alpha`.  Hence each `V_i` and each
`G_i=R_i-V_i` is continuous on the whole box.

On the face `p_(pi(i))=alpha`, first condition on the quitting set among
`K_i`.  Conditional on any such set, player `i`'s Quit reward has expectation

`(1-alpha) r(T union {i})_i
  + alpha r(T union {i,pi(i)})_i`,

so

`R_i(p) >= (1-alpha)H_i^- + alpha L_i^- > C_i^+`.

The normalized value `V_i(p)` is a convex combination of nonempty continuer
rows and is therefore at most `C_i^+`.  Thus `G_i(p)>0` on the entire lower
blocker face.  The same decomposition on `p_(pi(i))=beta` gives

`R_i(p) <= (1-beta)H_i^+ + beta L_i^+ < C_i^- <= V_i(p)`,

so `G_i(p)<0` on the entire upper blocker face.

Reindex by `F_j(p)=G_(pi^{-1}(j))(p)`.  Then `F_j` is positive on the lower
`j`-face and negative on the upper `j`-face.  Equivalently, for any fixed
positive `lambda`, the self-map

`Phi_j(p)=clamp_[alpha,beta](p_j+lambda F_j(p))`

maps the box continuously to itself.  Brouwer supplies a fixed point.  No
coordinate of a fixed point can lie on a boundary face, because the strict
inward sign would move it into the box.  Thus the fixed point lies in the open
box, where the clamp is inactive, and `F(p)=0`.  Consequently
`R_i(p)=V_i(p)` for every `i`.

Set `v=V(p)`.  The pure-Quit endpoint equals `v_i` by the root equation, and
the pure-Continue endpoint equals `W_i+c_i v_i=v_i` by the definition of
`V_i`.  Hence the stationary row is exact endpoint Nash and its one-stage
successor payoff is `v`.  Every player mixes with positive probability, so
the row is jointly absorbing; every player also has a positive-probability
quitting opponent, so all playerwise opponent continue masses are below one.
The checked theorem
`isUniformEquilibriumPayoff_of_stationaryEndpointCertificate_contracts`
therefore compiles this actual stationary certificate to the uniform-payoff
conclusion against all behavioral deviations.  The stronger exact
terminal-Nash assertion is compiled separately by
`isZeroAsymptoticNash_stationary_of_fixedPoint_endpointNash_contracts` under
the same fixed-point, endpoint-Nash, and contraction data.  QED.

### 19. Exact face-gap criterion and a standard-Q-side witness

### 20. Linear face certificates: beyond blocker matching

The permutation in Proposition 10 is only one way to identify the `|I|`
stationary gap equations with the `|I|` probability coordinates.  A genuinely
multi-blocker table may have no individual gap with a uniform sign switch on
any single pair of faces, even though invertible linear combinations of the
gaps do.

Retain a box `0<alpha<beta<=1` and the continuous gap vector

`G(p)=(R_i(p)-V_i(p))_(i in I)`.

Assume explicitly that `I` has at least two players.  Then every player has
an opponent whose probability is at least `alpha`, so all denominators
`1-c_i` are positive.  This replaces the derangement which supplied that fact
in Proposition 10.

**Proposition 11 (ordinary mathematics, not checked in Lean).**  Let

`A : R^I -> R^I`

be a linear map, not initially assumed invertible.  Suppose that, for every
coordinate `j`,

`(A G(p))_j>0` whenever `p_j=alpha`,

`(A G(p))_j<0` whenever `p_j=beta`,

uniformly over all other coordinates in the box.  Then `G` has a zero in the
open box.  The associated stationary product profile is an exact terminal
Nash profile, and its target `V(p)` is a uniform-equilibrium payoff against
unrestricted behavioral deviations.

**Proof.**  First, the strict face signs force `A` to be invertible.  At every
corner of the box, `A G` lies in `range(A)` and occupies the open orthant
prescribed by that corner.  If `range(A)` were proper, choose a nonzero vector
`y` orthogonal to it.  Choose the lower face in coordinate `j` when `y_j>0`
and the upper face when `y_j<0` (arbitrarily when `y_j=0`).  At the resulting
corner every nonzero term `y_j(A G)_j` is strictly positive, contradicting
orthogonality.  Thus `A` is surjective and hence invertible in the finite
square dimension.

Now apply the same clamped Brouwer argument directly to the vector field
`F=A G`.  The strict face signs force an interior fixed point and hence
`A G(p)=0`.  Invertibility gives `G(p)=0`.  The denominator,
fixed-point, endpoint-Nash, joint-absorption, and opponent-contraction
arguments are exactly those of Proposition 10 and use only `alpha>0`.  QED.

This also has a finite polynomial adapter.  Put `D_i=1-c_i>0` and
`N_i=D_iG_i=D_iR_i-W_i`.  Multiplication by the positive common denominator

`D_all=product_i D_i`

shows that the sign of `(A G)_j` is the sign of the polynomial

`P_j= sum_i A_(j,i) N_i product_(h!=i) D_h`.

On a coordinate face this polynomial has degree at most `|I|` in every
remaining variable.  Rescaling to the unit cube and checking the signs of all
tensor Bernstein coefficients of multidegree `|I|` is therefore a finite
exact-arithmetic sufficient certificate.  The bound is deliberately coarse;
the theorem needs only the face signs, not this particular checker.

#### Exact five-player multi-blocker test

Let `I=Z/5Z`, with indices modulo five.  Continuing players receive zero, and
a quitting player receives

`r(S)_i=1/2-1_(i+1 in S)-1_(i+2 in S)`.

Thus the pure-Quit stationary gap is

`G_i(p)=1/2-p_(i+1)-p_(i+2)`.

Define `H_i(p)=1/4-p_(i+1)` and the linear operator

`(B H)_i=H_i+H_(i+1)`.

Then `G=B H`.  The operator `B` is invertible: if `Bx=0`, then
`x_(i+1)=-x_i`; going around the odd five-cycle gives `x_i=-x_i`, hence every
coordinate is zero.  Let `Rot` be the coordinate rotation
`(Rot H)_j=H_(j-1)` and set

`A=Rot composed with B^{-1}`.

This is invertible and satisfies

`(A G(p))_j=1/4-p_j`.

On the box `[1/8,3/8]^I`, the transformed gap is exactly `1/8` on every lower
face and `-1/8` on every upper face.  Proposition 11 therefore gives the
unique zero `p_i=1/4` for all players.  Since continuers receive zero, the
target is zero, and the stationary compiler gives an exact terminal Nash
profile and uniform payoff zero.

No Proposition 10 blocker assignment can certify this example on any common
box.  For either probability on which `G_i` depends, a positive lower-face
sign would require

`alpha+beta<1/2`,

while a negative upper-face sign would require

`alpha+beta>1/2`.

The other coordinates do not affect `G_i`.  Thus no individual playerwise
face switch exists; the invertible combination is doing real work.

Nor is the mixed profile merely repairing a pure profile chosen elsewhere.
If `S` were a pure sure-exit coalition, a member `i` could have neither
`i+1` nor `i+2` in `S`, because even one such blocker makes its reward
negative.  An outsider must have at least one of those two successors in `S`,
or joining gives positive reward `1/2`.  The empty coalition fails the latter
condition.  Starting from any `i in S`, membership forces `i+1,i+2` outside;
the outsider condition at `i+1` then forces `i+3 in S`; membership of `i+3`
forces `i=(i+3)+2` outside, a contradiction.  Hence no pure sure-exit set
exists.

This exact example is circulant at the singleton level with normalized
off-diagonal entries `-1/2`, so its **existence** conclusion overlaps
`exists_uniformEquilibriumPayoff_of_pentagonCirculant_surplus_nonpos`
(`UniformEquilibrium/Quitting/Classification/Circulant/Trichotomy.lean`).
It is not a novelty witness at the existence level.  Its role is narrower and
exact: it falsifies the idea that a topological stationary-gap certificate
must assign one blocker coordinate to each player, and it tests an exact
fully mixed stationary conclusion which the cited singleton-surplus theorem
does not state via this adapter.  The general linear certificate applies to
noncirculant and nonzero-continuer tables as well, but that broader novelty has
not yet been independently audited.

#### Singular mixing is impossible under strict faces

The range--orthant argument is also the exact singular-edge audit.  A proper
linear subspace of `R^I` misses at least one open orthant: choose a nonzero
orthogonal vector and the orthant with its coordinate signs.  Since strict
face signs place `A G` in every open orthant at the corresponding corners, no
singular square `A` can satisfy the hypotheses.  Thus invertibility is derived,
not a hidden extra compatibility assumption.  With weak face signs this
fails sharply: the zero map `A=0` satisfies all weak inequalities for any
continuous nonzero `G`, yet `A G=0` says nothing about a stationary root.

### 21. Every singleton matrix has a dominant multi-quitter completion

The four-player `r*` witness is one instance of a general completion theorem.
It shows sharply that singleton LCP data alone cannot obstruct exact
stationary equilibrium once the higher-coalition rows are free to vary.

Let `I` have `n>=4` players, fix a derangement `pi`, and prescribe **arbitrary**
singleton rewards

`s_(i,j)=r({j})_i`

for every receiver `i` and singleton owner `j`.  Put `h_i=s_(i,i)`, let

`C_i^-=min(0,min_(j!=i)s_(i,j))`,

`C_i^+=max(0,max_(j!=i)s_(i,j))`,

and set `m=n-2>=2`.  Define

`a_m=1/2-(3/4)^(m+1)`.

This is positive because `(3/4)^(m+1)<=(3/4)^3=27/64<1/2`.  Choose one
constant `K>0` so large that, for every `i`,

`K a_m > C_i^+ + (3/4)|h_i|`,

`K/2 > (1/4)|h_i| - C_i^-`.

Such a common `K` exists because the player set is finite.  Complete the
reward table by retaining the prescribed singleton rows and, whenever
`|S|>=2`, putting

`r(S)_i=0` if `i notin S`,

`r(S)_i=-K` if `i in S` and `pi(i) in S`,

`r(S)_i=K` if `i in S` and `pi(i) notin S`.

**Proposition 12 (ordinary mathematics, not checked in Lean).**  Every reward
completion just defined has an interior exact stationary terminal Nash
profile.  Its endogenous stationary payoff is a uniform-equilibrium payoff
against unrestricted behavioral deviations.

**Proof.**  Use the box `[1/4,3/4]^I`.  Fix player `i`, and let `z` be the
probability that at least one of the `m` nondesignated background opponents
Quits.  Let `e=1-z` be their all-Continue probability.  Throughout the box,

`z>=1-(3/4)^m` and `0<=e<=1`.

**Proposition 17 (ordinary mathematics).**  Every four independent
countable quit-time law satisfies

`sqrt(a)+sqrt(b)<=1`, and equivalently `ell>=2*sqrt(a*b)` and
`ell^2>=4*a*b`.

**Proof.**  First truncate the desired events to the first `N` dates and work
backward.  Conditional on all four players being alive at one date, write
their Quit probabilities as `q_i` and `c_i=1-q_i`.  The probabilities at this
date of an `A` exit, a `B` exit, and joint continuation are

```text
A_0=q_1*q_2*c_3*c_4,
B_0=q_3*q_4*c_1*c_2,
C_0=c_1*c_2*c_3*c_4.
```

Set

```text
x=sqrt(c_1*c_2), y=sqrt(c_3*c_4),
u=sqrt(q_1*q_2), v=sqrt(q_3*q_4).
```

The elementary two-coordinate inequality

`sqrt((1-c_1)*(1-c_2)) <= 1-sqrt(c_1*c_2)`

follows after squaring from
`c_1+c_2>=2*sqrt(c_1*c_2)`.  Hence `u<=1-x` and
`v<=1-y`.  Therefore

```text
sqrt(A_0)+sqrt(B_0)+sqrt(C_0)
  =u*y+v*x+x*y
  <=(1-x)*y+(1-y)*x+x*y
  =x+y-x*y
  <=1.                                                   (CL)
```

Suppose the conditional desired-event probabilities after this date are
`a'` and `b'`, with `sqrt(a')+sqrt(b')<=1`.  Since every non-continuation
outcome other than the two desired pairs is permanent leakage,

`a=A_0+C_0*a'` and `b=B_0+C_0*b'`.

Using `sqrt(r+s)<=sqrt(r)+sqrt(s)` and `(CL)` gives

```text
sqrt(a)+sqrt(b)
 <=sqrt(A_0)+sqrt(B_0)+sqrt(C_0)*(sqrt(a')+sqrt(b'))
 <=1.
```

The terminal condition after date `N` is `a'=b'=0`, so backward induction
proves the inequality for every finite truncation.  The truncated events
increase to `A` and `B`; continuity from below and continuity of square root
give the infinite-horizon claim.  Finally
`sqrt(a)+sqrt(b)<=1` rearranges to
`ell=1-a-b>=2*sqrt(a*b)`, and both sides are nonnegative, so squaring is
valid.  QED.

The constant is sharp.  At date zero let players `1,2` Quit independently
with probability `1-delta`, while `3,4` Continue surely.  Conditional on all

`a=(1-delta)^2`, `b=delta^2`, and `ell=2*delta*(1-delta)`,

**Proposition 18 (ordinary mathematics).**  Partition `2m` distinct players

`sum_(k=1)^m sqrt(a_k) <= 1`.

```text

 <= sum_k (1-x_k)*product_(j!=k) x_j + product_j x_j
 <= 1.                                                   (MCL)
```

The last expression is the probability of at most one failure among

`sum_k a_k + 2*sum_(j<k)sqrt(a_j*a_k)<=1`,

### 28. On the hard branch, exact charge and payoff variation are equivalent

There is a useful global consequence of `(HC)`.  Let


be any exact path in the boxed punishment-floor-admissible Nash--Bellman

**Proposition 36 (ordinary mathematics; exact path variation sandwich).**

```text

  <= sum_(t<L) ||x_(t+1)-x_t||
  <= 2*M * sum_(t<L) q_t.                            (VS)
```

Hence, on exact floor-admissible paths, unbounded absorption charge is
equivalent to unbounded payoff variation.  Under a terminal exploitability
witness, the checked canonical prefix-charge bound `B` gives the uniform
exact-path estimate

```text
sum_(t<L) ||x_(t+1)-x_t|| <= 2*M*B.                 (VB)
```

**Proof.**  The lower inequality is `(HC)` summed edge by edge.  For the
upper inequality, if `q_t=0`, the edge root is all Continue and its payoff
does not move.  If `q_t>0`, let `h_t` be the root's payoff conditional on
absorption.  The Bellman identity is

```text
x_(t+1)-x_t = q_t*(h_t-x_t).
```

Both `h_t` and the boxed tail `x_t` have norm at most `M`, so the edge
distance is at most `2M*q_t`.  Sum.  The checked floor-prefix capacity bound
under a terminal exploitability witness bounds the charge of every global
admissible path, giving `(VB)`.  QED.

### 29. Strong singleton compensation controls every finite leakage outcome

Take four players, with designated pairs `A={1,2}` and `B={3,4}`.  For

```text

         0   otherwise.
```

For `i in B`, use the same rule with `B` in place of `A`.  Let `a,b` be the
exact first-coalition probabilities of `A,B`, let `ell_single` be the total
probability of singleton first coalitions, and let `ell_multi` be the total
probability of size-at-least-two first coalitions other than `A,B`.  Eternal
continuation is not included in either leakage term.

**Proposition 37 (ordinary mathematics, independently reviewed;
all-finite-leakage
compensated ledger).**  Every terminal `epsilon`-Nash profile for the displayed
table satisfies

```text
a+b >= ell_single+ell_multi-2*epsilon.               (FL)
```

**Proof.**  Fix `i in A` and couple the unilateral deviation which replaces
only player `i`'s quit time by `Never`.  If the prescribed finite first
coalition does not contain `i`, it is unchanged and both payoffs are one.  If
the coalition is `A`, deletion leaves the partner singleton, changing payoff
two to one and losing exactly one.  If it contains `i`, has size at least two,
and is not `A`, deletion leaves a nonempty coalition not containing `i`, so
the payoff rises from zero to one.

On the own-singleton event `{i}`, the prescribed payoff is `-2`.  After
deletion, either some later nonempty opponent coalition occurs and pays one,
or everyone else also plays `Never` and the payoff is zero.  Thus this event
gains at least two.  The prescribed all-Never event is unchanged.  Therefore
the deviation gain is at least

```text
2*p_{ {i} }
 + sum_(U: i in U, |U|>=2, U!=A) p_U
 - a.
```

The `epsilon`-Nash inequality gives the corresponding upper bound by
`epsilon`.  Sum it over the two members of `A` and the two members of `B`.
Every singleton leakage event is counted once with coefficient two.  Every
multi-coalition leakage event `U` is counted `|U|>=2` times.  The two target
pairs are excluded from their own members' positive sums.  Hence

```text
2*a+2*b >= 2*ell_single+2*ell_multi-4*epsilon,
```

which is `(FL)`.  QED.

The coefficient `-2` is the exact threshold for this symmetric summation:
an own-singleton payoff `-K` produces coefficient `K/2` after the four player
inequalities are divided by two.  Thus `K=2` makes singleton and
multi-coalition leakage enter with the same unit coefficient.

This is not a game counterexample.  Pure `A`, pure `B`, and all-Never are
still exact terminal Nash profiles.  In particular the stronger ledger
isolates, rather than solves, the remaining universal obligations: a viable
negative gadget must break all-Never without reopening an uncontrolled
positive-solo singleton account, and must balance `a` against `b` without
turning one designated pair into a sure-exit sink.  The sharp independent-clock
inequality cannot contradict `(FL)` until both tasks are met.

**Proposition 38 (ordinary mathematics, independently reviewed;
singleton-deletion versus


**Proof.**  Fix `i` and use the realization on which `i` Quits at date zero

replacing `i` by `Never`, nobody ever Quits and the terminal payoff is zero.

```text
0-r({i})_i >= kappa_i.
```

This proves the displayed inequality.  At the all-Never profile, any finite
pure quit time by player `i` yields the own singleton payoff, which is
strictly negative, while continuing forever yields zero.  Pure-time
extremality, or direct linearity over the deviator's stopping law, makes
all-Never exact terminal Nash.  QED.

### 34. Every cross-face solution has an exact first peel, but not a recursive one

**Proof.**  Standard-LCP nonnegativity gives `z_j>=0` and Proposition 44 gives

### 35. Fixed-horizon vanishing semantic loops force a homogeneous LCP

The raw active-gap coercivity in Proposition 58 of `CODEX_NOETHER`'s note has
an important semantic qualification.  The checked endpoint-Nash predicate

after one telescopes an entire returned block.

successor payoff from tail `t` and product root `p`, and let
`D_i(t,p)=Quit_i(t,p)-Continue_i(t,p)`.  Define the maximum Bellman error
`beta_n` by

```text
|v^n_(k,i)-T_i(v^n_(k+1),p^n_k)|<=beta_n             (VH3)
```

probability-weighted endpoint inequalities**


where `epsilon_n>=0`.

**Exact one-row estimates used below.**  Suppose `|t_i|<=K` for every player,
every terminal reward coordinate has absolute value at most `R`, and put
`q=sum_i p_i`.  Then

```text
|T_i(t,p)-t_i| <= (K+R)q,                            (VH4a)
|T_i(t,p)-t_i-sum_j p_j(r({j})_i-t_i)|
    <= 2(K+R)q^2,                                   (VH4b)
|D_i(t,p)-(s_i-t_i)| <= (K+3R)q.                    (VH4c)
```

For `(VH4a)`, absorption has probability at most `q`, and on absorption the
terminal coordinate differs from the tail by at most `K+R`.  For `(VH4b)`,
write `P_0` for the empty-coalition probability, `P_j` for singleton `j`, and
`P_multi` for the probability of at least two quitters.  The union bound and
the pair count give

```text
0 <= P_0-(1-q) <= q^2/2,
0 <= sum_j(p_j-P_j) <= q^2,
P_multi <= q^2/2.
```

Expanding the expectation by empty, singleton, and multiple coalitions now
bounds the remainder by

```text
(K/2+3R/2)q^2 <= 2(K+R)q^2.
```

Finally, after player `i` is forced to Quit, the probability that some
opponent quits is at most `q`, so its payoff differs from `s_i` by at most
`2Rq`.  After `i` is forced to Continue, its payoff differs from `t_i` by at
most `(K+R)q`.  The triangle inequality proves `(VH4c)`.

**Proposition 46 (independently reviewed ordinary mathematics; fixed-horizon
semantic tangent obstruction).**  If



After extracting a subsequence of one base value, every phase value is

### 36. Future-record selection removes sparse clock expansion

## 42. Semialgebraicity does not regularize the bounded charge bias

`CODEX_NOETHER` Proposition 79 gives an abstract compact relation with
uniformly bounded path charge but no continuous exact state-local bias.  Its
countable branch carrier leaves open whether the finite-dimensional
semialgebraic geometry of Nash--Bellman relations rules out that discontinuity.
The following one-dimensional example shows that semialgebraicity alone does
not help.  It is complementary to `CODEX_NOETHER` Proposition 81, which gives
a semialgebraic unbounded-sublinear prefix family; here total charge is
uniformly bounded.

Let `K=[0,1]`, and let `R subset K x K` be the union of two closed rational
semialgebraic line segments:

```text
(x,2x) in R  for 0<=x<=1/2,
(x,0)  in R  for 1/2<=x<=1.                         (SA1)
```

At `x=1/2`, both successors `1` and `0` are allowed.  Put `a(x)=x`.

**Proposition 56 (independently reviewed ordinary mathematics; semialgebraic
bias obstruction).**  The compact relation `(SA1)` is closed, semialgebraic, and
predecessor-serial; every state has a viable infinite future; and `a` is a
continuous polynomial nonnegative charge.  Every infinite path has total
charge strictly below `2`, but there is no continuous `W:K->R` satisfying

```text
a(x)+W(y)<=W(x)       for every (x,y) in R.          (SA2)
```

**Proof.**  Closedness and semialgebraicity follow from the displayed finite
union.  Every `y in [0,1]` has predecessor `y/2`, and `0` has its self-loop,
so predecessor seriality holds.  A path starting at `x>0` doubles until its
first state `y in [1/2,1]`, then moves to `0`; only at `y=1/2` may it instead
visit `1` once before moving to `0`.  Hence every path reaches the zero loop
after finitely many positive-charge rows.

Before the first state `y in [1/2,1]`, the positive states form a finite
geometric progression.  Including `y`, their charge is less than `2y<=2`.
If `y=1/2` and the path takes the optional detour through `1`, the charge
before that detour is `1-x`, and the extra state contributes `1`, for total
`2-x<2`.  Thus the uniform total-charge bound is `2`.

Suppose continuous `W` satisfied `(SA2)`.  For `x_n=2^{-n}`, take the legal
path

```text
2^{-n}->2^{-(n-1)}->...->1/2->0.
```

Telescoping `(SA2)` along it gives

```text
W(2^{-n})-W(0)>=2^{-n}+...+1/2=1-2^{-n}.            (SA3)
```

But `2^{-n}->0`, so continuity makes the left side tend to zero while the
right side tends to one, a contradiction.  QED.

## 45. Summable artificial seams quantitatively reconstruct actual exploitability

The revised conditioned-packet adapter permits nonsemantic candidate pairs.

Fix a literal root sequence `q_t`.  For one player, attach bounded candidate


```text

```

best-response cap of the literal root suffix beginning at `t`.  Assume the

```text
sum_t |f_t| <= A,      sum_t |g_t| <= G.              (AS2)
```

seam necessity).**  Under these assumptions,

```text

```

mismatches are `A_k,B_k`, then

```text
|f_t|=0, |g_t|=0                    off seams,
|f_t|<=A_k, |g_t|<=A_k+B_k          at seam k.
```

```text

 <= d_0 + 2 sum_k A_k + sum_k(A_k+B_k).              (AS4)
```

This bound is valid but nonsharp when the prescribed and cap seam mismatches
are separately available.  At a seam, the **cap residual** is exactly

```text
B_0-U_0 <= d_0 + sum_k A_k + sum_k B_k
          = d_0 + sum_k(A_k+B_k).                    (AS4')
```

`d_0+2A+G` theorem remains the applicable statement when only prescribed and
direct-debt residual ledgers, rather than a cap-residual ledger, are known.

**Proof.**  Put

```text

```

`c_t` is the joint Continue probability, exact prefix recursion of the actual

```text

```

secant `s_t` between the actual and candidate successors satisfying


```text

 = F_t.payoff + d_t-g_t
 = u_t-f_t+d_t-g_t
```


and `s_t` vanish; the latter products are dominated by the deleted-player


```text

```

which is `(AS3)`.  The seam-price inequalities give `(AS4)`, and direct

## 46. Recurrent cap drops inside a reached chronology force an opponent clock

Noether Proposition 92 prices favorable **block seams**.  The paid tight-cycle
route instead seeks one exactly reached Nash--Bellman owner word, so its block
seams would be zero.  There is a parallel account inside the reached word:
repeated strong-collider excess must later be discharged when the collider
becomes active, and bounded cap recursion must replenish those forward drops.

Fix a player `i` and a sequence of product rows.  Let `b_t` be a uniformly
bounded cap coordinate, `|b_t|<=K`, satisfying the exact one-row recursion

```text
b_t=max(Q_t,C_t+O_t b_(t+1)),                         (CV1)
```

where `O_t` is the probability every opponent of `i` Continues and `C_t` is
the unnormalized reward contribution when `i` Continues and some opponent
Quits.  If terminal rewards are bounded by `M`, then

```text
|C_t|<=M(1-O_t).                                      (CV2)
```

Define forward downward and upward cap variations

```text
D_N=sum_(t<N)(b_t-b_(t+1))_+,
U_N=sum_(t<N)(b_(t+1)-b_t)_+.                         (CV3)
```

**Proposition 60 (proved ordinary mathematics; unreviewed reached-cap
variation adapter).**  For every `N`,

```text
U_N <= (K+M)sum_(t<N)(1-O_t),                         (CV4)
D_N <= (K+M)sum_(t<N)(1-O_t)+2K.                      (CV5)
```

Consequently, if `D_N` is unbounded, the opponent-absorption sum diverges.
For a finite player set, some fixed `j!=i` then has divergent marginal Quit
hazard.  If `i` itself has divergent marginal hazard, `i,j` are two distinct
persistent labels and all joint and one-player-deleted suffix clocks vanish.

More generally, let `[s_k,t_k]` be pairwise disjoint chronological intervals
with `s_k<t_k` and

```text
b_(s_k)-b_(t_k) >= delta_k >= 0.                      (CV6)
```

Then

```text
sum_k delta_k <= lim_N D_N.                           (CV7)
```

Thus a divergent sum of reached cap discharges already implies the same
opponent-clock conclusion.

**Proof.**  The maximum in `(CV1)` dominates its Continue branch, so

```text
b_(t+1)-b_t <= (1-O_t)b_(t+1)-C_t.
```

Taking positive parts and using `(CV2)` and `|b_(t+1)|<=K` gives

```text
(b_(t+1)-b_t)_+ <= (K+M)(1-O_t).
```

Summation is `(CV4)`.  For each real increment `x`,
`(-x)_+-x_+=-x`; hence the exact variation telescope is

```text
D_N-U_N=b_0-b_N.
```

The boundary term is at most `2K`, proving `(CV5)`.  Divergence and finite
pigeonhole then proceed exactly through the rowwise union bound

```text
1-O_t <= sum_(j!=i)p_(t,j).
```

Finally, total downward variation on `[s_k,t_k]` is at least its net drop
`b_(s_k)-b_(t_k)`.  Disjointness permits summation, proving `(CV7)`.  QED.

**Strong-collider specialization.**  Suppose the chronology is exact
Nash--Bellman and a solo phase of owner `o` has hazard `p`, while player `i`
is punishment tight and has collision excess

```text
C=r({o,i})_i-P_i >= kappa>0.
```

Passive endpoint Nash gives the current cap/value excess

```text
b_s-P_i >= p C >= p kappa.                             (CV8)
```

If at a later phase `t` player `i` is the interior solo owner, active
indifference pins its current and successor cap/value to `P_i`.  Therefore
the interval from forcing to activation has net cap drop at least
`p*kappa`.  On pairwise disjoint such intervals, divergence of the selected
forcing-hazard sum implies the opponent clock in Proposition 60.  If the
activation phases also make `i` persistent, the two-label survival fields are
automatic.

This removes a separate clock hypothesis from any future **serially reached**
strong-collision-cycle construction.  It does not supply the construction:
the paid first-disagreement source currently gives only a static tight label
cycle, not exact reached phases in which every forced collider is later
activated.  Nor does cap variation produce the payoff near-return or exact
floor-admissible return required by the paid consumer.  Preemption-colored
edges remain the complementary support-seam obstruction described in Cedar
Section 24.
