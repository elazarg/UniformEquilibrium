# Computing the last part of a minimum-survival timing block

The probability of reaching any actual cut of a globally minimizing signed source equals the least survival of an approximately Nash timing block evaluated at the cut's own effective payoff. The statements below describe which final rows or portions can realize this least survival. [Signed source and proof of the cut identity](CODEX_ROOT__SURVIVAL_AT_CUTS_OF_A_SIGNED_MINIMUM.md#exact-minimum-survival-at-an-actual-cut)

## Exact optimal rows away from the two-zero boundary

Fix four native quitting rewards \(Z\) bounded by \(M\ge1\), with all four own singletons zero. A finite timing block pays \(Z(T)\) on an exit and a specified vector \(v\in\mathbb R^4\) on survival. The players independently choose one of the dates or waiting through. Write \(\sigma(v)\) for the minimum limiting survival of a sequence of such blocks with total strategic-form regret tending to zero. Lengths and profiles may vary. The achievable limits form a nonempty compact subset of \([0,1]\) by ordinary finite Nash existence and diagonal selection. For a one-date Nash row \(q\) at child \(v\), let \(S(q)=\prod_i(1-q_i)\) and let \(w(q,v)\) denote its four prescribed payoffs. Concatenation gives \(\sigma(v)\le S(q)\sigma(w(q,v))\), since every replacement in the earlier block either stops within it or reaches the last Nash row. The cap and prescribed payoff of any finite block are each 1-Lipschitz in its terminal vector, independently of length.

Define \(B_2\) as the set of nonnegative continuations having at least two zero coordinates. At every \(v\notin B_2\) with \(0<\sigma(v)<1\) there is a **nonblank** one-date Nash row attaining
\[
 \sigma(v)=S(q)\sigma(w(q,v)).
\]
The reduction is uniform in the following stronger sense. Fix a compact \(K\) disjoint from \(B_2\) and \(\eta>0\). There is \(d>0\), depending only on the game, \(K\), and \(\eta\), such that whenever \(v\in K\) and \(\eta\le\sigma(v)<1\), at most two consecutive optimal Nash rows, read backwards from \(v\), have combined survival at most \(1-d\). Every retained row has positive survival and individually attains the displayed equality at its own child. Consequently after at most \(1+\lfloor\log(1/\eta)/[-\log(1-d)]\rfloor\) such batches one leaves \(K\) or reaches \(\sigma=1\), where every one-date Nash row is blank. This is a reduction for finite timing blocks with unrestricted normal-form deviations, including prefixes of globally optimal signed sources.

To prove it, write \(v_{(1)}\le\cdots\le v_{(4)}\) for the sorted coordinates. Compactness supplies \(g>0\) such that every \(v\in K\) has either a coordinate at most \(-g\) or three coordinates at least \(g\), because \(\max(-v_{(1)},v_{(2)})\) vanishes only on \(B_2\). Let \(C=\max(M,\sup_K\|v\|_\infty,1)\). Use minimizing blocks with terminal vector \(v\) and survival tending to \(s=\sigma(v)\ge\eta\). Every public cut, and each individual's survival to that cut, are at least \(\eta/2\) eventually. Conditional regret in each reached suffix therefore tends to zero, since a player can switch its strategy just in that suffix, retaining its earlier law.

First suppose some \(v_i\le-g\). The last physical row of each nonempty block has a subsequential Nash limit at \(v\). If all hazards there were less than \(g/(16C)\), player \(i\)'s Quit offer would be at least \(-3C\max q\), while its wait offer is at most \(-g+6C\max q\). It must then quit surely, a contradiction. Thus this row has survival bounded above by \(1-g/(16C)\). Blocks can be assumed nonempty since \(s<1\).

Now suppose the other three coordinates, apart from a specified \(k\), are at least \(g\). Take \(0<\delta<\min(\eta/16,g\eta/(100C))\), also with \(\eta\le1\). The total marginal finite mass of each minimizing block eventually reaches \(\delta\). Otherwise pass to a subsequence with total mass below \(\delta\). For each of the three positive players, every finite deadline loses at least \(g-3C\delta>g/2\) to waiting through: with no rival finite deadline its payoff is zero and waiting gives its terminal coordinate. Hence all three have finite mass tending to zero. If \(v_k>0\) this now holds for \(k\) as well, contradicting \(s<1\). If \(v_k<0\), on any nonempty block a finite deadline beats waiting for \(k\) by \(-v_k-o(1)\), inconsistent with its waiting probability bounded below by \(\eta/2\). If \(v_k=0\), the solo row of \(k\) with hazard \(2\delta\) is Nash at \(v\): \(k\) is indifferent and the other three strictly wait by the same bounded-payoff estimate. Thus \(s\le1-2\delta\), whereas the current blocks have survival at least \(1-\delta\).

Take the last date \(t\) on which total *unconditional marginal* finite mass from \(t\) through the end reaches \(\delta\). After \(t\) the sum is below \(\delta\), hence below \(2\delta/\eta\) after conditioning. The same comparison shows that the three positive players have conditional finite mass \(o(1)\) after \(t\). When \(v_k>0\) this also holds for \(k\). When \(v_k<0\), a nonempty suffix after \(t\) would again give \(k\) a uniformly profitable finite reply over its positive mass on waiting through. Thus upon taking a subsequence the suffix is empty or has limiting play only by \(k\) with \(v_k=0\). In the latter case let \(x\le2\delta/\eta\) be its limiting conditional finite probability. Its prescribed payoff and survival are exactly those, in the limit, of one solo row of \(k\) with hazard \(x\) at \(v\). This row is Nash by strict waiting of all other players and indifference of \(k\). Its payoff is denoted by \(w_1\); in the empty or blank case take \(w_1=v\). Conditional regrets vanish, so the four continuation caps on that suffix also tend to \(w_1\).

The row at \(t\) has a subsequential Nash limit at \(w_1\), with output \(w\). This follows by testing its two actions with the actual conditional prescribed continuation, whose limit equals the cap. Together these at most two rows have survival \(S_*\le1-\delta/4\): some owner's unconditional finite mass from \(t\) onward is at least \(\delta/4\), and conditioning at its survival to \(t\) only increases that probability. Choose \(d=\min(g/(16C),\delta/4)\) for the two cases.

In either argument the historical word before the extracted rows has survival tending to \(s/S_*\). With the actual conditional cap at its boundary as terminal payoff, its full cap is the original block cap and its prescribed payoff is weakly larger than the original one. Moving this child to its limit \(w\) therefore still gives vanishing regret. Hence \(\sigma(w)\le s/S_*\). The opposite bound is concatenation, so the word attains equality. Every intermediate inequality must also be an equality because survivals are positive. Remove any blank row. Each batch divides \(\sigma\) by at most \(1-d\) in the backward direction; its value cannot exceed one. If a two-row batch has already left \(K\) or reached one at its first step one may stop there.

Assume additionally the native absorbing regret infimum \(A>0\). For any bounded family of nonnegative \(v\) the quantities \(\sigma(v)\) have a common positive lower bound. Indeed realize the finite block as actual native clocks by treating waiting through as Never. The native full cap is at most its block cap, because any deadline after the block earns the same as literal Never and waiting through in the block adds a nonnegative payoff on rival survival. The prescribed payoffs differ by \(\nu v_i\), where \(\nu\) is block survival. Redirect the smallest native Never marginal to a finite date to get \(A\le D_{\rm block}+\nu\sum_i v_i+14M\nu^{1/4}\) by coupling its four payoffs and three rival caps. Consequently under \(A>0\) the positive-cap region and its single-zero facets admit this finite optimal reduction uniformly on compact subsets away from \(B_2\). The argument does not give a uniform step size on approach to the two-zero boundary.

There is also a one-sided obstruction on any entire historical interval having vanishing regret at its terminal child \(v\), parent payoff tending to \(w\), limiting personal survivals \(t_i\), limiting full survival \(0<S=\prod_i t_i<1\) and limiting rival survivals \(H_i=\prod_{j\ne i}t_j<1\) for every player. Repeat that whole block independently forever. Its native prescribed payoff tends to \((w_i-Sv_i)/(1-S)\). Its periodic full cap is the maximum of its best in-block finite offer and the infinite passive return \(P_i/(1-H_i)\), with \(P_i\) the block passive reward on rival exit; this follows by the geometric series for every complete deadline. At \(v\) both the finite offer and \(P_i+H_i v_i\) are bounded by \(w_i+o(1)\). Hence
\[
 A\le \sum_i \frac{S}{1-S}(v_i-w_i)_+
   +\sum_i\frac{H_i-S}{(1-H_i)(1-S)}(w_i-v_i)_+.
\]
In particular, an absorbing positive-gap game precludes a return that fixes all participants' cap coordinates and weakly raises each always-quiet player's coordinate on going backwards. The inequality applies to intervals of arbitrary physical length.

## Exact last transitions at two-zero continuation values

A four-player native quitting table has finite nonempty coalition rewards Z and zero own singletons. In a finite private timing block every owner independently chooses a date or Wait. The first nonempty quit set S receives Z(S); if all wait, the given continuation is v. Regret tests every date and Wait, so it tests all complete deviations. Define σ(v) as the least limiting all-Wait probability along blocks of arbitrary finite lengths with total regret tending to zero, including the empty block. Minima are attained by diagonal selection; nonemptiness follows from finite Nash. This is an ordinary-mathematical result on this global function. It applies to every native table, including degenerate rewards.

Fix v with vₐ=vᵦ=0 and the two other coordinates strictly positive. Write Γᵢⱼ=Zᵢ({j}), Jᵢⱼ=Zᵢ({i,j}) and Lᵢⱼ=Jᵢⱼ−Γᵢⱼ. Put M=max(1,max|Zᵢ(S)|), B=max(M,‖v‖∞), δ=minₖ∉{a,b}vₖ. Write s=σ(v). The elementary two-zero Nash argument below gives s<1 at every such v. Assume s>0 and choose

    0<η<min(1−s, δ/(2M+B), 1).

Here is the exact menu for a final block of absorption probability ≤η. Only a,b use finite dates, with masses fₐ,fᵦ∈[0,1), S=(1−fₐ)(1−fᵦ)≥1−η. A direction i←j with fⱼ>0 must pass its first-date guard min(0,Jᵢⱼ)≤Γᵢⱼ, for i,j∈{a,b} distinct. If both are finite with positive probability, take numbers A,B',C≥0 summing to one, satisfying

    Γₐᵦ A=Lₐᵦ C,             Γᵦₐ B'=Lᵦₐ C.

They are the conditional strict and tied orders of the two finite draws (A for a<b, B' for b<a). The probabilities uₐ,uᵦ,uₐᵦ of the first nonempty sets {a},{b},{a,b} are

    uₐ=fₐ(1−fᵦ)+fₐfᵦ A,     uᵦ=fᵦ(1−fₐ)+fₐfᵦ B',    uₐᵦ=fₐfᵦ C.

When a mass vanishes omit the order restrictions and the product terms. The payoff w before this block has wₐ=Γₐᵦ fᵦ and wᵦ=Γᵦₐ fₐ. For either remaining owner k use wₖ=uₐΓₖₐ+uᵦΓₖᵦ+uₐᵦ Zₖ({a,b})+S vₖ.

**Bellman equality.** Precede any such final block by a single product Nash row q with continuation w. Write u for this row's payoff and d=S ∏ᵢ(1−qᵢ). Among candidates with d≤1−η there is one with positive d, and

    σ(v) = min d σ(u).

In fact every candidate satisfies d≥s. At an attaining one σ(u)=s/d≥s/(1−η)>s, and 1−d≥η. The row q may be blank when the final block alone reaches the threshold. On a compact two-zero stratum with a common bound on B and δ bounded below and with s≥s₀>0, one η works for every such endpoint. A common upper gap below one follows below. The increase is at least s₀η/(1−η). The predecessor u can have a different sign pattern.

**Necessity on a short suffix.** In any sequence of final blocks with all-Wait tending to S≥1−η and vanishing regret, each outsider's finite reply has payoff ≤M(η+o(1)), since only an earlier or simultaneous rival can give a nonzero reward. Its Wait payoff is ≥vₖ−(M+B)(η+o(1)). Thus each such outsider assigns o(1) finite mass. Moving it to Wait changes payoffs and all pure reply menus by o(1). Every player's Wait probability now stays bounded below by the joint survival. Consequently Wait is within o(1) of optimal for each, uniformly over the date menus. For i among the two zeros, waiting pays exactly Γᵢⱼ fⱼ on each edited block. Write mⱼ(t) for the unconditional rival mass at t, rⱼ(t) for its strictly later finite mass. A deadline at t improves on Wait by precisely

    Dᵢ(t)=Lᵢⱼ mⱼ(t)−Γᵢⱼ rⱼ(t).

Thus Dᵢ≤o(1) uniformly and the assigned weighted sum Σₜmᵢ(t)|Dᵢ(t)| tends to zero. At the first physical date the bound gives Jᵢⱼ mⱼ(1)≤Γᵢⱼ fⱼ+o(1), whence the first-date guard if fⱼ has a positive limit. With both limiting masses positive, divide by their product and sum over all dates: independence gives the two displayed order identities after subselection. The payoff formula for zeros follows from Wait optimality with positive assigned probability; the passive coordinates follow by conditioning on the first coalition. No fixed time parametrization or continuity of tie probabilities is needed.

**Realization of the complete menu.** Positive outsiders strictly wait by the same bound throughout these constructions. For C>0 choose independent conditional finite draws on dates n=1,2,…, with respective constant hazards

    hₐ=C/(B'+C),        hᵦ=C/(A+C).

The conditional finite laws are geometric. Owner i assigns unconditional date mass fᵢhᵢ(1−hᵢ)ⁿ⁻¹ and Wait mass 1−fᵢ. These hazards allow hᵢ=1. The ratios of strict to tied orders are (1−hᵦ)/hᵦ and (1−hₐ)/hₐ. At each date with rival finite survival, mⱼ=hⱼ(mⱼ+rⱼ); the order identities imply Jᵢⱼhⱼ=Γᵢⱼ. Hence every zero player's deadline ties Wait, including after the rival's finite law ends. Censoring sufficiently late draws to Wait incurs uniformly vanishing error over all available replies and the prescribed profile.

With just j active and i the other zero owner, the first-date guard is sufficient. If Lᵢⱼ≤0, put j's finite mass on the first date. If Lᵢⱼ>0 and Γᵢⱼ>0 take hⱼ=Γᵢⱼ/Jᵢⱼ∈(0,1) as above. If Lᵢⱼ>0 and Γᵢⱼ=0, disperse the finite mass uniformly over N dates and pass to the limit. The remaining case Γᵢⱼ<0<Lᵢⱼ violates the guard. No zero owner with zero finite mass needs assigned equality on a finite date.

For C=0 and A,B'>0 both Γ entries vanish. Give a an early uniform N-date interval with conditional probability A and a late one otherwise; give b a disjoint middle uniform N-date interval. Every deadline advantage is O(1/N). For A=1, Γₐᵦ=0. Let b be diffuse over a later N-date interval. If Γᵦₐ≥0 give a an earlier N-date uniform interval, so Dᵦ≤max(0,Lᵦₐ)fₐ/N. If Γᵦₐ<0 its guard forces Lᵦₐ≤0; give a all its finite mass at the very first date. Now Dᵦ≤0 always, with equality on b's later dates. In both cases Dₐ is O(1/N) in absolute value, and all scheduled finite dates of a tie Wait. Exchange names for B'=1. Blank play handles fₐ=fᵦ=0. These formulas retain the full first-coalition law, including at the nongeneric endpoints of the menu.

**Uniform separation from survival one.** If either Lᵢⱼ≤0, a sufficiently small positive hazard of j alone gives a Nash row at v: j ties, i weakly waits and both positive outsiders strictly wait. If both L entries are positive, suppose blank were the only Nash row. Perturb the two zero continuations to −ε. A convergent subsequence of finite-game Nash rows must tend to blank, with the two positive outsiders strictly waiting. Each zero owner's advantage there is Lᵢⱼqⱼ+ε(1−qⱼ)>0, forcing both to quit surely, a contradiction. Thus σ(v)<1 in all cases by a nonblank Nash row. This one-row existence is the earlier two-zero obstruction to a uniquely blank equilibrium.

In fact for every 0<η₀<min(1,δ/(2M+B)) we have σ(v)≤1−η₀. Otherwise the entire minimizing blocks are short in the preceding sense, with survival tending to s∈(1−η₀,1). Their menu has some positive fⱼ. Increase that positive mass until the joint survival is 1−η₀, retaining the same support and conditional order parameters. The guards are independent of the two mass sizes, and all positive outsiders still strictly wait. The realized enlarged suffix shows σ(v)≤1−η₀, a contradiction. Hence a uniform crossing parameter η=η₀/2 is available on the stated compact sets without any prior separation of σ from one.

**Equality and crossing.** Concatenate limiting equilibrium blocks: a prefix at u, the Nash row q at w, and a realized short final block at v. A unilateral choice is either a prefix deadline or waiting through to the remainder. Waiting through exposes its conditional optimal value instead of the prescribed value with error bounded by the remainder's regret. The same argument for the middle row and uniform continuity in each continuation give vanishing total regret. Survival multiplies. This proves σ(v)≤dσ(u) for every candidate, with σ(u)≤1.

Conversely take finite near-equilibrium blocks attaining σ(v). Their all-Wait probabilities tend to s>0, so every physical cut has reach bounded below, and conditional regret tends to zero: a deviation after a reached cut changes the initial payoff by its conditional gain times joint reach. From the end of each block, choose the crossing row for which the suffix strictly after it has conditional survival >1−η and including it gives ≤1−η. Such a row exists eventually since s<1−η. Extract limits of both survival probabilities, the row and the conditional data. The suffix has the menu just derived and predecessor w. Its conditional cap and prescribed vector both tend to w. The crossing row is Nash at w by testing head choices followed by the prescribed or optimal suffix. Its limiting predecessor is u. For the actual prefixes before this row, replacing the continuation with u gives vanishing regret: waiting through to the actual conditional prescribed payoff reproduces the original prescribed payoff, and a changed terminal value changes all offers by o(1). Thus σ(u)≤s/d. Since dσ(u)≥s already, equality holds. Neither infinitely late dates nor a vanishing limiting crossing row invalidate the argument. No finite profile is played after a literally infinite block; all comparisons use finite truncations.

**Generic timing.** Suppose both directed Γ and both directed L are nonzero. With both f positive, the ordered-simplex equations force all A,B',C positive and Γᵢⱼ,Lᵢⱼ to have equal signs in each direction. The conditional hazards are hⱼ=Γᵢⱼ/Jᵢⱼ∈(0,1). Every such exact conditional calendar already consists, up to gaps, of the same successive common geometric atoms. Indeed before the rival's finite support ends, assigned optimality forces mⱼ=hⱼ(mⱼ+rⱼ)>0 at almost every assigned date. Any shared atom has only finitely many shared predecessors, since each such predecessor consumes at least hⱼ times the rival tail there. There is a shared atom by C>0, and before the first there can be no other mass since the rival still has a positive tail. Removing the first leaves two positive tails to which the identical order argument applies; iterating exhausts both finite masses. In particular an exact block with both masses positive cannot have boundedly many occupied dates at this endpoint. A sequence with a fixed bound on its number of occupied dates would likewise compactify to a finite calendar (empty dates can be removed), contradicting the same conclusion. The realization and crossing equality require only the integrated order information, which applies even to sequences with varying unbounded numbers of physical dates. In the generic solo case one geometric or one single-date block suffices. If Γᵢⱼ<0, an interstitial empty date before later rival finite mass yields a strictly profitable Dᵢ; it cannot be inserted into an exact calendar. In the nongeneric case Γᵢⱼ=0<Lᵢⱼ, vanishingly small individual atoms can be necessary.

**Scope.** The common-geometric contact argument already appears in “Uniform three-owner tail dispersion,” §5.3, where it is used to test concentration of an infinite-game source. The earlier signed and unsigned two-zero continuation reductions test incoming words at an ALL-END minimum of a debt-plus-Never objective. Here the new statement is the exact finite-dimensional predecessor menu, including singular order laws, and the calibrated crossing identity for the unrestricted finite-block survival function σ. It controls a positive step at each regular two-zero stratum. It does not ensure that a subsequent predecessor remains on that stratum or settle arbitrary four-player games. The local transition equations and the compactness proof are ordinary mathematics.

## Two exceptional terminal coordinates, including nonzero offsets

Use the finite-block minimum-survival function \(\sigma\) of the preceding Bellman theorem. Write \(B_3\) for the nonnegative continuations with at least three zero coordinates. Assume every off-diagonal entry of \(\Gamma,J,J-\Gamma\) is nonzero. These are open dense conditions on the native table; no lower bound on the nonzero terminal coordinates is required.

For any compact \(K\) disjoint from \(B_3\), and any \(\eta>0\), there exists \(d>0\) with the following property. At each \(v\in K\) with \(\eta\le\sigma(v)<1\), an optimal batch, read backward from \(v\), either reaches a parent \(w\) with \(\sigma(w)=1\), or has survival at most \(1-d\). By optimal we mean \(\sigma(v)=S\sigma(w)\) for its combined survival \(S\). The batch contains at most one unrestricted Nash row and at most five ordered portions using only two exceptional owners. Each portion is one solo atom, a diffuse solo move, or a pair of geometric lists of simultaneous atoms on the same ordered dates. A geometric list can be finite of any length, or summable with one infinite end; the ratios on its consecutive physical masses are fixed by the pair data. These generalized portions mean limits of finite strategic-form timing blocks, and their cuts also attain the minimum-survival identity. In particular, on compact regions bounded away from \(B_3\) the number of batches before leaving the region or attaining one is bounded by \(1+\lfloor\log(1/\eta)/[-\log(1-d)]\rfloor\). This bound counts geometric or diffuse portions, not their physical dates.

**Reduction to two clocks.** There is \(g>0\) such that throughout \(K\) either some coordinate is at most \(-g\) or two coordinates are at least \(g\); take the minimum of \(\max(-v_{(1)},v_{(3)})\), with increasing order statistics. The negative case uses the last physical Nash row just as in the preceding theorem. Otherwise label two coordinates at least \(g\) as outsiders. Write \(C=\max(1,M,\sup_K\|v\|_\infty)\) and take \(0<\delta<\min(\eta/16,g\eta/(100C))\), assuming \(\eta\le1\). Use blocks realizing \(s=\sigma(v)\); their joint and all personal survivals are at least \(\eta/2\) eventually. If the total marginal finite mass reaches \(\delta\), choose the last date \(t\) whose remaining mass from that date through the end reaches \(\delta\). The conditional total marginal finite mass strictly after \(t\) is at most \(2\delta/\eta\). If no such date exists along a subsequence, use the entire block for this small suffix. Conditional regret vanishes by positive reach on the original block. Each positive outsider's finite deadline loses at least \(g-6C\delta/\eta>g/2\) to waiting through. Thus its total finite mass is \(o(1)\); move it to waiting through with \(o(1)\) change to each payoff and complete cap by coupling. The other two owners, denoted \(i,j\), have arbitrary signed terminal payoffs \(v_i,v_j\).

**Order representation.** On this small suffix use the unconditional-in-the-suffix finite marginal atom sizes \(a_i(t),a_j(t)\) and genuine waiting masses \(n_i,n_j\). Every limit of the latter is positive. Pass to a subsequence. Place each physical date of positive joint marginal size \(a=a_i+a_j\) at the midpoint of its interval of length \(a\) in cumulative joint marginal mass, inside \([0,2]\), and take weak limits \(\mu_i,\mu_j\) of the resulting finite measures, distinct from the waiting atoms. A positive atom of \(\mu_i+\mu_j\) is one literal date in the approximants: if two or more midpoints belong to an interval of length \(e\), their entire combined mass is at most \(2e\), by subtracting the first midpoint from the last and adding their half masses. Shrinking neighborhoods of a limiting atom therefore identify its individual masses at one common sequence of dates. Away from atoms, the mass of a date approaching a nonatomic point tends to zero and each opponent's cumulative distribution is continuous there. In particular independent samples from the two laws preserve the probabilities of each strict order and tie in the limit: use independent quantile couplings, with an equal limiting positive atom passed by the common physical date. Equal sampled nonatomic points have probability zero.

Write \(R_j(u)=\mu_j([u,2])\) and \(a_j(u)=\mu_j(\{u\})\). The difference between waiting through and taking deadline \(u\) in the pair is
\[
 \Gamma_{ij}R_j(u)+n_j v_i-J_{ij}a_j(u).                      \tag{1}
\]
This follows by cancelling all prior rival exits. On the original blocks each deadline has this difference bounded below by \(-o(1)\), since personal waiting probability stays positive and total regret vanishes. Its integral on player \(i\)'s finite marginal tends to zero. Consequently (1) vanishes \(\mu_i\)-almost everywhere. For example sample a finite date from that marginal by a fixed uniform quantile if the limiting marginal mass is positive; the rank converges almost surely. At a limiting atom use the identified physical date, and at a nonatomic point use continuity of the rival cumulative distributions. Bounded convergence gives the assertion, including the nonnegative sign at every positive-mass tested atom. The same holds with the two names interchanged.

**The five portions.** The part of \(i\)'s measure away from joint atoms lies, up to null sets, in
\[
 I_i=\{u:R_j(u)=-n_jv_i/\Gamma_{ij},\quad a_j(u)=0\}.
\]
Indeed the at most countable positive rival atoms have zero \(i\)-mass unless joint. Monotonicity of \(R_j\) makes \(I_i\) a single interval (possibly empty or a point), whose interior and included endpoints have zero rival mass. The other solo interval \(I_j\) has the same property. Their overlap is null for both clocks and may be trimmed or discarded. If one contains the other, the contained interval has no positive mass for either player there and can be discarded. The two solo intervals therefore leave at most three intervening ordered intervals carrying joint atoms alone.

For a joint atom, (1) gives
\[
  a_i(u)=\frac{\Gamma_{ji}}{J_{ji}}
           \left(R_i(u)+\frac{n_i v_j}{\Gamma_{ji}}\right).
\]
Between any two joint atoms of a single run there are finitely many other atoms: their \(i\)-masses are bounded below by the minimum of the two positive endpoint masses, by monotonicity of the displayed affine function of \(R_i\). Consecutive atoms have masses in ratio \(1-\Gamma_{ji}/J_{ji}\), by subtracting the earlier mass from \(R_i\); the other owner's ratio is \(1-\Gamma_{ij}/J_{ij}\). A nonpositive ratio forbids a run of length two. Every run is an integer interval in order, since only finitely many points lie between any two. A two-sided infinite positive geometric sequence cannot have finite total mass. This yields exactly the listed types of joint runs, including an infinite end preceding a visible atom.

Within an \(i\)-solo portion of mass \(m>0\), \(i\)'s own finite offers are unchanged when its dates are moved within the portion. Player \(j\)'s finite offers, apart from a constant from earlier exits, are \(\Gamma_{ji}b+J_{ji}a\) with \(b\) the earlier \(i\)-mass in this portion and \(a\) the current atom. Their original supremum is at least \(J_{ji}m\) if \(J_{ji}\le0\) (use the first atom or points with \(b\to0\)), or if \(\Gamma_{ji}\ge J_{ji}>0\) (use the last atom or points with \(b+a\to m\)). Thus in either case concentrate all \(m\) at a single date. Otherwise \(J_{ji}>0\) and \(\Gamma_{ji}<J_{ji}\); the same first and last tests give a supremum at least \(\max(0,\Gamma_{ji}m)\). Replace by \(N\) equally sized solo atoms and send \(N\to\infty\). The limiting maximum is at most this bound. The endpoint comparisons use points in the support of \(i\) where the original actual test passes to the limit, which exist arbitrarily close in marginal cumulative mass to either end, including any positive endpoint atom. They do not insert a new deadline after the terminal boundary.

Order across the portions and the first-exit law are preserved. To implement a canonical word, retain a growing finite portion of any infinite geometric run, assigning its vanishing discarded masses to waiting through, and perform the stated solo approximations. The offers at retained joint dates change by at most a constant times the discarded marginal masses by the cumulative formula (1). A player's offers within its own solo portion are the original ones, and the other player's new offers there cannot beat its original cap by the comparison above, up to \(o(1)\). No other physical dates are needed. Both exceptional players retain their original prescribed values and vanishing complete regret. For each positive outsider, the first-exit law determines its prescribed payoff. Because total small-suffix finite mass remains at most \(2\delta/\eta+o(1)\), waiting through still strictly dominates every finite reply for this player. Thus the whole canonical suffix realizes its former payoff and limiting survival with vanishing block regret.

If \(t\) exists, its actual row converges on a subsequence to Nash at that suffix payoff and the combined survival from \(t\) onward is at most \(1-\delta/4\) since some own marginal mass there was at least \(\delta/4\). The true caps at the beginning of the suffix converge to its prescribed values by positive reach and vanishing regret. The historical word before \(t\) has vanishing block regret at the output of the retained row: giving it the actual conditional cap in place of its terminal value preserves its start cap and only raises its prescribed payoff. The same conditioning and cap substitution applies at every retained positive-survival boundary. Grafting now gives equality \(\sigma(v)=S\sigma(w)\), with equality on the intermediate cuts. If the small suffix was the entire minimizing block, its canonical version realizes \(S=\sigma(v)>0\) with no preceding word. Grafting forces \(\sigma(w)=1\). This last case is why small total mass when two coordinates approach zero does not destroy uniform termination of the reduction.

The proof classifies an optimal family of finite-block limits. It does not claim that an infinite geometric run is an exact finite Nash word, or bound its number of physical dates. On the exact two-zero face the affine offsets vanish; the corresponding normalized two-clock geometries are also given by the earlier two-owner clock classification. The nonzero offsets and the uniform endpoint reduction permit approaching the face from either side.

## Proper supports and an explicit mass bound at a three-zero continuation

A finite four-player timing block pays a native reward Z(S) at the first nonempty quitting coalition S and a specified continuation v when all four wait through. Own singleton rewards are zero; clocks are independent. Every date and Wait are possible unilateral replacements. For a fixed T of three owners assume vᵢ=0 on T and vₖ>0 for the remaining owner k. Write Γᵢⱼ=Zᵢ({j}), Jᵢⱼ=Zᵢ({i,j}) and Lᵢⱼ=Jᵢⱼ−Γᵢⱼ (zero diagonals). Let M=max(1,max|Z|), B=max(M,‖v‖∞) and choose 0<η<min(1,vₖ/(2M+B)).

A short transition is a limit along finite blocks of arbitrary lengths with regret tending to zero and limiting absorption probability ≤η. At this limit owner k waits: a finite deadline earns at most M(η+o(1)) and Wait earns at least vₖ−(M+B)(η+o(1)). Hence its finite mass tends to zero and moving it to Wait changes all payoffs and offers by o(1). All four Wait probabilities stay bounded below by joint survival. Therefore every deadline's gain over Wait is ≤o(1), and the assigned finite mass weighted by the absolute values of those gains has sum o(1). Subselect the individual finite masses fᵢ on T and the first-coalition law. Write F=Σᵢ∈T fᵢ. All claims about a nonblank transition mean F>0 in this limit. A diffuse clock below means an approximating sequence of uniform draws over a growing interval of physical dates.

### A raw linear test valid on every table

For p in the three-owner simplex and 0≤z≤p define H(p,z)=maxᵢ∈T (Jz−Γp)ᵢ using the three-by-three matrices. Let r be the minimum of H and r∂ its minimum restricted to the boundary of the simplex; these are one and three finite linear programs respectively. Every nonblank short transition admits 0≤z≤p, pᵢ=fᵢ/F, with H(p,z)≤6MF.

Indeed at the first physical date put hⱼ=Pr(date 1 | j's finite draw) and zⱼ=pⱼhⱼ, using any hⱼ at a vanished mass. Pass to limits. For i∈T and its other two zero owners j,l, the gain over Wait upon quitting immediately on the event that j alone has a finite draw is Kᵢⱼ(hⱼ)=Jᵢⱼhⱼ−Γᵢⱼ. If both rivals are finite, the gain is at most 2M in absolute value. On the neither-finite event it is zero. Hence the difference between the actual gain and fⱼKᵢⱼ+fₗKᵢₗ is at most 6M fⱼfₗ. Wait optimality and division by F prove the claimed inequality, including when a rival has zero finite mass.

For owner j define the closed interval, possibly empty,

    Iⱼ={ h∈[0,1] : Jᵢⱼ h≤Γᵢⱼ for both i∈T∖{j} }.

Then r∂≤0 if and only if at least one Iⱼ is nonempty. A feasible vertex p=eⱼ means exactly h=zⱼ∈Iⱼ. If p has two positive coordinates a,b, the a,b inequalities require Kₐᵦ(hᵦ)≤0 and Kᵦₐ(hₐ)≤0. The remaining c inequality is pₐK꜀ₐ(hₐ)+pᵦK꜀ᵦ(hᵦ)≤0, so at least one owner also passes c's constraint. Conversely a feasible interval yields the corresponding vertex. For any j and h∈Iⱼ, j alone makes a short transition for each sufficiently small positive fⱼ: if h>0 its conditional finite dates have hazard h at each date, giving gain (1−h)ⁿfⱼKᵢⱼ(h)≤0 to a waiting zero owner at date n+1. If h=0 use a diffuse uniform block, where the possible positive gains are ≤M/N when Γᵢⱼ≥0 and |Jᵢⱼ|≤M (equivalently use the current-included tail form Jᵢⱼmⱼ−Γᵢⱼ(mⱼ+rⱼ)). The active zero owner ties Wait, and k strictly waits.

There are two quantitative consequences. If r>0 then F≥r/(6M), so a sufficiently short terminal segment must be blank. If all three Iⱼ are empty then r∂>0 and every nonblank transition satisfies

    minᵢ∈T fᵢ/F ≥ [r∂−6MF]/(4M).

For the second bound remove a least coordinate m from p and renormalize the others, keeping their hⱼ fixed. The new p differs in total absolute coordinates by 2m. Because |Kᵢⱼ|≤2M, H on the boundary is at most 6MF+4Mm. In particular F≤r∂/(12M) forces all three owners to have normalized mass at least r∂/(8M). Thus the three intervals decide, on every raw table, whether there are arbitrarily short nonblank transitions with a proper subset of T active. This includes cases where J or Γ entries vanish. If the full four-player native absorbing-regret infimum is positive, every h∈Iⱼ must satisfy Jₖⱼ h>Γₖⱼ: otherwise the same one-owner geometric (or diffuse at h=0), now with fⱼ=1, is an absorbing four-player equilibrium limit with zero native continuation. Geometric truncations with fⱼ=1 may instead keep the complete geometric clock on the original infinite timeline. Diffuse uniform clocks already absorb surely.

### Exact proper-support menu on generic directions

The blank and all single-owner short transitions have the preceding form as regards their absorption law, payoff and caps. In the single-owner case the first physical deadline of each other zero owner places a common subsequential conditional hazard h in Iⱼ, so its necessity does not presume a geometric clock. A single finite owner's prescribed payoff is zero and each other owner i receives fⱼΓᵢⱼ+(1−fⱼ)vᵢ. Suppose exactly a,b have positive finite mass. Move the o(1) finite mass of the remaining zero owner to Wait, changing all offers and prescribed values by o(1). Suppose Γₐᵦ,Γᵦₐ,Lₐᵦ,Lᵦₐ are all nonzero. Necessary and sufficient conditions for the menu with these two masses are as follows.

* Both Γᵢⱼ and Lᵢⱼ have the same sign in each direction i←j within {a,b}. Set hⱼ=Γᵢⱼ/Jᵢⱼ∈(0,1), ρⱼ=1−hⱼ, and require S=(1−fₐ)(1−fᵦ)≥1−η.
* Let c be the unique owner in T∖{a,b}. Define U and Q as its Wait and first-date Quit payoffs against both surely finite geometric clocks:

      U=[hₐρᵦΓ꜀ₐ+ρₐhᵦΓ꜀ᵦ+hₐhᵦ Z꜀({a,b})]/(1−ρₐρᵦ),
      Q=hₐρᵦJ꜀ₐ+ρₐhᵦJ꜀ᵦ+hₐhᵦ Z꜀({a,b,c}).

  Put X=fₐ(1−fᵦ)K꜀ₐ(hₐ), Y=(1−fₐ)fᵦK꜀ᵦ(hᵦ), E=fₐfᵦ(Q−U). Require

      Xρₐⁿ+Yρᵦⁿ+E(ρₐρᵦ)ⁿ ≤0  for every integer n≥0.       (1)

This is a finite test. Divide (1) by (ρₐρᵦ)ⁿ to obtain X bⁿ+Y aⁿ+E with b=1/ρᵦ, a=1/ρₐ. For b=a require X+Y≤0 and X+Y+E≤0. Otherwise swap labels if necessary so b>a>1. If X>0, or X=0<Y, the test fails. If X=0 and Y≤0, or if X<0 and Y≤0, the maximum is at n=0. When X<0<Y, the maximum is attained at the first n≥0 with (b/a)ⁿ≥Y(a−1)/[−X(b−1)], as follows by taking consecutive differences. Test (1) at that integer; equality of the consecutive values at the maximum is harmless.

For the necessity of the rates let mⱼ(t),rⱼ(t) be the atom at t and the strictly later finite mass in j's law conditional on being finite. For i=a,b the pure t gain over Wait is fⱼ[Lᵢⱼmⱼ(t)−Γᵢⱼrⱼ(t)]. With both limiting f positive, the expected absolute conditional gain on i's finite draws tends to zero. If A,B',C are the limiting strict orders and tie on the independent finite draws (A for a<b), integration yields ΓₐᵦA=LₐᵦC, ΓᵦₐB'=LᵦₐC, A+B'+C=1. Under the nonzero hypotheses all three probabilities are positive and the displayed hⱼ are forced.

To check c's necessary comparisons, the conditional finite clocks have shared geometric atoms of every fixed rank on moving physical dates. Here is the direct extraction. Choose ε>0 so |Γᵢⱼ|(1−ε)−|Lᵢⱼ|ε>0 in both directions. Before the first date where either conditional finite cumulative mass reaches ε, both absolute bracketed gains are bounded below by a common positive constant. The total assigned mass there tends to zero. At the selected date at least one atom thus has positive limiting mass after extraction; its assigned equality forces the rival atom to be hⱼ, and the rival's assigned equality forces the first atom to be hᵢ. Pass to the subsequent dates, divide by each remaining finite mass, and repeat. The remaining normalized masses after n selected dates tend to ρᵢⁿ, so no further mass survives n→∞. Thus for each fixed rank the two clocks' order, tie and remaining future law can be approximated using the first N selected dates with error O(ρₐᴺ+ρᵦᴺ), uniformly after a subsequence in the limit. Replacing deadlines of c by the respective selected physical ranks and then letting N grow gives (1). Dropping any extra physical blank dates introduces no new deviation. Against the compressed consecutive calendar its exact gain at rank n+1 is found by conditioning on which opponents are finite: the single-finite contributions are Xρₐⁿ and Yρᵦⁿ, while in the both-finite case only survival of both to that rank can matter. Geometric memorylessness gives E(ρₐρᵦ)ⁿ there.

Conversely use those two geometric clocks with independent masses fₐ,fᵦ, and let c,k always wait. Pair contacts tie at every date, (1) handles every date of c, and the short-block margin handles every date of k. Truncation approximates all menus and prescribed payoffs uniformly. Put D=1−ρₐρᵦ and (A,B',C)=(hₐρᵦ,ρₐhᵦ,hₐhᵦ)/D. The probabilities of the three nonempty first sets are uₐ=fₐ(1−fᵦ)+fₐfᵦA, uᵦ=(1−fₐ)fᵦ+fₐfᵦB', uₐᵦ=fₐfᵦC. At the parent a,b receive Γₐᵦfᵦ,Γᵦₐfₐ; for i=c,k use uₐΓᵢₐ+uᵦΓᵢᵦ+uₐᵦZᵢ({a,b})+Svᵢ. When ρₐ>ρᵦ the large-n comparison in (1) forces K꜀ₐ(hₐ)≤0, so the slower owner a satisfies hₐ∈Iₐ. At equal decay rates the large-n test gives X+Y≤0, so one owner again passes its solo interval.

### An unbounded chamber eliminating all four three-zero short blocks

The vector (Jz−Γp) ranges over the convex hull of the six columns −Γ₍·ⱼ₎ and L₍·ⱼ₎ on T, since each j mixes its two columns with weights pⱼ−zⱼ,zⱼ. In particular a nonnegative nonzero row w with strictly negative wΓ and strictly positive wL on those three columns gives an explicit r>0, using the least of the six signed margins divided by Σwᵢ.

Suppose there is a perfect matching on the four owners such that in both directions on the matched pairs Γ is strictly positive and L strictly negative, and on all remaining pairs Γ is strictly negative and L strictly positive. No magnitude restrictions enter. For each triple T its matched owners are a,b and its remaining owner is c. Set wₐ=wᵦ=1 and take w꜀ large. In column c the weighted Γ and L sums have their required strict signs already. In columns a,b the strictly signed c term dominates. Hence r>0 for every triple. At any terminal v with exactly three zeros and one positive entry, a sufficiently short suffix must be blank in the limit: F≤3η because each fᵢ is bounded by the joint absorption probability; choose 3η<r/(6M). For s=σ(v)∈(0,1), where σ is the least limiting all-Wait probability among blocks at v with regret tending to zero, choose also η<1−s. The first row which crosses η in backwards cumulative absorption is then a nonblank genuine Nash row at v. It is calibrated, namely s=d σ(u), with d its survival and u its payoff. Indeed the actual preceding histories show σ(u)≤s/d and comparison by arbitrary near-equilibrium prefixes at u gives the reverse bound. At positive s both the row and earlier cuts have positive limiting reach, so all the conditional Nash comparisons follow from original regret. This holds simultaneously on all four three-zero sets and throughout the full 24-entry sign chamber, with all higher coalition rewards arbitrary.

At two zero owners in this same chamber the two-owner order equations forbid both being finite: Γ and L have opposite signs in each direction. For matched zero owners a lone finite draw may be placed on the very first suffix date, since the quiet zero owner's L is negative. For unmatched owners Γ<0<L violates the first-date guard, so the short suffix must be blank. Thus here no diluted infinite pair is required on the two-zero sets either.

There is a corresponding restriction at v=0. The four-owner analogue of the first-date calculation gives maxᵢ(Jz−Γp)ᵢ≤3MF, because the probability of two or more finite rivals is at most F²/2 and the total error in removing the single-rival no-other-finite factors is at most 2MF². The four-owner boundary minimum r∂,₄ is positive: for any proper support choose a triple containing it, use that triple's lower bound r>0 on its rows, and take the least of the four such bounds. At any sufficiently short nonblank zero-continuation transition all four owners then have fᵢ/F≥[r∂,₄−3MF]/(4M), by the same least-coordinate deletion. This does not decide existence of a four-active transition at the origin.

The earlier common-geometric classification in “Uniform three-owner tail dispersion,” §5.3, and the earlier first-order Wait-minus-deadline expansion both apply to infinite-game sources. Here a strict positive fourth continuation removes only its own finite dates. The full quiet test for the third zero owner, the three-interval criterion, and the explicit boundary linear programs distinguish proper-support terminal transitions from cases requiring three comparably active owners. No sufficiency of the full-support linear program, uniform equilibrium for arbitrary tables, or rigidity at three active owners is asserted.

## Optimal finite-block survival away from the origin reduces to short three-player blocks

Fix centered four-player rewards Z with own singleton zero and |Z|≤M, M≥1. A finite block of private stopping times pays Z(A) on the first nonempty quitting set and v on waiting through. The empty block is allowed, even for signed v. Its only action for each player is waiting through. Write σ(v) for the minimum limiting joint survival over blocks with total Nash regret tending to zero. The cap tests every date and waiting through. Finite Nash existence, compactness and diagonal selection give this minimum. A block or a limit of blocks that is Nash at terminal value v with payoff w and survival S always satisfies σ(v)≤Sσ(w), by independent concatenation of any nearly optimal blocks at w. The prescribed and optimal payoffs are 1-Lipschitz in terminal values, uniformly in length.

### The local three-player data

Fix three active owners W, a signed terminal v and h∈(0,1). Consider all finite W timing blocks of survival at least h, retaining the first-exit probabilities p on the seven nonempty W coalitions and the waiting-through atom p₀. Put U_i(p)=Σ_A Z_i(A)p_A+v_i p₀ for all four recipients. The three true response caps compare every finite deadline of the block and waiting through. The compact upper epigraph consists of the limits (p,b) for which the three caps have limsup bounded above by b, with bounds restricted to [−C,C], C=max(M,‖v‖∞). The near-Nash slice is b_i=U_i(p) for i∈W.

There is an explicit finite approximation in this local problem. Fix 0<ξ≤1 and any rational T≥−log h, for example T=(1−h)/h for rational h. On each date let a=Σq_i. Since p₀=∏(1−q_i)≥h, the sum of a over the whole block is at most T. Keep each date with a>ξ separately. In each remaining run greedily close a cell when its total first reaches ξ. Its mass is at most 2ξ. Keep the possible unfinished cell of that run. There are at most N=1+⌈3T/ξ⌉ cells. Push each player's private deadlines to the corresponding cells; individual survival, p₀ and independence remain exact. With Q_i the chance i acts somewhere in a given small cell conditional on reaching it, ΣQ_i≤the cell mass. A change to the first coalition requires two owners to act in some small cell. Its probability is at most the sum of half the squared cell masses, hence at most ξT. The law therefore changes by at most 2ξT in ℓ¹. For any fixed tested deadline, earlier changes among its rivals have probability at most ξT. A rival acting in the tested small cell has probability at most 2ξ. Single-date cells do not change the within-cell comparison. Every compressed deadline has an original-date representative and waiting through is matched exactly as an action. The uniform change in all three caps is at most 2Cξ(T+2).

Consequently membership in the compact epigraph is equivalent to the following finite tests succeeding for every ξ=1/k. Use words of any length from zero to N, with terminal cap v_W, and compute their true law and caps by the date recursions below. Require p₀≥h, distance of their law from the target ≤2ξT in ℓ¹, and their caps ≤b+2Cξ(T+2). Compactness of the bounded-length word space gives these inequalities also on taking limits from the original attainable epigraph. Conversely the errors tend to zero and the testing words themselves realize the required limit. This is first-order real arithmetic for each degree of accuracy. The empty word is a separate choice because a blank date allows a solo reply. The aggregation estimate uses the small-cell compression argument already employed for renewable periodic blocks. Here it recovers all seven joint-coalition probabilities and all complete private caps at a supplied terminal vector.

One can alternatively keep a fixed compact edge set and use increasing polynomial degrees. The state x=(p,b) obeys p₀≥h and U_i(p)≤b_i≤C. The compact seed K={(δ₀,b):v_i≤b_i≤C}. For three-owner hazards q, write S=∏(1−q_i), a=Σq_i, e for their nonempty head coalition probabilities (zero at wait), H_i for rival survival, Q_i for forced Quit and P_i for the passive head reward. A child x and parent x' obey

    p'=e+S p,  b'_i≥Q_i,  b'_i≥P_i+H_i b_i,
    l=(1+6C)a+Σ_i(b'_i−b_i).

Every cap decrease is ≤2Ca so l≥a and d=(x'−x)/l is uniformly bounded on positive-charge edges. Take the compact semialgebraic closure E of the edge tuples (x,x',q,l,d) with l>0. For polynomial f use T_f=f(x')+[f(x')−f(x)]/l, with its secant extension at l=0. At each degree require, for every monomial up to that degree,

    f(y)=∫_K f dκ+∫_E T_f dρ,    κ,ρ≥0, κ(K)+ρ(E)=1.

Each test is a compact convex hull membership test and reduces to finite real arithmetic by Carathéodory. These tests have exactly the same individual endpoints y as the preceding word tests. One may also put a probability on the left and integrate f(y); the endpoints of any all-degree feasible probability are almost surely attainable.

Here are the details of the bounded-activity adaptation. The edge telescope puts fraction l/(1+l) of the remaining weight at each physical edge and the rest at its child, terminating at K. Conversely, enlarge probability coordinates to [−2,3], cap coordinates to [−C−2,C+2], and keep p₀≥h/2. Use the same l, impose l≥a, and permit uniform law and one-sided cap errors εl. Impose the compact one-edge upper bound l≤3(1+6C)+6C on this relaxed relation. Since 1−S≥a/3, telescoping the waiting coordinate along any such path gives (h/6)Σa≤5+εΣl. Also Σl≤(1+6C)Σa+6(C+2). Thus all path charges are uniformly bounded for ε<h/[12(1+6C)]. A relaxed path with outer endpoint near y and inner endpoint near K can be realized over the literal empty block: law error and harmful cap error are at most the seed error plus εΣl by induction on the path. If actual survival approaches a target ≥h from below, move enough finite mass of the smallest-survival player to waiting through to restore h. Its rival survival is at least h/2 eventually, and its available finite mass is bounded below when joint survival approaches h<1; the moved mass is o(1). This preserves the limiting law and caps.

If y is unattainable, some positive radii and ε exclude every relaxed path from the seed ball to the target ball. On the enlarged box take V at each outer point as the infimum over finite paths, including empty ones, of k(inner)∏(1+l)⁻¹. Use a continuous k∈[0,1] vanishing within half the excluded seed radius of K and equal to one outside the full radius. Compactness for each fixed path length makes this infimum Borel. It vanishes near K and is uniformly positive near y by the total charge bound. Concatenation gives (1+l)V(x')≤V(x). Extend V by zero outside the box and smooth by sufficiently small common positive translations of every coordinate. Every translated exact positive-charge edge still belongs to the relaxed relation: the law error is (1−S) times the translation, and raising each cap preserves its inequalities. All exact points have margin inside the enlarged box, including at p₀=h. Hence the smooth separator satisfies T_V≤0 on E by the secant formula, vanishes on K and is positive on a relative neighborhood of y. Uniform C¹ polynomial approximation on the box and the uniform bound on d extend the all-degree identities to this separator. Thus they exclude that neighborhood, also for any endpoint measure. A countable subcover completes the support argument. Nested compactness gives the all-degree measures when all finite tests succeed. This bounded-activity proof needs no terminal punishment value.

### The calibrated step

Fix a compact K of continuations excluding the origin and a lower bound e>0 on σ, with e≤1. Set C=max(M,1,sup_K‖v‖∞) and choose g>0 so that every v∈K has either some coordinate at most −g or some coordinate at least g. Choose 0<δ<min(e/16,ge/(100C)), put β=2δ/e and h=1−β. The allowed local steps at v are a single Nash row at v, or a three-owner near-Nash block law at v with p₀≥h and a quiet recipient j with v_j≥g, optionally preceded by a four-owner Nash row at that block's four payoff coordinates U(p). All raw inputs of the three-owner block are given above. Its fourth payoff is its passive reward including the specified terminal coordinate. Every one of its four complete comparisons is accounted for: j's waiting payoff is at least g−2Cβ, whereas any finite deadline earns at most Cβ since j alone gets zero. Thus j strictly waits in any nonempty such block. This remains valid on taking limits of blocks.

For every v∈K with e≤s=σ(v)<1 one of these steps, with positive survival S<1 and output w, attains

    σ(v)=Sσ(w).

It either has S≤1−d with d=min(g/(16C),δ/4)>0, or reaches σ(w)=1. Take minimizing blocks at v. Every historical cut has joint reach ≥e/2 eventually, hence its conditional regret tends to zero, by switching the complete policy only after that cut. If v_i≤−g, the last date has a limiting Nash row. When every hazard is <g/(16C), i's Quit pays at least −3C max q and waiting pays at most −g+6C max q, forcing a sure quit and contradicting the assumed small hazards. Thus the last date alone has the required drop.

If v_j≥g, measure activity by the sum of all individual finite probabilities on each interval, before conditioning. If the whole block has less than δ activity on a subsequence, waiting at its outset strictly beats every finite j deadline by the same bounded-payoff estimate. Thus j's total finite mass is o(1); move it to the terminal choice. This realizes the entire block as a three-owner near-Nash block of survival s≥1−δ, with a limiting output w. There is no earlier block. Concatenation forces σ(w)=1 since s>0.

Otherwise take the last date t from which total activity through the terminal boundary is ≥δ. The suffix strictly after t has total activity below δ, and conditional activity there is ≤β since each marginal survival is ≥e/2. The preceding comparison makes its conditional j mass o(1). Moving that mass to the terminal choice has vanishing total variation and preserves limit comparisons uniformly. Its limiting conditional law belongs to the three-player near-Nash slice with survival at least h; harmless o(1) violations of the survival bound can be repaired as above. The row at t has a subsequential Nash limit at its common child U(p), since the four actual conditional caps equal those payoffs in the limit. The combined row and suffix have survival S≤1−δ/4: some owner placed ≥δ/4 absolute first-quit mass from t onward, and conditioning can only increase this probability. All selected rows and suffixes have positive survival. The historical prefix's survival tends to s/S. At the actual conditional cap as terminal boundary its best response is the original block cap and its prescribed payoff weakly increases. Substituting the limiting output w changes each comparison by o(1). Hence σ(w)≤s/S, with the reverse inequality already supplied by concatenation. This argument also proves equality at each intermediate cut.

Iteration takes at most 1+⌊log(1/e)/[−log(1−d)]⌋ steps before leaving K or reaching σ=1, since every other step divides σ by S≤1−d. Starting at a bounded v, take K to be the entire closed shell outside a prescribed origin neighborhood up to max(M,‖v‖∞). Payoffs of the blocks stay in that outer box. The only stopping alternatives are the neighborhood and σ=1.

If the native absorbing regret infimum A>0, the lower bound e is uniform on bounded families with each v_i≥μ_i, even for signed v, where μ_i is the infimum of i's native cap over rival clocks. For a finite block of survival S choose the owner k of smallest marginal survival ≤S^(1/4). Append a tail where k's cap is at most μ_k+ε and make k surely finite there. This makes the original play absorbing. For k the true cap increases over the block cap by at most ε. For each other owner rival survival on the block is at most S^(1/4), so its cap increases by at most (M+C)S^(1/4). The prescribed payoffs change by at most (M+C)S. It follows that A≤D_block+ε+3(M+C)S^(1/4)+4(M+C)S. Moreover a block at v≥μ has true block cap at least μ_i for each i, by appending an i-specific punishment and then letting ε decrease to zero. Hence every limiting Nash parent remains in this collar. Actual conditional augmented caps of the signed minimum dominate μ coordinatewise because their Never bonuses are nonnegative. These bounds give uniform reduction on compact signed shells in that setting. The unresolved alternative is descent into arbitrarily small neighborhoods of the origin.

## The only boundary value needed by the three-player Bellman reduction

Assume throughout that the native absorbing gap A is positive. Retain the finite-block payoff and survival conventions. Write H for the convex hull of the fifteen native exit columns, μ for the native unilateral punishment infima and α=σ(0). The native absorbing gap A>0 implies α>0 by the uniform survival bound, since each μ_i≤0 against all rivals waiting forever. Moreover α is exactly the least joint Never mass among limits of native profiles with vanishing regret. Indeed finite native blocks at zero can append literal AllNever without creating an improving late reply: native own and perpetual payoffs both vanish. Conversely truncating sufficiently late native finite mass to Never approximates all complete comparisons in total variation.

Use the following fixed directed relation, so no shell-dependent choice enters the definition of a path. From a terminal value v one may move backwards across a one-date Nash row at v to its payoff w, or across a short block with strictly waiting owner j, optionally preceded by a one-date Nash row at its four resulting payoffs. In the latter case v_j>0 and the three-owner block law is any attainable near-Nash law of survival at least 1−β for some 0<β<1 satisfying (1−β)v_j−Mβ>Mβ. The four payoffs of this law include the passive recipient. Every block law is determined by the short-block tests. Label the move by the product of its survivals. Keep moves with label less than one; the empty path has product one. On the domain v≥μ every move has positive survival and its parent is still ≥μ. A finite path gives actual approximating finite timing blocks at v, with that product survival and prescribed and cap values tending to its final vertex w. This follows by concatenating its finitely many approximating words with the prescribed boundary vectors. Each conditional cap and payoff is 1-Lipschitz in that boundary. Accuracy for a finite path can be chosen after its length is specified.

Let P(v) be the infimum of survival products over all finite such paths from v. Write O(v) for the increasing limit, as r decreases to zero, of the infimum over finite paths ending in ‖w‖∞≤r. Use +∞ for an empty infimum. Then for every v≥μ,

    σ(v) = min{ P(v), α O(v) }.

In particular P and O need only one-stage four-player Nash rows and independent three-player short-block data. They are path infima; no bound on the number of steps to attain O is asserted.

For any finite path of product S to w we have σ(v)≤Sσ(w)≤S. For paths with w_n→0, approximate each path by a comparison block at v with initial cap and payoff within 1/n of w_n, and survival within 1/n of S_n. Prepend a block nearly realizing α at zero. Changing its terminal value from zero to the path's actual cap and prescribed payoff changes its regret by o(1), uniformly in its length. The concatenation thus gives σ(v)≤α liminf S_n by subsequence selection. This proves both upper bounds, including when the ball infima have to be approximated.

Take v≠0 with P(v)>σ(v). Then σ(v)<1. Fix 0<r<‖v‖∞. The off-origin theorem, on the shell up to max(M,‖v‖∞) and with positive lower survival at least σ(v), gives a finite calibrated path to σ=1 or to ‖w_r‖∞≤r. Its short blocks obey the margin in this relation by the proof of that theorem. The former alternative would give a path of product σ(v), contrary to P(v)>σ(v). For the latter write S_r for its product; σ(v)=S_rσ(w_r). The function σ is lower semicontinuous because terminal perturbations alter both prescribed payoff and cap by at most their size, uniformly over finite blocks. Passing to a convergent subsequence as r↓0 therefore gives σ(v)≥α lim S_r≥αO(v). This proves the equality. At v=0 the empty path and the preceding upper bounds also give the formula: O(0)≤1 while σ(0)≤αO(0), so O(0)=1. If σ(v)=1 every finite path has product one, and the formula follows from the upper bounds and P(v)=1.

There is a finite hull test for forcing termination. Suppose v≠0 and no finite calibrated path from v reaches σ=1. For every r the calibrated construction then ends in the ball. On any convergent subsequence as r↓0, lower semicontinuity and the upper bound from grafting at zero show that S_r→s/α and σ(w_r)→α, where s=σ(v)>0. Every path's first-exit law gives w_r=S_r v+(1−S_r)z_r with z_r∈H (choose any z_r when S_r=1). Compactness therefore forces

    s<α  and  −s v/(α−s) ∈ H.

Indeed s≤α by S_r≤1, and s=α would imply v=0. Equivalently, a finite calibrated path to σ=1 must exist whenever 0∉(s/α)v+(1−s/α)H with s<α, or whenever s≥α. This gives a linear feasibility test at each given s,α,v.

At a positive-reach cut of an optimal signed-price source, let s be its cumulative survival and v≠0 its effective cap. The exact cut identity gives σ(v)=s. The conditional native law absorbs, so v=u⁺+ρc/s for a single u⁺∈H and the original price weight c padded by zeros on winners. Consequently nontermination at any such cut requires

    −(ρ/α)c = (s/α)u⁺ + (1−s/α)z ∈ H.

If this test fails, every nonzero positive-reach cut of that source admits finite calibrated reduction to σ=1. Reaching σ=1 describes optimal timing blocks. It does not by itself settle uniform equilibrium of the original game.

The convex-hull test can be sharpened without computing additional response caps. Set λ=s/α. A nonterminating cut supplies actual limiting blocks at terminal v whose prescribed payoffs and all four caps converge to zero and whose survival converges to λ∈(0,1). Extract their full law p, with p₀=λ, and individual waiting masses t_i with product λ. Each t_i>0 makes waiting through an optimal full response in the limit. Let d_i be the first-exit law when i waits through and let H_i=λ/t_i be its survival. Coupling all clocks to the original draws shows that there are nonnegative x_i(B), indexed by all subsets B of the rivals, such that

    Σ_B x_i(B)=p_{ {i} },    d_i(∅)=λ+x_i(∅)=H_i,
    d_i(B)=p_B+p_{B∪{i}}+x_i(B)       for every nonempty rival set B.

Only an original solo first exit by i leaves the rival first coalition undetermined upon deleting i. Consequently one finite real-feasibility test requires the displayed probabilities, the product identity, and the four-player vector equality Σ_{A≠∅}p_A Z(A)+λv=0, together with

    Σ_{B≠∅}d_i(B) Z_i(B)+H_i v_i=0     for every i.

Failure rules out this nontermination alternative. Passing gives necessary constraints only.

### A common set of optimal effective caps

Use the signed price construction with weight vector c supported exactly on the losing owners and positive there. An optimum of price ρ has nonnegative private Never bonuses and zero augmented regret. At a positive-reach cut its conditional price is ρ/s and its common augmented cap and prescribed payoff are v. Each loser's realized private bonus, the product of its conditional Never mass and its conditional bonus, is c_iρ/s. All required zero winning marginals at the optimum persist on conditioning. These facts hold also for restricted minima whose constraints are stable under conditioning and prefixing.

Prefix this conditional source by the just-obtained blocks at v with limiting zero cap and payoff and survival λ=s/α. Under a prefix, multiply each private bonus by that owner's rival-survival probability; its newly realized bonus is the old realized bonus times joint survival. The effective full cap after graft is the block cap evaluated at the tail's effective cap. Its prescribed payoff is the block payoff evaluated at the tail's augmented prescribed payoff. These equalities follow by splitting a replacement into a deadline inside the block or waiting through and then optimizing. Approximate each block and tail with matching boundary errors tending to zero. Uniform terminal Lipschitz control allows the block lengths to grow. The composite thus has zero augmented regret, effective cap zero, and positive losing Never masses, since λ>0 and the conditional losing masses are positive. Winning zero marginals are preserved. The augmented-regret identity therefore makes the composite native point feasible at price ρ/α. Equivalently each losing credit has become c_iρ/α, and a finite-playing loser necessarily attains its full native cap on a finite reply in the closure.

Let W_α be the compact set of prescribed native payoff vectors of minimum-Never zero-regret limits at zero, with survival α. Choose any w∈W_α. Prefix the composite by a nearly Nash finite native block at zero of survival tending to α and payoff tending to w. Existence of such blocks follows by truncation as above. The same preceding identities give survival factor α, effective payoff and cap w, zero augmented regret, the same required clock roles, and scalar price α(ρ/α)=ρ. It is therefore a new optimum for the very same signed problem. For a genuinely unrestricted minimum this is global optimality over its whole carrier. For a restricted minimum it is optimality under those restrictions. If α=1 the only w is zero, since joint survival tending to one makes every native payoff tend to zero. Hence that case produces an optimum with effective cap zero. If several choices of weight or losing set have such a nonterminating cut, every one of their sets of optimal effective cap vectors contains the same W_α. This conclusion is conditional on nontermination at some nonzero cut of each chosen problem.

## A finite exclusion test at the origin price

Assume the centered native absorbing regret infimum A is positive. Fix a signed price problem on the same four-player table with minimum ρ>0, a nonempty proper set W of winners, positive weights c_j on its losing complement, and c_i=0 for winners. Allow specified individual winning Never probabilities to be zero in a restricted minimum. Let α=σ(0)>0 and r*=ρ/α. The origin alternative for this carrier asks whether a feasible point of price r* has all four **effective** caps zero.

There are two cases. If this point exists, any least-Never native equilibrium limit over zero, with survival α and payoff w, can be prefixed to it. This gives a price-optimal source with effective cap w and an actual limiting zero-cap cut of positive reach α. If no such point exists, then for every η>0 the positive-reach cuts s≥η of all price-optimal sources in this carrier have uniformly bounded finite calibrated reductions to σ=1. Each step uses a one-date four-player Nash row, a short three-player block, or the stated combination of the two.

For the uniform assertion, all the effective starting caps v at these cuts stay bounded since v=u⁺+ρc/s with u⁺ in the native exit hull. All subsequent block payoffs remain uniformly bounded. Suppose calibrated endpoints w_n along finite paths from such cuts approached zero. Write S_n for the path products and s_n for the initial reaches. Then s_n=S_n σ(w_n), so S_n≥η. Extract limits S,s. Lower semicontinuity gives α S≤s. Attach the path to the corresponding conditional source of price ρ/s_n. Upon choosing sufficiently accurate individual block and source approximations, the resulting limiting carrier point has effective cap zero and price ρS/s. All required zero winning marginals persist. Every losing marginal stays positive: the prices ρ/s_n are bounded below by ρ and feasibility gives N_j≥min(1,c_jρ/(2M)) before prefixing, while its marginal survival through the path is at least S_n. Prepending any optimal native α-block now gives a feasible point at price αρS/s, so minimality forces s≤αS. This produces exactly the excluded zero-cap point at r*. Thus these calibrated vertices stay uniformly away from zero. Choose a smaller avoided origin ball and apply the bounded-step shell reduction; it must stop at σ=1 in uniformly many steps. This also covers the empty path at the initial cut. Reaching σ=1 concerns block survival.

### Testing a price with the established common clock

Let C be the compact closure of original native clock data (U,B,e,N): prescribed payoff, full unilateral cap, payoff for literal Never, and individual Never probability. Use only the original winning role conditions ∏_{i∈W}N_i=0 and any prescribed winning zero coordinates. Write M≥1 for an absolute native reward bound. For r>0 form the residual

    G_r=max(0, max_i |U_i+c_i r|, max_i B_i,
               max_{j outside W}|N_j e_j+c_j r|).

Then D(r), its minimum on this closed role-restricted native carrier, vanishes exactly when a zero-effective-cap point of price r exists. In fact the constraints force each losing N_j>0 and its Never bonus λ_j=c_jr/N_j to satisfy e_j+λ_j=0. The prescribed augmented payoff is zero and the native full cap is at most zero, so all augmented regrets vanish. A finite-playing loser has native cap zero by averaging its finite assigned offers; a pure-Never loser can have smaller native cap. Winners' ordinary regrets vanish by the same comparison. Conversely augmented optimality and N_j>0 force e_j+λ_j=0, U_i=−c_i r and B_i≤0. A minimum exists by compactness, without strict constraints on the N_j in the searched carrier.

For integer k≥1 minimize G_r on independent stopping laws supported on the first 8k+1 finite dates and Never, with the same exact winning roles, and call the result D_k(r). Then

    D(r) ≤ D_k(r) ≤ D(r)+12M/k.

The common quantile-clock hierarchy already supplies the support length and the raw comparison error 12M/k for U and B, and preserves every N_i exactly. Its forward deviation estimate on the same compressed profile, with the original pure time set to Never, also transports e_i by at most 12M/k: the quotient maps Never to itself. Thus every term of G_r moves by at most that error, including N_j e_j. For a limiting carrier point compress its actual approximants at this fixed k and extract in the compact finite law simplices. The resulting finite clocks have exactly its limiting winning zero marginals, even if the original approximants only approach them. The finite profiles themselves belong to the native carrier, proving the other bound.

Each finite problem is real polynomial optimization on simplices. For a finite clock the full cap is the maximum of the finite-date offer polynomials and literal Never, since a still-later solo deadline against surviving rivals has the same native value as Never. For every fixed or exactly specified r one has D(r)>0 if and only if D_k(r)>12M/k for some k; all k>12M/D(r) reject in that case. For a supplied compact interval I in positive prices one can minimize both sides over r∈I, with the identical error guarantee and cutoff using min_I D. In particular an independently certified price interval containing ρ/α can select the uniform finite branch in one finite test. An uncertified estimate of the ratio is insufficient for that conclusion. A rejection on the full interval [0,M/max_j c_j] also suffices without a supplied price ratio: a zero-cap point necessarily has c_j r≤M for each losing j, and at r=0 the residual could vanish only at an absorbing native zero-regret point, excluded by A>0. The new constraints in this calculation concern one joint private-clock realization and the signed origin price. The clock compression and its quantitative rate are established results.
