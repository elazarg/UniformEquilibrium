## 2. Exact bridge from the paid splice to the cap port

\[

\qquad

\qquad

\tag{2.1}
\]

\[
d_p(P_n)-d_p(Y_n)=G_n.
\tag{2.2}

In particular,

\[
d_p(P_n)\ge G_n>G_0.
\tag{2.3}

\tag{2.4}

\tag{2.5}

\tag{2.5a}

## 2.1 A stronger bridge: one fixed response curl

The paid splice also enters the four-profile response-curl waist without any
additional compactness assumption.  Since $D(P_n)\to D_*$ and every target is
a carrier point,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j\ne p}\bigl(d_j(Y_n)-d_j(P_n)\bigr)
 &=D(Y_n)-D(P_n)+G_n\\
 &\ge G_0-o(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.7}
\]

After deleting finitely many ranks and applying finite pigeonhole, one fixed
nonmover $j$ satisfies

\[
d_j(Y_n)-d_j(P_n)>G_0/6.
\tag{2.8}
\]

Choose errors $\varepsilon_n\downarrow0$, with
$\varepsilon_n<G_0/24$, and one pure-time response $a_n$ of $j$ against
$Y_n$ within $\varepsilon_n$ of its unrestricted cap.  Put

\[
R_n=Y_n[j\leftarrow a_n],
\qquad
Q_n=P_n[j\leftarrow a_n].
\tag{2.9}
\]

The $p$- and $j$-updates commute.  Moreover

\[
d_j(R_n)\le \varepsilon_n\longrightarrow0,
\tag{2.10}
\]

and the common-response payoff curl obeys

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_n
 &:=[U_j(R_n)-U_j(Y_n)]-[U_j(Q_n)-U_j(P_n)]\\
 &\ge d_j(Y_n)-\varepsilon_n-d_j(P_n)\\
 &>G_0/8.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.11}
\]

Thus the postmark paid arm supplies one literal, source-attached response
rectangle with a fixed curl and vanishing observer debt after taking the
response tolerance to zero.  This is stronger than forgetting the two-cut
edge and reselecting an arbitrary terminal-gap row.

It still is not a consumer.  The reviewed response-curl theorem splits (2.11)
into a stable-regret-rise arm or an optimizer-switch arm; the latter further
gives a bounded active-response face or an escaping Never/timing bubble.  No
existing theorem turns either output into a renewable source rank.  The
positive-minimum information used in (2.7) is only the aggregate leakage
lower bound; it gives no coordinatewise preservation of old zeros.

## 4. Exact fully reached regression

The following table shows why a positive-hazard block need not have a
near-minimum exit, even when all local reach and exact-prefix data are ideal.

Let (I=\{0,1,2,3\}).  For every nonempty coalition (S), define

\[
r_0(S)=0,
\qquad
r_1(S)=1,
\qquad
r_2(S)=\mathbf 1_{\{2\in S\}},
\qquad
r_3(S)=0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For (N\ge1), let (sigma_N) prescribe:

* player (0): `QuitAt N`;
* players (1,2,3): `Never`.

The terminal coalition is surely ({0}), so

\[
U(\sigma_N)=(0,1,0,0).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Against these opponents:

* player (0)'s cap is (0);
* player (1)'s cap is (1), already attained by every terminating outcome;
* player (2)'s cap is (1), attained by quitting at any date no later than
  (N); and
* player (3)'s cap is (0).

Therefore

\[
B(\sigma_N)=(0,1,1,0),
\qquad
d(\sigma_N)=(0,0,1,0),
\qquad
D(\sigma_N)=1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Player (2)'s prescribed `Never` is a support atom of mass one.  Replacing it
by `QuitAt 0` is an actual complete unilateral response of gain one.  Its
first disagreement is date zero and has joint reach one.

Every all-Continue root is exact against (B(\sigma_N)), because the
singleton Quit endpoints are respectively

\[
(0,1,1,0)=B(\sigma_N).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus one may prefix arbitrarily many exact all-Continue roots.  They retain
the source pair, shift the paid row, and reach it with probability one.

Now inspect suffixes.  For every (0\le k\le N),
(operatorname{suffix}_k\sigma_N) is the same construction with player (0)
quitting at date (N-k), so it still has (4.2)--(4.3).  After that sure quit,

\[
\operatorname{suffix}_{N+1}\sigma_N=\text{all Never}.
\]

At all Never,

\[
U=0,
\qquad B=(0,1,1,0),
\qquad D=2.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The only positive prescribed hazard in (sigma_N) is player (0)'s unit
hazard at date (N).  Consequently every finite block with positive total
hazard must cross that date, and its exit suffix has debt (2), not the
source debt (1).  Every block whose exit suffix has debt (1) has zero
hazard.

This simultaneously realizes:

* arbitrary-depth compatible exact prefix ancestry;
* uniform paid-row reach and unit behavioral gain;
* a literal later unit-hazard event; and
* a fixed strict exit-debt rise.

It defeats the implication

\[
\text{uniformly reached paid row + later hazard}
\Longrightarrow
\text{positive-hazard near-return suffix}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The regression is not a counterexample to uniform equilibrium.  The profile
in which player (2) quits immediately has zero debt, so the table's global
minimum is zero.  Thus (4.6) might still be rescued by a theorem using the
actual positive global minimum and its hard source provenance.  It cannot be
rescued from the local reach, paid-row, hazard, or exact-prefix fields.

## 7. A charged maximal root cannot leave only affine cap translation

The generic maximal-root reduction has a charged arm in which one fixed
nonmover cap moves by a macroscopic amount.  Its affine-translation
regression correctly shows that this *selected* displacement need not change
any difference between that observer's responses.  The complete
positive-minimum source, however, forces a non-affine response effect in
some (possibly different) nonmover coordinate.

Let actual profiles `P_n,Y_n` differ only in player `p`'s complete strategy,
and assume

\[
 D(P_n)\longrightarrow D_*,\qquad
 U_p(Y_n)-U_p(P_n)\ge G>0,\qquad
 d_p(Y_n)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Own-cap invariance gives

\[
 d_p(Y_n)-d_p(P_n)
 =-[U_p(Y_n)-U_p(P_n)].
\tag{7.2}
\]

Since every `Y_n` is an actual carrier point, `D(Y_n)>=D_*`.  Hence,
eventually,

\[
 \sum_{j\ne p}\bigl(d_j(Y_n)-d_j(P_n)\bigr)
 =D(Y_n)-D(P_n)+U_p(Y_n)-U_p(P_n)
 \ge {3G\over4}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

After fixing one of the three nonmovers along a subsequence,

\[
 d_j(Y_n)-d_j(P_n)\ge {G\over4}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Choose a pure stopping time `a_n` whose payoff against `(Y_n)_{-j}` is
within `G/16` of `B_j(Y_n)`.  Player `j`'s prescribed complete strategy
`c_n` is literally the same in `P_n` and `Y_n`.  Therefore

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &[U_j(Y_n[j\leftarrow a_n])-U_j(Y_n)]
  -[U_j(P_n[j\leftarrow a_n])-U_j(P_n)]\\
 &\qquad\ge d_j(Y_n)-d_j(P_n)-{G\over16}
 \ge {3G\over16}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Thus the full paid-response packet always carries a fixed non-affine common-
response rectangle.  If every response value of every nonmover translated by
the same amount as its prescribed payoff, all three nonmover debts would be
unchanged, and (7.3) would be impossible.

When the maximal exact target root has absorption at least `alpha`, the
reviewed root budget independently supplies a fixed nonmover `k` with

\[
 |B_k(P_n)-B_k(Y_n)|\ge {\alpha D_*\over6}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Equations (7.5) and (7.6) coexist on one source subsequence.  They need not
use the same observer.  Even when `j=k`, (7.5) compares alternative whole
responses across the horizontal `p`-edge, whereas the exact root in (7.6)
is a predecessor only of the `Y_n` tail.  No theorem identifies the
response rectangle with a profitable action at that predecessor root or
makes the resulting four profiles a forward chronology.

This removes “pure affine translation of all caps” as a possible description
of the charged arm, but it does not consume it.  The response rectangle is
exactly the already checked endpoint-debt-rise output; the missing datum is
still **root/observer alignment or an extension-compatible splice**.  In
particular, the local affine regression for one observer is valid but cannot
serve as a full-source regression against (7.5).
