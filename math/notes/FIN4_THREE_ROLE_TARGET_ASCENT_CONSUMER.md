# Consume a four-player three-role target ascent

## Mathematical data

Let (z_*) be a source-attached minimum terminal-semantic/law point with
positive total debt (D_*).  Assume an actual sequence of source and endpoint
profiles with fixed mover (p), fixed recipient (q\ne p), and one routed
finite terminal atom of fixed positive mass.

At the joint endpoint limit (y):

- the mover's debt decreases by a fixed positive amount;
- the recipient's debt increases by a fixed positive amount;
- the actual endpoint profiles and joint terminal laws converge to (y); and
- (D(y)>D_*).

The source chronology, endpoint profiles, routed atom, and roles are part of
the data.

## Question

Produce one of:

1. terminal approximate Nash profiles for every positive error;
2. a uniform-equilibrium payoff;
3. a positive cumulative admissible-payoff near-return;
4. a transition into another stated source-attached completion component;
5. a regenerated minimum source with a strict finite rank decrease and a
   terminal consumer; or
6. a contradiction to the four-player hard residual.

An explicit all-behavior positive-gap table realizing the complete ascent is
an acceptable refutation.

## Main obstruction

The target lies strictly above the minimum fibre.  Moving it toward a minimizer
can lose the literal endpoint sequence, routed atom, fixed roles, and paid
edge.  Conversely, copying the endpoint operation through an exact outer
prefix need not preserve Nash--Bellman validity because other players'
unrestricted caps may change.

The required operation is a source-faithful normalization, return, or finite
rank transition which co-realizes the incoming minimum source and the actual
endpoint law.

## Nonanswers

- selecting an unrelated minimum point or atom;
- retaining only the semantic target and discarding its profiles or law;
- observing that total debt can descend from (D(y)) toward (D_*);
- another endpoint classification without finite rank;
- a response square or paid row without executable return; or
- invoking a canonical-pair result without proving that this ascent enters its
  hypotheses.
