# A coupled Nash and pair-return fixed point on every protected two-zero face

Normalize finite absorbing rewards by the own singleton level of their recipient. For player $r$, let $P_r(q)$ be the expected native passive reward on a nonempty rival exit, $Q_r(q)$ its expected native immediate Quit reward, and $H_r(q)$ the probability of no rival exit. Both native reward fields give zero against the empty rival set. Let $\Gamma_{rt}$ and $C_{rt}$ denote the respective passive and joining rewards when $t$ is the only rival to act, with zero diagonal. Assume $P_r+Q_r\ge0$ at every pure rival coalition and that no one-stage Nash row at native continuation zero has a sure quitter. These are raw tests. The latter also excludes a sure Nash row at every nonnegative continuation, since lowering the continuation only reinforces the sole sure player's choice and does not change the other players' comparisons. Fix two players $i,j$ with $\Gamma_{ij},\Gamma_{ji}<0$ and $C_{ij},C_{ji}>0$. Write $B$ for the other two players. Under strict protection $P_r+Q_r>0$ at each nonempty rival set, both joining conditions follow from the passive signs. The no-mutually-negative-singleton-pair theorem already disposes of the original existence problem when no pair has these passive signs.

**The reset.** Given a nonnegative vector $Y$, define an operation that returns the pair's values to zero. For $(r,s)=(i,j),(j,i)$ put $a_s=-\Gamma_{rs}>0$, $\theta_s=C_{rs}/(C_{rs}+a_s)\in(0,1)$ and $t_s=Y_r/(a_s+Y_r)$. In the $n$th prefixed row ($n=0,1,\ldots$), use pair hazards $h_s=(1-\theta_s)u_s/(1-\theta_s u_s)$ with $u_s=t_s\theta_s^n$, keeping the observers in $B$ quiet. The pair's indifferences follow from $h_s=v_r/(C_{rs}+a_s+v_r)$ and $v_r'=C_{rs}h_s$, which multiplies $v_r/(a_s+v_r)$ by $\theta_s$. Each finite prefix is played in reverse order. In the limiting arm, player $s$ attempts with probability $t_s$ and, conditional on an attempt, its index has probabilities $(1-\theta_s)\theta_s^n$. This follows by telescoping $1-h_s=(1-u_s)/(1-\theta_su_s)$. The larger of the two independent indices quits first.

For observer $k$, let $M_k$ be its expected passive reward conditional on both attempting. The relative probabilities of $i$ first, $j$ first and a tie are $\theta_i(1-\theta_j)$, $\theta_j(1-\theta_i)$ and $(1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j)$, divided by $1-\theta_i\theta_j$. Thus the unconditioned passive contribution is
\[
B_k(u)=\Gamma_{ki}u_i(1-u_j)+\Gamma_{kj}u_j(1-u_i)+M_ku_i u_j.
\]
Its value after the complete reset, assuming it always waits, is $R_k(Y)=(1-t_i)(1-t_j)Y_k+B_k(t)$. This defines a continuous rational map even when waiting would be unprofitable. The exact quiet inequalities can be checked at $u=(t_i\theta_i^n,t_j\theta_j^n)$, $n\ge0$. At each such pair require
\[
 R_k(Y)-B_k(\theta_i u_i,\theta_j u_j)\ \ge\ C_{ki}(1-\theta_i)u_i(1-u_j)+C_{kj}(1-\theta_j)u_j(1-u_i)+C_{k,ij}(1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j)u_i u_j,
\]
where $C_{k,ij}$ is the native reward for $k$ upon the triple $kij$. The left side is the assigned Wait value after this row multiplied by $(1-\theta_i u_i)(1-\theta_j u_j)$, by applying the same return formula to the remaining arm. The right side is the immediate Quit offer times that denominator. The difference is bilinear, so checking the four corners of $[0,t_i]\times[0,t_j]$ for each observer is a finite sufficient test.

**A guaranteed coupled root.** Fix $R$ strictly larger than the absolute value of every native reward, with $R>0$. For every $0<\eta<R$ there is a native continuation $w$ with $w_i=w_j=0$, $w_B\in[\eta,R)^2$, and a proper nonblank ordinary Nash row $q$ at that exact $w$. If $Y$ is its vector of Nash values, the observer coordinates satisfy
\[
w_k=\max\{\eta,R_k(Y)\}\quad(k\in B).
\]
No actual continuation, root, or continuous selection of equilibria is supplied as input.

Indeed apply Brouwer on the product of the hazard cube and the two-coordinate observer box. For $\varepsilon>0$ replace each hazard by the projection to $[0,1]$ of $q_r+Q_r-P_r-H_rw_r+\varepsilon {\bf1}_{r\in\{i,j\}}$. Replace the observer coordinates by the projection of $R_k(Y)$ to the observer interval, taking $Y_r=\max(Q_r,P_r+H_rw_r)$ throughout the domain. Protection makes $Y\ge0$, so these maps are continuous everywhere. Take a convergent subsequence of fixed points as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. This gives the exact Nash comparisons and reset equations. The limit cannot be blank: near a blank the two strictly positive observer continuations force both observers to wait for sure in the perturbed game, but then $i$ has strictly positive advantage $\varepsilon+(C_{ij}-\Gamma_{ij})q_j$, forcing $q_i=1$. The limit is proper by the no-sure hypothesis. Some observer must act, because at a pair-only row each positive pair hazard makes its counterpart strictly prefer Quit. A sole observer cannot mix against empty rivals and its own positive continuation. Thus at least two clocks act, and every player faces positive rival absorption in this row. For $w_k=R$ its optimal prescribed Wait value is strictly below $R$, since all passive rewards are below $R$ and $H_k<1$. The reset also yields a value strictly below $R$. This excludes upper fixed coordinates and upper clipping. If $a=\sum q_r$, any active observer's tie gives $\eta(1-a)\le2Ra$ when $a<1$, hence $a\ge\eta/(2R+\eta)$ always.

An unclipped root satisfying its quiet inequalities gives an original uniform-equilibrium payoff $s+w$. Truncate the reverse pair arm and repeat the finite word with the one-stage row. Its sole seam tends to zero, and each deviator faces fixed positive rival absorption per word already on the Nash row. The assigned Wait recursions and single stopping-date comparisons bound all terminal regrets and target errors by a constant times the seam. Each fixed word has geometric survival uniformly over replacements, giving the long-horizon assertion; signed original own levels add the same constants under all replacements.

**Removing the artificial floor.** Take a sequence $\eta\downarrow0$ and any such roots passing the true quiet inequalities. In that case $R_k(Y)\ge0$ since every finite assigned Wait step is optimal, the child values start nonnegative and protection keeps the optimal value nonnegative. Thus the seam of the complete word is at most $\eta$. If along a subsequence $q$ tends to a nonblank row, continuity gives an exact closed validated root at floor zero. It supplies the same construction whenever every deviator faces some rival exit in that limiting word. Under strict protection this last condition is automatic: a sole limiting mover in the pair is impossible by its counterpart's strict gain; a sole outside mover gives both pair members strictly positive $Y$ by protection, hence positive reset clocks.

Under strict protection the construction also succeeds if $a\to0$ and $\eta/a\to0$. Indeed a limiting direction $x=\lim q/a$ cannot be a pair singleton, since the other pair member's forced Wait value would be $a\Gamma_{rs}+o(a)<0$, violating protection. Each pair member thus faces rival hazard of at least a fixed multiple of $a$ on the subsequence. The minimum strict pure-cell margin yields $Y_r\ge(P_r+Q_r)/2\ge c a$ for $r=i,j$. Each reset clock attempts with probability at least $c'a$, so under any unilateral deletion one period absorbs with probability at least $c''a$ after sufficiently accurate finite truncation. The seam-to-absorption ratio tends to zero, which again gives vanishing original terminal regret and a fixed target by compactness. This proves a useful sufficient test: if the finitely specified corner guards hold on all Nash-and-clipping roots for some sequence of floors and their total rates dominate the floors, a uniform equilibrium payoff exists.

**The remaining small-rate directions.** Write $K=C-\Gamma$ for the matrix of linear joining gains. With $a\to0$ take subsequences such that $x=q/a\to x^*$ in the simplex, $w\to w^*$, and $\eta/a\to\lambda$ finite. Suppress stars on $x$. Properness implies $(Kx)_r\le0$, $x_r(Kx)_r=0$ for $r=i,j$, as well as $Y_r/a\to(\Gamma x)_r\ge0$. Define $y_k=x_k$ for the observers, $y_s=x_s+(\Gamma x)_r/a_s$ for the two pair members $s$ with opposite member $r$. This is the first-order total hazard of the initial date and its entire pair reset in units of $a$, so $(\Gamma y)_i=(\Gamma y)_j=0$. For an observer $k$ the complete Wait return obeys
\[
\frac{R_k(Y)-w_k}{a}\ \longrightarrow\ (\Gamma y)_k-(\|y\|_1-x_k)w^*_k.
\]
This follows directly from the bilinear return formula, $Y_k=P_k+H_kw_k$ and $t_s/a\to(\Gamma x)_r/a_s$. The right side is nonpositive and is zero along any subsequence on which $w_k>\eta$. If $w_k^*>0$ we have $x_k=0$ since its stage Wait is strictly optimal, hence $(\Gamma y)_k=\|y\|_1 w_k^*>0$. Otherwise $(\Gamma y)_k\le0$. After taking further subsequences set $\kappa_k=\lim w_k/a\in[\lambda,\infty]$. If $x_k>0$, its exact tie gives $\kappa_k=(Kx)_k$. If $\kappa_k$ is finite, then $\kappa_k\ge(Kx)_k$ by its Wait inequality. A strictly negative return drift forces $w_k=\eta$ eventually, so $\kappa_k=\lambda$. In that case a valid quiet arm would also force $\lambda+(\Gamma y)_k\ge0$ by its nonnegative final value. Thus a strict-protection counterexample for which all the corner guards pass must admit such a direction with $\lambda>0$ on any sequence of vanishing floors. These are raw linear or polynomial necessary conditions after partitioning the two observer faces. The test does not infer existence from a direction passing them.

For a simpler sufficient test write $A=\{i,j\}$, $H=-\Gamma_{AA}^{-1}\Gamma_{AB}$ and $S=\Gamma_{BB}+\Gamma_{BA}H$. Then $x_B\ne0$, as otherwise the strictly positive pair joining gains would contradict $(Kx)_A\le0$. We have $y_A=Hx_B\ge0$, $\Gamma_B y=Sx_B$. If every $0\ne z\ge0$ with $Hz\ge0$ has some $z_k>0$ and $(Sz)_k>0$, collapse is impossible. The test involves homogeneous linear inequalities in two variables. For a fixed algebraic root and table even the exact quiet checks are finite: their differences are sums of four geometric sequences with bases $1,\theta_i,\theta_j,\theta_i\theta_j$. Group equal bases. The sign is eventually the sign of the largest base with nonzero coefficient, and a finite cutoff follows once the sum of absolute remaining terms is smaller than that term.

**The one-head limit on an actual equilibrium class.** Use the original uniform-equilibrium example with compulsory grand first exits. Its native additive matrix is
\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}0&-8&6&-2\\9&0&2&-10\\-7&15&0&-4\\14&-3&-4&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The native passive field is $g=Gq$ and the joining field is $-g+b$ with
\[
b=((L+J_0q_2)q_1q_3,(K+Nq_0)q_2q_3,J_2q_0q_1q_3,0).
\]
Use own levels one and fix $L,K,J_0,J_2$ in the successive compulsory-grand ranges of that theorem. For all sufficiently large $N$ there is a positive regret lower bound for every periodic profile in which player 0 has positive hazard at at most one date per period. The period lengths, the other three hazards on every date and the location of the special date are unrestricted. In particular neither eligible mutually negative pair ($13$ and $23$) can yield a genuinely closed one-head return. The obstruction already holds for arbitrary behavior of the other three clocks throughout each period. Each fixed table's regret floor persists in a whole raw reward neighborhood by the uniform Lipschitz dependence of original regret on rewards.

To prove the bound, first fix the table. Under a contrary sequence of regrets tending to zero its compulsory grand first-exit bound is $d_N>0$. The common-date conclusion gives a copy of the special date (the head) with original reach and all four hazards bounded below by positive constants. The no-sure test bounds all four away from one on any date of low conditional regret. Indeed a limiting sure hazard with vanishing one-step loss at its bounded child best-response caps would be a sure Nash row there, hence also at punishment. Following the prescribed calendar to this head or its immediate successor therefore gives restart regret tending to zero. From the successor any grand first exit requires survival to the next copy of the head, so the cumulative survival through this entire restarted period is bounded below, also if the period has length one. Consequently the conditional regrets tend uniformly to zero throughout that period and all its hazards are bounded away from one. All these constants may depend on $N$.

Write $w_r(t)$ for the optimal native cap at phase $t$, nonnegative by half-hazard security. Every deletion absorbs geometrically per period because all four clocks have a positive head hazard. The cap dominates the Wait and Quit offers; its excess over the prescribed mixture of those offers is nonnegative, with survival-weighted sum over a period bounded by the restarted regret by telescoping against adherence. The Wait action has prescribed weight bounded away from zero everywhere, so
\[
 w_r(t)=g_r(t)+H_r(t)w_r(t+1)+\ell_r(t),\qquad \ell_r(t)\ge0,\qquad \sum_{t\text{ in period}}\ell_r(t)=o(1).
\]
At the head the Quit excess is also $o(1)$ for every player. Iterating Wait over successive periods gives $\sup_t w_r(t)\le C+o(1)$ for a common bound $C$ on native passive outcomes, independent of $N$: the total error is bounded by the one-period sum divided by its deletion absorption probability. Take the regret limit first at each fixed $N$.

The period sum gives $\sum_t g_r(t)\ge-o(1)$ since the remaining discount terms $(1-H_r)w_r(t+1)$ are nonnegative. At the head, the native Quit offer of 3 is $-g_3$, giving $g_3\le o(1)$. Everywhere else $q_0=0$ and $g_3=-3q_1-4q_2$. Thus the ordinary dates' total $q_1+q_2$ is $o(1)$, and $g_3$ at the head is $o(1)$. For every $r\ne3$, $\Gamma_{r3}<0$, so the total ordinary $g_r$ is bounded above by a constant times that same vanishing sum. Combining with the two head comparisons and extracting a convergent head at fixed $N$ gives $q>0$, $Gq\ge0$, $(Gq)_3=0$, $2(Gq)_r\le b_r(q)\le C'$ for fixed $C'$ independent of $N$.

On the compact simplex section $z\ge0$, $\sum z_r=1$, $Gz\ge0$, the first three coordinates are bounded below by a common positive constant. Indeed $z_0=0$ forces all zero by rows 3,1; $z_1=0$ by rows 2,3; and $z_2=0$ by rows 0,2. Thus $q_0,q_1,q_2\ge ca$. If such sequences existed for arbitrarily large $N$, their limiting heads would have $Nq_0q_2q_3\le C'$, forcing $q_3\to0$ since $q_3\le a$. The head inequalities yield $0\le g_0/a,g_2/a\le C_{L,J_0,J_2}q_3\to0$. A simplex limit of $q/a$ would satisfy $Gz=\lambda e_1$, $\lambda\ge0$, impossible as $G^{-1}e_1=(-160,-196,-413,-455)/2284$ is strictly negative.

In fact the same argument gives constants $c,\delta>0$ at each fixed table such that every periodic profile with regret at most $\delta$ satisfies $\sum_{t\text{ in period}}q_0(t)-\max_t q_0(t)\ge c$. Otherwise let this difference $D$ tend to zero together with regret. The common-date lower bound on each hazard makes that date the unique largest $q_0$ row for large indices. From any phase, grand first-exit mass at other dates is at most $D/h_N$, where $h_N>0$ bounds the probability of absorption on the head: an entire period has probability at most $D$ of an off-head grand exit by the union bound, and successive period survivals decrease geometrically. The successor's grand mass due to future head dates therefore remains at least $d_N-D/h_N$, which again bounds its survival until the next head. In the period sum, the total ordinary $g_3$ becomes $14D-\sum(3q_1+4q_2)$, and the other upper bounds gain at most $O(D)$. All head conclusions follow unchanged. This lower bound controls physical hazards summed over a period, allowing its length to grow with the accuracy.

The lower clipping can genuinely bind on this same family with every quiet guard satisfied. For pair $13$ take $q=(x,14x/3,0,9x/10)$, $w_0=\eta$ and $w_2=g_2/(1-H_2)$. Here $g=(-587x/15,0,297x/5,0)$. The sole remaining tie is $H_0\eta=1174x/15+(21L/5)x^2$, with positive small solution $x\sim15\eta/1174$. The pair arm is blank, $w_2\to1782/197>0$, and the 0 output is $587x/15+(21L/5)x^2\sim\eta/2$. It clips strictly to $\eta$. Player 2 strictly prefers Wait for small $\eta$ and the pair has no quiet loss. The seam divided by one-word absorption tends to $1174/197>0$. This branch persists under the small bias at each fixed floor: the two biased ties now require $g_1=g_3=\varepsilon/2$, giving $q_1=(14x-\varepsilon/2)/3$, $q_3=(9x-\varepsilon/2)/10$. The 0 tie retains its simple positive root. The resulting reset has total hazard $O(\varepsilon)$; the strictly clipped value and observer 2's strict Wait and affine fixed point survive.

The pair projection and four-corner algebra already appear in Section 7 of *Two-zero continuation reductions*. The additional conclusion here is existence of the coupled clipped Nash source at every positive floor, its physical implementation when clipping vanishes sufficiently quickly, and the explicit obstruction at the remaining scale. The construction classifies neither all Nash profiles nor all counterexamples to the four-player conjecture.
