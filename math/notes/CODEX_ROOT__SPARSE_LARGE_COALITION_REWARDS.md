# Large coalition rewards selected by the support of singleton payoffs

Four players choose Continue or Quit independently at each live date. The first nonempty quitting coalition $S$ pays $r_i(S)$ to player $i$. Live play and perpetual continuation pay zero. Write $s_i=r_i(\{i\})$, $Z_i(S)=r_i(S)-s_i$ on finite exits and $\Gamma_{ij}=Z_i(\{j\})$ off the zero diagonal. The own levels may have either sign.

For every nonsingleton recipient coordinate $a=(i,S)$ put $d_a=|S|-1$ and $b_a=Z_i(S)$ if $i\in S$, $b_a=-Z_i(S)$ otherwise. Fix selected coordinates $A$. Call a direction admissible when $x\ge0$, $\max_jx_j=1$ and $\Gamma x\ge0$. Require that for every admissible direction some $a=(i,S)\in A$ satisfies $x_j>0$ for all $j\in S\setminus\{i\}$. This depends only on $\Gamma$ and the selected coordinates. One may test it on the supports by linear feasibility, setting excluded coordinates to zero and normalizing with $\sum x_j=1$.

**Theorem.** Suppose $\Gamma$ is $R_0$ and the local degree of $x\mapsto\min(x,\Gamma x)$ at zero is nonzero. There are $K,\eta,T_0>0$, depending only on $\Gamma,A$, such that whenever $T\ge T_0$ and
\[
 b_a\ge T^{d_a}\quad(a\in A),\qquad b_a\ge-\eta T^{d_a}\quad(a\notin A),
\]
the game has an exact stationary terminal Nash profile with at least two positive hazards and $0<\max_j q_j<K/T$. It supplies a fixed original uniform-equilibrium payoff. All favorable rewards may overshoot their thresholds independently, including participant rewards at pairs, triples and the grand coalition as well as negative outsider rewards at nonsingleton coalitions. No bound on their ratios is needed. In the original four-player UE problem a necessary condition for a counterexample is that $\Gamma$ is $R_0$ of degree one. Consequently failure of this matrix condition already gives UE for every completion of the singleton data. The stationary conclusion above is asserted on its stated matrix branch.

**Proof.** For a row $q$ let $H_i=\prod_{j\ne i}(1-q_j)$ and let $P_i,Q_i$ be the expected native rewards from waiting and from quitting at that date, with zero contribution to $P_i$ if nobody else acts. Define $B_i=P_i-(1-H_i)Q_i$. On the compact set $D=\{x\ge0:\max x=1\}$ put
\[
 p_a(x)=\begin{cases}(\sum_{j\ne i}x_j)\prod_{j\in S\setminus\{i\}}x_j&i\in S,\\ \,\prod_{j\in S}x_j&i\notin S.\end{cases}
\]
The test says $\theta=\min_D\max\{-\min_i(\Gamma x)_i,\max_{a\in A}p_a(x)\}>0$. In the case $A=\varnothing$ use $0$ as the inner maximum. Fix $C=\max(1,\max_{ij}|\Gamma_{ij}|)$. Choose $K\ge1$ with $K\theta/32>3C+1$. Since $R_0$ excludes any nonnegative column, $c=\min_j\max_i(-\Gamma_{ij})>0$. Choose $\eta>0$ with $33\eta K^3<\min(1,\theta)/4$ and $K\eta<c/2$. Take $T_0$ large enough that $h=K/T\le1/2$ and $6Ch<\min(1,\theta)/4$ throughout.

On $q=h x$ the part of $B_i/h$ from passive singleton rewards differs from $(\Gamma x)_i$ by at most $6Ch$. Every nonsingleton coordinate of player $i$ contributes with sign opposite to $b_a$. Its exact factor multiplying $-b_a$ in $B_i/h$ lies between $h^{d_a}p_a(x)/32$ and $h^{d_a}p_a(x)$. For a participating recipient use
\[
 (1-h)^2\sum_{j\ne i}x_j\le (1-H_i)/h\le\sum_{j\ne i}x_j,
\]
where the lower bound retains disjoint single-opponent events. The remaining factors from nonquitting rivals cost at most three further factors $1-h$. For an absent recipient only those latter factors are needed. There are eleven nonsingleton coalitions, $p_a\le3$, and $d_a\le3$. Hence total adverse contributions to any row are at most $33\eta K^3$. If some $(\Gamma x)_i\le-\theta$, that row has $B_i<0$. Otherwise some selected $a$ satisfies $p_a(x)\ge\theta$. Its row has $B_i/h\le3C+6Ch+33\eta K^3-K\theta/32<0$. Thus $B(q)$ has a strictly negative entry on the entire outer positive face $\max q=h$.

For each fixed finite table, $B(q)=\Gamma q+O(\|q\|^2)$ at zero. The homogeneous $R_0$ map has norm bounded below by a positive constant times $\|q\|$, so the minimum map $N(q)=\min(q,B(q))$ has the stated local degree at its isolated origin. Here the polynomial $B$ is extended to all real coordinates. On the boundary of $[-h,h]^4$, a negative coordinate gives a negative coordinate of $N$; otherwise the previous outer-face estimate does. Consequently the boundary misses the entire ray $t\mathbf1$, $t\ge0$. Translating the target far along this ray shows the degree of $N$ in the full box at zero is zero. By excision there is a different zero $q$ in the box. Then $q\ge0$, $B(q)\ge0$ and $q_iB_i(q)=0$. If $q=ve_j$, any outsider $i$ with $\Gamma_{ij}\le-c$ has $B_i/v=\Gamma_{ij}-v Z_i(\{i,j\})\le-c+K\eta<0$, by the pair bound. Thus at least two hazards are positive.

Every player now faces geometric absorption by its rivals. Its native payoff from Never is $P_i/(1-H_i)\ge Q_i$, with equality if active. Any finite deadline is a convex combination of these two amounts, and private mixtures include all unilateral replacements. The prescribed stationary action attains the upper endpoint. Adding $s_i$ restores the original payoffs since every replacement absorbs almost surely. The opponents' geometric tail uniformly bounds the difference between finite-average and terminal rewards by a table-dependent constant divided by the horizon. This proves the uniform statement with the same profile.

**Which triples suffice?** A positive participant award to $i$ for joining two distinct rivals $j,k$ tests exactly whether $x_jx_k>0$. The choice between the two possible recipients has no effect on the support test. On any $R_0$ matrix an admissible direction has at least two positive coordinates. Thus choosing one recipient per unordered pair always suffices, for every four-player singleton matrix after the degree alternative. If each of the six selected native rewards is at least $L$, take $T=\sqrt L$. For bounded remaining data the resulting stationary maximum hazard on the degree branch is $O(L^{-1/2})$, uniformly even if the six grow at unrelated rates. Triple outsider rewards can increase by $o(L)$ and grand-coalition rewards can decrease by $o(L^{3/2})$ under this bound; arbitrary increases of grand rewards are allowed.

There is a two-pair choice whenever some row $Y$ has a unique strict positive supplier $X$ and its two remaining comparisons are strictly negative. Any admissible $x$ has $x_X>0$, since otherwise row $Y$ forces $x$ to be supported on $Y$ alone, contrary to the negative entry in that column. Pick $k$ with $\Gamma_{kX}<0$. A direction supported on $\{k,X\}$ makes row $k$ strictly negative. Hence one of the two coordinates in $I\setminus\{k,X\}$ must also be positive. The two pairs consisting of $X$ and one of these latter labels cover every admissible direction. Their recipients can be chosen independently and may coincide. This selects raw reward coordinates from singleton comparisons alone.

For example, such a row is automatic under the following numerical conditions: $\Gamma$ is $R_0$ of nonzero degree, all off-diagonals are nonzero, $\det\Gamma>0$, its inverse has a strictly positive column, and some pair has mutually negative comparisons. A nonzero degree gives a complementary solution to offset $\mathbf1$ (translate the target on a large ball using the homogeneous lower norm bound), so every row of $\Gamma$ has a strictly positive entry. Each column has a negative entry. If every row had two or more positive entries, counting negative entries would force exactly one negative in each row and column. They form a derangement. The mutually negative pair forces a transposition, hence a second transposition. Each principal triple then contains just one mutually negative pair; both terms of its determinant are strictly negative. Since $\det\Gamma>0$, every inverse diagonal would be negative, contradicting the positive column.

The number two is sharp for this support test, even under the degree-one and positive-inverse-column filters. For $\Gamma$ with rows $(0,10,-1,-2),(-8,0,18,4),(20,-20,0,20),(-1,12,-18,0)$, the vectors $(0,3,0,5)$ and $(16,15,8,0)$ have respective strictly positive images $(20,20,40,36)$ and $(142,16,20,20)$. Their supports intersect only at 1; hence no single pair works, also on an open neighborhood. Every column has a negative entry and all principal blocks of size at least two are nonsingular. At the target $(101,203,307,411)$ the complementary supports are $13,013,123$ with determinants $-48,152,5760$, so the degree is one. Its inverse column 2 is $(276,80,38,381)/11540$. This is sharpness of the directional test; a particular pair could yield UE for other reasons.

**A positive triple reward in a strictly negative inverse column.** Consider
\[
 G=\begin{pmatrix}0&-8&6&-2\\9&0&2&-10\\-7&15&0&-4\\14&-3&-4&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every admissible direction has $x_0,x_1,x_2>0$. If $x_0=0$, rows 3 and 1 force all coordinates to vanish. If $x_1=0$, rows 2 and 3 do so. If $x_2=0$, use rows 0 and 2. The positive reward to recipient 1 for joining $\{0,2\}$ therefore suffices by itself, including with all the independent favorable and controlled adverse rewards allowed above. The inverse column is $G^{-1}e_1=(-160,-196,-413,-455)/2284$. All principal blocks of size at least two are nonsingular and each column has a negative entry, giving $R_0$. At offset $\mathbf1$, testing each of the eleven such supports $S$ by $x_S=G_{SS}^{-1}\mathbf1$, $x_{S^c}=0$, $x_S>0$, $Gx\ge\mathbf1$ gives only the full support $(392,366,812,401)/1142$. Singletons cannot solve the active equation. The full determinant $4568$ makes its index $+1$.

For the sparse example with bounded remaining data one can also recover a three-active root by applying a positive-inverse-column construction to the restricted set $012$: its column 1 equals $(45,21,28)/461$ and the outside row evaluates to $455/461>0$ on this direction. Thus existence for this one special ray can also be shown on a proper subset of the four players.

There is a two-reward family on $G$ in which the just-mentioned three-player lift itself fails. Take $Z_1(012)=T^2$, $Z_3(0123)=T^4$, all other nonsingleton rewards fixed, and $T$ large. Each stationary root produced in the stated $K/T$ box has all four hazards positive. Indeed for any sequence of such roots, writing $v=\max q$ and passing to a subsequence of $x=q/v$, the positive awards only decrease residuals and bounded remainders give $0\le B_i(q)/v\le(Gx)_i+O(v)$. Any limiting direction is admissible, so $q_0,q_1,q_2$ are bounded below by fixed positive multiples of $v$. If $q_3=0$, the first three active equations reduce to the invertible principal $G_{012,012}$ with errors $O(v^2+T^2v^3)$ before division by $v$. They give $v\ge c_1/T$ for some $c_1>0$, since the normalized first three coordinates have maximum one. But the outside residual divided by $v$ contains the negative term $-T^4[(1-H_3)/v]q_0q_1q_2\le -c_2 T^4 v^3$, whereas its remaining terms are bounded. This contradicts $B_3\ge0$ for large $T$. Thus no proper-support stationary lift supplies this small equilibrium. No all-interior leading direction based on the positive triple reward alone can be proportional to $G^{-1}e_1$, because this vector is strictly negative. In a scaling based on the grand reward alone the natural hazard size is $T^{-4/3}$; the other growing coefficient $T^2$ already exceeds the $T^{4/3}$ scale for a uniform bound on arbitrary ignored reward parameters. The two-reward conclusion follows without a common ratio bound or a preselected positive inverse direction.
