## 1. Question

At a positive global minimum terminal debt \(D_*>0\), the checked paid-cap
lift retains one literal profitable first-disagreement row through a
summable exact cap-Nash prefix orbit.  Its only unconsumed terminal mode is
the zero-charge all-Continue stall.  Can that row be closed by either:

1. cycling through actual carrier sources and exact roots tested at their
   unrestricted caps; or
2. constructing an exact Nash--Bellman path which returns to the prescribed
   payoff face of the global minimum?

The answer to both specific architectures is no.

## 2. Carrier-cap cycles pay a positive seam toll

Let \(z^k=(U^k,B^k)\) be actual terminal-semantic carrier points with

\[
D(z^k)\ge D_*>0.
\]

Let \(q_k\) be exact product roots against the literal caps \(B^k\), let

\[
w^k=\operatorname{Prefix}(q_k,z^k),
\qquad a_k=\Pr_{q_k}(\text{absorption}),
\]

and rebase \(w^k\) to carrier targets with the same stationary source and
target frequency.  More precisely, let \(\beta\) be a probability vector and
let \(\lambda_{k\ell}\ge0\) have both marginals equal to \(\beta\).  Exact
debt scaling gives

\[
D(w^k)=(1-a_k)D(z^k).
\]

Consequently

\[
\sum_{k,\ell}\lambda_{k\ell}
 \bigl(D(z^\ell)-D(w^k)\bigr)
=\sum_k\beta_ka_kD(z^k)
\ge D_*\sum_k\beta_ka_k.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus neither a deterministic cycle, a permutation of phases, nor a
stationary randomized rematching can cancel actual carrier rebase seams at
little-oh of total exact-root charge.  In Fin4 the full prescribed/cap seam
obeys

\[
\sum_{k,\ell}\lambda_{k\ell}
 \lVert z^\ell-w^k\rVert_\infty
\ge {D_*\over8}\sum_k\beta_ka_k.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The same identities allow an ordinary root-error correction, but then root
error of the same order as charge is not an accepted exact or vanishing-error
consumer.

## 3. Exact prescribed-payoff return paths have zero charge

Let \(K\) be the compact prescribed-payoff projection of the positive global
minimum fibre in the no-uniform-payoff Fin4 branch.  The reviewed strict
all-Continue basin theorem supplies an open neighborhood \(N\supset K\) and
\(c>0\) such that

\[
c\,A(q)\le \operatorname{Def}(V,q)
\qquad(V\in N).
\tag{3.1}
\]

It also supplies the successor-linked bootstrap: if

\[
V_t=T_{q_t}(V_{t+1})
\tag{3.2}
\]

is an approximate Nash--Bellman path ending sufficiently near \(K\), then
small aggregate root error keeps the whole path inside \(N\) and bounds its
total absorption by that aggregate error.

At exact error zero and terminal node \(V_L\in K\), every \(q_t\) therefore
has zero absorption.  Every row is literally all Continue and

\[
V_0=V_1=\cdots=V_L.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Hence no positive-charge exact Nash--Bellman excursion can return to the
minimum prescribed-payoff face.  A nonlocal detour does not evade this: the
successor-linked bootstrap itself prevents the first exit from the strict
basin at zero aggregate error.

## 4. Application to the literal inert paid port

In the zero-charge arm of the checked paid-cap trichotomy, every selected
cap root is all Continue.  The actual profile at depth \(n\) is only the
original paid profile delayed by \(n\) silent dates.  The shifted paid row
retains its original positive whole-strategy gain and full source ancestry,
but the exact root charge is identically zero.

The row therefore cannot be spent by architecture 1: any positive-charge
carrier restart would incur the toll (2.1).  It cannot be spent by
architecture 2: an exact Bellman return to the minimum prescribed face has
zero charge by (3.3).

This is not a contradiction.  The paid comparison concerns two complete
pure stopping times, often with a disagreement moving to temporal infinity.
One-row cap complementarity sees only the immediate Quit/Continue endpoints.
The all-Continue root may remain exact while a remote stopping-time response
is uniformly profitable.

## 6. Universal annotation-seam versus root-error coercivity

The compact strict-basin theorem has a useful global corollary.  Choose
\(\rho>0\) such that

\[
\operatorname{dist}_\infty(V,K)<\rho\Longrightarrow V\in N,
\tag{6.1}
\]

and put

\[
\gamma=\min\{c,\rho\}>0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For every payoff annotation \(V\) and every product root \(q\),

\[
\boxed{
\gamma A(q)
\le
\operatorname{Def}(V,q)+\operatorname{dist}_\infty(V,K).}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Indeed, inside the \(\rho\)-tube equation (3.1) pays the left side.  Outside
the tube,

\[
\operatorname{dist}_\infty(V,K)\ge\rho\ge\gamma A(q),
\]

because \(A(q)\le1\).  No floor or carrier assumption on \(V\) is needed.

In particular, for every minimum prescribed source \(U\in K\),

\[
\gamma A(q)
\le
\operatorname{Def}(V,q)+\lVert V-U\rVert_\infty.
\tag{6.4}
\]

If \(q\) is an ordinary \(\eta\)-Nash root on Fin4, then

\[
\gamma A(q)
\le4\eta+\lVert V-U\rVert_\infty.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus an arbitrary floor-admissible noncarrier annotation attached directly
to a minimum source pays either root error or prescribed-annotation seam

the sharper basin statement says \(V\notin N\), hence

\[
\operatorname{dist}_\infty(V,K)\ge\rho.
\tag{6.6}

This is the exact resource exposed by the noncarrier route.  It is not by
itself a consumer: one macroscopic entrance may support many later rows
without paying \(\lVert V-U\rVert_\infty\) again.  Counting (6.4) once per
later row would be invalid.  A successful use still needs either an actual
return to \(K\), where the successor-linked basin theorem applies, or a
separate bounded-capacity argument controlling the entire persistent

## 7. The canonical noncarrier singleton probe is exact but macroscopically
seamed

The most direct noncarrier construction makes the boundary explicit.  Let
\(s_i=r_i(\{i\})\), assume punishment normality \(P_i\le s_i\), and take a
positive global-minimum pair \((U,B)\).  The minimum singleton margin gives

\[
B_j-s_j\ge D_*
\qquad(j\in I).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Fix one player \(i\).  Define the noncarrier tail

\[
V_i=s_i,\qquad V_j=B_j\quad(j\ne i),
\tag{7.2}
\]

and let \(q_h\) make only \(i\) Quit, with probability \(h>0\).  Player \(i\)
is indifferent exactly.  For \(j\ne i\), Continue minus Quit is

\[
(1-h)(B_j-s_j)
+h\bigl(r_j(\{i\})-r_j(\{i,j\})\bigr).
\tag{7.3}
\]

If all rewards are bounded by \(M\), then every

\[
0<h\le {D_*\over2(D_*+2M)}
\tag{7.4}
\]

makes (7.3) at least \(D_*/2\).  Hence \(q_h\) is an exact product root
against \(V\), it has absorption \(h\), and \(V\ge P\).  This is a literal
exact, floor-admissible, positive-charge noncarrier row.

But its entrance is necessarily macroscopic:

\[
B_i-V_i=B_i-s_i\ge D_*.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Under the uniform minimum-fibre prescribed singleton isolation
\(U_i-s_i\ge\delta>0\), it is also separated from the prescribed source by

\[
U_i-V_i\ge\delta.
\tag{7.6}
\]

The alternative is a first-order root defect.  If only the \(i\)-coordinate
of the annotation is changed to \(\widetilde V_i\ge s_i\), then the exact
coordinate defect of \(q_h\) is

\[
\operatorname{Def}_i(\widetilde V,q_h)
=h(\widetilde V_i-s_i).
\tag{7.7}
\]

Thus keeping \(\widetilde V_i\) within \(\delta/2\) of \(U_i\) costs at least
\(h\delta/2\) root error, while making the root exact costs the fixed
annotation seam (7.6).  The singleton probe therefore realizes, rather than
removes, the general strict-basin dichotomy.

Choosing \(i\) to be the paid observer does not transport the paid
first-disagreement passport to this row.  The probe uses only the singleton
payoff and the displayed cap; its absorption event occurs before the remote

### 10.2 A universal reached-mark packet helper

There is a stronger use of the same sure-owner row which was missed in
Section 10.1.  It does not exactify the owner's profitable response.  It
prices an auxiliary sure-Quit row at the owner's punishment value, and then
uses only literal row overwrites.

Fix an owner \(i\), put \(F=I\setminus\{i\}\), and choose a product mixed
Nash point \(x\) of the finite binary game

\[
u_j(A)=r_j(\{i\}\cup A),
\qquad j\in F,\quad A\subseteq F.
\tag{10.6}
\]

Let \(q\) make \(i\) Quit surely and use \(x\) on \(F\), and let \(p(A)\)
be the product probability of the free quitter set \(A\subseteq F\).  Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q&=\sum_{A\subseteq F}p(A)\,r_i(\{i\}\cup A),\\
C_P&=\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq F}p(A)\,r_i(A)
     +p(\varnothing)P_i,
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.7}
\]

where \(P_i\) is player \(i\)'s exact behavioral punishment value.  The root
has \(i\) Quit surely, so it absorbs on path.  The free players' exact induced
Nash inequalities are precisely their complete date-zero endpoint
inequalities: a unilateral free-player replacement cannot remove the sure
quitter \(i\), and no continuation is reached.

Consequently, if

\[
C_P\le Q,
\tag{10.8}
\]

the root is a `QuittingSingletonBaseCertificate`: the owner-floor balance is
(10.8), and the other endpoint conditions are the induced Nash conditions.
The checked punishment completion
`QuittingSingletonBaseCertificate.isUniformEquilibriumPayoff` then supplies
one fixed uniform-equilibrium payoff.  This is an all-behavior conclusion;
the off-path continuation is a near-minmax punishment of \(i\), not the
literal response tail.

In the hypothetical hard residual, (10.8) is therefore impossible.  Hence

\[
\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq F}
 p(A)\bigl(r_i(A)-r_i(\{i\}\cup A)\bigr)
>
p(\varnothing)\bigl(r_i(\{i\})-P_i\bigr).
\tag{10.9}
\]

All-player punishment normality gives

\[
P_i\le r_i(\{i\}),
\tag{10.10}
\]

so the right side of (10.9) is nonnegative.  It follows that one fixed
nonempty \(A\subseteq F\) satisfies

\[
p(A)>0,
\qquad
r_i(A)>r_i(\{i\}\cup A).
\tag{10.11}
\]

For a quantitative source family, this can be made uniform.  The checked
compact alternative `exists_uniformPayoff_or_singletonBase_pos_gap`, applied
to owner \(i\) and its full complement \(F\), gives in the no-uniform-payoff
branch one \(\gamma_i>0\) such that

\[
C_P-Q\ge\gamma_i
\tag{10.12}
\]

at every induced Nash point.  There are seven nonempty cells.  After one
finite cell selection and subsequence stabilization, a fixed nonempty \(A\)
satisfies

\[
p(A)\bigl(r_i(A)-r_i(\{i\}\cup A)\bigr)
\ge {\gamma_i\over7}.
\tag{10.13}
\]

If \(|r_j(S)|\le M\), then \(D_*>0\) forces \(M>0\), and

\[
p(A)\ge {\gamma_i\over14M}.
\tag{10.14}
\]

Now start from any supplied actual profile \(P_n\) and marked date \(t_n\)
whose joint reach is \(L_n>0\).  Keep every rule before and after \(t_n\)
unchanged.  At \(t_n\), first overwrite the three free marginals by the
selected induced Nash point \(x_n\) and the owner marginal by sure Quit; then
overwrite only the owner marginal from Quit to Continue.  Call the final
profile \(Y_n\).  These are finite one-player row replacements of an actual
profile.  They are not temporal or Nash--Bellman edges.

Nothing at date \(t_n\) affects survival before that date.  Therefore the
exact stage mass of \(A\) in \(Y_n\) is

\[
\Pr_{Y_n}(\text{coalition }A\text{ at }t_n)
=L_n p_n(A).
\tag{10.15}
\]

If the quantitative paid-port passport supplies \(L_n\ge L_*>0\), then

\[
\Pr_{Y_n}(A\text{ at }t_n)
\ge {L_*\gamma_i\over14M}>0.
\tag{10.16}
\]

This is a positive causal cell.  If \(A\) is a singleton, it directly builds
a `FinFourSingletonStageStrongConcentratedPacket`.  If \(|A|\ge2\), the
checked arbitrary-profile pure-nonsingleton screening theorem begins with a
simultaneous pure overwrite of that row, then routes the positive pure stage
mass through at most three strict edges to a literal singleton and builds the
same packet.  The overwrite is a source-attached sibling, not a unilateral
chronological transport of the original mixed cell.  The existing consumer
nevertheless accepts the resulting static packet and returns its
strategic-versus-collision-minimum alternatives on the incoming complete
minimum source.

Thus the correct source-faithful dichotomy is

\[
\boxed{
\text{quantitative reached mark}
