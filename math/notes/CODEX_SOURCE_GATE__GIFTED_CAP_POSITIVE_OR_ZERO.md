# Positive or zero continuations at the signed-price minimum

This is ordinary mathematics. It applies the finite-word proof in Sections 3–8 of [the least-Never source reduction](../exports/GLOBAL_LEAST_NEVER_SOURCE_REDUCTION.md) to a different minimization. The finite words, complementarity degree and hyperbolic orbit in that proof are mathematical inputs. No checked Lean declaration for this full use of those sections has been identified.

Let a four-player native quitting table have zero own singletons and positive absorbing regret infimum. At a minimizing point of the extreme signed-price variational problem let b be its vector of private Never-bonus cap values. The property of b used here is that on finite timing blocks with terminal value b, every sequence of independent profiles with vanishing complete block regret has exit probability tending to zero. This follows by grafting those blocks to the minimizing point. Every losing recipient's realized bonus is multiplied by the probability of surviving the entire block. A positive surviving probability below one contradicts the optimum, and vanishing survival would give zero native debt on an absorbing point. This argument permits the number of dates to tend to infinity.

**Conclusion.** If all off-diagonal joining increments and all principal three-by-three determinants of their hollow matrix are nonzero, either every b_i is strictly positive, or every b_i is zero. In the second case every native approximate-equilibrium sequence tends to the all-Never first-exit law. Thus if the same native table already has a nonblank compact zero-regret point, every such b is strictly positive, for every choice of winning and losing sets and price weights. When exactly one recipient loses, b is the actual native full-cap vector at that minimizing point. The source can be selected with these generic restrictions whenever the conjecture fails, by openness of positive native absorbing gap and density of the polynomial nonvanishings before selecting the new source.

To prove the claim, a one-date equilibrium exists and the block property forces all players to continue, so b≥0. With one zero a small solo hazard of the indifferent player is an equilibrium. With precisely two zeros i,j, put L_ij=Z_i({i,j})−Z_i({j}). If L_ij≤0 then a small solo-j hazard is Nash, and the same holds after swapping i,j. Otherwise lower both zero continuations to −ε and take a Nash equilibrium. Its limit would have to be blank. Then both positive owners keep waiting, while i has strict Quit gain ε(1−q_j)+L_ij q_j and likewise for j, contradicting convergence to blank.

For exactly three zeros write b=B e_p with B>0 and let T be the zero set. Use G=Γ_T, J the joining-pair block, L=J−G. The following checks specify the transfer from the cited finite-word proof. Start each comparison at the diagonal terminal block annotation (U,cap)=(b,b). Observer rows thus start at zero. During words of total hazard at most B/(16M), the quiet positive player's continuations exceed every Quit offer, where M≥max(1,‖Z‖∞,B). Its prescribed payoff equals its unrestricted block cap. Sections 4–7 of the cited proof produce finite words with observer regret tending to zero, and survival bounded away from both zero and one, whenever any of the following occurs.

- G has a nonzero homogeneous complementarity vector.
- G has a strictly positive inverse.
- G−tJ has a nonnegative column for some 0<t<1.
- L⁻¹J has a strictly positive eigenvector with eigenvalue greater than one.

In the first case the words are finite diffuse repetitions, in the second they are subdivided expanding axis cycles, in the third they are truncated one-owner geometric words, and in the fourth they are finite positive backward orbits at small amplitude. The cited arguments compute their block payoffs and caps from the diagonal child (b,b), with errors uniform in the word length. Temporary nonzero observer child values in the second and fourth constructions tend to zero and are removed by the sup-norm nonexpansiveness of the actual block payoff and cap updates. Each construction contradicts the block property above. The proof uses the earlier proof's constructions only from the word-building stage onward; the two minimizing objectives are distinct.

For clarity, the exclusions contradict one another. Every column of L has a positive entry, since otherwise a small solo hazard is Nash at b. The stated genericity makes −L R0 by checking the singleton, pair and triple supports. Perturb the zero continuations by εz. All equilibrium limits are blank, and the positive owner stays quiet. On the three remaining owners, the slack is εz−Lq+O(‖q‖²+ε‖q‖). Homogeneous rescaling using R0 gives q=O(ε), then subsequential limits of q/ε solve every offset complementarity problem for −L. At offset −1, each row of L must have a negative entry, and the column-positive signs now give a strict oriented three-cycle with product of negative magnitudes greater than the product of positives. Its homogeneous degree is one, since at offset −1 the unique complementary solution is interior and det(−L)>0. A homogeneous crossing of G−tJ for 0<t<1 would have a singleton support (giving a nonnegative column), a pair support (the reciprocal entries vanish, implying a nonnegative column), or a positive triple support (giving an expanding L⁻¹J eigenvalue). Hence the entire pencil is R0. Homotopy transports degree one to G. The offset −1 therefore has a solution for G, forcing one positive entry in every row. R0 already forces a negative entry in every column. Again the signs form a strict oriented three-cycle, and the inequalities Gx≥1 force the product of positive entries to exceed that of the negative magnitudes. Its inverse is strictly positive, excluded above. This finishes the three-zero case. If b=0, apply the block property to the usual finite truncations of native profiles with vanishing total debt. When there is one losing recipient j, minimizing its sole price bound gives d_j=c_jρ even if n_j=1; in either case its gifted and native caps coincide. Winners always retain their native caps.

**Four independent original exit columns when b=0.** The following strengthening works for every prescribed nonempty winner set W and its nonempty complement L. In addition choose Z generically so that for every selection of three distinct nonempty-coalition columns and every recipient, deleting that recipient's coordinate leaves a nonsingular three-by-three matrix. These conditions are open dense in the 56-dimensional native table. Indeed each selected column has at most one forced zero, the owner's coordinate for a singleton, and distinct columns have distinct forced-zero rows when both are singletons. In any three-row minor one can select a permutation avoiding the forbidden cells. Its product of payoff entries is a monomial occurring just once in the determinant. The finite list of determinant polynomials therefore has nonzero product. The same perturbation can guarantee the joining-generic conditions above and preserve A>0 by continuity of the absorbing regret infimum.

No span of at most three reward columns contains any coordinate axis. Otherwise projecting away that axis would give dependent column images, contradicting the generic minor condition. Consequently each such span meets the coordinate subspace of the losing rows in a proper linear subspace. Choose the positive vector of weights c in that subspace outside their finite union, padding it by zero on W. At b=0 the original prescribed payoff is exactly U=−ρc, because the realized Never credit is zero on W and c_jρ on L. The payoff is also the convex combination of original reward columns with coefficients given by the limiting first-exit law: the positive absorbing gap gives ρ>0, and winner survival product vanishes. Therefore the coalitions carrying positive mass must include four linearly independent original reward columns. At least three different players participate in the original first exit, since two players generate only three nonempty coalitions. In particular with a single winner at least two distinct losers participate positively.

These conclusions include a uniform positive mass floor for four independent columns on the subfamily of minimizing points with b=0. Retain the entire first-exit law in the compact data, and maximize the least of its four masses over the finitely many independent four-column sets. This is a continuous function, strictly positive on that compact subfamily, hence bounded below by a positive constant if it is nonempty. The price choice, perturbation and lower bound are at the one selected native table. The bound transfers along sufficiently accurate original sequences whose limiting caps are zero. The earlier two-clock projection gives the one-winner participation conclusion with weaker algebraic restrictions on Z; the rank argument here also detects four independent coalition rewards. The rank strengthening is due to the coordinator.

The unresolved alternatives are a strictly positive cap vector and the all-zero cap vector at a native AllNever-only table. Positive native regret remains possible under the present constraints.
