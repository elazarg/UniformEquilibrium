Let a four-player native quitting table have zero own singletons and positive absorbing regret infimum. At a minimizing point of the extreme signed-price variational problem let b be its vector of private Never-bonus cap values. The property of b used here is that on finite timing blocks with terminal value b, every sequence of independent profiles with vanishing complete block regret has exit probability tending to zero. This follows by grafting those blocks to the minimizing point. Every losing recipient's realized bonus is multiplied by the probability of surviving the entire block. A positive surviving probability below one contradicts the optimum, and vanishing survival would give zero native debt on an absorbing point. This argument permits the number of dates to tend to infinity.

## Which winning clocks can be required to absorb

There is an unrestricted signed-price minimum having **three surely finite winning clocks and exactly one debtor** for at least one choice of the losing row. For *every* prescribed losing row the same conclusion holds at a possibly larger minimum taken with the three winning clocks required to be surely finite. Here a surely finite clock means a zero genuine marginal Never coordinate in the native carrier. The approximating native profiles may have the designated marginals set exactly to zero by coupling. These conclusions describe selectable native minima and do not transfer a particular marked physical date from an earlier realizing sequence.

**Completion at the unrestricted price.** Suppose the minimizer has only one winning clock r with n_r=0 and another winner p with n_p>0. On each actual approximant censor remote finite tails to Never with vanishing total-variation cost, choosing a last retained old date. Then move all of r's tiny Never marginal to that old finite date, so r is surely finite within the retained head. When two anchors are used below, repair both in this same order. Put all of p's old Never mass on a uniform block of m fresh dates beyond it, with m→∞. The actual reference payoff is unchanged because r always stops during the old dates. All full caps and Never responses except r's are unchanged in the limit, because every other recipient faces the unchanged finite anchor r. The new value of r's literal Never reply is e_r+n_{−r}Γ_rp, where n_{−r}=∏_{i≠r}n_i. Its new full cap is max(B_r,e_r+n_{−r}Γ_rp). Indeed all old deadlines are preserved. On new dates or after the block, r's finite responses interpolate between e_r and e_r+n_{−r}Γ_rp with at most 2M/m error from ties. This calculation concerns only the event where all old rival clocks chose Never; earlier rival payoffs cancel in the comparison. In particular d_r stays zero if n_{−r}Γ_rp≤Δ_r=B_r−e_r. The modification preserves the losing data and the same minimum price, while making both p and r surely finite. With two surely finite clocks, changing another winner's Never mass to a sufficiently late finite date preserves every payoff and cap in the limit: each deviator faces at least one unchanged absorbing rival. Thus if every possible sole winning anchor r has another winner p with Γ_rp≤0, the *unrestricted* minimum can be achieved with all winning clocks surely finite. Earlier finite-clock completion in Sections F6–F8 of [the four-clock theorem](../exports/FOUR_FINITE_CLOCKS_AT_ORIGINAL_MINIMA.md) performs the same late diffusion under weak blockers; the exact threshold n_{−r}Γ_rp≤Δ_r can also admit a positive entry.
