# Two refinements of the native counterexample search

The first result concerns a minimum with zero and positive payoff coordinates. The second gives a test on 21 raw entries excluding the AllNever-only branch for every completion of 35 other rewards. Both are ordinary mathematical results. [Native reduction and case definitions](CODEX_ROOT__FIN4_COVERAGE_MAP.md#native-source-geometry-three-disjoint-table-branches)

## Recruitment at a proper boundary minimum

Use a four-player native game with zero live, own-singleton and perpetual rewards. Let $C$ be the Euclidean closure of the actual independent-law tuples $(U,B,e,N)$, with prescribed payoff, unrestricted cap, Never offer and original Never marginal respectively. Write $E=C\cap\{U=B\}$, $\nu=\prod_iN_i$, and let $A_Z$ be the infimum of total debt over absorbing profiles. Assume $A_Z>0$. Choose any minimizer $X$ of

$$
 m=\min\{\nu(Y):Y\in E,\ \min_i U_i(Y)\le0\}
$$

whose payoff $u=U(X)$ is nonnegative with proper nonempty zero set $T$. This includes every proper boundary minimizer in the branch called Nb. Let $\Gamma_{ij}=Z_i(\{j\})$, $J_{ij}=Z_i(\{i,j\})$, $L=J-\Gamma$, with zero diagonals. Assume $L_{ij}\ne0$ for $i\ne j$ and every principal triple of $L$ is nonsingular. All results use this same restricted minimum. A minimizer with a strictly negative payoff and the all-zero endpoint have different geometry.

For a product hazard $q$, let $Q_i(q)$ be its forced Quit reward, $P_i(q)$ the expected reward from waiting and an immediate rival exit, and $H_i(q)=\prod_{j\ne i}(1-q_j)$. A Nash row at $u$ compares $Q_i$ with $P_i+H_i u_i$. The output is their common value for a mixer and the chosen value otherwise. A positive owner means an owner outside $T$.

**Recruitment.** The macroscopic Nash roots at $u$, taken together outside a sufficiently small ball around zero, have total fixed-point index $+1$. Every such root is proper, has strictly positive outputs in all four coordinates, and is active for at least two owners. If $|T|=2$, there is such a root using a positive owner. If $|T|=3$, the same holds except possibly for the following fully specified three-owner candidate.

For each $i\in T$ let $j,k$ be the remaining two owners of $T$, and put $C_i=Z_i(T)-Z_i(T\setminus\{i\})$, $B_{ii}=0$, $B_{ij}=L_{ik}$. If no positive owner enters any Nash row, all three conditions below hold.

1. $\det L_T>0$ and $y=-B^{-1}C\gg0$.
2. At $q_i^*=1/(1+y_i)$ on $T$ and $q_o^*=0$ for the sole outsider $o$, all three active outputs $Q_i(q^*)$ are strictly positive. The outsider has $P_o(q^*)+H_o(q^*)u_o>0$ and $d_o(q^*)=P_o(q^*)+H_o(q^*)u_o-Q_o(q^*)\ge0$.
3. At equality $d_o(q^*)=0$, uniquely continue the three active indifference equations near $q^*$ with outsider hazard $t$, obtaining core hazards $q_T(t)$. Then $d_o(q_T(t),t)>0$ for every sufficiently small $t>0$.

Failure of any required test forces an actual Nash prefix using $o$. The first test and the three active output tests use only the rewards internal to $T$. Passing all the tests allows the single core candidate to account for the whole index and does not establish absence of other Nash rows.

**Proof.** The accepted proper-zero source theorem gives $m>0$, $|T|=2$ or 3, and $-L_T$ is $R_0$ of complementarity degree zero. For two zeros it also gives $L_{ij},L_{ji}>0$. Its prefix identity preserves $E$ at every Nash row and multiplies its original Never product by $h=\prod_i(1-q_i)$. Thus a sure row would contradict $A_Z>0$, and a proper nonzero row with any nonpositive output would contradict this very $m$. A sole active owner's native output would equal its own singleton zero. These facts apply to semantic $X$ by prefixing actual approximants. The source has $m<1$ because original Never product one forces payoff zero. [Proper-zero reduction](../exports/NONNEGATIVE_MINIMUM_SOURCE_PENCIL_REDUCTION.md)

Write $d_i=P_i+H_i u_i-Q_i$, and use the clipped Nash fixed-point map $q\mapsto\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}(q-d(q))$. The degree of identity minus this map on a large ball is one by homotopy to a constant. Near zero all positive owners strictly wait. Their residuals are just their coordinates and can be eliminated. The core residual becomes $\min(q_T,-L_Tq_T)+O(\|q_T\|^2)$. The full-sphere coercivity of this $R_0$ map isolates zero and gives it the same degree, namely zero. Excision now gives index one outside this ball, including at nonisolated collections of roots. Compactness also gives a neighborhood of $u$ where the full root set outside the small ball retains total index one and consists of proper roots with uniformly positive outputs and two hazards bounded below.

If two zero owners acted alone at a proper root, each active tie at zero continuation would give $q_j L_{ij}=0$, impossible by genericity. Combined with the sole-owner exclusion this proves recruitment when $|T|=2$. When $|T|=3$, a root excluding $o$ must therefore use all three owners of $T$. In positive odds $x_i=q_i/(1-q_i)$ its active equations are

$$
 L_{ij}x_j+L_{ik}x_k+C_ix_jx_k=0 .
$$

Dividing by $x_jx_k$ gives $B y=-C$, where $y_i=1/x_i$. Swapping the two off-diagonal coefficients in each row interchanges the two cyclic products in a three-by-three zero-diagonal determinant, so $\det B=\det L_T\ne0$. This proves uniqueness of the candidate. In the active residuals $d_T$, the left-hand side $B y+C$ is multiplied by a strictly negative diagonal matrix, and each derivative $dy_i/dq_i$ is strictly negative. Hence the core Jacobian is invertible and its determinant has sign $\operatorname{sgn}\det L_T$.

Assume that no root recruits $o$. The candidate must then be the only macroscopic root and have local index one. For $d_o(q^*)>0$ elimination immediately gives its index as the sign of the core determinant. At a tie, use $(d_T,t)$ as local coordinates by the implicit function theorem. The core fixed-point residual is exactly $d_T$ near the candidate because all three hazards are interior. On the slice $d_T=0$, the outsider residual equals $\min(t,d_o(q_T(t),t))$ near zero, including negative $t$ for the index calculation, because its upper clipping bound is inactive. Elimination preserves degree up to the sign of the core determinant. Indeed one can set the spectator variables $d_T$ to zero inside the scalar residual by homotopy on a product neighborhood, since any simultaneous zero already has $d_T=0$. On the negative half-line the scalar residual is strictly negative. On the positive half-line it cannot vanish near zero under the no-recruitment assumption, because such a zero would be a proper four-owner Nash row. It has a constant strict sign by continuity, with scalar degree one for positive sign and zero for negative sign. This proves items 1 and 3. If positive-side roots accumulate at the candidate, they already recruit the outsider, so no isolated-root assumption is needed. Item 2 is the Nash condition and the strict output restriction proved above.

Every recruited root prepended to $X$ yields an $E$ point with strictly positive payoff and original Never strictly below $m$. In particular, the positive-sublevel finite-height theorem applies to this point. Every maximal chain of further nonzero Nash prefixes is finite and ends at an all-Continue-only root game. This connects that positive region to the chosen boundary minimum through the indicated owner. A return from there and unrestricted Fin4 remain open.

## A local return with 35 unrestricted entries

Work in the native game, with all own singletons, live and Never rewards zero. Write Γᵢⱼ=Zᵢ({j}), Jᵢⱼ=Zᵢ({i,j}) for i≠j. Let E be the intersection of the zero-total-debt set with the closure of actual (prescribed payoff, full cap, Never reply, original marginal Never masses). Write α=min_E ∏Nᵢ. This is the original-N carrier used in *Native collision floor and global mass dichotomy*.

**Local concatenation.** Suppose for every sufficiently small T>0 there is a continuous return R(T)e₂ from child T e₂, with R(0)=0 and

    (1+κ)T ≤ R(T) ≤ KT                    (κ>0).

Suppose each return admits arbitrarily accurate finite words whose rowwise Wait equations, prescribed equations and comparisons for the alternate Quit action give cumulative annotation error tending to zero, allowing an additional error paid only at the deviator's single attempted Quit. All annotations refer to native coordinates. Suppose their total hazards lie between cT and CT for fixed positive constants, uniformly in sufficiently fine approximations. The returns below have these properties, including the longer geometric pair arms. Then for every sufficiently small T₀>0, E contains a point with payoff, full cap and Never reply all equal to T₀e₂ and with

    1−C′T₀ ≤ ∏Nᵢ ≤ 1−c′T₀.

Indeed choose Tₙ₊₁ with R(Tₙ₊₁)=Tₙ by the intermediate value theorem, using the interval [0,Tₙ/(1+κ)]. Thus Tₙ/K≤Tₙ₊₁≤Tₙ/(1+κ). Concatenate in physical order the first n words, starting at output T₀e₂, and close with AllNever at Tₙ. Choose errors for the finite list summing to at most 1/n. Finite Bellman iteration bounds prescribed values, Never payoffs and every unilateral stopping payoff by the corresponding annotations up to 1/n+Tₙ. This also prices a deadline after the last word: native own singletons are zero, so it has the same terminal contribution as Never. For a split-solo mesh error paid on stopping, bound it once, or by the sum of the per-word maxima. The sum of all hazards is at most CT₀/κ, and at least cT₀/K from the rootmost word. Independence and the two bounds on Π(1−q) give the asserted original-N bounds for small T₀. Compact extraction gives the claimed point, hence α<1. Neither absorption nor a fixed positive seed of the finite-amplitude return is used.

**A pair-data class, all higher entries free.** Fix Γ at

    (0,−8,6,−2; 9,0,2,−10; −7,15,0,1; 14,−3,−4,0)

and J₍ᵢⱼ₎=−Γᵢⱼ+ε on its twelve off-diagonals. For all sufficiently small positive ε there is an ordinary open neighborhood of these 24 entries for which the local conclusion holds for EVERY finite completion of the other 32 native rewards. The maximum seed can depend on the completed table. This excludes an α=1 counterexample at these pair data even if its absorbing native debt is positive. It does not assert a positive absorbing gap in any example.

Use the three solo preparations and recurring 013 head plus geometric 13 reset in the [eight-coalition construction](../exports/EIGHT_COMPULSORY_FIRST_QUITTER_COALITIONS.md). Only its germ at zero is needed. At the additive center the two prepared values are Uₛ=(89/35)T+O(T²), Dₛ=(8/35)T+O(T²), and Dₛ/Uₛ=8/89. Choose d=1/20. For the active head (a,p,0,y), the first two zero-continuation ties give p=14a/3, y=9a/10. Its price satisfies U/a→1174/15, its output A/a→587/15 and its passive 2 output is (639/10)a+H₂D. Thus the recurring return contracts U with limiting factor 1/2, and the zero-scale head and reset margins for observers and the buffer D≥dU are strictly positive after division by a. At the additive center the reset is blank, so its four corners per observer equal the head output for each. The shooting slope is 8/35+(1917/1174)(89/35)>1.

All these first-order coefficients depend only on Γ,J, including the reset: on the limiting small arm the two total attempts are linear to first order in its two head outputs and their joint probability is quadratic. The two initially zero outgoing values for owners 1,3 turn strictly positive after the ε perturbation, because at a tie with child zero, 2Qᵢ=Pᵢ+Qᵢ=ε∑ⱼ≠ᵢqⱼ+O(a²). This yields positive slopes throughout an open neighborhood of the 24 entries. The active ties have nonsingular rational charts, and every other strict first-order sign just checked persists there. An arbitrary choice of the 32 higher native rewards contributes only O(a²) to a head field and O(a²) to its four-corner observer expressions. It therefore changes the admissible small interval but preserves all comparisons on some interval. The four return tests and geometric contraction of the cited construction now apply throughout that interval; the total hazards in its inner sequence are O(T) and the first solo-0 block gives a positive lower multiple of T. Truncations and mesh refinement give the local concatenation conditions. This argument never applies the earlier large-seed sign test.

In fact only 21 of the 24 singleton and joining coefficients need testing. The three joining rewards $J_{02},J_{12},J_{32}$ are already arbitrary in the underlying construction. Player 2 waits on every joint date. On a solo-2 block, one of the other players can encounter its changed pair reward only by quitting on a single pulse of the arbitrarily fine subdivision. Its resulting error tends to zero for each fixed completion. These three entries and all 32 higher entries therefore range independently over the full real line. The maximum admissible seed and the required pulse mesh may depend on their values.

The difference in hypotheses is real. Keep the other data fixed at a sufficiently small strict joining perturbation of the additive table and add a huge positive number to the native grand joining reward of player 2. At any fixed positive seed, the first preparation's initial 013 head and the alternative joint preparation's initial 013 head have a,p,y>0 independent of this reward (by the exact active equations and continuity from the center). The alternative joint entry exists at a sufficiently small fixed positive seed by its nonsingular zero-scale equations. At that fixed seed the bonus adds its amount times apy to 2's Quit offer and leaves Wait unchanged. A large finite bonus defeats both global preparations at this seed, while the local conclusion still holds for all sufficiently small seeds. Other equilibrium constructions are not excluded by this comparison.

**Actual profiles and the closure.** On an ordinary open subset of the 56-dimensional native reward space in the preceding pair-data region, AllNever is the ONLY actual native Nash profile. Nevertheless the nonzero diagonal payoffs just constructed belong to E. This illustrates a restriction on approaches that would seek an actual nonblank native profile. This open subset already has zero absorbing native debt by the existing global equilibrium construction. It is not an example of a positive native absorbing gap.

Take the additive center above (the zero-premium member of the eight-outcome proof), then raise all 28 nontrivial joining cells slightly, and take a sufficiently small raw neighborhood satisfying strict protection Pᵢ+Qᵢ>0 on every nonempty rival cell. Each player guarantees native zero by a constant independent half hazard. The center excludes sure openings even at native child zero, as shown in the eight-outcome proof for every L≥0, including L=0. The exclusion is compact and persists nearby; for an alleged sole sure player, waiting and then securing zero is a valid deviation, and every player with a sure rival absorbs immediately. Thus an actual exact profile is proper forever and every finite restart is exact.

Write Wᵢ=Pᵢ+HᵢWᵢ⁺≥Qᵢ for its native value. Security gives Wᵢ≥0. If Wᵢ=0 then Pᵢ,Qᵢ≤0; strict protection forces all rivals quiet on that date. Now Wᵢ⁺=0, so every rival remains quiet on the whole tail. If i ever acted, an outsider with Γⱼᵢ<0 would have strictly negative prescribed native payoff on this tail, contradicting security. Each singleton column has such an outsider throughout this open neighborhood. Consequently a nonblank tail has Wᵢ>0 in each coordinate, and no date can have just one actor (that actor's native Quit offer is zero).

The pointwise joint-2 estimate in the eight-outcome proof holds at every finite L≥0 and nearby protected tables: ∑qᵢ(−Qᵢ)₊≥c h(a+p+y), q=(a,p,h,y). Exactness and security make its left side zero. Hence 2 could never act jointly, and cannot act alone either. With h=0 the passive field of 0 satisfies P₀≤−(p+y) throughout the neighborhood, because the center gives −8p−2y and the raw error is at most ε′(p+y). Whenever a>0 either p>0 or y>0 by the sole-actor exclusion. If p>0 the 1 offer is −9a+10y+O(ε′)(a+y)≥0, and if y>0 the 3 offer is −14a+3p+O(ε′)(a+p)≥0. Thus a≤2(p+y). The Wait recursion and W₀⁺≥0 yield W₀≤W₀⁺−(p+y). All native annotations are bounded, so total p+y, hence total hazard, is finite. The conditional probability of any later exit consequently tends to zero and the true native tail values tend to zero. Summing the same inequality all the way to infinity gives W₀≤−∑(p+y); all hazards must vanish. The AllNever profile itself is native Nash.

They cannot cover this pair-data set at the strict center, which has the explicit actual-uniqueness completion above. The native moment and global-mass reduction leaves both order-t total mass and relative pair-sized shared bursts.
