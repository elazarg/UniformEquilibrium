## 3. Exact noncarrier forward rows

For \(a\in[0,1]\), put

\[
 V(a):=(a,1,0,0).
 \tag{3.1}
\]

\[
 F(q_h,V(a))=V\bigl(a+h(1-a)\bigr).
 \tag{3.2}
\]

The root \(q_h\) is exact Nash against \(V(a)\):

- player \(1\) is indifferent, since Quit and Continue both pay \(1\);
- player \(0\)'s Quit payoff is zero, while Continue pays
  \((1-h)a+h\ge0\);
- each of players \(2,3\) obtains \(-1\) by Quit and zero by Continue.

Because a binary player's mixed replacement payoff is affine in that
player's replacement probability, these pure endpoint comparisons prove the
full mixed one-row best-response condition.

\[
 a_{n+1}=a_n+h_n(1-a_n).

\]

\[
 V(a_0)\xrightarrow{q_{h_0}}V(a_1)
 \xrightarrow{q_{h_1}}\cdots

 \tag{3.4}

 \tag{3.4a}

## 4. Exact multiplicative seam and logarithmic exit budget

Iteration of (3.3) gives

\[
 1-a_N=(1-t)\prod_{n<N}(1-h_n).
 \tag{4.1}
\]

In sup norm, the distance to the complete actual prescribed-payoff
hyperplane is

\[
 \delta_N:=\operatorname{dist}_\infty(V(a_N),\mathcal A)
 =\frac{1-a_N}{2}
 =\delta_0\prod_{n<N}(1-h_n),
 \qquad \delta_0=\frac{1-t}{2}.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Thus each exact active row spends a multiplicative fraction of the remaining
noncarrier defect, rather than paying the original entrance seam again. The
additive clock is

\[
 R_N:=\log\frac{\delta_0}{\delta_N}
 =\sum_{n<N}-\log(1-h_n).
 \tag{4.3}
\]

Since \(h\le-\log(1-h)\) on \([0,1)\),

\[
 H_N\le R_N.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

This yields the exit budget for this regression:

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_N\ge\varepsilon>0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 H_N\le\log\frac{\delta_0}{\varepsilon}.}
 \tag{4.5}
\]

If, additionally, \(h_n\le\bar h<1\), then

\[
 H_N\le R_N\le\frac{H_N}{1-\bar h}.
 \tag{4.6}
\]

The second inequality follows from
\(-\log(1-h)=\int_0^h(1-s)^{-1}ds\le h/(1-\bar h)\).

1. A packet which remains uniformly separated from the actual semantic face

## 6. General reward-span quotient theorem

The preceding scalar identity is not special to the table. Let

\[
 \mathcal R:=\operatorname{span}\{r(S):\varnothing\ne S\subseteq I\}
 \subseteq\mathbb R^I.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Every actual behavioral prescribed payoff belongs to \(\mathcal R\): it is
an expectation of the finitely many terminal reward vectors and the Never
vector zero. For an arbitrary payoff annotation \(V\) and product root \(q\),
write

\[
 c(q):=\Pr_q(\text{all Continue}),\qquad
 a(q):=1-c(q).
\]

The Bellman formula splits as

\[
 F(q,V)=c(q)V+w(q),\qquad w(q)\in\mathcal R.                 \tag{6.2}
\]

Therefore the quotient class in \(\mathbb R^I/\mathcal R\) and its quotient
norm satisfy

\[
 [F(q,V)]=c(q)[V],\qquad
 \operatorname{dist}(F(q,V),\mathcal R)
 =c(q)\operatorname{dist}(V,\mathcal R).                    \tag{6.3}
\]

This uses no Nash, floor, or source hypothesis. It is the exact transverse
Bellman equation.

For any finite forward packet \(V_{n+1}=F(q_n,V_n)\), put
\(\Delta_n=\operatorname{dist}(V_n,\mathcal R)\) and let \(p_{n,i}\) be the
marginal Quit probabilities. If \(\Delta_N>0\), then every
\(p_{n,i}<1\) and

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_N
   &=\Delta_0\prod_{n<N}c(q_n)
     =\Delta_0\prod_{n<N}\prod_i(1-p_{n,i}),\\
 \sum_{n<N}\sum_i p_{n,i}
   &\le \sum_{n<N}\sum_i-\log(1-p_{n,i})
    =\log\frac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Consequently the exact marginal-hazard exit budget is

\[
 \boxed{\Delta_N\ge\varepsilon>0
 \Longrightarrow
 \sum_{n<N}\sum_i p_{n,i}
 \le\log\frac{\Delta_0}{\varepsilon}.}
 \tag{6.5}
\]

Thus only the component transverse to the terminal-reward span has a
universal multiplicative clock. If the reward vectors span all of
\(\mathbb R^4\), the theorem is vacuous; it makes no claim about annotations
inside the reward span but outside the actual convex payoff carrier.

### Proposition (bounded-capacity transverse-ray dichotomy)

Assume a Fin4 table has no uniform-equilibrium payoff. Let
\((V_n,q_n)_{n\ge0}\) be an exact forward ray in the canonical reward box:

\[
 V_{n+1}=F(q_n,V_n),\qquad q_n\text{ exact Nash against }V_n.
 \tag{6.6}
\]

Then the counterexample-side declaration
finFour_hasBoundedFiniteExactNashBellmanHazardCapacity_of_no_uniformPayoff
forces

\[
 \sum_n\sum_i p_{n,i}<\infty.                               \tag{6.7}
\]

Indeed, the first \(N\) rows of (6.6) give one canonical finite exact block
under the shifted reverse pairing

\[
 \operatorname{state}(k).1=V_{N-k},\qquad
 \operatorname{state}(k).2=q_{N-k-1}\quad(k<N),
 \tag{6.6a}
\]

with an arbitrary terminal simplex coordinate. Its charge is
\(\sum_{n<N}\sum_i p_{n,i}\). One common capacity bound controls all \(N\),
and monotone convergence gives (6.7).

The forward ray therefore converges to a limit \(V_\infty\), because the
one-row movement bound is at most \(2M a(q_n)\) per coordinate and
\(a(q_n)\le\sum_i p_{n,i}\). Also \(q_n\to\) all Continue, and closedness of
the exact root inequalities gives:

\[
 \text{all Continue is exact Nash against }V_\infty.        \tag{6.8}
\]

For the transverse component there are exactly two possibilities.

1. Some \(c(q_n)=0\). Then \(V_{n+1}\in\mathcal R\), and every later value
   stays in \(\mathcal R\).
2. Every \(c(q_n)>0\). Then (6.7) makes
   \(\prod_n c(q_n)>0\), so

   \[
   \operatorname{dist}(V_\infty,\mathcal R)
   =\operatorname{dist}(V_0,\mathcal R)\prod_n c(q_n).
   \tag{6.9}
   \]

   In particular, a transverse entrance remains strictly transverse at its
   terminal all-Continue limit.

This is the promised positive result from bounded exact capacity: the
summable continuation cannot drift indefinitely through transverse
annotations. It either lands in the reward span at one sure-absorption row,
or converges to a genuinely transverse all-Continue phantom. It remains
source-free until the annotations are tied to actual cap prefixes, which is
the next proposition.

## 8. The longitudinal motion is a positive coalition occupation measure

The reward-span quotient forgets too much once every cap lies in
\(\mathcal R\).  The complete terminal law and the debt costate recover an
exact finite-dimensional direction: after dividing by total debt, a cap-prefix
chain can move only in the **positive cone** generated by terminal reward
vectors, not arbitrarily in their linear span.

### Theorem (debt-normalized law occupation identity)

Let

\[
 Y_0=(X_0,\mu_0)=((U_0,B_0),\mu_0)
\]

belong to the joint terminal semantic/law carrier, with total debt \(D_0>0\).
Recursively let \(q_n\) be exact Nash against \(B_n\), and use the coupled
semantic/law prefix maps to define

\[
 Y_{n+1}=((U_{n+1},B_{n+1}),\mu_{n+1}).                 \tag{8.1}
\]

Write

\[
 c_n=\Pr_{q_n}(\text{all Continue}),\qquad
 \alpha_n(S)=\Pr_{q_n}(\text{exactly }S\text{ Quits})
 \quad(S\ne\varnothing),                                \tag{8.2}
\]

and let \(D_n\) be the total debt of \(X_n\).  Suppose every \(D_n>0\); in
particular this holds when the carrier has positive global minimum debt
\(D_*>0\).  Define the finite coalition measure

\[
 \kappa_N(S):=\sum_{n<N}\frac{\alpha_n(S)}{D_{n+1}}.
                                                               \tag{8.3}
\]

Then, for every \(N\) and every nonempty coalition \(S\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\mu_N(\mathsf{Never})}{D_N}
   =\frac{\mu_0(\mathsf{Never})}{D_0},\qquad
 \frac{\mu_N(S)}{D_N}
   =\frac{\mu_0(S)}{D_0}+\kappa_N(S).}                    \tag{8.4}
\]

Moreover

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{U_N}{D_N}=\frac{U_0}{D_0}
       +\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}\kappa_N(S)r(S),\qquad
 \frac{B_N}{D_N}=\frac{B_0}{D_0}
       +\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}\kappa_N(S)r(S).}          \tag{8.5}
\]

The total occupation has the exact scalar budget

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{S\ne\varnothing}\kappa_N(S)
   =\frac1{D_N}-\frac1{D_0}.}                             \tag{8.6}
\]

Consequently, if \(D_n\ge D_*>0\),

\[
 \sum_S\kappa_N(S)\le\frac1{D_*}-\frac1{D_0}.            \tag{8.7}
\]

### Proof

The exact cap--Nash debt theorem gives

\[
 D_{n+1}=c_nD_n.                                          \tag{8.8}
\]

The affine law prefix is

\[
 \mu_{n+1}(\mathsf{Never})=c_n\mu_n(\mathsf{Never}),
 \qquad
 \mu_{n+1}(S)=\alpha_n(S)+c_n\mu_n(S).                   \tag{8.9}
\]

Divide (8.9) by (8.8).  The Never ratio is unchanged, while

\[
 \frac{\mu_{n+1}(S)}{D_{n+1}}
 =\frac{\mu_n(S)}{D_n}+\frac{\alpha_n(S)}{D_{n+1}}.
                                                               \tag{8.10}
\]

Iteration is (8.4).  The joint-carrier reward-moment identity evaluates
(8.4) against \(r(S)\) and gives the prescribed formula in (8.5).  Every
playerwise debt coordinate also scales by \(c_n\); hence the normalized debt
vector \((B_n-U_n)/D_n\) is constant, giving the cap formula.

Finally, sum (8.10) over all nonempty coalitions.  Since
\(\sum_S\alpha_n(S)=1-c_n\),

\[
 \sum_S\frac{\alpha_n(S)}{D_{n+1}}
 =\frac{1-c_n}{c_nD_n}
 =\frac1{D_{n+1}}-\frac1{D_n}.                            \tag{8.11}
\]

Telescoping proves (8.6), and the positive minimum proves (8.7). `QED`

and multiply (8.4) by \(D_N\).  One gets

moves, not through a paid sibling response.  Its exact finite-root content at
a contact point is as follows.

Let \(z_*=(u,b)\in\mathcal K_r\) satisfy
\(d(z_*)=Q(z_*)=\eta>0\), and write

\[
 \delta_i=b_i-u_i,qquad
 \kappa_i=b_i-r_i(\{i\}).                              \tag{8.54}
\]

Assume the strict singleton moat \(\kappa_i>0\) for every player.  Prefix a
root in which only player \(j\) Quits, with probability \(t>0\).  For all
sufficiently small \(t\), the strict moat fixes the Continue branch in every
other player's cap maximum.  The prefixed debts are then exactly

\[
 \delta_i(t)=(1-t)\delta_i\quad(i\ne j),
 \qquad
 \delta_j(t)=\delta_j+t(\kappa_j-\delta_j).            \tag{8.55}
\]

Carrier invariance and contact minimality require
\(\max_i\delta_i(t)\ge\eta\).  If \(\delta_j<\eta\), all initially active
coordinates occur outside \(j\) and shrink strictly in (8.55), while the
\(j\)-coordinate stays below \(\eta\) for small \(t\), a contradiction.
Applying this to every \(j\) gives

\[
 \boxed{\delta_i=\eta\quad\text{for every }i},
 \qquad \boxed{\kappa_i\ge\eta\quad\text{for every }i}. 
                                                               \tag{8.56}
\]

For Fin4, the contact point therefore has total debt \(4\eta\).  If it is
also a total-debt minimizer, then necessarily \(D_*=4\eta\).  The second
box equivalently says \(u_i\ge r_i(\{i\})\) at every coordinate.

Pure-set controller moves give the finite reward-table shadow.  For a
coalition \(S\) with \(|S|\ge2\), prefixing the deterministic root which
makes exactly \(S\) Quit kills the tail.  Its maximum debt is

\[
 \max\left\{
  \max_{i\in S}\bigl(r_i(S\setminus\{i\})-r_i(S)\bigr)_+,
  \max_{i\notin S}\bigl(r_i(S\cup\{i\})-r_i(S)\bigr)_+
 \right\}.                                             \tag{8.57}
\]

Since this prefixed point lies in \(\mathcal K_r\), (8.57) is at least
\(\eta\).  Thus every nonsingleton coalition has an \(\eta\)-sized member
leave or outsider join toggle.  For \(S=\{j\}\), the owner Continue branch
reaches the tail and contributes \(\kappa_j\); the corresponding condition is

\[
 \max\left\{\kappa_j,
   \max_{i\ne j}\bigl(r_i(\{i,j\})-r_i(\{j\})\bigr)_+
 \right\}\ge\eta,                                     \tag{8.58}
\]

already implied by (8.56) in its first entry.

These conditions are strong but not additional barrier dynamics.  From the
definition,

\[
 Q(z_*)=\eta\le Q(T_xz_*)\le d(T_xz_*)                \tag{8.59}
\]

for every controller root \(x\).  The left inequality follows automatically
because \(T_xz_*\in\mathcal K_r\) and \(\eta\) is the carrier minimum.
Equations (8.55)--(8.58) are precisely first-order and vertex consequences
of that same carrier invariance; upper semicontinuity supplies no reverse
inequality, minimizing root, or extra costate equation.

Most importantly, a paid response \(z_\sigma\to z_\tau\) is not of the form
\(z_\tau=T_xz_\sigma\).  Barrier feasibility therefore gives only the two
separate facts \(Q(z_\sigma),Q(z_\tau)\ge\eta\); it gives no oriented
comparison between them.  The compulsory transfer (8.35) is fully compatible
with (8.56), by rotating which coordinate carries the maximum.  Hence the
greatest-USC contact calculation yields no contradiction beyond the already
known uniform toggle/barrier passport.  A concrete positive Fin4 barrier or
a causal response-to-prefix adapter remains necessary.

### Simultaneous first-order contact and the harmonic moat

The singleton calculation does not exhaust the first-order content.  Let
\(\alpha_i\ge0\), put \(A=\sum_i\alpha_i\), and prefix the product root

\[
 x_i(t)=t\alpha_i.
\]

For small \(t\), the strict singleton moat keeps the Continue expression as
the cap branch for every player.  Writing \(C_i^u(x)\) for player \(i\)'s
Continue value into the prescribed tail and \(q_i(x)\) for its Quit value,
the exact debt formula is

\[
 \delta_i(x)=\chi_i(x)\delta_i
       +x_i\bigl(C_i^u(x)-q_i(x)\bigr).
\]

At \(x=0\), \(C_i^u(0)=u_i\), \(q_i(0)=r_i(\{i\})\), and
\(\delta_i=\eta\).  Consequently

\[
 \delta_i(x(t))
  =\eta+t\bigl(\alpha_i\kappa_i-\eta A\bigr)+O(t^2).
                                                               \tag{8.60}
\]

If all four displayed derivatives were negative, the prefixed maximum debt
would be below \(\eta\) for small positive \(t\).  Thus every
\(\alpha\in\mathbb R_+^I\) satisfies

\[
 \max_i \alpha_i\kappa_i\ge\eta\sum_i\alpha_i.
                                                               \tag{8.61}
\]

Minimizing the left side on \(\sum_i\alpha_i=1\) gives the exact harmonic
condition

\[
 \boxed{\quad \eta\sum_i{1\over\kappa_i}\le1.\quad}           \tag{8.62}
\]

Indeed, the minimum is attained at
\(\alpha_i=(\kappa_i\sum_j1/\kappa_j)^{-1}\).  In Fin4, (8.62) implies
that at least one singleton moat is at least \(4\eta\).

There are two useful selection boundaries.  If \(z_*\) merely minimizes
total debt among the \(\eta\)-contact points, then (8.55) rules out
\(\kappa_j=\eta\): that equality would keep the \(j\)-coordinate exactly at
\(\eta\), shrink all other coordinates, and produce another contact point
with smaller total debt.  Thus this lexicographic choice gives only
\(\kappa_j>\eta\) for every \(j\).  If, much more strongly, the same point is
also a **global** total-debt minimizer, then summing (8.55) gives

\[
 D(T_{j,t}z_*)=4\eta+t(\kappa_j-4\eta),
\]

and hence \(\kappa_j\ge4\eta\) for every \(j\).  No checked selection theorem
puts a maximum-debt contact point on the global total-debt minimum face.
Downward same-outcome-law closure does not do so: a nontrivial prefix changes
the outcome law, and the maximum- and total-debt minimizers may lie in
different law fibres.

### A contact-sharp actual Fin4 regression

The failure is not just an abstract mismatch between two minimizations.
There is an actual Fin4 source on which all of the local conclusions above
are sharp.  Let

\[
 r_i(S)=\begin{cases}-1,&i\in S,\\0,&i\notin S.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{8.63}
\]

At dates \(0,1,2,3\), let only the correspondingly numbered player have a
positive Quit hazard, respectively

\[
 {1\over4},\quad {1\over3},\quad {1\over2},\quad 1,
\]

and let all other actions be Continue.  The terminal law is uniform on the
four singleton coalitions.  Hence its prescribed payoff and unrestricted
behavioral cap are

\[
 u_i=-{1\over4},\qquad b_i=0,qquad
 \delta_i={1\over4}.                                  \tag{8.64}
\]

The cap calculation covers every behavioral deviation.  Never gives payoff
zero, while every path on which the deviator Quits gives that player payoff
\(-1\); therefore no randomized or calendar-dependent replacement can
exceed zero.

Prefix an arbitrary product root \(x\), and put
\(c=\prod_i(1-x_i)\).  The new cap is still zero and the debts are exactly

\[
 \delta_i(x)=x_i+{c\over4}.                            \tag{8.65}
\]

If \(m=\max_i x_i\), then

\[
 \max_i\delta_i(x)=m+{c\over4}
 \ge m+{(1-m)^4\over4}\ge{1\over4},                  \tag{8.66}
\]

with equality only at all Continue.  Moreover

\[
 \sum_i\delta_i(x)=\sum_i x_i+c\ge1                  \tag{8.67}
\]

by the union bound.  More intrinsically, every finite prefix of this source
still absorbs surely; its cap is zero and its debt vector records the
terminal membership probabilities.  Thus every point in the closed forward
orbit of this source satisfies

\[
 \sum_i\delta_i=\mathbb E|S|\ge1,
 \qquad \max_i\delta_i\ge{1\over4}.                  \tag{8.68}
\]

The source simultaneously minimizes total and maximum debt on this local
forward invariant set.  Its singleton moats are
\(\kappa_i=1=4\eta\), so both (8.62) and the common-minimum consequence are
equalities.  The cap root is uniquely all Continue: Quit has value \(-1\)
and Continue has value zero for every player at every product root.  The
terminal law fixes the prescribed payoff, and the same-law cap is already
minimal because Never forces every cap coordinate to be at least zero.

There is also an exact paid-transfer edge.  If player \(0\) changes its
complete strategy to Never, the new singleton probabilities of players
\(1,2,3\) are each \(1/3\).  The debt vectors before and after are

\[
 (1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)
 \longrightarrow (0,1/3,1/3,1/3).                  \tag{8.69}
\]

The mover gains \(1/4\), each outsider debt rises by \(1/12\), and total
debt stays exactly one.  Thus the paid response realizes the compulsory
transfer by rotating the maximum away from the mover.

Finally, every nonempty pure coalition has a strict member-leave toggle of
gain one.  Those toggles monotonically delete members and terminate at a
singleton.  At a singleton, the owner gains \(3/4=\kappa_i-\eta\) by
Continuing into the displayed tail.  This is fully compatible with the
checked Fin4 monodromy impossibility: pair vertices are terminal singleton
routes in that theorem, so the deletion orientation creates no retained
nonsingleton closed segment.

The regression is deliberately **not** a counterexample.  The global carrier
also contains all Never, with semantic pair \((0,0)\), so both global minima
are zero.  It proves the exact boundary: even a common local max/total
minimum, harmonic and coordinatewise moat saturation, same-law minimality,
unique all Continue, a positive finite law, pure-coalition orientation, and
an exact paid transfer do not replace the missing attachment to the global
positive minimum.

## 9. Exact boundary for the noncarrier route

The regression rules out the following proposed universal inference:

> one macroscopic noncarrier entrance implies a toll proportional to every
> later exact row, or implies a finite amount of exact forward charge.

It also identifies a positive replacement. An exact noncarrier excursion is
consumable if one proves a **terminal separation invariant** analogous to

For a hypothetical Fin4 counterexample, bounded exact-block capacity gives a
global finite charge bound but no numerical value and no source transition.
The genuinely source-facing next lemma is consequently:

> From the incoming paid/minimum/atom passport, either attach a positive

The example shows why an entrance-only debt ledger cannot prove this lemma.

## 12. A global alternative: the one-dimensional no-escape Simon theorem

### 12.1 Statement

This section is logically independent of Sections 2--11. It addresses the
global topological route isolated by
Math.Topology.SimonViability.QuestionOneHypotheses and the open note
SIMON_RESTARTABILITY_ONE_POLYTOPE.md.

**Theorem 12.1 (ordinary mathematics; not Lean-checked).** Suppose Simon's
seven Question 1 hypotheses hold in a one-dimensional Euclidean space with
one polytope \(P\). Add the no-escape hypothesis

\[
 (s,t)\in H(P,1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s\in P.
 \tag{12.1}
\]

Then the full graph \(J\) admits an extended orbit of unbounded Euclidean
variation. Equivalently, QuestionOneConclusion \(J\) holds.

This is the dimension-one, one-polytope specialization of the strengthened
question denoted Question1NoEscapeAffirmative in
Literature/Simon2012.lean. No claim is made here for the unrestricted
Question 1, for dimension at least two, or for a finite union of polytopes.

### 12.2 Reduction to an oriented terminal curve

After an affine choice of coordinate, write the full-dimensional compact
convex polytope as the nondegenerate interval \(P=[a,b]\). Put

\[
 H(x,1)=(p(x),q(x)),\qquad d(x)=q(x)-p(x).
 \tag{12.2}
\]

Continuity of the homotopy gives continuity of \(p,q,d\). Boundary fixation
gives

\[
 p(a)=q(a)=a,\qquad p(b)=q(b)=b.
 \tag{12.3}
\]

The terminal-diagonal condition must be read carefully: \(d(x)=0\) is allowed
at an interior source parameter \(x\), but then the common terminal value
\(p(x)=q(x)\) must lie in \(\{a,b\}\). The no-escape hypothesis is precisely

\[
 p([a,b])\subseteq[a,b].
 \tag{12.4}
\]

There is nevertheless a zero-free **bridge component** on which \(d\) has a
constant orientation and whose terminal first coordinate runs from one
endpoint of \(P\) to the other. To see this, let

\[
 Z_a=\{x:d(x)=0,\ p(x)=a\},\qquad
 Z_b=\{x:d(x)=0,\ p(x)=b\}.
 \tag{12.4a}
\]

These are disjoint nonempty compact sets containing \(a\) and \(b\),
respectively, and together exhaust \(Z=\{d=0\}\). Let \(\beta=\min Z_b\) and
\(\alpha=\max(Z_a\cap[a,\beta])\). Then \(\alpha<\beta\), there is no zero of
\(d\) in \((\alpha,\beta)\), and

\[
 p(\alpha)=q(\alpha)=a,\qquad
 p(\beta)=q(\beta)=b.
 \tag{12.4b}
\]

Thus \(d\) has one strict sign throughout \((\alpha,\beta)\). If \(d>0\), use
the parameter endpoint \(\beta\) and the state-space endpoint \(b\). If
\(d<0\), reflect the following argument, using \(\alpha\) and \(a\). This
bridge lemma is the step that replaces the false inference that \(d\) has one
sign on the whole source interval.

Let \(\omega>0\) be the common scale in hypotheses (6)--(7). Applying (7) at
the boundary point \(b\) and the unique piece gives a point \(z\in P\) such
that

\[
 |b-z|\ge\omega,\qquad [z,b]\subseteq G(b)\subseteq J(b).
 \tag{12.5}
\]

Indeed the distance-to-piece inequality forces the target into the closed
interval \(P\), so \(z\le b-\omega\).

We will also use the following elementary one-dimensional consequence of the
local-fibre hypothesis. If \((s,t)\in G\), then

\[
 [\min(s,t),\max(s,t)]\subseteq G(s).
 \tag{12.6}
\]

The fibre \(G(s)\subseteq\mathbb R\) is contractible and contains \(s\).
Hence it is path connected, and every connected subset of the real line
contains the interval between any two of its points. In particular, every
terminal edge of length less than \(\omega\) lies in \(G\) by hypothesis (6)
and can be stopped at any intermediate real point.

### 12.3 A return segment at the oriented endpoint

We construct a \(J\)-orbit beginning at \(b\), having positive variation,
and converging back to \(b\). Repeating that segment in the extended-orbit
grammar will prove the theorem.

Assume the bridge orientation is \(d>0\). First suppose there are points
arbitrarily close to the parameter endpoint \(\beta\) from the left at which
\(p(x)<b\). By continuity, first choose a tail interval in
\((\alpha,\beta]\) on which
\(p(x)\in V\) and \(0\le q(x)-p(x)<\omega\) for every \(x\) in the
interval. Then choose \(x_0\) with \(p(x_0)<b\), sufficiently far into that
tail that

\[
 z<p(x_0)<b,\qquad p(x_0)\in V,\qquad
 0<q(x_0)-p(x_0)<\omega.
 \tag{12.7}
\]

Here \(V\) is the compact neighborhood from the seven hypotheses; the choice
is possible because \(b\) lies in its interior and \(p,q\to b\). Set
\(y_0=p(x_0)\). Equation (12.5) supplies the first edge \(b\to y_0\).

If \(q(x_0)\ge b\), the terminal edge
\(p(x_0)\to q(x_0)\) is a strict small edge and hence is local. By (12.6) it
contains the edge \(y_0\to b\), giving a two-edge return.

Otherwise set \(y_1=q(x_0)<b\). Since

\[
 p(x_0)=y_0<y_1<p(\beta)=b,
\]

the intermediate value theorem, applied between \(x_0\) and \(\beta\), gives
\(x_1\in(x_0,\beta)\) with
\(p(x_1)=y_1\). Continue inductively. At step \(n\), if
\(q(x_n)\ge b\), use the same small-local-fibre argument to end at \(b\).
If \(q(x_n)<b\), put

\[
 y_{n+1}=q(x_n)>p(x_n)=y_n
 \tag{12.8}
\]

and choose \(x_{n+1}\in(x_n,\beta)\) with
\(p(x_{n+1})=y_{n+1}\). All \(x_n\) remain in the tail chosen before
(12.7), so every possible crossing of \(b\) is a strict small local edge.

If the construction never ends, both \(x_n\) and \(y_n\) increase and have
limits \(x_*\le\beta\) and \(y_*\le b\). Continuity and (12.8) give

\[
 p(x_*)=y_*=q(x_*).
 \tag{12.9}
\]

Since \(d\) has no zero in \((\alpha,\beta)\) and \(x_*>x_0>\alpha\),
(12.9) forces \(x_*=\beta\). Equation (12.4b) then gives \(y_*=b\). Thus

\[
 b,y_0,y_1,y_2,\ldots
 \tag{12.10}
\]

is an infinite \(J\)-orbit converging to \(b\), with first-edge variation
\(b-y_0>0\).

It remains to handle the alternative that \(p(x)=b\) throughout some
left-neighborhood of the parameter endpoint \(\beta\) inside the bridge.
Choose \(x_0\in(\alpha,\beta)\) in that neighborhood so close to \(\beta\)
that

\[
 0<q(x_0)-b<\omega,\qquad q(x_0)\in V,\qquad
 \operatorname{dist}(q(x_0),\partial P)<\omega.
 \tag{12.11}
\]

The terminal image gives the edge \(b\to q(x_0)\). Apply hypothesis (7) at
the exterior point \(q(x_0)>b\). Its target \(w\) is no farther from \(P\)
than \(q(x_0)\), but is at distance at least \(\omega\) from \(q(x_0)\).
Because \(q(x_0)-b<\omega\), these two inequalities force \(w<b\).
The certified segment from \(q(x_0)\) to \(w\) therefore contains \(b\), and
gives the return edge \(q(x_0)\to b\).

Consequently, in every subcase there is either a finite positive-variation
return orbit at \(b\), or an infinite positive-variation orbit converging to
\(b\). For \(d<0\), the reflected construction uses \(a\).

### 12.4 Compilation to an extended orbit

Use countably many identical copies of the return segment. A finite return
segment is stitched to the next copy at its last point. An infinite return
segment is stitched to the next copy using its proved convergence to the
same endpoint. This is exactly the finiteStitch/infiniteStitch grammar of
ExtendedOrbitData.

Each active segment contributes at least the fixed positive first-edge cost

\[
 c=|b-y_0|>0
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 c=|q(x_0)-b|>0.
 \tag{12.12}
\]

The rectangular prefix containing the first edge of \(m\) segments has
variation at least \(mc\). Hence the extended variation is unbounded, which
is QuestionOneConclusion \(J\).

### 12.5 Exact scope and next check

The proof avoids the stronger restart-set demand in
SIMON_RESTARTABILITY_ONE_POLYTOPE.md: an infinite return may have terminal
edges whose lengths tend to zero. Extended-orbit stitching, rather than a
uniformly charged edge at every state, is the correct global consumer.

The no-escape condition (12.1) is used exactly in the bridge and
intermediate-value arguments: it keeps the first coordinates \(p(x_n)\) in
the interval and
prevents the terminal curve from placing every accessible first coordinate
beyond the endpoint. Removing it is not justified by this proof. The next
adversarial check should focus on (i) the zero-set bridge construction
(12.4a)--(12.4b), (ii) the endpoint dichotomy for \(p\), (iii) the real-line
interval consequence (12.6) of intrinsic contractibility, and (iv) the
exterior-target inference in (12.11). If those pass, this is a bounded,
theorem-level topology packet suitable for independent review.

There is currently **no adapter from the Fin4 hard-residual packet to this
theorem**. Simon's game-facing construction lives in the separate literature
lane: Lemma 4.5, which produces the seven hypotheses for a higher-dimensional
union of player-indexed polytopes, remains unproved, and it does not supply
the one-dimensional/one-piece specialization used here. The present theorem
is therefore a genuine Simon special case, not progress from the current
positive-minimum Fin4 paid port to a uniform equilibrium.

## 13. Cardinal descent forces an actual double-debtor source

### 13.1 Statement

Let \(I\) be a finite player set with at least two players, let \(r\) be a
quitting reward table, and suppose

\[
 \operatorname{Expl}_r(\sigma)\ge\gamma>0
 \quad\hbox{for every behavioral profile }\sigma.
 \tag{13.1}
\]

Assume that for two distinct players \(a,b\), the games obtained by deleting
\(a\) and by deleting \(b\) each have a uniform-equilibrium payoff.

**Theorem 13.1 (ordinary mathematics; not Lean-checked).** There is one
actual ambient behavioral profile \(\sigma_*\) and two distinct players
\(i\ne j\) such that

\[
 d_i(\sigma_*)\ge\gamma,\qquad d_j(\sigma_*)\ge\gamma.
 \tag{13.2}
\]

In particular, every cardinal-minimal counterexample with at least two
players has an actual double-debtor source at its full terminal gap. For
Fin4, all four one-player deletions are covered by the checked three-player
uniform-equilibrium theorem, so any two labels may be used as the endpoints
of the construction.

### 13.2 The two uniquely labeled endpoint profiles

Fix \(0<\varepsilon<\gamma\). Choose a terminal
\(\varepsilon\)-Nash profile of the game deleting \(a\), and lift it to the
ambient game by making \(a\) play literal Never. Call the lift
\(\lambda_a\). Deletion naturality preserves both prescribed payoff and the
unrestricted behavioral cap of every survivor. Hence

\[
 d_k(\lambda_a)\le\varepsilon\qquad(k\ne a).
 \tag{13.3}
\]

The ambient gap (13.1) forces some coordinate debt to be at least \(\gamma\).
Since \(\varepsilon<\gamma\), that coordinate must be \(a\):

\[
 d_a(\lambda_a)\ge\gamma.
 \tag{13.4}
\]

The same construction for the deletion of \(b\) gives

\[
 d_k(\lambda_b)\le\varepsilon\quad(k\ne b),\qquad
 d_b(\lambda_b)\ge\gamma.
 \tag{13.5}
\]

Thus \(\lambda_a\) belongs to the full-gap debtor set of \(a\) and to no
other full-gap debtor set, while \(\lambda_b\) is uniquely labeled by \(b\).
This uses the checked deletion transport against arbitrary behavioral
deviations; no stationary or finite-clock restriction is present.

### 13.3 An actual continuous stopping-law path

For each player \(k\), let \(\mu_k^a,\mu_k^b\) be the complete stopping laws
of that player's strategies in \(\lambda_a,\lambda_b\). For
\(t\in[0,1]\), set

\[
 \mu_k^t=(1-t)\mu_k^a+t\mu_k^b.
 \tag{13.6}
\]

The stopping-law mixture compiler realizes \(\mu_k^t\) by an actual
behavioral strategy. Applying it independently in every coordinate gives an
actual profile \(\lambda(t)\), with
\(\lambda(0)=\lambda_a\) and \(\lambda(1)=\lambda_b\).

Every debt coordinate \(t\mapsto d_k(\lambda(t))\) is continuous. Here is a
direct argument that avoids any weak-limit or late-mass issue. The joint
product of the laws in (13.6) expands into the finite mixture

\[
 \bigotimes_{k\in I}\mu_k^t
 =
 \sum_{\xi\in\{a,b\}^{I}}w_\xi(t)
 \bigotimes_{k\in I}\mu_k^{\xi_k},
 \tag{13.7}
\]

where every \(w_\xi(t)\) is a polynomial in \(t\). Bounded terminal payoff is
therefore continuous in \(t\).

For player \(k\)'s cap, fix any pure stopping time \(s\in\mathbb N\cup
\{\mathrm{Never}\}\). The payoff from \(s\) against the mixed opponents has
the analogous \(2^{|I|-1}\)-term expansion

\[
 V_k^t(s)=
 \sum_{\xi\in\{a,b\}^{I\setminus\{k\}}}
 w_{\xi,-k}(t)V_k^\xi(s).
 \tag{13.8}
\]

All \(V_k^\xi(s)\) are uniformly bounded by the reward bound. Consequently
the right side is continuous in \(t\), uniformly over every finite time and
Never. Taking the supremum over \(s\) preserves continuity:

\[
 |B_k(\lambda(t))-B_k(\lambda(t'))|
 \le C_k\sum_\xi|w_{\xi,-k}(t)-w_{\xi,-k}(t')|
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{13.9}
\]

Pure-time extremality identifies this supremum with the unrestricted
behavioral cap. Subtracting the continuous prescribed payoff proves
continuity of \(d_k\). Notice that (13.7)--(13.9) use a fixed finite mixture
of complete laws. They do not assert the false general continuity of terminal
semantics under arbitrary weakly convergent moving profiles.

### 13.4 Closed-cover overlap

For each player \(k\), define the closed subset of the path parameter

\[
 A_k=\{t\in[0,1]:d_k(\lambda(t))\ge\gamma\}.
 \tag{13.10}
\]

The gap assumption (13.1) says

\[
 [0,1]=\bigcup_{k\in I}A_k.
 \tag{13.11}
\]

Equations (13.3)--(13.5) say that \(0\) belongs only to \(A_a\), while \(1\)
belongs only to \(A_b\). If all the \(A_k\) were pairwise disjoint, then,
because this is a finite closed cover, every nonempty \(A_k\) would also be
open in \([0,1]\). The connected interval would be partitioned into at least
the two nonempty clopen sets containing \(0\) and \(1\), a contradiction.
Therefore \(A_i\cap A_j\ne\varnothing\) for some \(i\ne j\). Any
\(t_*\) in that intersection gives the actual profile
\(\sigma_*=\lambda(t_*)\) and (13.2).

### 13.5 What this changes, and the remaining induction invariant

Theorem 13.1 is stronger than the existing proper-face outsider passport in
one precise respect: it converts two independently selected child
equilibria into **one actual parent profile** carrying two simultaneous
full-gap debts. It is nonchronological and uses the ambient positive-gap
hypothesis globally along an entire path, so the arbitrary-selector and
positive-reach nesting regressions do not refute it.

It still does not compile a parent equilibrium. The overlap labels \(i,j\)
need not equal the endpoint labels \(a,b\), and nothing here makes either
profitable response preserve the other debt or re-enter a lower-cardinality
face. The genuinely stronger induction invariant suggested by the proof is
therefore not another outsider label: it is a projectively coherent family
of child equilibria whose coordinatewise stopping-law interpolations retain
low survivor debt on the relevant parameter faces. Cardinal minimality
supplies the endpoint profiles but not that facewise control.

For the current Fin4 architecture, (13.2) can be fed twice into the checked
actual-reach paid-row extractor, yielding two source-supported paid rows on
the same literal profile (with the usual constant loss). No checked consumer
currently turns those two rows into a compatible Bellman chronology, so no
Fin4 closure is claimed.

## 14. The full-gap debt-collision graph is connected

The pair in Theorem 13.1 need not contain either of the two deletion labels
chosen at its endpoints.  Repeating the path argument for all pairs extracts
a stronger invariant which keeps every endpoint label.

### 14.1 Definition and theorem

Under (13.1), define a simple graph \(G_\gamma(r)\) on the player set \(I\) by

\[
 \{i,j\}\in E(G_\gamma(r))
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 i\ne j\ \hbox{ and }\
 \exists\hbox{ actual behavioral }\sigma,
 \ d_i(\sigma)\ge\gamma,\ d_j(\sigma)\ge\gamma.
 \tag{14.1}
\]

**Theorem 14.1 (ordinary mathematics; not Lean-checked).** Suppose every
one-player deletion of \(r\) has terminal \(\varepsilon\)-Nash profiles for
every \(\varepsilon>0\), against unrestricted behavioral deviations. Then
\(G_\gamma(r)\) is connected.

In particular this applies to every cardinal-minimal counterexample.  It also
applies directly to Fin4 using the checked at-most-three-player theorem.

### 14.2 Proof by the nerve of each two-face path

Fix arbitrary distinct labels \(a,b\). Choose
\(0<\varepsilon<\gamma\), form the uniquely labeled quiet lifts
\(\lambda_a,\lambda_b\) from Section 13.2, and join them by the actual
coordinatewise stopping-law path \(\lambda(t)\) of Section 13.3. Define

\[
 A_k=\{t\in[0,1]:d_k(\lambda(t))\ge\gamma\}.
 \tag{14.2}
\]

These are a finite closed cover of the interval. Moreover \(0\) belongs only
to \(A_a\), and \(1\) belongs only to \(A_b\).

Make the intersection graph \(N_{a,b}\) whose vertices are the labels \(k\)
with \(A_k\ne\varnothing\), joining \(i,j\) when
\(A_i\cap A_j\ne\varnothing\). This graph is connected. Indeed, if its
vertices split into two nonempty unions \(L,R\) with no crossing edge, then

\[
 \bigcup_{i\in L}A_i
 \qquad\hbox{and}\qquad
 \bigcup_{j\in R}A_j
 \tag{14.3}
\]

are disjoint nonempty closed sets covering the connected interval, a
contradiction. The graph contains \(a\) and \(b\), because of the two endpoint
labels. Every edge of \(N_{a,b}\) is an edge of \(G_\gamma(r)\): any point in
the corresponding intersection gives the required literal profile
\(\lambda(t)\). Hence \(G_\gamma(r)\) contains a path from \(a\) to \(b\).
As \(a,b\) were arbitrary, \(G_\gamma(r)\) is connected.

This uses no compact limit of behavioral profiles.  Every edge witness is an
actual member of one fixed finite complete-stopping-law mixture path, and the
continuity of its unrestricted debts is exactly (13.7)--(13.9).

### 14.3 Fin4 source dichotomy

Every connected graph on four vertices contains a three-edge spanning tree.
Choose one actual profile witnessing each tree edge.  Then exactly one of the
following holds:

1. some actual profile has at least three distinct debts at least
   \(\gamma\); or
2. the three chosen tree edges can be witnessed by three distinct actual
   profiles, each with at least its two endpoint debts at least \(\gamma\).

For if one profile witnessed two distinct tree edges, the union of their
endpoint labels would have cardinality at least three, giving the first arm.
Thus in the absence of a triple debtor, edge witnesses for distinct tree
edges are necessarily distinct.  In either arm every one of the four player
labels occurs in an actual full-gap collision source.

This is stronger than selecting one outsider on each quiet face.  It rules
out any proposed positive-gap certificate whose full-gap debtor regions have
a disconnected pairwise-intersection graph, and it supplies a finite actual
source atlas on which all labels occur.  It does **not** identify the profiles
on different edges, preserve one edge while moving to another, or reduce the
unrestricted deviation problem to a child game.  Thus it is a cross-face
necessary invariant and a falsifier for candidate certificates, not yet the
compiler requested in
`questions/CARDINAL_MINIMAL_OUTSIDER_CONSUMER.md`.

## 15. A deleted-player half reset keeps the child profile and forces a receiver

The preceding path can be made source-matched to one chosen deletion label.
This produces more than an abstract collision edge: one endpoint is a quiet
lift of a child approximate equilibrium, only the deleted player moves, and
the child strategies remain literally unchanged.

### 15.1 Quantitative source-matched theorem

Assume

\[
 |r_i(S)|\le M,\qquad M>0,\qquad
 \max_i d_i(\sigma)\ge\gamma>0
 \quad\hbox{for every actual profile }\sigma.
 \tag{15.1}
\]

Fix a player \(w\). Suppose the game deleting \(w\) has a terminal
\(\varepsilon\)-Nash profile against every behavioral deviation, where

\[
 0\le\varepsilon<\gamma/2.
 \tag{15.2}
\]

**Theorem 15.1 (ordinary mathematics; not Lean-checked).** There are actual
profiles \(q,x\) and a player \(j\ne w\) such that:

1. \(q\) is the quiet lift of the displayed child profile, so \(q_w\) is
   literal Never and \(d_i(q)\le\varepsilon\) for every \(i\ne w\);
2. \(x=q[w\leftarrow x_w]\), while every child strategy is literally
   unchanged;
3. the update is a paid half reset with

   \[
   B_w(x)=B_w(q),\qquad d_w(x)=\gamma/2,
   \qquad U_w(x)-U_w(q)=d_w(q)-\gamma/2\ge\gamma/2;
   \tag{15.3}
   \]

4. the same endpoint has a full-gap survivor receiver,

   \[
   d_j(x)\ge\gamma;
   \tag{15.4}
   \]

5. if \(\alpha(x_w)\) is the ever-Quit mass of \(w\)'s complete stopping law
   against all-Continue opponents, then

   \[
   \alpha(x_w)\ge{\gamma-\varepsilon\over4M}
   >{\gamma\over8M}.
   \tag{15.5}
   \]

Thus every one-player deletion supplies a literal, macroscopically non-Never,
source-attached entrance from a child approximate equilibrium to a
two-debtor parent profile. The mass in (15.5) is own-law ever-Quit mass; it is
not silently promoted to actual preemption or terminal absorption mass.

### 15.2 Proof

Deletion naturality gives \(d_i(q)\le\varepsilon\) for all survivors. The
ambient gap and (15.2) therefore force

\[
 d_0:=d_w(q)\ge\gamma.
 \tag{15.6}
\]

Choose a behavioral strategy \(\beta_w\) whose payoff against the fixed child
opponents is within a strict error smaller than \(\gamma/2\) of \(w\)'s
unrestricted cap. Put

\[
 q^\beta=q[w\leftarrow\beta_w],\qquad
 d_1=d_w(q^\beta)<\gamma/2.
 \tag{15.7}
\]

No cap is assumed attained. The definition of the supremum supplies such a
strict approximation.

Mix the complete stopping laws of literal Never and \(\beta_w\), changing
only coordinate \(w\), and call the resulting actual profile \(x(t)\).
The moved player's cap depends only on its fixed opponents, its prescribed
payoff is affine in this complete-law mixture, and hence

\[
 d_w(x(t))=(1-t)d_0+t d_1.
 \tag{15.8}
\]

This is also the checked theorem
**quittingTerminalSemanticDebt_stoppingLawMixture_eq_self** in
TerminalSemanticStoppingLawDebtConvexity.lean. Since
\(d_0\ge\gamma>\gamma/2>d_1\), there is a unique \(t_*\in(0,1)\) with

\[
 d_w(x(t_*))=\gamma/2.
 \tag{15.9}
\]

Set \(x=x(t_*)\). Fixed opponents give the cap identity in (15.3), and
subtracting the two debts gives its exact payoff identity. At \(x\), player
\(w\)'s debt is strictly below the ambient gap. Therefore (15.1) selects a
different player \(j\) with (15.4). This is the key source matching: no
compact limit, second face selection, or endpoint relabeling is used.

It remains to prove the mass floor. Couple \(x_w\) to literal Never by using
the stopping-law mixture coin. The two parent plays can differ only on the
event that the \(x_w\) law ever Quits, whose probability is
\(\alpha(x_w)\). For every fixed strategy of survivor \(j\), terminal payoffs
under the two opponent environments differ by at most
\(2M\alpha(x_w)\). Taking suprema over all behavioral deviations preserves
the same cap bound, while the prescribed payoff has the same bound. Hence

\[
 d_j(x)\le d_j(q)+4M\alpha(x_w)
 \le\varepsilon+4M\alpha(x_w).
 \tag{15.10}
\]

Combining (15.4) and (15.10) proves (15.5).

### 15.3 Exact operational dichotomy at the receiver

The same proof exposes where the survivor debt came from. Write

\[
 \Delta B_j=B_j(x)-B_j(q),\qquad
 \Delta U_j=U_j(x)-U_j(q).
\]

Then

\[
 \Delta B_j-\Delta U_j
 =d_j(x)-d_j(q)\ge\gamma-\varepsilon.
 \tag{15.11}
\]

Consequently at least one of

\[
 \Delta B_j\ge(\gamma-\varepsilon)/2,\qquad
 -\Delta U_j\ge(\gamma-\varepsilon)/2
 \tag{15.12}
\]

holds. The first arm means that one arbitrarily close behavioral response of
\(j\) has a macroscopic payoff rise when \(w\)'s law is activated; the second
means that the unchanged prescribed child strategy has a macroscopic payoff
loss. This is a genuine parent-versus-deleted-face operational effect on the
same two profiles, not a static reward-table label.

The perturbation estimate in (15.10) overlaps the independently reviewed
macroscopic outsider-repair bound in
CHATGPT_EXTERNAL__CODIMENSION_ONE_QUIET_FACE_ATOM.md. The new point here is
the exact half-debt selection (15.8)--(15.9), which simultaneously retains
the child profile, produces the paid deleted-player entrance, and forces the
full-gap receiver (15.4) at that same endpoint.

For Fin4, this construction may be run for every \(w\) using the checked
three-player theorem. It supplies the nontrivial source-attached entrance
that quiet deletion alone lacks. It still does not condition the receiver's
unrestricted deviation into a three-player deviation, because that deviation
may exploit \(w\)'s activated law. Nor does own-law mass (15.5) guarantee
that \(w\) survives opponent preemption. The remaining cardinal consumer must
use the operational dichotomy (15.12), rather than deleting \(w\) again and
losing the very effect which created the receiver.

## 16. Exact regression: four source-matched entrances need not compose

Theorem 15.1 may be run for all four deleted labels. Selecting one receiver
at each endpoint gives a directed graph with out-degree at least one, hence a
directed label cycle. That cycle is still not a profile chronology. The
following exact game shows that even a connected four-cycle of source-matched
half resets, all in the cap-rise arm of (15.12), does not itself force a
block or a contradiction.

### 16.1 The sparse table

Identify players with \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\). For each \(w\), put

\[
 k(w)=w+2,\qquad j(w)=w+1\pmod 4.
\]

Set every reward coordinate equal to zero except

\[
 r_w(\{w,k(w)\})=2,\qquad
 r_{j(w)}(\{w,j(w),k(w)\})=2
 \quad(w=0,1,2,3).
 \tag{16.1}
\]

The eight exceptional coordinates are distinct as player-coordinate pairs.
Equivalently, they are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{coordinate}&\text{value}\\ \hline
r_0(\{0,2\}),\ r_1(\{1,3\}),\
r_2(\{0,2\}),\ r_3(\{1,3\})&2\\
r_1(\{0,1,2\}),\ r_2(\{1,2,3\}),\
r_3(\{0,2,3\}),\ r_0(\{0,1,3\})&2.
\end{array}
\tag{16.2}
\]

The reward bound is \(M=2\).

### 16.2 Four exact child faces and their half resets

For each \(w\), let \(q^w\) make player \(k(w)\) Quit surely at date zero and
make every other player, including \(w\), play literal Never. In the game
deleting \(w\), this is an exact terminal Nash profile against every
behavioral deviation. The anchor receives its singleton reward zero and is
indifferent to Never; either remaining survivor receives zero, and quitting
at date zero merely forms an unspecified pair whose payoff coordinate is
zero. Since the anchor quits surely at date zero, no later behavioral choice
can do better.

In the ambient game, player \(w\) receives zero at \(q^w\), while quitting at
date zero forms \(\{w,k(w)\}\) and pays two. Hence

\[
 d_w(q^w)=2,\qquad d_i(q^w)=0\quad(i\ne w).
 \tag{16.3}
\]

Now let \(x^w\) keep the three child strategies unchanged and replace only
\(w\)'s strategy by: Quit at date zero with probability \(1/2\), otherwise
Never. Then

\[
 U_w(x^w)=1,\qquad B_w(x^w)=2,\qquad d_w(x^w)=1.
 \tag{16.4}
\]

Thus \(q^w\to x^w\) is exactly the paid half reset of Theorem 15.1 at
\(\gamma=1\), with gain one, fixed cap, and ever-Quit mass \(1/2\).

The receiver \(j(w)\) gets prescribed payoff zero at \(x^w\): whether or not
\(w\) Quits, the realized singleton/pair coordinate for that observer is
unspecified and hence zero. If \(j(w)\) Quits at date zero, it gets zero when
\(w\) plays Never and gets two when \(w\) also Quits. Therefore

\[
 U_{j(w)}(x^w)=0,\qquad B_{j(w)}(x^w)=1,\qquad
 d_{j(w)}(x^w)=1.
 \tag{16.5}
\]

Again the anchor's sure date-zero Quit reduces every behavioral deviation to
this Quit-now versus Continue comparison, so the cap equality is
unrestricted, not stationary-only. The receiver effect is exactly

\[
 B_{j(w)}(x^w)-B_{j(w)}(q^w)=1,\qquad
 U_{j(w)}(x^w)-U_{j(w)}(q^w)=0.
 \tag{16.6}
\]

Thus all four sources lie in the cap-rise arm of (15.12). Their simultaneous
debt labels contain the connected cycle

\[
 0-1-2-3-0.
 \tag{16.7}
\]

### 16.3 Why this is a regression, not a counterexample

The full game has the exact all-Never equilibrium with payoff zero. Every own
singleton reward is zero, so every finite or randomized behavioral stopping
deviation still receives zero. The same profile is an exact Nash profile in
every finite horizon and therefore gives a uniform-equilibrium payoff.

Consequently the following implication is false:

> four source-matched child equilibria + four macroscopic paid half resets +
> full-gap receivers + a connected receiver cycle imply a composable block or
> a parent contradiction.

The missing datum is genuinely global. In a cardinal-minimal counterexample,
the positive terminal gap holds at every interpolation and at every attempted
splice between the four source profiles. The local packets (16.3)--(16.7) do
not remember that property, and the four child profiles are mutually
unrelated. In particular, a consumer cannot concatenate the label cycle
without an actual profile-to-profile seam or a rule controlling exploitability
on the connecting complete-law paths.

This regression does not refute Theorems 14.1 or 15.1: the sparse game lacks
their global positive-gap premise. It instead proves that any next cardinal
step must use that premise again after the four sources have been produced.
Merely extracting supported rows, receiver labels, or cap-rise orientations
from the endpoints discards the only hypothesis capable of relating them.

### 16.4 Joint interpolation still has a positive gap

The regression is stronger than an endpoint list. Let
\(\lambda=(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_3)\) range over the standard simplex and,
for each player separately, take the complete-stopping-law mixture of that
player's four strategies in \(x^0,x^1,x^2,x^3\) with common coefficients
\(\lambda\). This is an actual product behavioral profile
\(\sigma(\lambda)\); no public lottery is used.

Every endpoint strategy stops only at date zero or Never. Therefore
\(\sigma(\lambda)\) is the one-row product profile with date-zero hazards

\[
 h_i={1\over2}\lambda_i+\lambda_{i-2},
 \qquad \sum_i h_i={3\over2}.
 \tag{16.8}
\]

Despite the exact uniform equilibrium outside this simplex, every profile in
the joint interpolation has a uniform positive terminal gap:

\[
 \boxed{\max_i d_i(\sigma(\lambda))\ge {3\over64}.}
 \tag{16.9}
\]

To prove this, partition the players into the two opposite pairs
\(\{0,2\}\) and \(\{1,3\}\). One pair has total hazard
\(s\ge3/4\). Inside that pair choose \(k\) with the larger hazard and \(w\)
with the smaller hazard. Then

\[
 h_k\ge s/2\ge3/8,\qquad
 h_w\le s/2\le3/4.
 \tag{16.10}
\]

If \(r,t\) are the two adjacent players outside this opposite pair, then

\[
 h_r+h_t={3\over2}-s\le3/4,
\qquad
 (1-h_r)(1-h_t)\ge1-h_r-h_t\ge1/4.
 \tag{16.11}
\]

Player \(w\)'s Quit-now payoff contains the nonnegative event on which exactly
its opposite \(k\) Quits among the three opponents. On that event the
coalition is \(\{w,k\}\) and (16.1) pays \(w\) exactly two. Thus

\[
 Q_w\ge2h_k(1-h_r)(1-h_t).
 \tag{16.12}
\]

Every nonzero reward coordinate of player \(w\) contains \(w\). Its Continue
payoff at this one-row profile is therefore zero. Since \(Q_w\ge0\), its
prescribed payoff is \(h_wQ_w\), its cap is \(Q_w\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
 d_w(\sigma(\lambda))
  &=(1-h_w)Q_w\\
  &\ge (1/4)\,2\,(3/8)\,(1/4)=3/64.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16.13}
\]

This proves (16.9).

Hence even the following strengthened finite-averaging principle is false:

> if four source-matched activated-owner endpoints have a connected receiver
> cycle and the ambient exploitability gap stays uniformly positive at every
> actual point of their joint complete-law interpolation simplex, then the
> endpoints compile a parent equilibrium or force one child gap.

In the regression, the premise holds with the explicit floor \(3/64\), all
four child games have the displayed exact equilibria, and the parent itself
has the all-Never uniform equilibrium. The positive gap disappears only after
leaving the joint interpolation simplex.

Thus a cardinal-minimal proof cannot use the global hypothesis merely as a
finite cover of the convex hull of the four selected sources. It must enlarge
the strategy complex in response to the deviations witnessing that cover, or
control a seam from the interpolation simplex to a new profile. That
enlargement is exactly where arbitrarily late deviations and the absence of a
finite strategy-class completeness theorem re-enter.

## 17. Global response saturation compiles unless it reaches the Never singularity

The preceding regression asks for a response enlargement, rather than one
more interpolation of the four selected endpoints.  There are two distinct
meanings of response closure which must first be separated.

Let

\[
 K=\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}
\]

be the one-point compactification of the pure quitting times.  A complete
behavioral strategy is terminally equivalent to a probability law in
\(\Delta(K)\), implemented by its live hazards.  All products below are
products of independently implemented laws; no public lottery is used.

If a nonempty set of profiles is closed under replacing an arbitrary one
player by **every** complete behavioral strategy, then it is the whole
profile space: starting from one of its points, replace the coordinates one
at a time by those of an arbitrary target profile.  Thus literal closure
under all one-player replacements contains no cardinal information.  The
nontrivial notion is cap completeness of a menu.

For each player \(i\), let \(C_i\subseteq\Delta(K)\) be a nonempty strategy
menu and put \(C=\prod_i C_i\).  Call the product menu **cap complete** if

\[
 B_i(q)=\sup_{a_i\in C_i}U_i(a_i,q_{-i})
 \qquad(q\in C,\ i\in I).
 \tag{17.1}
\]

This equality permits nonattainment in the definition.  In the compact
absorbing case below, continuity will make the right-hand supremum a maximum.

### 17.1 Finite response-basis compiler

**Theorem 17.1 (ordinary mathematics; not Lean-checked).**  If every \(C_i\)
is finite and (17.1) holds, then the quitting game has an exact unrestricted
terminal Nash profile.

**Proof.**  Regard the members of \(C_i\) as the pure actions of a finite
normal-form game, with payoff equal to the quitting terminal payoff of the
corresponding behavioral profile.  Take a mixed Nash equilibrium.  For each
player independently mix the complete stopping laws of the strategies in
its mixed action.  Stopping-law multilinearity gives an actual behavioral
profile \(q^*\) with exactly the same joint payoff and the same payoff from
each unilateral menu action.

Finite-game Nash optimality therefore gives

\[
 \max_{a_i\in C_i}U_i(a_i,q^*_{-i})=U_i(q^*).
 \tag{17.2}
\]

Equation (17.1) upgrades (17.2) to \(B_i(q^*)=U_i(q^*)\) for every player.
Thus \(q^*\) is exact Nash against every behavioral deviation. \(\square\)

This proves the finite-component arm sought by a response-closure approach.
It also shows that no positive-gap counterexample can have a finite global
response basis.  Finitely many labels attached only to their source profiles
are not such a basis: (17.1) must hold against every opponent profile in the
product mixture complex.

### 17.2 Compact sure-absorption response-hull compiler

The same argument extends to an infinite weakly compact menu away from the
unique terminal-law singularity.

**Theorem 17.2 (ordinary mathematics; not Lean-checked).**  Suppose every
\(C_i\) is a nonempty weakly compact convex subset of \(\Delta(K)\), the
product menu is cap complete, and every \(q\in C\) absorbs almost surely:

\[
 \prod_i q_i(\{\infty\})=0.
 \tag{17.3}
\]

Then the quitting game has an exact unrestricted terminal Nash profile.

**Proof.**  The pure planned-time payoff is locally constant away from the
single profile \((\infty,\ldots,\infty)\).  Its only possible discontinuity is
there.  If \(q^n\to q\) weakly in \(C\), the associated independent product
laws converge weakly.  By (17.3), the limiting product law assigns zero mass
to the sole discontinuity.  The bounded almost-everywhere-continuity theorem
for weak convergence therefore gives

\[
 U_i(q^n)\longrightarrow U_i(q).
 \tag{17.4}
\]

Thus all payoffs are continuous on the compact convex product \(C\), and
they are affine in each player's own law.  The compact-convex Nash theorem
gives a Nash profile \(q^*\) relative to the menus \(C_i\).  Continuity makes
the right side of (17.1) a maximum, relative Nash identifies that maximum
with \(U_i(q^*)\), and cap completeness upgrades it to the unrestricted cap.
Hence every debt is zero. \(\square\)

**Corollary 17.3 (singular exposure).**  In a game with a positive terminal
exploitability gap, every weakly compact convex cap-complete product menu
contains a profile \(q\) with

\[
 q_i(\{\infty\})>0\quad\hbox{for every }i,
 \qquad
 \prod_iq_i(\{\infty\})>0.
 \tag{17.5}
\]

Indeed, otherwise (17.3) holds throughout the menu and Theorem 17.2
contradicts the gap.  This is a genuinely global alternative: a saturated
response hull either compiles an equilibrium, or it reaches a profile whose
product law charges the all-Never singular outcome.  It is stronger than the
finite interpolation no-go in Section 16, but (17.5) is not yet a deleted
face and does not itself select a smaller counterexample.

### 17.3 Seeded column generation and the exact late-clock arm

There is also a constructive finite-stage version.  Start from arbitrary
nonempty finite menus \(A_i^0\), for example all complete strategies appearing
in the finitely many child lifts and activated-owner endpoints.  At stage
\(n\), take a mixed Nash equilibrium of the finite game with actions
\(A_i^n\), and compile it by independent stopping-law mixtures to an actual
profile \(q^n\).

Assume the ambient game has terminal gap at least \(\gamma>0\).  Some player
\(i_n\) has

\[
 B_{i_n}(q^n)-U_{i_n}(q^n)\ge\gamma.
 \tag{17.6}
\]

Behavioral pure-time extremality supplies a pure time
\(t_n\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}\) with

\[
 U_{i_n}(Q_{t_n}^{i_n},q^n_{-i_n})-U_{i_n}(q^n)>\gamma/2.
 \tag{17.7}
\]

Every action already in \(A_{i_n}^n\) has payoff at most
\(U_{i_n}(q^n)\) by restricted Nash optimality.  Hence \(Q_{t_n}^{i_n}\) is
not already in that player's menu.  Adjoin it and repeat.

The process cannot terminate under the positive gap.  Since there are only
finitely many players, one player receives infinitely many distinct pure
times.  There is only one \(\infty\) action, so that player's newly added
finite times are unbounded.  Thus the exact seeded alternative is

\[
 \boxed{\text{finite cap-complete compiler, or one fixed unbounded pure-time column.}}
 \tag{17.8}
\]

This is a source-seeded version of the reviewed finite-deadline horizon
escape, not a new claim that escape has been consumed.  The deadline producer
and its adjusted debt identity are in
CODEX_NOETHER__QUIT_TIME_COMPACTIFICATION.md, and the concise global-gap
version is in CODEX_FERMAT__FINITE_DEADLINE_NASH_HORIZON_ESCAPE.md.  The
strategically precompact complete-reply theorem in Section 6BK of
CODEX_RAMSEY__POSITIVE_ATOM_ROOT_REPAIR_FLOOR.md is a stronger abstract
all-errors consumer than the compact sure-absorption special case above.
The new cardinal-facing point here is the exact application to arbitrary
finite child/source seeds and the identification of (17.5), rather than mere
nontermination, as the only possible compact saturated-hull boundary.

Column generation at the restricted Nash profiles does not by itself prove
that the closure of the growing menus satisfies (17.1) at every newly created
product profile.  The cap graph is not weakly closed at the all-Never point.
Declaring the topological closure to be response complete would therefore
assume precisely the missing boundary estimate.

## 18. Exact regression: the outward Never witness does not descend

The singular output (17.5) is real, but cardinal minimality does not turn one
such profile into a positive gap for a deleted game.  The following Fin4
table gives an exact regression with a positive-gap late-response orbit and a
positive-gap Never boundary, while every deletion has an exact terminal Nash
profile.

Let players \(0,1\) be active and players \(2,3\) be payoff-zero dummies.  For
\(i\in\{0,1\}\), write \(\bar i=1-i\) and define, for every nonempty
coalition \(S\),

\[
 r_i(S)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&i\in S,\\
 2,&i\notin S\text{ and }\bar i\in S,\\
 0,&S\cap\{0,1\}=\varnothing,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 r_2(S)=r_3(S)=0.
 \tag{18.1}
\]

For \(k\ge0\), define

\[
 \sigma^{2k}=(Q_{2k}^0,Q_{2k+1}^1,\infty,\infty),
 \qquad
 \sigma^{2k+1}=(Q_{2k+2}^0,Q_{2k+1}^1,\infty,\infty).
 \tag{18.2}
\]

At each \(\sigma^n\), the earlier active player receives one and the later
active player receives two.  The earlier player can move to the first time
strictly after the other player's time and receive two.  This is an exact
best response of gain one and produces \(\sigma^{n+1}\).  The later active
player already receives its cap two, and the dummies have cap zero.  Hence

\[
 \max_i d_i(\sigma^n)=1\qquad(n\ge0).
 \tag{18.3}
\]

Both active times tend to infinity, so the complete laws converge to the
all-Never profile \(q^\infty\).  At that boundary profile every prescribed
payoff is zero, while either active player can quit alone and receive one.
Therefore

\[
 \max_i d_i(q^\infty)=1.
 \tag{18.4}
\]

The convergent-sequence compactum
\(\{\sigma^n:n\ge0\}\cup\{q^\infty\}\) thus carries the same positive gap at
every point, including its singular boundary.  The displayed successor
edges are actual one-player exact-best-response edges, not annotated labels.

Nevertheless the parent game has an exact terminal Nash profile: player
\(0\) quits at date zero and everyone else plays Never.  Player \(0\) gets
one and cannot improve; player \(1\) gets two and cannot improve; the dummies
always get zero.  This pure profile is also an exact finite-horizon strategy
profile and hence supplies a uniform-equilibrium payoff.

Every one-player deletion likewise has an exact terminal Nash profile.  If a
dummy is deleted, use the same profile.  If active player \(0\) is deleted,
make player \(1\) quit at date zero; if active player \(1\) is deleted, make
player \(0\) quit at date zero.  The sole remaining active player gets its
singleton payoff one and no deviation improves it, while every dummy remains
indifferent at zero.

Thus even the following implication is false:

> an actual unbounded exact-best-response orbit generated from a finite
> strategy seed, whose compact Never boundary still has a fixed positive
> ambient gap, forces a positive global gap in one deleted game.

The example is deliberately not a counterexample to Theorem 17.2.  Its
selected orbit is not cap complete at the boundary: an active finite Quit
response to \(q^\infty\) leaves the displayed compactum and reaches a parent
equilibrium after the other player is kept at Never.  Exhaustively saturating
the response menu therefore finds the finite compiler arm in this game.

What the regression proves is narrower and exactly cardinal-facing.  An
outward witness, even with positive debt at its limit and exact response
ancestry, gives only a high-debt **profile** after deletion.  Cardinal
minimality needs a gap over **every** profile of one fixed child game, and all
four children in (18.1) have exact equilibria elsewhere.  To finish the
cardinal route one still needs at least one of:

1. a quantitative projection theorem which makes one deleted-game debt lower
   bound uniform over all child profiles;
2. a compiler which feeds arbitrary child profiles into one common saturated
   parent response hull; or
3. a boundary conditioning rule preserving unrestricted caps while one
   player's Never mass is raised all the way to one.

Neither response saturation, unbounded pure-time escape, nor the four
source-matched entrances supplies any of these statements by itself.

## 19. All quiet faces force the singularity, and conditioning does not project it

Theorem 17.2 suggests trying to build one compact cap-complete product menu
from the approximate equilibria of all one-player deletions while retaining
almost-sure absorption throughout.  Literal quiet lifting makes this
impossible before any best replies are added.

### 19.1 Cross-face product recombination obstruction

For every player \(w\), let \(q^w\) be any quiet lift of any profile of the
game deleting \(w\).  Thus

\[
 q_w^w=\delta_\infty.
 \tag{19.1}
\]

Suppose coordinate menus \(C_i\) contain every strategy appearing in all the
profiles \(q^w\).  In particular, taking \(w=i\) in (19.1) gives

\[
 \delta_\infty\in C_i\qquad(i\in I).
 \tag{19.2}
\]

Therefore their independent product contains

\[
 (\delta_\infty,\ldots,\delta_\infty)\in\prod_iC_i.
 \tag{19.3}
\]

This profile has joint Never mass one.  Convexifying, taking weak closure, or
adding best replies cannot remove it.  Consequently:

**Proposition 19.1 (exact product obstruction; ordinary mathematics).**  No
independent product menu which contains one literal quiet lift from every
one-player deletion can satisfy the sure-absorption hypothesis (17.3).

The obstruction is not a poor choice of child equilibria.  It uses only the
definition of quiet lift.  A nonproduct union of the face profiles may omit
the cross-face recombination (19.3), but a mixed equilibrium over such joint
profiles uses a public face label and is a correlated device, not an actual
product behavioral profile.  The finite response-basis compiler in Theorem
17.1 needs coordinate menus precisely to avoid that hidden correlation.

In a game with terminal gap \(\gamma\), applying the gap at (19.3) yields a
positive-solo coordinate:

\[
 \max_i \bigl(r_i(\{i\})\bigr)_+\ge\gamma.
 \tag{19.4}
\]

Indeed, against all-Never opponents every finite pure time pays the own
singleton reward and Never pays zero.  Thus the unrestricted debt is exactly
the positive part in (19.4).  This is a genuine global use of the gap, but it
still gives only a singleton deviation at one profile.

### 19.2 The two possible conditionings

Let \(q=\bigotimes_iq_i\) be a product planned-time law with

\[
 a_i=q_i(\{\infty\})>0,\qquad p=\prod_i a_i>0.
 \tag{19.5}
\]

There are two natural attempts to remove its singular mass.

First condition on eventual absorption
\(A=\{(T_i)_i\ne(\infty)_i\}\).  The conditional joint law is

\[
 {\bigotimes_iq_i-p\,\delta_{(\infty,\ldots,\infty)}\over1-p}.
 \tag{19.6}
\]

In general (19.6) is not a product law.  For example, with two players its
conditional probability that both clocks are Never is zero, while both
conditional one-coordinate Never marginals are positive whenever
\(0<a_0,a_1<1\).  Hence conditioning on absorption creates correlation and
does not define an actual behavioral profile in the original quitting game.

Conditioning further on the first finite quitting date and coalition merely
disintegrates (19.6) over pairs \((t,S)\).  At each such event the quitting
game has already terminated with coalition \(S\).  There is no live suffix on
which to run a smaller quitting game or a child equilibrium.  Thus
first-finite-quitter conditioning is an outcome decomposition, not a
cardinal compiler.

The relevant coordinate conditioning which preserves the opponents' product
structure is to fix one player's own clock event.  If \(a_w=1\), that
coordinate is already literal Never.  If \(0<a_w<1\), write

\[
 q_w=a_w\delta_\infty+(1-a_w)q_w^{<\infty},
 \tag{19.7}
\]

where \(q_w^{<\infty}\) is the conditional finite-clock law.  Replacing
\(q_w\) by \(\delta_\infty\) gives the quiet lift of the genuine child profile
\(q_{-w}\).  But the gain of any fixed response \(h_j\) of a survivor
\(j\ne w\) decomposes exactly as

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &U_j(h_j,q_{-j})-U_j(q)\\
 &\quad=a_w\Bigl[U_j(h_j,(q[w\leftarrow\infty])_{-j})
                 -U_j(q[w\leftarrow\infty])\Bigr]\\
 &\qquad +(1-a_w)\Bigl[U_j(h_j,(q[w\leftarrow q_w^{<\infty}])_{-j})
                 -U_j(q[w\leftarrow q_w^{<\infty}])\Bigr].
 \tag{19.8}
\end{aligned}
\]

This is just affinity in the opponent's complete stopping law, applied both
to the response payoff and to the prescribed payoff.  A positive left side
forces one conditional arm to be positive, but supplies no reason for it to
be the first, child-face arm.

The cap-rise branch of the exact Section 16 regression realizes the bad
orientation with no slack.  There, for receiver \(j(w)\), the quiet endpoint
\(q^w\) has debt zero; after the deleted player \(w\) is activated with finite
mass \(1/2\), the same receiver has debt one, entirely because its Quit-now
cap rises on the finite-\(w\) arm.  Conditioning \(w\) back to Never deletes
the whole receiver obstruction.

### 19.3 Exact conclusion for the cardinal route

Combining Propositions 17.1--17.3 and 19.1 gives the precise response-hull
boundary:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{l}
\text{a finite cap-complete menu compiles exactly, while a strategically}\\
\text{precompact complete menu compiles terminal approximations at all errors;}\\
\text{an all-face independent product menu necessarily contains all-Never;}\\
\text{removing that atom by conditioning either correlates players or loses}\\
\text{the profitable arm.}
\end{array}}
\tag{19.9}
\]

The positive solo output (19.4) is not a smaller-game gap.  If \(w\ne i\),
the all-Never profile of the game deleting \(w\) still has player \(i\)'s
debt at least \(\gamma\), but cardinal minimality concerns the infimum over
all child profiles.  Section 18 has exactly this configuration while every
deleted game has an exact equilibrium elsewhere.  Therefore no uniform
projection theorem can use only the singular profile, its first-quitter
disintegration, and the existence of child equilibria.

The remaining possible strengthening must retain additional global structure
through conditioning, for example a lower bound valid for every child profile
or a source-indexed parent compiler which uses private randomization without
independently recombining all quiet coordinates.  The current all-face source
data do not provide such a structure.
