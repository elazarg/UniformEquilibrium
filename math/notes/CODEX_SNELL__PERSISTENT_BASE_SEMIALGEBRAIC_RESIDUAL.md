## 3. Positive persistent excess is already a literal source exit

### Theorem 3.1 (same-source realization of `G`)

Fix an induced Nash point `x` and suppose `gamma<=G(x)` with `gamma>0`.
Then there is a player `i` and a literal unilateral behavioral replacement
of `i` at `sigma_x` whose payoff gain is at least `gamma`.  More precisely:

* if `|B|>=2`, `i` may be chosen in `B` and its replacement is Always
  Continue (equivalently Never at this immediately absorbing source);
* if `B={b}` and the owner component realizes the positive maximum, `i=b`
  and the replacement is Always Continue/Never;
* if an outsider component realizes the positive maximum, `i` is that
  outsider and the replacement is immediate Quit.

In the first Fin4 case there are no outsiders, so the first alternative is
always a base leave.  No Nash point or opponent profile is reselected: the
receiver differs from the displayed source in exactly the named player's
strategy.

#### Proof

For `|B|>=2`, every unilateral replacement still leaves another base member
quitting surely at date zero.  A base member's Continue-minus-Quit payoff is
therefore exactly its component of `G`; an outsider's Quit-minus-Continue
payoff is exactly its component.  Free components are zero.  A finite maximum
at least `gamma>0` must therefore be realized by one of the displayed literal
updates.

Now let `B={b}` and suppose the owner component is at least `gamma`.  Put

```text
Q = U_b(sigma_x),
beta = probability that all free players Continue in one row,
H = b's one-row absorbing contribution after b Continues,
P = b's punishment value,
N = payoff of b's literal Never replacement.          (3.1)
```

For `beta<1`, `N=H/(1-beta)`; for `beta=1`, `N=0`.  The stationary cap is
`max(Q,N)`, while the owner-floor component is

```text
K=H+beta P-Q.                                         (3.2)
```

Since punishment is bounded above by the stationary cap,
`P<=max(Q,N)`.  If `Q>=N`, then

```text
H+beta P <= H+beta Q <= Q,
```

so `K<=0`.  If `N>=Q`, then `P<=N` and

```text
K<=H+beta N-Q=N-Q.                                   (3.3)
```

The same conclusion holds at `beta=1`: when `Q>=0=N`, (3.2) is nonpositive;
when `0=N>=Q`, it is at most `N-Q`.  Thus in all cases

```text
K <= max(0,N-Q).                                     (3.4)
```

Consequently `K>=gamma>0` forces

```text
N-Q>=gamma.                                          (3.5)
```

Because `b` quits surely in `sigma_x`, `Q` is exactly its prescribed payoff,
and the literal Always-Continue update has payoff `N`.  This proves the owner
case.  The outsider case again absorbs at date zero because `b` remains sure
Quit, so its join component is the exact immediate-Quit gain.  QED.

The checked theorem
`singletonBase_inducedNash_floorExcess_semantics` proves the cap-debt
inequality used in (3.4) without dividing by `1-beta`; the calculation above
records why the actual profitable orientation is necessarily Never rather
than Quit now.

The external excess is not controlled by the topology or index of the
induced finite game.

Take `I={b,1,2,3}`, base `{b}`, and let `p_i` denote the Quit probability of
free player `i`.  Choose the three induced Quit-minus-Continue differences


containing `b`.  The pure best-response graph contains the strict six-cycle



Indeed `p_1<1/2` forces successively `p_2=0,p_3=0,p_1=1`, and `p_1>1/2`

free players Quit, and to zero otherwise.  Never guarantees zero, while


This leaves every free-player induced payoff and every cycle edge unchanged,

These are local finite-table regressions, not counterexamples to uniform

## 10. Exact first-order chart at all-Continue

Let

```text
M_ij = r_{ {j} }(i)-r_{ {i} }(i)                    (10.1)
```

be the normalized singleton matrix.  For a probability vector `z` supported
on `F`, put `p=s z`.  Because a passive coordinate has `p_o=0`, its expression
`P_o` is the same division-free face numerator as `H_o`: in both cases the
player's own coordinate is absent from the opponents' product law.  Expanding
the finite product law at `s=0` gives, uniformly for `z` in the closed
simplex supported on `F`,

```text
H_i(sz)/s -> -(Mz)_i                  (i in F),
P_o(sz)/s -> -(Mz)_o                  (o notin F).    (10.2)
```

For example, the singleton terms are

```text
1-c_(-i) = s sum_(j!=i) z_j + O(s^2),
Q_i       = r_{ {i} }(i)+O(s),
N_i       = s sum_(j!=i) z_j r_{ {j} }(i)+O(s^2),   (10.3)
```

and the `z_i` term in `(Mz)_i` is zero because `M_ii=0`.

Define the compact directional defect

```text
L_F(z)=max( max_(i in F) |(Mz)_i|,
            max_(o notin F) (-(Mz)_o)_+ ).           (10.4)
```

### Theorem 10.1 (empty-base radial blow-up obstruction)

Assume

```text
not HasHomogeneousSimplexSolution(M).                (10.5)
```

Then there are constants `s_0>0` and `c_F>0` such that, for every probability
vector `z` supported on `F` and every `0<s<=s_0`,

```text
W(s z) >= c_F s.                                    (10.6)
```

Equivalently, no family approaching all-Continue can satisfy

```text
W(p_n)=o(sum_i p_n(i)).                              (10.7)
```

#### Proof

If `L_F(z)=0`, then

```text
(Mz)_i=0  for i in F,
(Mz)_o>=0 for o notin F.                             (10.8)
```

Since `z>=0`, `sum z=1`, and `z_o=0` off `F`, (10.8) is a homogeneous
simplex-LCP solution: `Mz>=0` and `z_i(Mz)_i=0` for every `i`.  This
contradicts (10.5).  Hence `L_F` is strictly positive on the compact simplex,
and its attained minimum `ell_F` is positive.

Every coordinate expression in (9.2) is a finite polynomial in `(s,z)` and
vanishes at `s=0`.  Dividing by `s` therefore has a continuous polynomial
extension to `s=0`; by (10.2), the corresponding max/absolute-value

```text

```

for all sufficiently small positive `s`, proving (10.6) with
`c_F=ell_F/2`.  QED.

The signs can be checked on the two-player matrix

For `z=(t,1-t)`, (10.4) is `max(t,1-t)>=1/2`; the cleared residual points in

## 11. Supplied boundary families: full hard forces the apex

Now suppose a sequence on the empty-base face is genuinely supplied:

```text

```

nonpositive at `p_*`.  Let

```text
C={i:p_*(i)>0}.                                      (11.2)
```

The following alternatives are exact.

1. If `|C|>=2`, every player has a positive-hazard opponent.  The limiting
   equations and inequalities are therefore exactly the endpoint Nash
   conditions, and the stationary endpoint compiler controls every
   behavioral stopping deviation and Never.  This gives a uniform-equilibrium
   payoff.
2. In the terminal-exploitability branch, `|C|<=1`.
3. If `C={j}`, the limiting row is a positive-rate solo stationary root.
   The limiting `W=0` equations are exactly

   ```text
   (1-p_*(j)) r_{ {i} }(i)+p_*(j)r_{ {i,j} }(i)
      <= r_{ {j} }(i)                       for every i!=j. (11.3)
   ```

   Therefore it is exact endpoint Nash against the singleton payoff vector
   `r_{ {j} }`.  The checked atomic-monopoly theorems give

   ```text
   r_{ {j} }(j) <= -Gamma,
   r_{ {j} }(j) < chi_j,                              (11.4)
   ```

   where `Gamma` is the terminal exploitability gap and `chi_j` is the
   behavioral punishment value.
4. In the quantitative full-support hard residual every player is checked
   punishment-normal, so

   ```text
   chi_j <= r_{ {j} }(j).                             (11.5)
   ```

   Equations (11.4)--(11.5) contradict one another.  Hence the unique-clock
   alternative is impossible in the full hard residual.

The key point in item 3 is that one must use the endpoint value
`r_{ {j} }`, not the repository nontermination payoff zero.  The owner is
exactly indifferent between Quit and the declared successor at the one-row
Bellman level.  Its profitable Never deviation is precisely what forces the
quantitative negative/punishment-moat conclusions (11.4).  This is the
checked `AtomicSoloLimitConsequences` obstruction, and it is stronger here
than extracting another singleton collision edge.

It follows that, in the full Fin4 hard branch, every supplied positive-hazard
family satisfying (11.1) must obey

```text

```

Indeed, otherwise compactness supplies a nonzero limit, and items 1--4 give
a contradiction.  Writing

```text
s_n=sum_i p_n(i),   z_n=p_n/s_n,
```

Theorem 10.1 now applies uniformly to the moving directions `z_n` and gives

```text
W(p_n) >= c_F s_n                                  (eventually). (11.7)
```

Thus the full-hard B.2 boundary is only the radial all-Continue obstruction.
There is no surviving lower-player lift or persistent-clock chronology:
multiple clocks compile, and the unique clock contradicts punishment
normality.  This is stronger than the bare assertion that some active rate
approaches zero or one.  It still does not turn independently supplied radial
rows into one chronological play.

## 12. Why B.2 does not implement the deadline shadow price

The finite-deadline continuation lane produces a source-coherent first entry
of a late action at an artificial shadow price `kappa>=D_*/4`.  Removing the
price leaves a macroscopic actual-game gain even while the late-action mass
tends to zero.  The radial calculation above explains, but does not repair,
that phenomenon:

* `W` is a cleared residual, so a first-order residual `c_F s` tends to zero
  in absolute size while remaining macroscopic after division by hazard;
* the empty-base unknown is only the hazard vector `p`; its active equation
  identifies the successor value with the same row's pure-Quit value and has
  no independent continuation coordinate or action-price coordinate;
* implementing `kappa` would require an enlarged affine Bellman system with
  a retained successor value (or an actual preceding block), not a boundary
  point of the zero-tail system.

This is a type obstruction, not merely a failure to choose the right radial
direction.  Moreover the checked ambient returned-block relative-error gap
says that, in a four-player counterexample, bounded actual continuations on a
returned block cannot make both Bellman and endpoint errors `o(totalHazard)`.
Thus a continuation proposed to replace the artificial price must pay a
comparable error or escape the returned-block hypotheses.  B.2 supplies no
actual value implementing `kappa` and no source seam from the deadline entry
to an empty-base boundary row.

## 15. Approximate-forward question: support accuracy cannot be amortized in the minimum chamber

The next target is the finite forward packet from

#### Proof

`isQuittingRootEndpointNash_of_supportApproxNash`, followed by

```text

```

```text
 = Join_i(q)+(1-b_i)(r_i({i})-v_i),
```

## 20. Global maximal-support orbit trichotomy

There is a useful global completion of the local trap.  Fix `0<delta<=delta0`
and a coordinate box

```text
B_C={v: |v_i|<=C for every i}
```

containing every terminal reward vector.  For each `v in B_C`, let

```text
A_delta(v)={q: IsQuittingRootSupportApproxNash reward v delta q}.
```

This set is nonempty: a finite exact root Nash point exists and is
support-`0`, hence support-`delta`.  It is compact.  Indeed, for a fixed
coordinate each conditional support clause can be written as the union

```text
{played probability=0} union {the corresponding closed endpoint inequality},
```

and there are finitely many coordinates.  Absorption is continuous, so its
maximum on `A_delta(v)` is attained.  Choose a maximizer `q(v)`, with no claim
that this selector is continuous, and define the forward predecessor orbit

```text
v_(t+1)=F(q(v_t),v_t).                                 (20.1)
```

Every successor stays in `B_C`, because it is a convex combination of
coordinates of `v_t` and terminal reward coordinates.

### Theorem 20.1 (maximal-support orbit trichotomy)

For the orbit (20.1), exactly one of the following exhaustive outcomes occurs.

1. The partial sums of `a(q(v_t))` are unbounded.  Hence its finite prefixes
   have arbitrarily large charge, with one fixed support tolerance `delta`
   and literal Bellman successors.
2. Some `v_s` belongs to `N0`.  Then Theorem 16.1 applies and the orbit is
   identically `v_s` with all-Continue roots from date `s` onward.
3. The orbit never enters `N0`, its total charge is finite, and there is a
   limit `v_infty in B_C\N0` such that

   ```text
   v_t -> v_infty,
   q(v_t) -> allContinue,
   F(allContinue,v_infty)=v_infty,                    (20.2)
   allContinue is the unique exact root Nash at v_infty,
   v_infty_i-r_i({i}) >= delta for every i.            (20.3)
   ```

#### Proof of the summable arm

If the nonnegative charge series is summable, then `a(q(v_t))->0`.  Every
Quit marginal is at most absorption, so the roots converge coordinatewise to
all-Continue.  The checked movement estimate

```text
|v_(t+1,i)-v_(t,i)| <= 2 C a(q(v_t))
```

makes every payoff coordinate Cauchy; call the limit `v_infty`.  If no date
enters the open set `N0`, closedness of its complement gives
`v_infty notin N0`.

It remains to use maximality.  Let `r` be any exact root Nash point at
`v_infty`.  It is support-`0`.  Because `delta>0` and all endpoint
differences are continuous in the displayed tail, the same fixed `r` is
support-`delta` at `v_t` for all sufficiently large `t`.  Therefore

```text
a(r) <= a(q(v_t)) -> 0.
```

Thus `a(r)=0`, so `r=allContinue`.  Existence of an exact root at `v_infty`
then proves both that all-Continue is exact and that it is the unique exact
root.  The Bellman fixed-point identity in (20.2) is literal for every tail.

Finally suppose for some `i` that

```text
0 <= v_infty_i-r_i({i}) < delta.
```

Choose `delta'<delta` larger than this gap and give only player `i` a small
positive Quit probability.  Its two endpoint inequalities hold at tolerance
`delta'`; all other players play Continue, and their endpoint differences
start nonpositive at the all-Continue root, so continuity makes their
Continue inequalities hold for a sufficiently small Quit probability.  This
is a positive-absorption support-`delta'` root at `v_infty`.  By the same
tolerance-slack argument it is support-`delta` at all large `v_t`,
contradicting maximality and `a(q(v_t))->0`.  Hence (20.3).

The first arm becomes a literal `QuittingFiniteForwardPacket` whenever the
selected orbit also remains above the packet's punishment floor.  That
rationality is not automatic for the unrestricted maximizing selector, so
Theorem 20.1 by itself is a geometric support--Bellman trichotomy, not a new
producer for the public packet structure.  This is the one additional datum
needed to feed arm 1 to the existing projective lasso compiler.

## 27. The only viable degree target: apex index versus the global minimum

The local contribution at joint infinity can be stated without choosing a
continuous extension of `E`.  Put `h_i=1-p_i` and let `M` be the normalized
singleton matrix.  Near `h=0`, stationary gain complementarity linearizes to

```text
h >= 0,       M h >= 0,       h_i (M h)_i=0.            (27.1)
```

Indeed `G(h)=-Mh+O(|h|^2)`, a positive Continue probability requires
`G_i<=0`, and a positive Quit probability requires `G_i>=0`.  Thus the
checked `no_homogeneous` field says exactly that the apex has no nonzero
first-order complementary direction.

For a generic inward vector `b`, the one-sided local index is the LCP degree
of

```text
Phi_M(x)=x^+-M x^-,                                    (27.2)
```

evaluated at `q=-b`.  A complementary solution with hazard weight `w`
satisfies

```text
w>=0,       z=-b+Mw>=0,       w_i z_i=0.               (27.3)
```

For a regular solution its contribution is the sign of the complementary
basis determinant.  The full-core hard fields make `M` an `R0` matrix and a
standard Q-matrix, so (27.2) is proper and (27.3) is solvable for every `b`.
They do not assert that its signed degree is zero.  Section 25 computes degree
`+1` for the paired-singleton matrix at `b=1`.

### Theorem 27.1 (terminal translation leaves the apex field fixed)

Fix any reward table `r` and any playerwise vector `c`.  Define a translated
terminal table by

```text
r^c_S(i)=r_S(i)+c_i                                    (27.4)
```

for every nonempty quitting coalition `S`.  Then:

```text
normalizedSoloMatrix(r^c)=normalizedSoloMatrix(r),
quittingStationaryGain(r^c,p,i)=quittingStationaryGain(r,p,i)     (27.5)
```

for every stationary root `p` and player `i`.  In particular the complete
nonlinear cleared field, its apex derivative, and every local LCP index are
unchanged.

If `sigma_infty` is the literal all-Continue behavioral profile, its translated
semantic debt is instead

```text
d_i^infty(r^c)=max(r_{ {i} }(i)+c_i,0).                (27.6)
```

Consequently the all-Continue debt vector can be made strictly positive, and
as large as desired coordinatewise, without changing the apex index.  If the
common normalized matrix has full normal core, punishment normality also
survives: the checked theorem
`all_punishmentNormal_of_normalCore_eq_univ` derives it from that unchanged
matrix.

#### Proof

The first identity in (27.5) follows by subtracting player `i`'s own singleton
row from every singleton row: the same `c_i` cancels.  For the second, write
`c_-i(p)` for the probability that every opponent Continues.  Translating the
pure-Quit value adds `c_i`, while translating the unnormalized absorbing
contribution after `i` Continues adds `(1-c_-i(p))c_i`.  These additions cancel
in

```text
G_i=(1-c_-i(p))Q_i-N_i.
```

Against opponents who Continue forever, an arbitrary strategy of `i` either
Quits alone at some finite date or never Quits.  Its expected payoff is
`alpha(r_{ {i} }(i)+c_i)` for some `0<=alpha<=1`; the source payoff is zero.
Taking the supremum over `alpha` proves (27.6). QED.
