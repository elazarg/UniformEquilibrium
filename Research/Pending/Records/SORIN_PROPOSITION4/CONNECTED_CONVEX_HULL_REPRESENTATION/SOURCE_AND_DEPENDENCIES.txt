classical proof is Bernard Teissier, Monomial Ideals, Binomial Ideals,
Polynomial Ideals, MSRI Publications 51 (2004), Proposition 4.2, pp.220-221:
https://library.slmath.org/books/Book51/files/07teissier.pdf
Teissier explicitly follows Hanner--Radstrom (1951). Their proof considers
the reflected simplex cones. An actual source point on a cone boundary
replaces two old vertices by one source point. If no boundary meets the
source, the cones partition it into nonempty relatively open pieces.

SOURCE-PROOF ADJUSTMENTS
The implementation expresses those same cones by strict maxima of
barycentric coordinates normalized by the internally chosen positive
coordinates of the target. A tied maximum gives the boundary replacement;
continuity of the finite discrete winner label contradicts preconnectedness.
The normalized construction avoids selecting an auxiliary Euclidean metric
or spelling out the reflected-simplex cones. It is the same classical proof,
not a path connectedness argument or a favorable supplied simplex premise.
The connected-set case is proved; the more general component-count theorem
printed by Teissier is not claimed here. Dimension zero is handled by the
bound max(1, finrank); membership itself rules out an empty source.

EXACT API AND QUANTIFIERS
For every real finite-dimensional normed vector space E, every subset source
of E that is IsPreconnected, and every actual point of convexHull R source,
the theorem internally selects a nonempty finite family contained in source,
whose convex hull contains the original point and whose cardinality is at
most max(1, Module.finrank R E). No compactness, path, supplied coefficients,
supplied simplex, maximizer, finite source, or source-nonemptiness premise.
The strict-winner lemma is separately reusable for an arbitrary topological
source and a finite nontrivial collection of continuous real scores.
