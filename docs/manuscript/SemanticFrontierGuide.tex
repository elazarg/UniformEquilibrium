\title{\vspace{-1.2cm}\textbf{A Semantic Frontier for Finite Quitting Games}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This guide organizes the remaining obstruction to uniform equilibrium in
finite quitting games.  A behavioral profile is encoded by its prescribed
terminal payoff together with its coordinatewise unilateral best-response
payoff; under one-row prefixing, this pair evolves by a finite-dimensional
transport law.  If uniform equilibrium fails, the closed attainable set has
a positive minimum of total debt, and a stopping-law reduction expresses the
obstruction through five coupled alternatives.  In the positive-collision
alternatives, an exact repair cannot retain both the same mixed-action root
and the same continuation value: it must change one of them or incur a
definite Nash error.  Algebraic necessary conditions and invariant-set
criteria further restrict the counterexample search, but do not themselves
produce a counterexample.
\end{abstract}

All mathematical assertions in this note have been formalized in Lean.

\section{Finite quitting games and uniform equilibrium}
\label{sec:quitting-games}

Fix a finite nonempty player set $I$.  At every stage each player chooses
Continue or Quit.  The first nonempty quitting coalition $S$ absorbs the
game and gives player $i$ the constant payoff $r_i(S)$ thereafter; perpetual
continuation pays $0$.  Thus the data are the finite table
\[
 r=(r_i(S))_{i\in I,\,\varnothing\ne S\subseteq I}.
\]
A behavioral profile is denoted by $\sigma$, a unilateral behavioral
deviation by $\tau_i$, and expected $T$-stage average payoff by $U_i^T$.
We use the standard uniform quantifier order: $v\in\R^I$ is a
uniform-equilibrium payoff if
\[
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\sigma\ \exists T_0\
 \ \forall T\ge T_0:
 \quad
 \begin{cases}
 U_i^T(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i})\le U_i^T(\sigma)+\varepsilon
     &\text{for every }i\text{ and every }\tau_i,\\
 |U_i^T(\sigma)-v_i|\le\varepsilon&\text{for every }i.
 \end{cases}
\]
Write $\UE(r)$ for existence of such a vector.  For infinite play, write
$U_i(\sigma)$ for expected terminal payoff.  For each behavioral profile
$\sigma$, define player $i$'s unilateral best-response payoff against the fixed
opponents by
\[
 B_i(\sigma)=\sup_{\tau_i}U_i(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i}).
\]
The terminal reduction is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:terminal-reduction}
 \neg\UE(r)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\gamma>0\ \forall\sigma\ \exists i,\tau_i,
 \quad U_i(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i})-U_i(\sigma)\ge\gamma.
\end{equation}
Thus one fixed $\gamma$ defeats every profile, with the deviator allowed to
depend on the profile.

Before absorption there is one public history of each length, namely a word
of unanimous Continue outcomes.  Thus every behavioral profile is exactly a
sequence of product rows $x_t=(x_{t,i})_{i\in I}$, called product roots when
viewed as one-stage prefix operators, with
$x_{t,i}\in\Delta\{\mathrm C,\mathrm Q\}$; this is not a stationarity
restriction.  Write $q_i=x_i(\mathrm Q)$, $c_i=1-q_i$, and
$c(x)=\prod_i c_i$.  If $h_t$ is the probability of reaching row $t$, its
unconditional stage-$S$ mass is
\[
 h_t\prod_{i\in S}q_{t,i}\prod_{j\notin S}c_{t,j}.
\]

\paragraph{State of affairs.}
The established conclusions and the live gap can be read as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item Every quitting game with at most three players has an ordinary
      uniform-equilibrium payoff.
\item Any four-player counterexample has full corrected core; its normalized
      singleton matrix is standard $Q$ and has no homogeneous simplex
      solution.  This algebraic candidate class is nonempty.
\item Failure of uniform equilibrium gives a positive semantic minimum and at
      least one of the five outputs in Section~\ref{sec:five-alternatives}:
      \begin{enumerate}
      \item There is a fixed signed terminal-coalition payoff comparison.
      \item Observer-absent terminal mass on an actual profile leads to a legal
            gain or to an account sensitive to cancellation or reversal.
      \item The singleton case gives a static coalition toggle or exact player
            deletion, with deletion excluded for a minimal counterexample.
      \item A negative collision gives an actual source row with a fixed
            outsider gain or a punishment-refusal bound, but no observer-debt
            control there.
      \item A positive collision gives an actual target row with a fixed legal
            deviator and vanishing observer debt, hence a uniform lower bound
            on same-fibre repair error.
      \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

The common live task is to turn whichever output occurs into a contradiction
while accounting for any change of root or continuation, or to construct a
genuinely coupled positive barrier.

\section{Semantic pairs, prefixes, caps, and complementarity}
\label{sec:semantics}

Associate to $\sigma$ the semantic pair
\[
 z(\sigma)=(U(\sigma),B(\sigma))=(u,b)\in\R^I\times\R^I.
\]
Define its debt coordinates by
\[
 d_i(z)=b_i-u_i\ge0,
 \qquad D(z)=\sum_{i\in I}d_i(z).
\]
For a profile we abbreviate $d_i(\sigma)=d_i(z(\sigma))$ and
$D(\sigma)=D(z(\sigma))$.  These are its coordinate and total terminal
exploitability against fixed opponents.  Here ``debt'' is simply this
single-profile best-reply advantage; $D$ is the note's additive aggregation
over players, not an accumulation over dates.
The compact semantic carrier is
\[
 \K=\overline{\{z(\sigma):\sigma\text{ is a behavioral profile}\}}.
\]
Its canonical min--max exploitability is
\[
 \eta(r):=\min_{z=(u,b)\in\K}\max_{i\in I}(b_i-u_i).
\]
Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eta-threshold}
 \eta(r)=\inf_\sigma\max_{i\in I}
       \bigl(B_i(\sigma)-U_i(\sigma)\bigr).
\end{equation}
A profile is a terminal $\varepsilon$-equilibrium exactly when the maximum
in \eqref{eq:eta-threshold} is at most $\varepsilon$.  Consequently every
$\varepsilon>\eta(r)$ is attainable and no $\varepsilon<\eta(r)$ is; the
endpoint need not be attained by a profile, although the minimum defining
$\eta$ is attained in the closure $\K$.

A number $\gamma$ is an admissible terminal gap
when every profile has an actual unilateral deviation of gain at least
$\gamma$.  The exact relation is
\[
 \gamma\text{ admissible}\Longrightarrow\gamma\le\eta(r),
 \qquad
 \gamma'<\eta(r)\Longrightarrow\gamma'\text{ admissible}.
\]
Thus $\eta(r)$ is the supremum of the admissible gap constants; equality at
the endpoint is not asserted because a unilateral best-response supremum
need not be attained.  In particular,
\[
 \neg\UE(r)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\eta(r)>0.
\]
This is unrelated to the playerwise punishment value
\[
 \chi_i=\inf_{\sigma_{-i}}\sup_{\tau_i}U_i(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i}).
\]
The former minimizes maximum exploitability over complete profiles; the
latter is a payoff level obtained by minimizing player $i$'s best-response
value over opponents' profiles.
Let $e_\infty$ be the semantic pair of perpetual Continue.  Its prescribed
coordinate is $0$; its cap is optimal stopping against opponents who never
Quit.  We call this terminal continuation Never.

Fix a product root $x$ and a continuation payoff $v$.  For
$A\subseteq I\setminus\{i\}$, let
\[
 p^x_{-i}(A)=\prod_{j\in A}q_j
              \prod_{j\in I\setminus(A\cup\{i\})}c_j
\]
be the probability that exactly the opponents in $A$ Quit.  Player $i$'s
two pure-action values are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_i(x_{-i})
   &=\sum_{A\subseteq I\setminus\{i\}}p^x_{-i}(A)
        r_i(A\cup\{i\}),\\
 C_i(x_{-i};v_i)
   &=p^x_{-i}(\varnothing)v_i
     +\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq I\setminus\{i\}}
        p^x_{-i}(A)r_i(A).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Only the scalar $v_i$ is used after unanimous Continue.  The payoff from
the prescribed marginal and its one-row Nash defect are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_i(x;v_i)&=q_iQ_i(x_{-i})+c_iC_i(x_{-i};v_i),\\
 \rho_i(x;v_i)&=\max\{Q_i(x_{-i}),C_i(x_{-i};v_i)\}-F_i(x;v_i)\ge0.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Prefixing a semantic pair $z=(u,b)$ by $x$ gives a new pair $T_xz=(u',b')$.
Its prescribed part is
\[
 u'_i=F_i(x;u_i).
\]
The cap $b_i$ is the standard unilateral best-reply payoff against the fixed
opponents.  At the prefixed row, player $i$ may choose its better current
endpoint; if it
Continues, its future best-response value is $b_i$.  Thus
\[
 b'_i=\max\bigl\{Q_i(x_{-i}),C_i(x_{-i};b_i)\bigr\}.
\]
This defines the continuous prefix operator $T_x$ on $(u,b)$; carrying the
cap coordinate is exactly what makes it independent of a representing
profile.

For an arbitrary root, the debt recursion has the exact form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:debt-recursion}
 d_i(T_xz)=\rho_i(x;b_i)+c(x)d_i(z).
\end{equation}
Thus changing the root is paid for by its Nash defect against the tail cap,
while changing the tail is discounted by the probability of unanimous
Continue.  This identity requires no Nash hypothesis.

A row is an exact Nash row against $v$ when neither pure endpoint improves
on the prescribed mixture, equivalently $\rho_i(x;v_i)=0$ for every $i$.
Writing
$\Delta_i(x;v)=Q_i(x_{-i})-C_i(x_{-i};v_i)$, the familiar
complementarity inequalities are
\[
 c_i\Delta_i\le0\le q_i\Delta_i.
\]
An exact Nash--Bellman edge additionally identifies the current payoff with
$F(x;v)$.  A cap--Nash prefix is different: for $z=(u,b)$, the root is an
exact Nash row against the envelope $b$, while the continuation actually
executed after unanimous Continue still has prescribed payoff $u$.  The
prefix recursion then gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cap-nash-scaling}
 d_i(T_xz)=c(x)d_i(z)\qquad(i\in I).
\end{equation}
Thus a cap--Nash play path transports prescribed payoff and unilateral cap
together.

For a finite word $w=x_1\cdots x_m$, put
$T_w=T_{x_1}\circ\cdots\circ T_{x_m}$.  Finite prefixes followed by
perpetual Continue generate the entire carrier after closure:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:carrier-generation}
 \boxed{\K=\overline{\{T_we_\infty:w\text{ is a finite word of product roots}\}}.}
\end{equation}

\section{The reduction and the quantitative obstruction}
\label{sec:reduction}

\begin{guidebox}{GuideRed}
\textbf{Reduction and open compatibility problem.}

If $\UE(r)$ fails, there is a terminal exploitability gap $\gamma>0$ and a
point $p=(u^*,b^*)\in\K$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:minimum-package}
 D_*=D(p)>0,
 \qquad \gamma\le D_*,
 \qquad D_*\le D(z)\quad(z\in\K).
\end{equation}
Nonnegativity of every debt coordinate gives the two-sided bound
\[
 \eta(r)\le D_*\le |I|\eta(r).
\]
The sum $D_*$ is used because the prefix transport and reached-row accounts
are additive over players; $\eta$ remains the exact min--max terminal
equilibrium threshold.

Global minimality of $p$ implies that the all-Continue root is exact Nash
against $u^*$, and in particular $u_i^*\ge r_i(\{i\})$ for every $i$.
Since $b_i^*\ge u_i^*$, the same root is also cap--Nash against $b^*$; its
Continue mass is one, so prefixing fixes the whole pair $p$.

The localization starts from one unilateral behavioral replacement and
expands its signed payoff difference into finitely many terminal-coalition
contributions.  A selected nonvanishing contribution yields either a direct
comparison or, after choosing a pure stopping time for an observer, a coupled
two-profile certificate whose four geometries are determined by whether the
observer belongs to the coalition and by the sign of that observer's payoff.

The target implication is to derive a contradiction from each
alternative without separating data that were obtained together.  It would
suffice to construct a point of $\K$ with total debt below $D_*$, a sequence
of Nash--Bellman edges returning to the same semantic pair while
incorporating the positive payoff comparison, or terminal
$\varepsilon$-Nash profiles for every $\varepsilon>0$.  Such an argument
must keep the relevant profile, reached row, continuation payoff, named
terminal event, unilateral replacement, and gain on one executable
play path.

Call an $\varepsilon$-Nash row a \emph{same-fibre repair} when it keeps both
the current product row and the literal continuation payoff after unanimous
Continue fixed.  Here and below ``literal'' means that the row, tail, or payoff
comes from the displayed behavioral profile, not from an unrelated limit or
auxiliary profile.  The first obstruction is quantitative: if a player has
gain $g>0$ from changing only its action at a reached row, then every
same-fibre repair satisfies
\[
 g\le\varepsilon.
\]
In particular, that row cannot be inserted unchanged into an exact
Nash--Bellman path or periodic cycle.  This rules out the proof architecture
that first localizes a profitable row and then treats the same fibre as an
exact equilibrium edge.  Any successful argument must
change the root, change the continuation payoff, or include the row as a
non-Nash step and account quantitatively for its error.  Replacing one
alternative by a finer case distinction does not solve this compatibility
problem.
\end{guidebox}

Fix any $0<\gamma<\eta(r)$; this is an admissible terminal gap.  The two
scales are not identified, but they satisfy $\gamma\le D_*$.  Indeed,
$\gamma$ lower-bounds literal terminal exploitability; this passes to the
closed semantic carrier, and maximum coordinate debt is at most total debt.
They still play different roles: $\gamma$ is a uniform terminal deviation
gap, whereas $D_*$ is the minimum sum of best-response debts on the compact
semantic carrier.  The reduction takes only $\gamma$ as quantitative input.  It
follows from this gap that there is a finite constant
$C_{r,\gamma}$ such that every finite exact Nash--Bellman prefix whose value
vectors stay above the punishment values satisfies
\[
 \sum_t\bigl(1-c(x_t)\bigr)\le C_{r,\gamma}.
\]
This bound on cumulative absorption is derived, not an independent
hypothesis or parameter.  For ordinary exact Nash--Bellman prefixing, total
debt is monotone: if $\pi_i(x)=\prod_{j\ne i}c_j$, then
\[
 d_i(T_xz)\le \pi_i(x)d_i(z).
\]
What is missing is a contraction rate controlled by $1-c(x)$.  For example,
fix $0<\epsilon<1$, let player $i$ Quit with probability $1-\epsilon$, and
let every opponent Continue surely.  Then
\[
 \pi_i(x)=1,\qquad c(x)=\epsilon,
 \qquad 1-c(x)=1-\epsilon.
\]
Unlike a sure-Quit row, this row has positive continuation mass
$\epsilon$.  Nevertheless, the ordinary Nash--Bellman estimate supplies no
contraction at all for debt concentrated on player $i$.  The capacity result
for these rows is therefore qualitative.

Cap--Nash words have the sharper identity already displayed.  If a finite
cap--Nash word ends at a literal profile $\sigma$, then
\[
 \sum_t(1-c(x_t))
 \le-\log\prod_t c(x_t)
 \le\log\frac{D(\sigma)}{D_*}.
\]
Writing $D^{\max}=\max_{z\in\K}D(z)$ gives the uniform bound
$\log(D^{\max}/D_*)$.  The distinction is the cancellation of the current
Quit mixture in both the prescribed payoff and the cap: it improves the
coordinate factor from $\pi_i(x)$ to the joint factor $c(x)$.  Exact
cap--Nash words exist at every prescribed finite length, while the displayed
upper bound is independent of that length.  Thus length can grow without
cumulative absorption growing: the added dates have bounded total hazard.

\paragraph{Concrete calibrations.}
Three explicit checks delimit what the general statements do and do not say.
The $\epsilon$-root above shows why ordinary Nash--Bellman rows lack an
absorption-mass-controlled contraction rate.  The iterated-core witness in
Section~\ref{sec:corrected-core} shows that corrected-core deletion genuinely
iterates.  Section~\ref{sec:barriers} excludes two natural coarse invariant-set
languages while leaving genuinely coupled barriers open.

\section{The five alternatives}
\label{sec:five-alternatives}

Let
\[
 R=\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq I}\sum_{i\in I}|r_i(S)|,
 \qquad
 N=1+(2^{|I|}-1)=2^{|I|}.
\]
The extra outcome counted by $N$ is nonabsorption.
Every alternative has a positive scale $\kappa$.  For alternatives 2--5 set
\[
 \ell=\frac{\kappa/4}{NR}>0.
\]
Positivity of the payoff comparison forces $R>0$.  The constant $\ell$ is a
proved lower bound on the selected profile's total terminal $S$-mass in the
observer-absent arm, and on a named unconditional stage-$S$ mass in either
collision arm.  The factors $1/2$ and $1/4$ are the constants
in the two quantitative outputs; no comparison between their witnesses is
intended.

The global gap $\gamma$ already says that some player can profit somewhere
from every profile.  Each alternative below adds localization or algebraic
structure that this global statement does not contain.

There is already substantial play-path structure before the ensuing alternative is
chosen.  The relevant suffix profiles can be placed after exact Nash-root
words of length $n+1$, with each root evaluated against the literal payoff
of the suffix following it, while the total debt of the prefixed profiles
tends to $D_*$.  These are arbitrarily long finite plays, not prefixes of one
infinite play: exact word length can increase while cumulative absorption
stays bounded, and the suffix moves to later dates while its payoff comparison
may escape to infinity.

\subsection{Alternative 1: a prescribed payoff comparison}
\label{alt:direct-comparison}

There are a player $a$ with $d_a(p)>0$, another player $o$, a number
$\kappa>0$, profiles $\sigma_n$, unilateral replacements $\theta_{a,n}$,
and a fixed nonempty coalition $S$ such that $o\ne a$ and
\[
 \frac{\kappa}{2}
 \le N\bigl(\Pr_{\sigma_n}[S]
       -\Pr_{\sigma_n[a\leftarrow\theta_{a,n}]}[S]\bigr)r_o(S)
\]
along a strict subsequence.  Thus the same player replacement, payoff
coordinate, and absorbing coalition give a uniformly positive signed payoff
comparison.  What is not supplied is a bounded position for this comparison
inside one infinite equilibrium play path.

\subsection*{Data shared by Alternatives 2--5}
\label{alt:shared-data}

The other output has the same types of $a,o,\kappa,\sigma_n,\theta_{a,n}$
and $S$, together with a pure stopping time
$q_n\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}$ for $o$.  Define
\[
 X_n=\sigma_n[a\leftarrow\theta_{a,n},o\leftarrow q_n],
 \qquad
 Y_n=\sigma_n[o\leftarrow q_n].
\]
The two profiles differ only in whether player $a$ uses the selected
replacement.  The stopping time $q_n$ is selected so that its associated
pure strategy approximates the observer's terminal best reply against the
target opponents in $X_n$, with error tending to zero; hence the observer
debt vanishes on the target side.  No corresponding best-reply statement is
supplied for the different opponents in $Y_n$.  The profiles satisfy
\[
 \frac{\kappa}{4}
 \le N\bigl(\Pr_{X_n}[S]-\Pr_{Y_n}[S]\bigr)r_o(S),
 \qquad d_o(X_n)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The four alternatives below are the mutually exclusive possibilities for
the position of $o$ in $S$ and the sign of $r_o(S)$.

To state two alternatives, recall the punishment value $\chi_i$ defined
above.
At a row $x$ where player $k$ is forced to Quit, its punishment-based
advantage from refusing to Quit is
\[
 \mathfrak b_k(x)
 =\max\{0,C_k(x_{-k};\chi_k)-Q_k(x_{-k})\}.
\]
This is not automatically the gain from following the literal continuation
of the profile, because $\chi_k$ replaces that continuation value.

\subsection{Alternative 2: observer absent}
\label{alt:observer-absent}

Here $o\notin S$.  Put $Z_n=X_n$ when $r_o(S)>0$ and $Z_n=Y_n$ when
$r_o(S)<0$.  Then
\[
 \Pr_{Z_n}[S]\ge\ell.
\]
There is one fixed $k\in S$.  For every row $t$ before $o$'s selected
stopping time, let $x_{n,t}$ be the row actually played by $Z_n$, let
$v_{n,t}$ be the payoff of its literal continuation after unanimous
Continue, and let $h_{n,t}$ be its reach probability.  Write
$c_{n,t,j}$ for player $j$'s Continue probability in $x_{n,t}$, and let
\[
 \widetilde x_{n,t}
   =x_{n,t}[k\leftarrow\text{sure Quit}].
\]
The terminal probability above is the sum (or infinite sum) of the actual
stage-$S$ masses $m_{n,t}$.  At every such $t$,
\[
 \max\left\{
   \max_{j\ne k}\rho_j(\widetilde x_{n,t};v_{n,t,j}),
   \mathfrak b_k(\widetilde x_{n,t})
 \right\}\ge\gamma,
\]
and the same inequality holds after multiplication by $m_{n,t}$.  Thus a
fixed member of $S$ can be forced to Quit without losing the $S$-event, and
the resulting row has either a large Nash defect for another player or a
large punishment-based refusal advantage for $k$.

The root $\widetilde x_{n,t}$ is counterfactual, but the comparison has also
been transferred back to the actual profile $Z_n$.  With explicit positive
constants, one obtains either a legal unilateral gain at $Z_n$, or a positive
lower bound for a finite sum of terms of the form
\[
 h_{n,t}c_{n,t,j}p^{x_{n,t}}_{-j}(A)
 \max\{r_j(A\cup\{j\})-\widehat r_j(A;v_{n,t}),0\},
\]
where $\widehat r_j(A;v)=r_j(A)$ for $A\ne\varnothing$ and
$\widehat r_j(\varnothing;v)=v_j$.  This positive-part sum is an eventwise
account, not a deviation payoff.  The legal pure-Quit gain uses the signed
sum over $A$; cancellation gives only
\[
 \max\{\text{pure-Quit gain},0\}
 \le \sum_A p^x_{-j}(A)
       \max\{r_j(A\cup\{j\})-\widehat r_j(A;v),0\}
\]
after including the same reach and action-probability factors.  A positive
lower bound on the right therefore does not by itself imply a profitable
deviation.  The other possibility is a row at
which player $j$'s better pure action reverses when $k$ is changed from
Continue to Quit.  In that case the corresponding
two-by-two difference of deviation gains is strictly positive and its loss
on the actual $k$-Continue row has a quantitative lower bound.  The open
tasks are different in the two branches.  The positive-part account must
first be converted into one legal deviation gain despite cancellation, and
only then placed in an equilibrium play path.  The reversal branch already
provides a quantitative difference of two one-row deviation gains; its missing step
is transport into, or use within, one such path.  Returning to an actual profile
has already been achieved in both branches.

\subsection{Alternative 3: singleton}
\label{alt:singleton}

Here $S=\{o\}$.  The compression theorem gives one of two
static conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item players $k\ne j$ and a nonempty coalition $Q$ with $k\notin Q$ such
      that
      \[
      r_k(Q)<r_k(Q\cup\{k\}),
      \]
      while, at the pure row where exactly $Q\cup\{k\}$ Quits, player $j$
      has a strictly profitable pure one-row action change;
\item deleting $o$ leaves a nonempty smaller quitting game with the same
      terminal gap $\gamma$.
\end{enumerate}
Positive punishment value, which occurs at an intermediate stage of the
proof, also produces the first alternative unless it already produces a
uniform equilibrium.  It is therefore not a third alternative.  This adds a
static payoff comparison or exact cardinal reduction, but it does not retain
the selected singleton row or join the static conclusion to its original
play path.

One may, without loss of generality, choose a hypothetical counterexample
with the smallest possible number of players.  In that refinement every
smaller nonempty quitting game has a uniform-equilibrium payoff, so the exact
deletion alternative is impossible.  The singleton conclusion then consists
only of the static coalition-insertion toggle and its unstable pure row.  The
play-path attachment problem is unchanged.

\subsection{Alternative 4: negative collision}
\label{alt:negative-collision}

Here $o\in S$, $|S|>1$, and $r_o(S)<0$.  Each $q_n$ is finite.  At an actual
reached row $t_n$ of the source profile $Y_n$, the observer Quits surely and
the unconditional stage-$S$ mass is at least $\ell$.  Let $x_n$ be the row
played there, $v_n$ the literal continuation payoff after unanimous
Continue, $h_n$ the probability of reaching $t_n$, and abbreviate
$\rho_{j,n}=\rho_j(x_n;v_{n,j})$.  At this selected row, for each $n$, either
some $j\ne o$ has
\[
 \rho_{j,n}\ge\gamma,
 \qquad g_{j,n}=h_n\rho_{j,n}\ge\ell\gamma,
\]
where $g_{j,n}$ is the exact gain from a legal one-row endpoint deviation at
that same source, or
\[
 \mathfrak b_o(x_n)\ge\gamma,
 \qquad \ell\gamma\le
   (\text{stage-}S\text{ mass})\,\mathfrak b_o(x_n).
\]
After passing to a further strict subsequence, this dichotomy can be frozen:
either the refusal alternative holds at every selected row, or one fixed
$j\ne o$ has the displayed defect and legal gain at every selected row.
In addition, the common reached-row lemma below gives
\[
 D_*-\frac{d_o(Y_n)}{\ell}
 \le\sum_{j\ne o}\rho_{j,n}.
\]
This adds an actual source row and either an actual outsider gain or a
weighted punishment bound.  The construction controls $d_o(X_n)\to0$, not
$d_o(Y_n)$, and supplies no estimate transporting this debt across player
$a$'s full strategy replacement.

\subsection{Alternative 5: positive collision}
\label{alt:positive-collision}

Here $o\in S$, $|S|>1$, and $r_o(S)>0$.  After a strict subsequence and
discarding finitely many terms, $q_n$ is finite and an actual reached row
$t_n$ of the target profile $X_n$ has $o$ Quit surely and stage-$S$ mass at
least $\ell$.  Let $x_n$ be the row played there, $v_n$ its literal
continuation payoff after unanimous Continue, $h_n$ the probability of
reaching $t_n$, and put
\[
 \rho_{j,n}=\rho_j(x_n;v_{n,j}),\qquad \eta_n=d_o(X_n)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Then
\[
 D_*-\frac{\eta_n}{\ell}\le\sum_{j\ne o}\rho_{j,n}.
\]
After a further strict subsequence, one fixed $j\ne o$ has a legal one-row
endpoint deviation from the same $X_n$ with exact gain
\[
 g_{j,n}=h_n\rho_{j,n},
 \qquad
 \frac{\ell D_*}{2}\le(|I|-1)g_{j,n}.
\]
This retains one fixed deviator, the actual profile, reached row, product
mixed action, literal continuation payoff, coalition event, and uniformly
positive gain.  The lower bound uses $D_*$, not $\gamma$.  Its very
positivity is what makes the general same-fibre obstruction quantitative.

If $o\in S$, $|S|>1$, and $r_o(S)=0$, the signed payoff comparison is zero,
so the strict inequality is impossible.  Thus the four
configurations are exhaustive and mutually exclusive for the fixed
two-profile comparison.

\section{The common surely-absorbing reached-row lemma}
\label{sec:reached-row}

Let $p\in\K$ minimize $D$.  Consider any literal profile $\pi$, a stage $t$,
and a number $\ell>0$.  Assume only that the row is reached with probability
$h_t\ge\ell$, and that observer $o$ Quits surely at its live product row
$x$.  Let $v$ be the literal payoff of the tail after unanimous continuation
at that row.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reached-row}
 \boxed{
 D(p)-\frac{d_o(\pi)}{\ell}
 \le\sum_{j\ne o}\rho_j(x;v_j).}
\end{equation}
For every $j\ne o$, replacing only $j$'s action at that row by its better pure
endpoint and then following the same tail is a legal behavioral deviation
from $\pi$, with exact terminal gain
\[
 g_j=h_t\rho_j(x;v_j).
\]

A named coalition with unconditional stage mass at least $\ell$ is used in
the collision alternatives only to certify $h_t\ge\ell$; its identity plays
no role in this lemma.  Equivalently, if the row's joint Continue mass is
zero, some sure quitter exists and the conclusion holds after selecting one.
Zero joint Continue mass alone does not identify a prescribed observer.  The
lemma is valuable because it localizes debt on an executable row; the global
terminal gap alone does not supply that play path.

\section{The same-fibre repair obstruction}
\label{sec:same-fibre}

Consider a profile $\pi$, a reached stage $t$, player $i$, the product row
$x$ played there, and the payoff $v$ of the literal continuation after
unanimous Continue.  An $\varepsilon$-Nash \emph{same-fibre repair} uses this
fixed literal product row $x$ and fixed literal next prescribed continuation
payoff $v$.  Every such repair satisfies, for $\varepsilon\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:same-fibre-bound}
 \rho_i(x;v_i)\le\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \boxed{g_i=h_t\rho_i(x;v_i)\le\varepsilon,}
\end{equation}
where $g_i$ is the terminal gain from changing only player $i$'s action at
stage $t$ to its better pure endpoint and then following the original tail.
The equality is exact; the last inequality uses $0\le h_t\le1$.

Consequently, if $g_i>0$, no exact Nash--Bellman edge can simultaneously
match the source payoff, use the same product row $x$, and have literal next
prescribed continuation payoff $v$.
Exact complementarity would set $\rho_i(x;v_i)=0$.  Requiring the entire
behavioral source or tail to agree is stronger, so it cannot help.  The
unchanged source profile is not a terminal $\varepsilon$-equilibrium for
any $\varepsilon<g_i$.

Apply this statement to the rows in the positive-collision alternative,
Section~\ref{alt:positive-collision}.  After relabelling the selected
subsequence, for every selected $n$ and every $\varepsilon\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:positive-collision-repair-floor}
 \left.
 \begin{array}{c}
 \text{the fixed literal row and literal continuation form a}\\
 \text{same-fibre }\varepsilon\text{-Nash--Bellman repair}
 \end{array}
 \right\}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{
 \varepsilon\ge g_{j,n}\ge
   \frac{\ell D_*}{2(|I|-1)}>0.}
\end{equation}
Consequently no sequence of same-fibre repairs can have Nash error tending
to zero.

There is also an exact potential for changes that preserve every opponent's
entire behavioral strategy.  If only player $i$ replaces its strategy, then
its best-response envelope is unchanged.  Writing
$\sigma'=(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i})$, one has
\[
 B_i(\sigma')=B_i(\sigma),
 \qquad
 d_i(\sigma')=d_i(\sigma)-
   \bigl(U_i(\sigma')-U_i(\sigma)\bigr).
\]
Hence a positive payoff gain is exactly a decrease of that player's debt.
If the replacement is only $\varepsilon$-optimal against the same opponents,
then any amount $g\le d_i(\sigma)$ obeys
\[
 g\le d_i(\sigma)-d_i(\sigma')+\varepsilon.
\]
This accounts for simultaneous changes to that player's root and tail, but
does not cover transports that also change an opponent's strategy.

This rules out one precise proof architecture: localize a profitable
root--continuation fibre, insert the row and literal continuation unchanged
into an exact Nash--Bellman path or periodic cycle, and invoke the resulting
exact play path.  The five alternatives
are not contradicted by this result.  A viable argument must instead:
\begin{itemize}
\item change the root and quantify how much literal event mass and gain are
      retained;
\item change the continuation payoff and account for the transported value;
\item place the row in an approximate play path and pay Nash error at least
      as large as the retained gain; or
\item retain it as a non-Nash row and offset its positive defect in an
      explicit telescoping account that returns to the same semantic state.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{A singleton-tight boundary calculation}

One natural solo-prefix iteration is completely explicit.  Suppose a
minimum point has total debt $D_*>0$, one owner carries all of that debt,
and the owner's prescribed payoff is its payoff from quitting alone; denote
the owner by $h$.  Global
minimality then gives, for every nonowner $j$,
\[
 u_j-r_j(\{j\})\ge D_*.
\]
Let
the owner Quit with probability $q\in(0,1]$ while every other player
Continues.  Assume there is $L\ge0$ such that every other player's payoff
increase from joining the owner is at most $L$ and
\[
 qL\le(1-q)D_*.
\]
For a nonowner $j$, the Quit-minus-Continue endpoint difference is at most
\[
 \begin{aligned}
Q_j-C_j
&=q\bigl(r_j(\{h,j\})-r_j(\{h\})\bigr)
   +(1-q)\bigl(r_j(\{j\})-u_j\bigr)\\
 &\le qL-(1-q)D_*\le0;
 \end{aligned}
\]
the owner's two endpoints agree by singleton tightness.  Thus this row is
exact Nash against the prescribed continuation, but its
owner defect against the retained cap is $qD_*$.  The exact cap account is
therefore
\[
 D_*=qD_*+(1-q)D_*;
\]
the solo prefix preserves the minimum face rather than contracting the
owner's debt.

Finite repetition converges, as a sequence of prefixed semantic pairs, to
the payoff vector generated by the owner's singleton outcome while retaining
the cap $r_h(\{h\})+D_*$ at the owner.  The actual stationary solo profile
instead has owner cap $\max\{r_h(\{h\}),0\}$.  The two semantic pairs agree
exactly when
\[
 \max\{r_h(\{h\}),0\}=r_h(\{h\})+D_*.
\]
Separately, the same hypotheses together with
$r_h(\{h\})\ge0$ imply that the stationary solo profile has the owner's
singleton payoff vector as a uniform-equilibrium payoff.  This conclusion
uses the stationary best-reply comparison and does not follow from agreement
of the two displayed semantic limits.  The finite iteration itself does not
turn a positive-debt minimum into an exact cap--Nash play path; the retained
owner cap is the precise obstruction.

\section{The corrected-core standard-Q restriction}
\label{sec:corrected-core}

Define the normalized singleton comparison matrix
\[
 M_{ij}=r_i(\{j\})-r_i(\{i\}).
\]
Every finite real zero-diagonal matrix is realized by some quitting table as
such a normalized singleton-comparison matrix.

Starting with $I_0=I$, recursively delete players that have no distinct
nonpositive comparison inside the current set:
\[
 I_{n+1}=\{i\in I_n:\exists j\in I_n,
             \ j\ne i,\ M_{ij}\le0\},
 \qquad I_*=\bigcap_{n\ge0}I_n.
\]
The first step
\[
 I_1=\{i:\exists j\ne i,\ M_{ij}\le0\}
\]
is the ordinary one-step normal-player screen.  The core used here is
iterated: a player survives the next round only if it has a nonpositive
comparison with another player who has also survived.  Deleting a witness
can therefore trigger further deletions, so $I_*$ can be strictly smaller
than $I_1$.

\paragraph{Iteration witness.}
The phenomenon already occurs for
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&1&1\\[-1mm]
 -1&0&1\\[-1mm]
 1&-1&0
 \end{pmatrix}:
 \qquad I_1=\{2,3\},\quad I_2=\{3\},\quad I_3=\varnothing.
\]
In particular, a matrix statement about the principal matrix on
$I_*$ is not a statement about the principal matrix on $I_1$, and neither is
a statement about the full matrix.

Let $M_*$ be the principal matrix indexed by $I_*$.  A homogeneous simplex
solution is a vector $z\ge0$ such that
\[
 \sum_i z_i=1,
 \qquad M_*z\ge0,
 \qquad z_i(M_*z)_i=0\quad(i\in I_*).
\]
The matrix $M_*$ is a standard $Q$-matrix when, for every
$q\in\R^{I_*}$, there exist $z,w\ge0$ satisfying
\[
 w=q+M_*z,
 \qquad z_iw_i=0\quad(i\in I_*).
\]

The necessary condition is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:core-q-gate}
 \boxed{
 \neg\UE(r)\Longrightarrow
 I_*\ne\varnothing,\quad
 M_*\text{ has no homogeneous simplex solution},\quad
 M_*\text{ is standard }Q.}
\end{equation}
The empty corrected core, the homogeneous branch, and the ordinary non-$Q$
branch each produce an ordinary uniform-equilibrium payoff.  This theorem is
about the corrected-core principal matrix $M_*$, not the full matrix $M$.
It is a necessary condition for a counterexample, not a sufficient one, and
it does not close the standard-$Q$ side of the game-theoretic problem.  The
notion of a $Q$-matrix is standard; the additional content here is that the
game-theoretic argument passes through every round of the distinct-witness
deletion, and that the empty-core, homogeneous, and non-$Q$ outcomes on the
stabilized core have all been converted to ordinary uniform-equilibrium
payoffs.

The recursion is an algebraic screen, not a reduction of the quitting game:
it forms a principal singleton-comparison matrix but does not remove actions
or restrict the coalition-payoff table.  The three equilibrium constructions above
return equilibria of the original game.  This is distinct from exact player
deletion, which restricts every coalition reward to a smaller player set and
must separately preserve the same positive exploitability gap.

For four players the necessary condition is sharper:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:four-full-core}
 \boxed{\neg\UE(r)\Longrightarrow I_*=I.}
\end{equation}
Thus any four-player counterexample has full corrected core.  The full-core
algebraic branch is nonempty.  For example, take
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&0&-1&2\\
 0&0&-1&2\\
 2&2&0&-1\\
 -1&-1&2&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
All four players survive every corrected screen; the matrix is standard $Q$
and has no homogeneous simplex solution.  This matrix only passes the
necessary algebraic screen; it does not supply a positive debt floor or a
counterexample.

\section{Invariant sets generated from perpetual Continue}
\label{sec:barriers}

There is an independent, constructive route for searching for a
counterexample.  For $\delta\in\R$, seek a set
$\mathcal B\subseteq\R^I\times\R^I$ satisfying
\[
 e_\infty\in\mathcal B,
 \qquad z\in\mathcal B\Longrightarrow T_xz\in\mathcal B
       \quad\text{for every product root }x,
 \qquad z\in\mathcal B\Longrightarrow D(z)\ge\delta.
\]
No closedness assumption and no inclusion $\mathcal B\subseteq\K$ are required.
Sure-solo and sure-joint terminal boundaries are already obtained by one
prefix from $e_\infty$, so Never is the only generator needed.  Finite-word
density yields the exact logical characterization
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:barrier-characterization}
 \boxed{
 \exists\mathcal B\text{ with the three properties above}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 D(z)\ge\delta\text{ for every }z\in\K.}
\end{equation}
The canonical witness $\mathcal B=\K$ is complete but gives no effective
description.\footnote{A finite sufficient condition is known.  Form the
debt-safe hull by convexifying prescribed payoffs and then permitting them
only to decrease, while permitting best-response caps only to increase.  If
the states reachable in at most $N+1$ prefixes lie in the debt-safe hull of
those reachable in at most $N$ prefixes, then that hull is an invariant
positive-debt barrier.  Whether $D_*>0$ forces this condition, or any rational
semialgebraic barrier, remains open.}  Evaluating the floor at the mandatory Never point
already gives the table-level ceiling
\[
 \delta\le \sum_{i\in I}\max\{0,r_i(\{i\})\}.
\]
If $\delta>0$, the barrier yields an executable
terminal exploitability gap
\[
 \frac{\delta}{2|I|}>0,
\]
where the factor $2$ pays for approximating a supremal best response by an
actual behavioral deviation.  Hence a positive barrier proves
$\neg\UE(r)$.

The description of the invariant set cannot be too coarse.  Say that
$\mathcal B$ is \emph{coordinatewise order-rectangular} if, whenever
$u,b\in\R^I$ satisfy $u_i\le b_i$ for every $i$, each $u_i$ occurs as the
$i$th prescribed coordinate of some pair in $\mathcal B$, and each $b_i$
occurs as the $i$th cap coordinate of some pair in $\mathcal B$, then
$(u,b)\in\mathcal B$.  Such a barrier would contain a diagonal pair $(s,s)$,
where
\[
 s_i=\max\{0,r_i(\{i\})\},
\]
and therefore would contain a zero-debt point.  On every nonempty finite
player set, a \emph{debt-saturated} barrier is also impossible.  This means
that membership is constant on each complete debt fibre: if $b-u=b'-u'$,
then $(u,b)\in\mathcal B$ exactly when $(u',b')\in\mathcal B$; equivalently,
once one pair of debt $d$ is present, the whole affine set
$\{(u,u+d):u\in\R^I\}$ is present.  Such simultaneous translations permit a
calibrated product-row prefix that contracts total debt below any positive
floor.  A successful barrier must therefore retain coupled absolute payoff
and cap levels.

\paragraph{Search scope.}
The corrected-core/LCP screen isolates, for four players, the full-core,
standard-$Q$, no-homogeneous-solution candidate space, and that space is
nonempty.  Coordinatewise order-rectangular and debt-saturated barrier
languages have been excluded; no positive coupled barrier has been produced,
and no finite coupled barrier language has a completeness theorem.  No
exhaustive search beyond these screens is claimed.

\section{The exact gap}

To prove
ordinary uniform equilibrium for every finite quitting game, it is enough---
and, given the reduction, equivalent---to show that the positive-minimum
configuration cannot satisfy any of the five alternatives with all of its
displayed data coupled as stated.

The localized information consists of fixed players and terminal
coalitions, actual reached rows in the collision cases, exact unilateral
gain identities, arbitrarily deep finite exact prefixes, and a sharp lower
bound on the error of any same-fibre repair.  What is not supplied is a
single infinite or returning play path that preserves enough of one of
these quantities to contradict $D_*>0$.  The invariant-barrier formulation
poses the opposite task: construct such a positive minimum directly.  The
iterated-core standard-$Q$ condition restricts the finite payoff tables on
which either task can succeed.
