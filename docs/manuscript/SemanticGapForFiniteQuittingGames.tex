\begin{abstract}
The open problem is represented by prescribed-payoff/best-response pairs.
Failure of ordinary uniform equilibrium yields a positive minimum of total
debt together with finitely many localized stopping-law alternatives.  In
the positive observer-containing alternative, an unchanged mixed-action row and continuation
cannot be repaired with vanishing Nash error; the root or tail must change,
or the error must be paid explicitly.  Algebraic necessary conditions and
prefix-invariant positive-debt sets provide complementary filters for a
counterexample search.  This ledger states the localized alternatives, their
proved consequences, and the coupled implication equivalent to the problem.
\end{abstract}

All mathematical assertions in this note have been formalized in Lean.

\section{Semantic state and counterexample normal form}

Let $I$ be a finite nonempty set of players.  At every stage each player
chooses Continue or Quit.  If the nonempty set $S\subseteq I$ quits, the game
absorbs and player $i$ receives $r_i(S)$; infinite continuation pays $0$.
Put
\[
 R_r=\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq I}\sum_{i\in I}|r_i(S)|.
\]
Before absorption the only public history is a string of all-Continue
outcomes.  A behavioral profile is therefore a sequence of product
mixed-action rows.  For such a row $x$, let $c_i(x)$ be player $i$'s
Continue probability and put $c(x)=\prod_i c_i(x)$.

For an infinite profile $\sigma$, let $U_i(\sigma)$ be player $i$'s expected
terminal payoff and let
\[
 B_i(\sigma)=\sup_{\tau_i}U_i(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i})
\]
be its unilateral terminal cap.  Define
\[
 d_i(\sigma)=B_i(\sigma)-U_i(\sigma),
 \qquad D(\sigma)=\sum_{i\in I}d_i(\sigma).
\]
The compact terminal semantic carrier is
\[
 \K=\overline{\{(U(\sigma),B(\sigma)):\sigma
       \text{ is a behavioral profile}\}}.
\]
For $z=(u,b)\in\K$, write $d_i(z)=b_i-u_i$ and
$D(z)=\sum_i d_i(z)$.
The canonical min--max exploitability is
\[
 \eta(r):=\min_{z=(u,b)\in\K}\max_{i\in I}(b_i-u_i).
\]
Compactness makes this a minimum on the closure.  Equivalently,
\[
 \eta(r)=\inf_\sigma\max_{i\in I}
   \bigl(B_i(\sigma)-U_i(\sigma)\bigr),
\]
and no minimizing actual profile is asserted.  It is distinct from the
playerwise punishment value
\[
 \chi_i=\inf_{\sigma_{-i}}\sup_{\tau_i}U_i(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i}):
\]
$\eta(r)$ is the infimum of maximum exploitability over complete profiles, whereas
$\chi_i$ is a payoff level obtained by minimizing player $i$'s best-response
value over opponents' profiles.

A product row $x$ defines a continuous prefix map $T_x$ on semantic pairs:
play $x$ now and use the represented continuation after unanimous
continuation.  Let $e_\infty$ be the semantic pair of perpetual continuation.
For a finite word $w=x_1\cdots x_m$, put
$T_w=T_{x_1}\circ\cdots\circ T_{x_m}$.  Then
\[
 \boxed{\K=\overline{\{T_w e_\infty:
       w\text{ is a finite word of product rows}\}}.}
\]

Write $\UE(r)$ for existence of an ordinary uniform-equilibrium payoff.  Its
meaning is the following: for every $\varepsilon>0$, one behavioral profile
is an $\varepsilon$-equilibrium in every sufficiently long finite horizon,
and its expected finite-horizon average payoffs are eventually within
$\varepsilon$ of one fixed vector.  Its
negation has the exact terminal form
\[
 \neg\UE(r)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\gamma>0\ \forall\sigma\ \exists i,\tau_i,
 \quad
 U_i(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i})-U_i(\sigma)\ge\gamma.
\]
More precisely, every $\gamma$ satisfying the right-hand condition obeys
$\gamma\le\eta(r)$, while every $\gamma'<\eta(r)$ satisfies it.  Hence
$\eta(r)$ is the supremum of admissible terminal gaps; the endpoint need not
be attained by an actual deviation.  Equivalently,
\[
 \neg\UE(r)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\eta(r)>0.
\]
Fix any $0<\gamma<\eta(r)$; it is an admissible counterexample gap.  The
associated construction gives a
point $p\in\K$ at which total debt has a strictly positive global minimum:
\[
 D_*=D(p)>0,
 \qquad \gamma\le D_*,
 \qquad D_*\le D(z)\quad(z\in\K).
\]
Since all debt coordinates are nonnegative, minimization of maximum and
total debt gives
\[
 \boxed{\eta(r)\le D_*\le |I|\eta(r).}
\]
The inequality $\gamma\le D_*$ follows by comparing the terminal
exploitability gap with terminal exploitability at every realized semantic
pair and then with the sum of its nonnegative coordinate debts.  The
counterexample package stores the terminal gap as its only scalar datum.
Finiteness of the canonical cumulative-absorption bound for exact
punishment-floor prefixes is derived qualitatively from the gap and is not a
second hypothesis.  For ordinary exact Nash--Bellman rows one has the
coordinatewise monotonicity
\[
 d_i(T_xz)\le\left(\prod_{j\ne i}c_j\right)d_i(z),
\]
but this supplies no rate controlled by $1-c(x)$.  Indeed, if one player
Quits with probability $1-\epsilon$, where $0<\epsilon<1$, and every
opponent Continues surely, then that player's displayed factor is one while
$c(x)=\epsilon$ and the row absorption charge is $1-\epsilon$.  For the
narrower cap--Nash class, exact joint
scaling does give the sharp finite-word bound
\[
 \sum_t(1-c(x_t))\le-\log\prod_t c(x_t)
 \le\log\frac{D(\sigma)}{D_*},
\]
where $\sigma$ is the literal terminal profile of the word.  Thus the
qualitative arbitrary-prefix capacity and the quantitative cap--Nash budget
are distinct statements.  Exact cap--Nash words exist at every prescribed
finite length, while the displayed upper bound is independent of that
length.  Increasing exact word length is therefore compatible with bounded
cumulative absorption.
It also gives realizing profiles approaching $p$, unilateral reset targets,
strict subsequences, and the stopping-law data below.  Every object in one
alternative belongs to the same reward table and the same construction.

\section{Row quantities and the common localization lemma}

For a product row $x$ and continuation payoff $v$, let $Q_i(x;v)$ and
$C_i(x;v)$ be player $i$'s payoff when its current action is forced to Quit or
Continue, respectively, while the opponents use $x_{-i}$ and $v$ is used
after unanimous continuation.  Let $F_i(x;v)$ be the payoff when player $i$
uses its prescribed marginal in $x$.  The one-row defect is
\[
 \rho_i(x;v_i)=\max\{Q_i(x;v_i),C_i(x;v_i)\}-F_i(x;v_i)\ge0.
\]
For every semantic pair $z=(u,b)$ and product row $x$, prefix debt satisfies
the exact decomposition
\[
 d_i(T_xz)=\rho_i(x;b_i)+c(x)d_i(z).
\]
Thus a root change is paid for by its defect against the tail cap, while tail
debt is weighted by joint continuation.  No Nash hypothesis is used.

Consider a literal profile $\pi$, a stage $t$, its product row $x$, and the
literal tail payoff $v$ after all players Continue at $t$.  Let $h$ be the
probability of reaching $t$.  Replacing player $i$'s current action by its
better pure endpoint and then following the same tail is a legal behavioral
deviation with exact terminal gain
\[
 g_i=h\rho_i(x;v_i).
\]

This same defect is the obstruction to literal exact return.  An exact
Nash--Bellman edge using the same root $x$ and the same continuation payoff
$v$ satisfies endpoint complementarity, and hence
\[
 \rho_i(x;v_i)=0\qquad(i\in I).
\]
Therefore $g_i>0$ rules out such an edge.  More quantitatively, for every
$\varepsilon\ge0$, if the same root--tail fibre is $\varepsilon$-Nash, then
\[
 g_i\le\varepsilon.
\]
The certificate may retain the literal source profile, reached stage, deviator,
and any named positive-mass terminal event.  The incompatibility already
holds for the weaker embedding test that matches only the source payoff,
root, and next continuation payoff.  Matching the whole source profile or
behavioral tail is stronger and cannot remove it.

Changing one player's entire behavioral strategy while keeping every opponent
fixed has an exact potential.  If $\sigma'=(\tau_i,\sigma_{-i})$, then
\[
 B_i(\sigma')=B_i(\sigma),
 \qquad
 d_i(\sigma')=d_i(\sigma)-
   \bigl(U_i(\sigma')-U_i(\sigma)\bigr).
\]
Hence a positive own-strategy payoff gain is exactly a decrease in that
player's debt.  If the replacement is only $\varepsilon$-optimal against the
same opponents, any amount $g\le d_i(\sigma)$ satisfies
\[
 g\le d_i(\sigma)-d_i(\sigma')+\varepsilon.
\]
This identity does not control transports that change an opponent's
strategy, which is precisely the mismatch between $X_n$ and $Y_n$ in the
negative observer-containing alternative.

The following single lemma supplies both observer-containing localizations.  Suppose
the row is reached with probability $h\ge\ell>0$ and an observer $o$ Quits
surely there.  Let
$e_o=d_o(\pi)$ be the observer's debt at the initial profile.  Then
\[
 \boxed{
 D_*-\frac{e_o}{\ell}
 \le\sum_{j\ne o}\rho_j(x;v_j),
 \qquad
 g_j=h\rho_j(x;v_j)\quad(j\ne o).}
\]
Indeed, the sure Quit action kills continuation for every $j\ne o$, so the
current-suffix debt of $j$ is exactly $\rho_j(x;v_j)$.  Global minimality bounds
the sum of current-suffix debts below by $D_*$.  Finally, the observer's
survival-weighted current debt is bounded by $e_o$, while $h\ge\ell$.  A
named coalition is used only to certify the reach bound.  Alternatively,
zero all-Continue mass at the row guarantees the existence of some sure
quitter and hence an existential version of the same conclusion; it does not
identify a prescribed observer.

The importance of this lemma is localization, not the bare existence of a
profitable deviation: the counterexample gap already supplies some global
deviation.  Here the deviator, profile, reached row, continuation, event, and
gain identity are retained together.

For the refusal alternative used below, recall the punishment value $\chi_i$
defined above.
At a row where $o$ Quits surely, let $O_o(x)$ be its obedience payoff.  Let
$P_o(x)$ be its payoff when forced to Continue, with continuation value
$\chi_o$ only in the event that every outsider also Continues.  Define the
atomic blocker certificate
\[
 \mathfrak b_o(x)=\max\{0,P_o(x)-O_o(x)\}.
\]
This is a punishment-value certificate, not the gain of a deviation followed
by the literal tail.

\section{Exhaustive stopping-law certificate}

For profiles $\sigma,\tau$, observer $o$, and terminal outcome $S$, set
\[
 A_o(\sigma,\tau;S)
 =\bigl(\Pr_\sigma[S]-\Pr_\tau[S]\bigr)r_o(S).
\]
Let $N$ be the number of terminal outcomes, including nonabsorption.  A
rectangle charge $\kappa>0$ has mass scale
\[
 \ell=\frac{\kappa/4}{NR_r}>0.
\]
The rectangle charge forces $R_r>0$.  The order of the atom's arguments is
important: the prescribed atom below is source minus reset endpoint, whereas
the rectangle atom is reset target minus source.

Every positive-minimum counterexample construction produces a prescribed
atom or one rectangle certificate.  A prescribed atom consists of an active mover
$a$ with $d_a(p)>0$, an observer $o\ne a$, a charge $\kappa>0$, a strict
subsequence, a fixed nonempty coalition $S$, source profiles $\sigma_n$, and
selected unilateral reset strategies $\theta_{a,n}$ such that
\[
 \frac{\kappa}{2}
 \le N A_o\bigl(\sigma_n,
       \sigma_n[a\leftarrow\theta_{a,n}];S\bigr)
\]
for every selected $n$.

The same atom-producing suffix can already be placed behind an exact
state-matched root word of length tending to infinity.  The total debt of the
whole prefixed profile tends to $D_*$, while the literal suffix and its reset
comparison are unchanged.  The finite-word theorem permits this increasing
length while cumulative absorption stays bounded.  What is not supplied is
one infinite chronology, or a bounded date on such a chronology, at which the
atom can be consumed.

A rectangle certificate consists of the same kind of mover, observer, charge,
subsequence, and coalition, together with pure stopping times $q_n$ for the
observer.  Define
\[
 X_n=\sigma_n[a\leftarrow\theta_{a,n},o\leftarrow q_n],
 \qquad
 Y_n=\sigma_n[o\leftarrow q_n].
\]
The certificate satisfies
\[
 \frac{\kappa}{4}\le N A_o(X_n,Y_n;S),
 \qquad d_o(X_n)\longrightarrow0.
\]
For a fixed rectangle certificate, exactly one of the following four
orientations applies.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Observer absent.}  If $o\notin S$, a fixed owner in $S$ is
      counterfactually forced to Quit on the rows carrying the selected
      $S$-mass.  On one fixed side of the rectangle the total mass is at
      least $\ell$ and decomposes over the rows before the observer's stopping
      time.  Every such forced row has either an outsider defect at least
      $\gamma$ or an atomic blocker certificate at least $\gamma$, with the
      corresponding stage-mass weighting.  No individual row is asserted to
      carry mass $\ell$.  These are counterfactual rows, not rows of the
      selected profile.  For both finite $q_n$ and $q_n=\infty$, the total
      lower bound can nevertheless be transferred to the actual profile
      $Z_n$.  Up to fixed constants depending only on the finite player and
      action sets, it yields at least one of the following: a legal behavioral
      deviation with positive payoff gain; a positive finite sum, over actual
      reached rows and opponent coalitions, of reach probabilities times
      positive pure-Quit payoff advantages; or an actual reached row at which
      one player's selected pure action is profitable when $k$ is forced to
      Quit but unprofitable when $k$ is forced to Continue.  The
      positive-part sum is not itself a deviation gain: the legal
      pure-Quit gain uses the signed sum over coalitions, so cancellation
      yields only an upper bound of the gain's positive part by the atom sum.
      A positive lower bound on that account therefore does not imply a
      profitable deviation.  In the last case the corresponding difference
      of the two one-row deviation gains is
      positive with a quantitative lower bound.  The positive-part branch
      first needs a conversion from eventwise accounting to one legal gain,
      before any chronological use is possible.  The reversal branch already
      supplies a quantitative difference of two one-row gains; its missing
      step is to transport or consume that difference along one exact
      equilibrium chronology.

\item \textbf{Singleton (impossible).}

\item \textbf{Negative observer-containing case.}  If $o\in S$ and
      $r_o(S)<0$, then for each $n$ a selected actual row of the source
      profile $Y_n$ has the observer Quit surely and stage-$S$ mass at least
      $\ell$.  The common lemma gives
      \[
       D_*-\frac{d_o(Y_n)}{\ell}
       \le\sum_{j\ne o}\rho_{j,n}.
      \]
      In addition, either an outsider has $\rho_{j,n}\ge\gamma$, hence a
      legal deviation at that same profile and row with exact gain
      $g_{j,n}=h_n\rho_{j,n}\ge\ell\gamma$, or
      $\mathfrak b_o(x_n)\ge\gamma$ with the corresponding weighted bound.

      The certificate controls $d_o(X_n)\to0$, not $d_o(Y_n)$.  The profiles
      $X_n$ and $Y_n$ differ by the mover's full reset; the certificate supplies
      neither a smallness bound for this reset nor an observer-debt transport
      inequality across it.  The exact negative observer-containing localization
      therefore retains the term $d_o(Y_n)/\ell$.
      Nevertheless the rowwise dichotomy itself can be frozen: after a
      further strict subsequence, either one fixed outsider $j\ne o$ realizes
      the outsider alternative at every selected row, with the exact gain
      identity and lower bound above, or the observer refusal alternative
      holds at every selected row.  This removes variation of the outsider
      and of the dichotomy, but not the uncontrolled debt term.

\item \textbf{Positive observer-containing case.}  If $o\in S$ and
      $r_o(S)>0$, then, after a strict subsequence and deletion of finitely
      many terms, the actual target profile $X_n$ reaches a marked row where
      the observer Quits surely and the stage-$S$ mass is at least $\ell$.
      Here $e_n=d_o(X_n)\to0$, so the common lemma gives
      \[
       D_*-\frac{e_n}{\ell}
       \le\sum_{j\ne o}\rho_{j,n}.
      \]
      After a further strict subsequence, one fixed $j\ne o$ and its better
      pure row action give a legal deviation from the same $X_n$, with
      \[
       g_{j,n}=h_n\rho_{j,n},
       \qquad
       \frac{\ell D_*}{2}\le(|I|-1)g_{j,n}.
      \]
      Consequently, for every $\varepsilon\ge0$, every
      $\varepsilon$-Nash repair with that same root and continuation
      satisfies the uniform floor
      \[
       \varepsilon\ge g_{j,n}\ge
         \frac{\ell D_*}{2(|I|-1)}>0.
      \]
      The profile, row, coalition, tail, and deviation are unchanged by this
      localization.  In particular $g_{j,n}>0$, so this literal root and tail
      cannot be an exact Nash--Bellman edge.  Exact repair must change the
      root, change the continuation payoff, or pay Nash error at least
      $g_{j,n}$; merely appending an exact suffix leaves the source deviation
      intact.
\end{enumerate}

The case $r_o(S)=0$ and $o\in S$ is impossible because the
rectangle atom is strictly positive.

\section{The exact open implication}

Let $\Front(r,p,\gamma)$ mean the disjunction just stated, with the reward table,
minimum point, profiles, reset targets, subsequence, mover, observer,
coalition, stopping times, rows, and literal tails coupled exactly as in the
construction.  The reduction is
\[
 \neg\UE(r)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists\gamma>0\ \exists p\in\K,\quad
 \begin{gathered}
 \gamma\text{ is a terminal exploitability gap},\\
 D(p)=\min_{z\in\K}D(z)>0,
 \qquad \Front(r,p,\gamma).
 \end{gathered}
\]

\begin{statementbox}{OpenRed}
\textbf{Exact unresolved implication.}
\[
 \boxed{
 \forall r\ \forall\gamma>0\ \forall p\in\K,\qquad
 \left[
 \begin{gathered}
 \gamma\text{ is a terminal exploitability gap},\\
 D(p)=\min_{z\in\K}D(z)>0,
 \qquad \Front(r,p,\gamma)
 \end{gathered}
 \right]
 \Longrightarrow\bot.}
\]
Together with the reduction, this implication is equivalent to
the finite quitting-game uniform-equilibrium conjecture.  This is a logical
equivalence, not an independently proved simplification of the conjecture.
\end{statementbox}

An adequate proof may derive debt below $D_*$, a return to the same semantic
state with every intervening Nash error explicitly bounded, or terminal
$\varepsilon$-Nash profiles for every $\varepsilon>0$.  It must use the
actual coupled certificate.  In particular, it must turn the
prescribed atom's arbitrarily deep finite exact-prefix placement into an
infinite or bounded-date chronological use, consume the observer-absent
Quit-defect atom or endpoint flip on one chronology, consume the negative
blocker arm, or use the positive target-row gain to fund a
controlled change of root or continuation payoff.  An exact edge on the
unchanged positive root--tail fibre is impossible, and an approximate repair
on that fibre costs at least the retained gain.  Thus any change must account
quantitatively for the coupled data it loses.  A further subdivision of one
alternative does not prove the boxed implication.

\section{Invariant synthesis for a counterexample}

For $\delta\in\R$, an invariant debt barrier is a set
$C\subseteq\R^I\times\R^I$ satisfying
\[
 e_\infty\in C,
 \qquad
 z\in C\Longrightarrow T_xz\in C
       \quad\text{for every product row }x,
 \qquad
 z\in C\Longrightarrow D(z)\ge\delta.
\]
No closedness assumption on $C$ is needed.  Prefix density gives the exact
characterization
\[
 \bigl(\exists C\text{ satisfying these conditions}\bigr)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 D(z)\ge\delta\quad\text{for every }z\in\K.
\]
For the reverse implication one may take $C=\K$; that proves logical
completeness but supplies no finite description.\footnote{A finite sufficient
condition is known.  Form the debt-safe hull by convexifying prescribed
payoffs and then permitting them only to decrease, while permitting
best-response caps only to increase.  If the states reachable in at most
$N+1$ prefixes lie in the debt-safe hull of those reachable in at most $N$
prefixes, then that hull is an invariant positive-debt barrier.  Whether
$D_*>0$ forces this condition, or any rational semialgebraic barrier, remains
open.}  If $\delta>0$, the barrier
gives a fixed terminal exploitability gap and therefore a counterexample.
Thus the constructive problem on this side is invariant synthesis: exhibit a
finite reward table, $\delta>0$, and an explicit set $C$ satisfying all three
conditions.

The mandatory point $e_\infty$ gives the immediate table-level ceiling
\[
 \boxed{\delta\le
   \sum_{i\in I}\max\{0,r_i(\{i\})\}.}
\]
There is also a dimension-free restriction on the form of $C$.  On every
nonempty finite player set, the three barrier conditions with $\delta>0$ are
inconsistent with membership depending only on the debt vector $b-u$.
Indeed, equality of debt vectors would then permit simultaneous translation
of prescribed payoffs and caps; a calibrated product-row prefix
would multiply every debt coordinate by an arbitrarily small common
Continue probability.  Thus a positive barrier must retain absolute payoff
and cap levels, not only their difference.

\paragraph{Auxiliary algebraic seam.}
For the singleton comparison matrix
\[
 A_{ij}=r_i(\{j\})-r_i(\{i\}),
\]
every finite real zero-diagonal matrix is realized by some quitting table as
such a normalized singleton-comparison matrix.  Define $I_0=I$ and
\[
 I_{n+1}=\{i\in I_n:\exists j\in I_n,
      \ j\ne i,\ A_{ij}\le0\},
 \qquad I_*=\bigcap_{n\ge0}I_n.
\]
Let $A_*$ be the principal matrix on $I_*$.  A homogeneous simplex solution
is a vector $z\ge0$ with $\sum_i z_i=1$, $A_*z\ge0$, and
$z_i(A_*z)_i=0$ for all $i$.  The matrix $A_*$ is standard $Q$ if, for every
vector $q$, there are $z,w\ge0$ with
\[
 w=q+A_*z,
 \qquad z_iw_i=0\quad(i\in I_*).
\]
The empty-core, homogeneous, and non-$Q$ branches each produce an ordinary
uniform-equilibrium payoff.  Hence the sharp restriction is
\[
 \boxed{
 \neg\UE(r)\Longrightarrow
 I_*\ne\varnothing,\quad
 A_*\text{ has no homogeneous simplex solution},\quad
 A_*\text{ is standard }Q.}
\]
This necessary condition is independent of the unresolved implication above
and is not another stopping-law alternative.  It is not a sufficient
condition for a counterexample.  The algebraic condition is satisfiable by
an explicit four-player quitting table, including one whose stabilized core
contains all four players.

For four players this restriction is sharper:
\[
 \boxed{\neg\UE(r)\Longrightarrow I_*=I.}
\]
Thus any four-player counterexample has full corrected core.  This full-core
algebraic branch is nonempty, but it has no complete classification here and
is only an input to invariant synthesis.
